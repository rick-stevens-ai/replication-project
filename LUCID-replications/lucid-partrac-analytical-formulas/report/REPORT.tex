\section*{Verdict}
\textbf{PARTIAL / analytical-figure replication.}

The paper's two published analytical formulas were transcribed and implemented from source text, and LET-dependent DNA-damage yield curves for the fitted ion set (H, He, C, Ne and additional light ions listed in Tables~1--2) were regenerated for SB, SSB, DSB, DSB clusters and DSB sites. Low-LET headline baselines match the paper's prose values, and the qualitative trends (monotonically decreasing SB/SSB, increasing DSB, RBE-shaped DSB-site curves) are reproduced. However, this is \emph{not} a full PARTRAC replication because (a)~the PARTRAC Monte Carlo simulator itself is closed in-house GSF/HMGU code, (b)~the raw simulation-point tables plotted as symbols in Figs.~1--5 are not deposited in the paper, supplement, or any companion dataset available here, and (c)~one identified domain-of-validity anomaly (H-proton DSB-site row with \texttt{N.A.} overkill parameters) causes unbounded extrapolation past the fitted LET range, in tension with the paper's own $\sim$15 sites/Gy/Gbp peak at 100--200~keV/\textmu m.

Because the paper's headline is analytical parameterization (the closed-form fits themselves), and those \emph{are} fully recoverable from the published Tables~1--2 alone, a case can be made that the analytical headline is exercised. The replication is nonetheless recorded as \textbf{PARTIAL} rather than upgraded to \textbf{REPLICATED} because the paper's central quantitative validation claim (``$<2\%$ RMS deviation from PARTRAC, DSB clusters up to $\sim$9\%'') cannot be independently checked without the underlying MC simulation points, and one formula-table combination is internally inconsistent with the prose.

\section{Model Reconstructed}

The paper compresses PARTRAC Monte Carlo outputs into two analytical yield formulas.

\paragraph{SB/SSB (Eq.~1):}
\begin{equation}
Y_{\text{SB,SSB}}(\text{LET}) \;=\; p_1 - (p_2\,\text{LET})^{p_3} - \frac{p_4}{1 + \log^2(\text{LET}/p_5)}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{DSB, DSB clusters, DSB sites (Eq.~2):}
\begin{equation}
Y_{\text{DSB}}(\text{LET}) \;=\; \frac{p_1 + (p_2\,\text{LET})^{p_3}}{1 + (p_4\,\text{LET})^{p_5}}
\end{equation}

with yield in Gy$^{-1}$~Gbp$^{-1}$ and LET in keV/\textmu m. Entries marked \texttt{N.A.} in Tables~1--2 cause the corresponding term to be dropped, per the paper's stated convention.

\section{Critique --- What Was Done vs.\ Paper's Headline}

\paragraph{What was genuinely exercised.} The paper's central deliverable is a pair of \emph{closed-form analytical parameterizations} of PARTRAC MC outputs (Eqs.~1--2) together with fitted coefficient tables (Tables~1--2). Both equations were transcribed from the source markdown and implemented as \texttt{code/formulas.py}; the fitted coefficients for the reported ion set were transcribed into \texttt{code/parameters.py} and used to regenerate the yield-vs-LET curves shown in the paper's Figs.~1--4. In that specific sense --- ``can the paper's fitted analytical surrogate be re-evaluated and used?'' --- the answer is yes.

\paragraph{What was NOT done.} Several things are missing that a full replication would demand:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The underlying PARTRAC Monte Carlo was not re-run.} PARTRAC is a closed in-house GSF/HMGU track-structure code; there is no public source release and no runnable binary in our environment. We produced no independent MC yield points against which the fits could be cross-validated.
  \item \textbf{The fitted coefficients were not independently re-derived.} Because we lack the raw simulation points (the symbols in Figs.~1--5), we cannot re-fit Eqs.~1--2 from scratch. We can only reuse the paper's own published coefficients. Any error in transcription or in the paper's own tables propagates silently through our curves.
  \item \textbf{The paper's key validation metric was not independently verified.} The paper claims $<2\%$ RMS deviation between fits and PARTRAC (DSB clusters up to $\sim$9\%). Without the raw simulation points this claim cannot be tested; we can only note that our re-evaluation of the fits matches the paper's plotted fit-line values.
  \item \textbf{No new ion/energy predictions were generated.} We stayed strictly within the fitted LET ranges. Extension to heavy ions beyond the paper's set, or to sub-keV/\textmu m electrons or very-high-LET regimes, would require the PARTRAC MC itself.
  \item \textbf{One internal inconsistency was surfaced but not resolved.} The H-proton row's DSB-site parameters lack the overkill $p_4,p_5$ denominator (\texttt{N.A.}), so evaluated past the fitted range the H curve grows without bound, contradicting the paper's prose peak claim. We logged this rather than silently patching it.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Verdict rationale.} If the paper's headline is defined narrowly as ``a working analytical surrogate for PARTRAC yields exists and is usable,'' our replication meets it. If the headline is defined more strictly as ``the surrogate has been validated against the underlying MC to the paper's stated tolerance and is safe to use across the fitted range,'' our replication does \emph{not} meet it, because the second half (validation against MC) is untestable here and because the DSB-site H-proton domain issue is a live counterexample. On balance --- and consistent with the queue's original PARTIAL verdict --- we keep \textbf{PARTIAL}.
