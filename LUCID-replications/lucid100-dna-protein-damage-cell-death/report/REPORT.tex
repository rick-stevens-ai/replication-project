\section*{TL;DR}
A closed-form 5-equation, 4-parameter mechanistic survival model that splits radiation-induced
bacterial cell death into a DNA-damage channel $Q_1$ (whose double-strand-break repair fidelity
decays with cumulative protein carbonylation $P$) and a direct protein-damage channel
$Q_2 = P^X$, with survival $S = Q_1 \cdot Q_2$. Re-implemented from the PMC JATS XML in pure
Python; forward-run for all 5 strain/condition rows under both $\gamma$ and UV. Qualitative
dominant-mechanism map (Table 2 of the paper) reproduced in \textbf{10/10 rows}. Quantitative
$S(D)$ diverges by 2--60 orders of magnitude at the high-dose tail because $F(D)$ is a
placeholder logistic, not the digitized Krisko \& Radman (2010) PNAS data used by the
original fit. Fitting was \emph{not} re-run; the paper's Table~1 best-fit parameters were used
as inputs. \textbf{Verdict: PARTIAL} (queue label ``REPLICATED'' was optimistic --- see
\S\ref{sec:critique}).

\section{Paper summary}
Shuryak \& Brenner propose a mechanistic radiation-survival model for prokaryotes that
explicitly couples two damage modalities:
\begin{align}
P(D)   &= 1 - \frac{F(D) - F_0}{F_{\max} - F_0} \\
Q_1(D) &= \exp\left[-K_{\text{dam}} D \exp(-K_{\text{rep}} P(D))\right] \\
Q_2(D) &= P(D)^X \\
S(D)   &= Q_1(D) \cdot Q_2(D) \qquad (S = Q_2 \text{ for } \lambda \text{ infective centers})
\end{align}
where $F(D)$ is cumulative protein carbonylation (from Krisko \& Radman 2010 PNAS data);
$F_{\max}$ is a saturation ceiling; $K_{\text{dam}}, K_{\text{rep}}, X$ are fit parameters.
The novelty is that DSB \emph{repair fidelity} (not DSB yield) is modulated by protein damage
--- proteomic oxidation kills the repair machinery, so accumulated $P$ inflates the effective
DSB kill constant. The model is fit jointly across 5 strain/condition rows
(\textit{D.~radiodurans} R1 WT and recA$^-$; \textit{E.~coli} MG1655 WT and CB1000/CB2000
radioresistant; $\lambda$ infective centers in \textit{E.~coli}) under $\gamma$ and UV, and
Table~2 tabulates which channel ($Q_1$ vs $Q_2$) dominates in each of the 10 cells.

\section{Method}
Model equations were transcribed verbatim from the PMC JATS XML
(\texttt{artifacts/paper\_oai.xml}) into
\texttt{scripts/smoke\_shuryak\_2012.py}, a pure-Python NumPy implementation.
Parameters from paper Table~1 were hard-coded: $F_{\max} = 8.5\ \text{nmol/mg}$,
$K_{\text{dam}}^\gamma = 10\ \text{kGy}^{-1}$, $K_{\text{dam}}^{UV} = 3.99\ \text{m}^2/\text{kJ}$,
$K_{\text{rep}} = 13.9$ (0 for recA$^-$), $X = 3.88$ (\textit{D.r.}) or $6.76$
(\textit{E.c.} + $\lambda$). $F(D)$ was represented by \emph{strain-specific logistic
placeholders} calibrated only to reproduce the qualitative shape of the Krisko \& Radman
2010 curves. Forward simulation over the paper's stated dose ranges produced
\texttt{results/summary.csv} plus 10 per-strain full-grid CSVs and two log-survival plots.
No refit was performed. Compute: $<1$~s on CherryRd CPU; no GPU; no paid endpoint.

\subsection*{Quantitative: end-of-range survival}
Placeholder $F(D)$ over-saturates $P \to 0$, and $Q_2 = P^X$ with $X \approx 6.76$ amplifies
small $P$ errors into many decades of $S$ error:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textit{D.r.} R1 WT, $\gamma$ at 20 kGy: $S = 6.7\times10^{-4}$ (paper $\sim 10^{-2}$)
        --- off by $\sim$2 orders.
  \item \textit{E.c.} MG1655 WT, $\gamma$ at 4 kGy: $S = 1.4\times10^{-61}$ (paper $\sim 10^{-5}$)
        --- catastrophically off, entirely traceable to the logistic placeholder.
  \item $\lambda$ IC, UV at 0.36 kJ/m$^2$: $S = 1.1\times10^{-11}$ (paper: low but finite)
        --- same root cause.
\end{itemize}
The $Q_1/Q_2$ \emph{ratio} is much more robust than either absolute value, which is why the
qualitative Table~2 map holds despite the quantitative divergence.

\subsection*{Was the protein-damage arm independently tested?}
No. This is the central identifiability concern. The protein-damage arm enters the model in two
places: (i) as a multiplicative kill channel $Q_2 = P^X$ with a strain-grouped exponent $X$,
and (ii) as a fidelity-modulator in $Q_1$ through $\exp(-K_{\text{rep}} P)$. The paper's fit
determines $(K_{\text{dam}}, K_{\text{rep}}, X)$ jointly against $S(D)$ observations while
using an independently-measured $F(D)$ curve (Krisko \& Radman 2010). Because $P$ is a monotone
transform of $F$, and $F(D)$ is empirically measured, in principle the two arms are
distinguishable --- but only if the $S(D)$ data are informative enough to break the degeneracy
between ``more protein-damage kill'' ($X$ large) and ``worse DSB repair fidelity''
($K_{\text{rep}}$ large). \textbf{This replication did not re-fit and therefore could not
audit that degeneracy}. A profile-likelihood or Fisher-information analysis on
$(K_{\text{rep}}, X)$ would be the right diagnostic. See open question Q1.

\subsection*{Was DNA-vs-protein contribution decomposable or degenerate?}
The paper's diagnostic is the $\log Q_i / \log S$ ratio (Table~2). This is a
\emph{decomposition of the fitted model}, not an independent orthogonal measurement. The
apparent ``mechanism assignment'' therefore inherits whatever priors the fit imposed. In the
recA$^-$ strain $K_{\text{rep}}$ is held at 0 (by biological argument: no repair fidelity to
degrade), which \emph{forces} $Q_1$ dominance. In WT strains $K_{\text{rep}} = 13.9$ is fit
freely, but any pair of $(K_{\text{rep}}, X)$ that gives the same $S(D)$ trace would produce
different Table~2 rows --- so the ``10/10 dominance match'' this replication reports is
partly a consequence of using the same parameters, not an independent test. The test would
need to include held-out cross-strain predictions.

\subsection*{Was the model validated on distinct cell-death modes?}
No, and neither did the paper. The output is a single scalar $S(D)$ = viable colony fraction.
Bacterial cell death is not partitioned into apoptosis / mitotic-catastrophe / necrosis in
this framework (nor is the mechanistic distinction as sharp in prokaryotes as in eukaryotes,
but there \emph{is} literature on programmed lysis in \textit{E.~coli} via SOS/RecA-mediated
prophage induction which would specifically affect the $\lambda$ IC row). The model conflates
all modes. This is a structural limitation, not a replication gap.

\subsection*{Low-dose vs high-dose regime}
The dominant-mechanism ratios shift with dose: $Q_2$ tends to dominate at intermediate doses
and $Q_1$ takes over as $P \to 0$ at very high doses (visible in the $D.r.$ R1 $\gamma$ row
where $\log Q_1 / \log S$ climbs from below 0.5 at moderate doses toward parity by 20 kGy).
The paper acknowledges this but does not stratify Table~2 by dose regime. This replication
inherits the same coarse-graining.

\subsection*{Quantitative divergence root cause}
Entirely the placeholder $F(D)$ logistic. Not a model bug. Fixed by digitizing Krisko \&
Radman 2010 Fig.~2 --- blocked because PMC/EuropePMC figure endpoints returned reCAPTCHA HTML
under headless fetch, and PNAS PDF returned HTTP 403.

\subsection*{Reproducibility of the fitter}
The author's FORTRAN random-restart simulated-annealing code is not on GitHub, Zenodo, or
Figshare. A SciPy \texttt{least\_squares} or \texttt{dual\_annealing} substitute is trivial
but would be an \emph{independent} implementation, not a fitter replication.
