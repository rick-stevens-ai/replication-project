\section{Paper Summary}
The Rusin et al.\ (2021) study asks whether \emph{dose rate}, at a fixed total dose (2 Gy $^{137}$Cs $\gamma$),
modulates the proliferation, cell-cycle distribution, apoptosis stage, gene expression (CD44, TP53),
and senescence response of primary human adipose-derived mesenchymal stem cells (hADSCs, ATCC PCS-500-011).
Two dose rates are compared: LDR = 1.40 Gy/min and HDR = 7.31 Gy/min (a $\sim$5.2$\times$ ratio),
against a 0-Gy sham control.

The core biological claim is that HDR induces a greater G$_2$/M arrest than LDR at Days 1--3 post-irradiation,
with only modest effects on total proliferation (MTS), a small elevation of late-apoptotic populations at
12 h and Day 2, no measurable TP53 up-regulation or nuclear p53 activation, and no SA-$\beta$-gal senescence
increase. The paper's mechanistic interpretation is that hADSCs handle 2 Gy at moderate dose rates
predominantly by transient checkpoint arrest rather than by committing to apoptosis or senescence.

\section{Method (Replication)}
This is an \emph{in-silico re-derivation} from the authors' openly deposited raw data. No wet-lab
irradiation was performed. For each quantitative figure the workflow was:
(i) parse per-replicate values from the Mendeley .xlsx source-data;
(ii) recompute per-condition mean and $n{-}1$ SD;
(iii) cross-check against the authors' typed summary block in the same sheet;
(iv) re-run pairwise Welch's $t$-test (\texttt{scipy.stats.ttest\_ind(equal\_var=False)}) across
\{Control, LDR, HDR\};
(v) compare each explicit paper-claimed pairwise significance to the recomputed $p$-value.
The paper used JMP Pro 12 one-way ANOVA with an unspecified post-hoc; Welch is used here as an
independent (not exactly-equivalent) check.

Replication scripts: \texttt{replicate\_prolif.py}, \texttt{replicate\_cellcycle.py},
\texttt{replicate\_apoptosis.py}, \texttt{replicate\_gene\_expr.py}, plus \texttt{find\_subset.py}
(diagnostic that isolated an off-by-one row-index artifact in the initial cell-cycle parse).
All scripts CPU-only, no LLM dependency. Free endpoints only.

\section{Results vs Paper}
\subsection{Cell-cycle phase fractions (Fig 3)}
27/27 mean cells and 27/27 SD cells reproduce within $\pm$0.05 percentage-point absolute tolerance
across 3 timepoints (Day 1--3) $\times$ 3 conditions (PC, LDR, HDR) $\times$ 3 phases
(G$_0$/G$_1$, S, G$_2$/M). Day 3 LDR uses $n{=}2$ because the third replicate row is a
\texttt{0,0,0} sentinel in the source spreadsheet (implicit exclusion).

\subsection{Apoptosis (Fig 4)}
48/48 mean+SD cells across 4 timepoints $\times$ 3 conditions $\times$ 4 stages
(Healthy / Early / Mid / Late) reproduce to $\le$0.005\%.

\subsection{Proliferation (Fig 1--2)}
The published standard curve $y = 160797\,x - 29124$ reproduces the ``Cell Number'' column exactly
from the absorbance triplicates. All 8 proliferation significance claims verified, including the
paper's headline ``no statistical difference except LDR at Day 5'' (PC$\leftrightarrow$LDR at 120 h:
paper $p=0.0350$, replication $p=0.0350$).

\subsection{Gene expression (Fig 5)}
CD44 and TP53 mean fold-changes match. 6/8 pairwise significance claims agree. The two disagreements
are the LDR-vs-HDR contrasts (CD44 Day 3, TP53 Day 3) where Welch $p \approx 0.10$ but the paper
declared significance --- consistent with a more-powerful JMP post-hoc (likely Tukey HSD with
pooled variance) on $n{=}3$.

\subsection{Aggregate significance-pattern agreement}
Fig 1 = 8/8, Fig 3 = 22/27, Fig 4 = 5/9, Fig 5 = 6/8. Total: 41/52 = 78.8\%. Disagreements cluster at
$p \in [0.05, 0.12]$ and are attributable to test choice, not data discrepancy.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED (PARTIAL on stat-pattern boundary).} All quantitative means and SDs reproduce to
numerical precision from the openly deposited raw data. The 11/52 pairwise-significance disagreements
sit on the Welch-vs-JMP-post-hoc boundary and do not overturn the paper's biological conclusion.
Verdict is preserved from the source REPORT.md.

\section{Critique (Honest)}
The replication is arithmetically strong but has meaningful methodological limits that should be
called out rather than papered over:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Statistical substitution is not neutral.} Welch's $t$-test and JMP one-way ANOVA
  with a Tukey/Dunnett-class post-hoc are not equivalent on $n{=}3$; they use different variance
  pools. The 79\% agreement is not a failure of the paper's data --- but neither is it a full
  vindication of the paper's inferential claims. Without the paper naming the JMP post-hoc, we
  cannot close the 11-cell gap.
  \item \textbf{Cell-cycle synchronization was not validated.} The paper does not stain or gate
  for a pre-irradiation baseline synchronization; the reported ``arrest'' is inferred from post-hoc
  phase-fraction drift, not from a validated synchronization checkpoint. The replication inherits
  this limitation --- it cannot distinguish arrest from differential mortality of phase-specific
  subpopulations.
  \item \textbf{Only two dose-rate points.} A single LDR vs HDR contrast at $\sim$5$\times$
  separation is a two-point curve, not a dose-rate response. The mechanism claim (dose-rate effect
  on hADSCs) is under-determined by two points; a monotonic or biphasic dose-rate curve is
  observationally indistinguishable here.
  \item \textbf{ADSC-specific findings are not compared to a baseline lineage.} The paper does not
  contrast hADSCs against a matched lymphocyte or fibroblast arm irradiated on the same rig at the
  same rates. Without that comparator, the ``ADSC-specific'' framing is under-supported: the
  observed G$_2$/M drift could be a generic MSC / adherent-culture response.
  \item \textbf{Image-only figures un-tested.} Fig 6 (p53 IF), Fig 7 (p21 IF), and Fig 9
  (SA-$\beta$-gal) are qualitative micrographs; Fig 8's normalized nuclear p21 xlsx exists but was
  not re-analyzed at per-nucleus distribution level. Coverage on strict per-figure count is 5/9
  ($\sim$56\%); rescoped to per-quantitative-claim units it is $\sim$88\%. Both numbers are true;
  neither should be quoted in isolation.
  \item \textbf{Passage number / donor variability not addressed.} ATCC PCS-500-011 is a
  primary-cell product with lot- and passage-dependent behavior. The paper does not report the
  passage used, and $n{=}3$ per condition cannot separate donor from dose-rate variance.
\end{itemize}

None of these overturn the numerical replication --- but they scope what ``REPLICATED'' can honestly
claim about the underlying biology.
