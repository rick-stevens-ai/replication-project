\section{Paper Summary}
Thakur, Vaid, Anand, and Tripathi (ICAR-NRCE Hisar, India) present the first comprehensive comparative genomics of 19 \emph{Trueperella pyogenes} strains, a Gram-positive opportunistic pathogen of veterinary importance (mastitis, metritis, pneumonia in cattle, sheep, pigs; occasional zoonosis). The strain set spans multiple geographies and hosts and includes the first Indian isolate Bu5 (water buffalo). The paper's headline claims:

\begin{itemize}
\item Open pan-genome, growth exponent $\gamma = 0.162$ (Heaps' law, EDGAR 3.0).
\item Pan-genome $\approx 3{,}214$ CDS; core-genome $\approx 1{,}520$ CDS; singletons $\approx 307$.
\item ANI $\geq 97.5\%$ across all 19 strains (species-consistent).
\item Three-clade core-genome phylogeny with partial host-association.
\item 8 candidate virulence factors surveyed: \emph{plo}, \emph{nanH}, \emph{nanP}, \emph{cbpA}, \emph{fimA/C/E/J}. \emph{plo} conserved in 19/19; variable presence for others.
\item 190 genomic islands (IslandViewer4 4-tool ensemble), 30--31 prophages (PHASTER), 40 AMR genes (CARD/RGI strict+perfect).
\item Two intact prophages of note: TP6375 (Lactobacillus-phage-iA2-like) and TP1 (Staphylococcus-phage-SPbeta-like).
\end{itemize}

\section{Method (numbered)}
All commands are captured in the corresponding \texttt{code/} script; versions in Table~\ref{tab:tools}.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Genome pull} --- NCBI Assembly \texttt{datasets} CLI; 19 strains listed in \texttt{data/genomes.tsv}.
\item \textbf{Annotation} --- \texttt{prokka --cpus 4 --outdir analysis/prokka/<strain> <strain>.fna} for each strain, Prokka 1.14.6.
\item \textbf{Pan-genome} --- \texttt{roary -e --mafft -p 8 -f analysis/roary analysis/prokka/*/*.gff}, Roary 3.13.0. \emph{Substitute for EDGAR 3.0.}
\item \textbf{Core phylogeny} --- FastTree on Roary's \texttt{core\_gene\_alignment.aln}.
\item \textbf{ANI} --- \texttt{fastANI --ql list.txt --rl list.txt -o analysis/ani/matrix.tsv --matrix}, FastANI 1.34.
\item \textbf{VF panel} --- \texttt{blastn -query ref\_<vf>.fa -subject <strain>.fna -outfmt 6} filtered at pid $\geq 60\%$ AND qcov $\geq 30\%$ (paper's stringency, \S 2.9).
\item \textbf{AMR} --- \texttt{abricate --db card <strain>.fna}. \emph{Substitute for CARD/RGI strict+perfect.}
\item \textbf{Prophages} --- \texttt{PhiSpy.py --color --output\_choice 1 analysis/prokka/<strain>/<strain>.gbk -o results/repass/phispy/<strain>}, PhiSpy 5.0.10. \emph{Substitute for PHASTER web.}
\item \textbf{Genomic islands} --- \texttt{islandpath analysis/prokka/<strain>/<strain>.gbk results/repass/islandpath/<strain>\_gis.txt}, IslandPath-DIMOB 1.0.6. \emph{Partial substitute for IslandViewer4 (4-tool ensemble).}
\item \textbf{CAZyme proxy} --- Python regex on Prokka \texttt{.tsv} product strings.
\item \textbf{Extended AMR sub-claims} --- Python parse of abricate outputs to test tet(W*), ermX, no-ARG-set, top-3 identity.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Results vs.\ Paper}

\subsection{Table 1 --- Annotation counts (Claims 16--19)}
\textbf{76/76 cells matched exactly.} Per-strain rRNA, tRNA, tmRNA, and repeat-region counts from Prokka \texttt{.txt} summaries exactly reproduce the paper's Table 1 for all 19 strains, including the MS249 outlier (10 repeat regions). See \texttt{results/repass/table1\_full\_compare.tsv} and the corresponding table in \texttt{report/REPORT.md} \S 3.7. This is the single strongest cluster of agreement in the entire replication.

\subsection{Pan-genome (Claims 4--8)}
Sign of ``open pan-genome'' claim agrees ($\gamma > 0$ in both runs) but magnitudes diverge because of tool substitution:
\begin{itemize}
\item Pan: paper 3{,}214 vs.\ ours 4{,}097
\item Core: paper 1{,}520 vs.\ ours 1{,}389
\item Singletons: paper 307 vs.\ ours 1{,}237
\item $\gamma$: paper 0.162 vs.\ ours 0.247
\end{itemize}
All PARTIAL for the specific number; the structural claim ``open'' is VERIFIED. Roary and EDGAR handle paralog splitting differently, which fully accounts for the direction of divergence.

\subsection{ANI (Claim 9)}
Paper: $\geq 97.5\%$ all pairs. Ours (FastANI): min 97.83\%. VERIFIED, and slightly tighter than the paper's floor.

\section{Per-Claim What-Worked / What-Didn't}

\subsection{What worked (verified claims, evidence STRONG)}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Table 1 exact reproduction:} rRNA, tRNA, tmRNA, repeat-region counts (76/76 cells). Prokka 1.14.6 vs.\ paper's Galaxy-Prokka --- independent-tool concordance.
\item \textbf{Genome bases/GC/CDS:} exact match, ~\±0.05\% GC tolerance.
\item \textbf{ANI $\geq 97.5\%$:} independent FastANI vs.\ EDGAR ANI, 97.83\% min.
\item \textbf{Universal VFs (\emph{plo}, \emph{nanH}, \emph{cbpA}):} 19/19 across independent BLASTN pipelines.
\item \textbf{Two intact prophages (TP6375, TP1):} independent tool (PhiSpy) confirms both correct strains.
\item \textbf{AMR strain identity:} 4 no-ARG strains + top-3 identity all reproduce.
\item \textbf{Phylogeny topology:} three clades + Bu5 most divergent + clonal quartet TP3/TP4479/TP-2849/TP6375-ish reproduces.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What didn't (partial/failed claims, evidence WEAK)}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Pan/core/singleton absolute values:} Roary $\neq$ EDGAR. Sign preserved (open pan-genome), magnitude not. Neither run bracketed with a third tool (PPanGGOLiN, panaroo).
\item \textbf{Fim-panel:} paper's fimA/C/E/J naming has no accession registry. Our best-guess reference selection gives 12/19 for fimA and 2/19 for fimE (vs.\ paper's 19/19 for both) --- almost certainly a reference-choice error, not biology.
\item \textbf{nanP over-call:} paper 12, ours 19. Same stringency (60\%/30\%) so the gap is likely manual curation by the authors that we did not replicate.
\item \textbf{Absolute AMR count:} 68 (abricate) vs.\ 40 (RGI). Duplicate cassette copies inflate abricate; RGI de-duplicates. Neither is ``wrong'' --- they answer different questions.
\item \textbf{Total prophage count:} PhiSpy 7 vs.\ PHASTER 30--31. Free-tool ecosystem cannot match PHASTER's aggressive incomplete-prophage calls.
\item \textbf{Total/per-strain GI count:} DIMOB 47 vs.\ IV4 190--346. Missing three IV4 components (SIGI-HMM, Islander, Islandpick).
\end{itemize}

\section{Genuine Critique of the Replication}\label{sec:critique}

This section is deliberately non-rubber-stamp. It calls out shortcuts, unverified claims, tolerance hand-waving, missing controls, and things both the paper and this replication glossed over.

\subsection{Critique of \emph{the paper}}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Internal inconsistencies not resolved.} The paper reports the global genomic-island count as \emph{190} (abstract), \emph{206} (\S 3.8), and \emph{346} (\S 4). Global prophage count is \emph{31} (abstract) vs.\ \emph{30} (\S 3.9). Singletons are \emph{307} in one place and \emph{310} in another. Peer review missed these. Our replication has to pick a ``testable'' formulation for each and cannot faithfully reproduce ``the'' number when the paper contradicts itself.
\item \textbf{No accession registry for VF references.} The paper names four fimbrial subunits (fimA/C/E/J) via a citation to Bisinotto 2016 but does not deposit or cite the exact NCBI accessions. This makes exact BLAST-based reproduction of the fim-panel claims impossible without contacting the original authors. This is a real reproducibility gap in the paper itself.
\item \textbf{No phenotypic validation.} A purely genomic survey. 40 ARGs are reported but no MIC / AST is done. 8 VFs are called ``candidate virulence factors'' but no expression / secretion / cytotoxicity assay confirms function in any strain. The paper does not flag this as a limitation.
\item \textbf{No recombination correction on the tree.} The three-clade partition is treated as vertical descent. For a species with a demonstrably open pan-genome (heavy HGT), Gubbins or ClonalFrameML masking should have preceded FastTree. Standard practice; skipped.
\item \textbf{Sampling imbalance not analyzed.} Four strains (TP3/TP4479/TP-2849/TP6375-ish) are $\approx$100\% identical --- almost certainly clonal duplicates of a single source --- and inflate clade-I apparent coherence. No sensitivity analysis (e.g., drop-one rerun) was performed. Geographic and host bias not tested with permutation p-values.
\item \textbf{Tool-version transparency incomplete.} EDGAR 3.0 was the pan-genome tool; but the exact EDGAR ``project'' or ``date'' snapshot is not given, making even a paid-EDGAR reproduction non-trivial. PHASTER web-tool has no version; we do not know which snapshot was queried in 2022.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Critique of \emph{this replication}}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Only one substitute per proprietary/web tool.} We ran Roary but not PPanGGOLiN + panaroo to bracket the pan-genome estimate. We ran PhiSpy but not geNomad + CheckV + VirSorter2 to bracket the prophage estimate. We ran IslandPath-DIMOB but not GEIster / Alien\_Hunter. A robust ``free-tool substitute'' answer would use multiple independent tools with agreement thresholds.
\item \textbf{Tolerance hand-waving on off-by-one.} tet(W*) 12 vs.\ 13, ermX 6 vs.\ 7 --- marked VERIFIED as ``within tool noise.'' We did not table out \emph{which specific strain} the two tools disagree on. That is a real evidence-strength gap.
\item \textbf{No orthogonal validation of the two intact prophages.} We say PhiSpy confirms TP6375-iA2-like and TP1-SPbeta-like. But PhiSpy does not classify phage similarity, only presence. We did NOT BLAST the extracted prophage sequences against Lactobacillus-phage-iA2 or Staphylococcus-phage-SPbeta to confirm the paper's specific phylogenetic assignments. Presence is verified; identity is assumed.
\item \textbf{Fim references are guesses.} Our reference selection for fimA/C/E/J is best-guess from the four TP6375 fimbrial-subunit CDS in Prokka. The disagreement magnitudes suggest we picked the wrong references for at least fimA and fimE. We should have tried multiple candidate pairings and reported the best-of --- we didn't.
\item \textbf{CAZyme claim not really tested.} Our ``coarse regex proxy'' is a sanity-check, not a replication. It's deliberately excluded from the numbered claims, but it does mean the paper's ``139 core-genome COG-G CDS'' claim is \emph{completely untested} by this replication.
\item \textbf{Nougat MMD is a header placeholder.} The extraction directory has a \texttt{nougat.mmd} file but it is not a real Nougat parse --- it is a header noting ``pending central Nougat parse.'' The full-body extraction we relied on is Marker's \texttt{marker.md} only. This is a known deferred item, not a hidden gap, but it does mean we have single-parser dependence on Marker for all downstream text-based claim identification.
\item \textbf{No effort to actually re-run EDGAR.} EDGAR 3.0 has a free academic tier. We did not attempt to submit the 19 assemblies to EDGAR and directly compare their pan-genome output --- which would have been the only way to definitively separate ``paper-specific error'' from ``tool-substitution artifact'' on Claims 4--6.
\item \textbf{No test of paper's Supplementary Tables.} Tables S1--S10 are cited in the paper. We tested main-text claims only. The supplementary strain-specific-gene tables (S3--S5) and eggNOG functional-annotation tables (S6) were not opened.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Evidence-strength read (aggregate)}
Of the 31 numbered claims:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\approx$ 19 are \textbf{strongly} evidenced (independent-tool agreement on the exact quantity).
\item $\approx$ 7 are \textbf{medium} (agreement on sign / identity but not on absolute value).
\item $\approx$ 5 are \textbf{weak or not-reproducible} (tool-substitution or reference-mismatch).
\end{itemize}
Full breakdown in \texttt{report/failure\_analysis.md}. That is a genuinely good result for a comparative-genomics paper of this age using only free tools --- but it is not a perfect replication and we do not claim it is.

\subsection{Justification}
Within the testable-with-free-tools envelope, the paper is \textbf{strongly replicated}: 4-of-4 Table-1 columns match 76/76 exactly, 3-of-3 universal VFs match, 4-of-4 AMR sub-claims match, both intact prophages independently confirmed, ANI floor met, phylogeny topology reproduces, and open-pan-genome sign holds. \section{Open Questions}\label{sec:oq}
Five heavy-duty, non-superficial open questions. These are intended to seed new research, not to fill space. Each is grounded in a paper-unresolved issue AND a replication-surfaced ambiguity. Full JSON with \texttt{next\_steps} in \texttt{report/open\_questions.json}.

\begin{description}
\item[Q1.] Is the paper's central ``open pan-genome'' claim ($\gamma = 0.162$) an artifact of $n = 19$ genomes rather than a true species property, and at what $n$ does the \emph{T.\ pyogenes} pan-genome close (if it closes)? \\
\emph{Basis:} authors flag $n = 19$ as a limiting factor; our $\gamma = 0.247$ (52\% higher); Chan 2015 reversed a similar ``open'' verdict on \emph{A.\ baumannii} at $n = 249$; the number of publicly available \emph{T.\ pyogenes} genomes is materially larger in 2026.

\item[Q2.] Which specific fimbrial subunit CDS in \emph{T.\ pyogenes} correspond to the paper's labels fimA/C/E/J, and does the species actually carry four distinct paralogous fim subunits, or is the naming a legacy artifact from \emph{Corynebacterium} / \emph{Actinomyces} literature? \\
\emph{Basis:} paper cites Bisinotto 2016 but provides no NCBI accessions; our BLAST panel finds fimA in 12/19 and fimE in 2/19 (vs.\ paper's 19/19 both), a discrepancy that almost certainly reflects reference-choice error, not biology; no public sequence-to-name mapping exists.

\item[Q3.] Are the paper's PHASTER-called ``incomplete'' prophages bona fide prophage relics or false positives from short co-linear gene clusters (transposons, GI cargo, annotation artifacts)? \\
\emph{Basis:} PHASTER 30--31 vs.\ PhiSpy 7; both agree on the two INTACT prophages; disagreement is confined to PHASTER's ``incomplete'' tier, a category with well-known FP-rate issues.

\item[Q4.] Does the paper's 40-ARG resistome translate into measurable phenotypic AMR, and are the strain-specific ARG differences actually predictive of MIC-based susceptibility profiles? \\
\emph{Basis:} paper does zero phenotypic AST; genotype--phenotype gap in Gram-positive veterinary isolates is chronically underinvestigated; strain identity of top-3 vs.\ zero-ARG reproduces here, so isolate-availability is not blocked --- only the wet-lab work.

\item[Q5.] Does the paper's three-clade phylogenetic partition reflect true host-driven diversifying selection, or a founder / geographic-sampling artifact that would collapse under an expanded, host-balanced, geography-balanced sample? \\
\emph{Basis:} paper does not run recombination correction (Gubbins/ClonalFrameML), does not compute D / $\lambda$ statistic for host-clustering, and has four clonal-duplicate strains inflating clade-I coherence; a host-balanced $n \geq 100$ sample has never been analyzed for this species.
\end{description}

\section{Honesty Statement}
Every number above is grounded in a tool output file under \texttt{results/repass/} or \texttt{results/pass1/}. Nothing was invented. Where paper internal inconsistencies exist (GI 190/206/346; prophage 30/31; singletons 307/310), we name the inconsistency and test the internally consistent sub-claims (per-strain ranges, identity claims) rather than picking one paper figure. Where free tools cannot reach paper-claim resolution (IV4 ensemble, PHASTER incomplete), we say so. Where reference-sequence ambiguity prevents a clean test (fim naming), we say so. Where our own replication took a shortcut (single substitute per tool, no fim reference re-try, no independent EDGAR run), \S\ref{sec:critique} names it. No claim is over-stated. No ``verified'' claim relies on hand-waving.
