\begin{abstract}
We replicate three core numerical claims of Achi Brandt, \emph{``Multi-Level Adaptive Solutions to Boundary-Value Problems,''} \emph{Math. Comp.} \textbf{31}(138), 333--390, April 1977. Using a from-scratch V(2,1) multigrid solver on Brandt's own Appendix~B model problem (5-point Poisson on the unit square), we confirm: (C1) grid-independent per-cycle convergence factor $\rho \in [0.049, 0.061]$ across $N \in \{33, \dots, 513\}$; (C2) $O(N)$ work --- an identical 5 cycles are needed to reach $10^{-6}$ relative residual reduction on every grid; (C3) 2nd-order spatial accuracy with fitted order $p = 2.000$. \textbf{Verdict: REPLICATED.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper}
Achi Brandt, \emph{Multi-Level Adaptive Solutions to Boundary-Value Problems}, Math. Comp. 31(138), pp.~333--390, April 1977. AMS open-access PDF (SHA-256 \texttt{d4f187bd5bcdb5262214598ab33a98d83affe390800e3b246964746d35089e5b}, 6.1~MB). This is the foundational multigrid paper: it introduces the Multi-Level Adaptive Techniques (MLAT), the Multi-Grid Method (MG), the Full Approximation Scheme (FAS) for nonlinear problems, and the local-refinement / $\infty$-order adaptive extensions.

\section{Claims targeted}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{C1}: The V-cycle for the 5-point Poisson problem has a per-cycle convergence factor $\mu$ \emph{independent} of grid size $N$. Brandt Table~1 (theoretical, SOR $\omega=1$, 2D, mesh ratio 1{:}2): $\mu = 0.595$. Brandt Appendix~B (measured, Cycle~C on $33^2$): $\mu \approx 0.686$.
\item \textbf{C2}: Total work to solve to truncation-error accuracy is $O(n)$ (Brandt's abstract: $\sim 40n$ additions for Poisson).
\item \textbf{C3}: The 5-point Laplacian is 2nd-order accurate, $\|u_h - u^*\| = O(h^2)$.
\end{itemize}

Out-of-scope for this minimal rerun: C4 (insensitivity to domain shape --- partially covered), C5 ($\infty$-order via adaptive refinement, requires local-refinement / FAS machinery), C6 (nonlinear / transonic-flow via FAS).

\section{Method}

\subsection{Model problem (Brandt Appendix~B, verbatim)}
\begin{align}
\Delta u(x,y) &= f(x,y), \quad (x,y) \in \Omega = (0,1)^2, \\
u(x,y) &= g(x,y), \quad (x,y) \in \partial\Omega, \\
f(x,y) &= \sin(3(x+y)), \\
g(x,y) &= \cos(2(x+y)).
\end{align}

Discretization: standard 5-point Laplacian on a uniform $N \times N$ grid with $h = 1/(N-1)$.

\subsection{Multigrid components}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Levels}: hierarchy $N_k - 1 = 2^k$ intervals per side, coarsest $3 \times 3$, $L = \log_2(N-1)$.
\item \textbf{Smoother}: red-black lexicographic Gauss--Seidel (same asymptotic factor as Brandt's SOR $\omega=1$ GS-Lex, vectorizes cleanly).
\item \textbf{Restriction}: full weighting ($\alpha=1$; Brandt Appendix~B uses trivial injection, but \S A.4 flags full weighting as the ``proper'' choice for a faster factor).
\item \textbf{Prolongation}: bilinear interpolation of the coarse-grid correction, zero Dirichlet on $\partial\Omega$.
\item \textbf{Coarse solver}: dense \texttt{numpy.linalg.solve} on the $3 \times 3$ interior (one unknown).
\item \textbf{Cycle}: V(2,1) --- 2 pre-smoothing, recursive coarse correction, 1 post-smoothing sweep.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Experiments}
Grids $N \in \{33, 65, 129, 257, 513\}$ (up to $\sim 2.6 \cdot 10^5$ unknowns, 5--9 levels).

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{C1}: solve Brandt's Appendix~B problem to $\|r\|_2 < 10^{-10}$, record per-cycle residual, compute $\rho_\infty$ as the geometric mean of the tail of $\rho_k = \|r_{k+1}\|/\|r_k\|$.
\item \textbf{C2}: solve the same problem to $10^{-6}$ relative residual reduction, record cycle count $n_{\text{cyc}}$. Convert to Work Units: V(2,1) in 2D gives $\text{WU/cycle} = 3 \cdot (1 + 1/4 + 1/16 + \dots) \to 4$~WU. Record wall-clock per grid point.
\item \textbf{C3}: switch to a manufactured solution $u^* = \sin(\pi x) \sin(\pi y)$, $f = -2\pi^2 u^*$, zero Dirichlet. Solve to $\|r\|_2 < 10^{-12}$ so iteration error is negligible. Measure $\|u_h - u^*\|_\infty$ and $\|u_h - u^*\|_2$; fit $\log \varepsilon$ vs $\log h$.
\end{itemize}

\section{GENUINE CRITIQUE}
\label{sec:critique}

This section documents the honest weaknesses and limitations of the replication --- what would be needed to strengthen it, what the LLM-judge scoring is and is not evidence for, and what parts of Brandt's paper remain untested.

\subsection{Scope: three claims out of six}
We tested only C1--C3, the linear-elliptic core. Brandt's paper is much larger:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{C4 (domain-shape insensitivity)} is only \emph{partially} tested. We ran a single-shape unit square; Brandt claims the convergence factor is insensitive to $\Omega$, which we cannot corroborate without at least an L-shaped or slit domain.
\item \textbf{C5 ($\infty$-order via adaptive local refinement)} is not tested. This is arguably the most novel contribution of the 1977 paper --- the MLAT extension --- and requires implementing local refinement and the FAS bookkeeping.
\item \textbf{C6 (FAS / nonlinear, transonic flow)} is not tested. Brandt's transonic-flow example (\S 6.5) is a landmark demonstration but well outside a from-scratch V-cycle rerun.
\end{itemize}
Calling this a full replication of the 1977 paper would be a stretch. It is a replication of the \emph{load-bearing linear-elliptic beam} that makes the rest possible.

\subsection{C1 discrepancy: our factor is $\sim$10$\times$ better than Brandt's}
Brandt reports $\mu \approx 0.686$ (Appendix~B) and $\mu = 0.595$ (Table 1, theoretical). We measure $\rho \in [0.049, 0.061]$. This is a \emph{good} discrepancy in the sense that Brandt's own \S A.4 predicts full-weighting restriction ($\alpha=1$) will beat trivial injection ($\alpha=0$), and $\text{V}(2,1)$ pre/post beats $\text{V}(1,0)$ Cycle~C sweep counts. But we did not run the \emph{matching} configuration (injection $+$ V(1,0) $+$ $p^{-1}$ stopping) to close the loop by numerically reproducing Brandt's exact 0.686. That verification is the honest missing experiment; we defer it as a follow-up.

\subsection{C2 work-per-cycle vs Brandt's ``$40n$ additions''}
We claim ``20 WU'' compatible with Brandt's ``$\sim 40n$ additions''. The relationship between Work Units (Brandt's fine-grid stencil-application unit) and raw additions is not fully spelled out in our report --- it depends on the smoother's operation count per point. A rigorous WU-to-flops accounting would strengthen this claim. As written, ``$20 \text{ WU} \Leftrightarrow 40n$ additions'' is an order-of-magnitude compatibility argument, not a decisive match.

\subsection{Smoother substitution: red-black GS vs Brandt's GS-Lex + SOR}
Brandt uses lexicographic Gauss--Seidel and, for the theoretical Table~1, SOR with $\omega=1$. We use red-black Gauss--Seidel. The two-grid analysis says these have the same asymptotic smoothing factor ($\mu_s = 1/2$ for the 5-point Laplacian), but they are \emph{not} identical iterations. Any tiny numerical mismatch beyond the injection-vs-full-weighting story could be masked by this substitution. Justified but noted.

\subsection{Coarse solver: dense direct instead of continued V-cycling}
We terminate the recursion at $3 \times 3$ (1 interior unknown) and solve directly. Brandt would either recurse further or accept the coarsest level. For our depth this is trivial and cannot affect $\rho$, but on very small grids the recursion structure changes what ``a cycle'' actually costs, which slightly complicates the WU accounting in C2.

\subsection{LLM-judge scoring: soft evidence, not proof}
The verdict of \texttt{argo:claude-sonnet-4.6} is one line of evidence toward REPLICATED. It is \emph{not} independent of the numerics --- the judge is scoring the same numerical results we present. It is useful as an over-claim guardrail (the judge grades C1 as \emph{PARTIAL / qualitative}, not \emph{REPRODUCED}, and correctly identifies the smoother/restriction rationale for the discrepancy). But nothing about the LLM judgment strengthens the numerics themselves. The load-bearing evidence is the numbers in \S3, not the judge.

\subsection{Single-machine, single-implementation}
All experiments ran on one machine (CherryRd macOS, CPU, NumPy). One from-scratch $\sim$330 LOC implementation. No cross-check against PyAMG, hypre, or a second independent implementation. Grid-independence and $O(N)$ scaling would be considerably more robust with a second implementation confirming the same $\rho$ and cycle-count table.

\subsection{Iteration-error / discretization-error separation for C3}
We solve to $\|r\|_2 < 10^{-12}$ for C3 to push iteration error below discretization error. The fit $p = 2.000$ is clean, but we did not \emph{prove} the separation of the two error sources numerically --- e.g. by showing the fitted $p$ is insensitive to the residual tolerance. This is a standard check we skipped.

\subsection{Bottom line}
The replication is quantitatively sharp for C2 and C3, and qualitatively sharp for C1, all on the core linear-elliptic model problem. The out-of-scope claims C4--C6 are simply not tested here, and calling this a replication of Brandt's \emph{entire} 1977 paper would be an over-claim. Within its actual scope --- ``the linear V-cycle machinery works as advertised'' --- the answer is a clean REPLICATED.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED.} All three targeted claims (C1--C3) are independently reproduced. Out-of-scope claims (C5 $\infty$-order adaptive, C6 FAS/nonlinear) are not tested and no claim is made about them.
