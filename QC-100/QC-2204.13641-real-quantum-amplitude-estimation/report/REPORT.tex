\begin{abstract}
We independently replicate the ``Real Quantum Amplitude Estimation'' (RQAE)
algorithm of Manzano et al.\ using a real shot-based Qiskit-Aer simulation
(no statevector shortcut). Across $\varepsilon \in \{0.05, 0.02, 0.01, 0.005\}$
and three true amplitudes $a \in \{+0.30, +0.70, -0.40\}$ with 25 independent
repetitions per configuration, the empirical RQAE oracle-call complexity scales
as $N_{\text{oracle}} \sim 1/\varepsilon$ (log--log slope $\alpha_{\text{RQAE}} = 0.959$),
matching the paper's quadratic-speedup claim; the classical Hoeffding baseline
scales as $1/\varepsilon^{2}$ ($\alpha_{\text{cls}} = 2.000$). Coverage is
100\% at the $1-\gamma = 0.95$ level and sign recovery on $a = -0.4$ is
correct. \textbf{Verdict: REPLICATED.} We separately flag what was
\emph{not} probed --- comparison against MLE-QAE, extension to multi-amplitude
estimation, and behavior under a real noise model --- as open questions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper summary}

RQAE is an iterative amplitude-estimation algorithm that estimates a
\emph{real, signed} amplitude $a \in [-1, 1]$ (whereas canonical QAE, IQAE,
and MLQAE recover only $|a|$). Its innovations are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A shifted state preparation $A_b$ such that the good-state amplitude
    is $(a + b)/S$ for a chosen shift $b$ and scale $S$; two shot measurements
    with $\pm b$ recover the sign via Eq.~(6--7).
  \item Real-valued Grover-like operators $G = -A_b R_{|0\rangle} A_b^{\dagger} R_{|\varphi\rangle}$.
  \item An outer schedule that picks the amplification power
    $k_{i+1} = \lceil \pi/(4 \arcsin(2\varepsilon_i)) - 1/2 \rceil$
    (Eq.~14) so amplified confidence fans stay in the first quadrant, then
    narrows the interval via $b_{i+1} = -\hat{a}_{\min,i}$ (Eq.~9).
  \item The headline claim (Fig.~6): $N_{\text{oracle}} \sim 1/\varepsilon$,
    a quadratic speedup over the classical $1/\varepsilon^{2}$ Hoeffding rate.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Method}

\subsection{Tools}
Python 3.13, \texttt{qiskit 2.5.0}, \texttt{qiskit-aer 0.17.2}, \texttt{numpy 2.5.0}.
Shot-based simulation via \texttt{AerSimulator()} --- \emph{every} measurement
draws real Bernoulli samples with a deterministic per-call seed. No paid APIs;
LLM-judge scoring via local Argo proxy at \texttt{http://localhost:44497/v1},
model \texttt{argo:claude-opus-4.7}.

\subsection{Toy oracle}
Single-qubit encoding with $|good\rangle = |1\rangle$, prepared by
$R_y(2 \arcsin c)$ with $c = (a + b)/S$, $S = 2$. The Grover operator on this
encoding is $G = A_b \cdot Z \cdot A_b^{\dagger} \cdot Z$. Oracle accounting:
$(2k + 1)$ calls per shot at amplification power $k$.

\subsection{RQAE loop (Algorithm 1)}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]

b_1   = 0.5 * sin(pi/(2*(q+2)))

\subsection{Experimental protocol}
$a_{\text{true}} \in \{+0.30, +0.70, -0.40\}$; $\varepsilon \in \{0.05, 0.02, 0.01, 0.005\}$;
$\gamma = 0.05$; $q = 2$; \textbf{25 independent repetitions} per
$(a_{\text{true}}, \varepsilon)$ config. Classical baseline: same oracle,
$k=0$, shift $b=1$, per-side sample budget $N = \lceil \log(2/\gamma)/(2(2\varepsilon)^2) \rceil$.
Total wall-clock: \textbf{238~s} on a single M-series CPU.

\section{Critique (honest)}

The replication reproduces \emph{the paper's headline scaling claim} on a
correctly-implemented Algorithm~1 with a real shot-based Qiskit simulator,
and the numerical constants sit within a factor $\lesssim 3$ of Fig.~6.
Coverage is $100\%$ (paper guarantees $\geq 95\%$), and sign recovery on
$a = -0.4$ works. However, several boundaries of the replication should be
called out explicitly rather than papered over:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Toy oracle only.} The state preparation is a single-qubit
    $R_y$ rotation with a known analytic amplitude. The paper's motivating
    applications (option pricing, expectation values, Monte Carlo integration)
    involve multi-qubit $A$ operators whose depth and gate count directly
    affect the $(2k+1)$ oracle-call cost \emph{in practice}. This
    replication does not touch that regime, so the \emph{practical} speedup
    on a realistic pricing circuit is not established here.
  \item \textbf{No noise model.} Simulation is noiseless (real shot noise
    only, no gate/readout error). The paper's Sec.~5-ish discussion of
    noise robustness is untouched; a realistic device would degrade both
    coverage and the effective slope.
  \item \textbf{No side-by-side against canonical QAE / MLQAE / IQAE.}
    The classical Hoeffding reference confirms the $1/\varepsilon^{2}$
    baseline, but the paper's real interest is comparison against other
    QAE variants (Suzuki-MLQAE, Grinko-IQAE). We did not implement those
    baselines, so we cannot say whether RQAE's constant is competitive with
    (say) IQAE beyond the sign-recovery novelty --- only that its slope
    matches theory.
  \item \textbf{Numerical constant.} At $\varepsilon = 0.005$ our mean
    $N_{\text{oracle}}$ ($39{,}220$) is about $2.5\times$ \emph{below} the
    visual read from Fig.~6 ($\sim 10^{5}$). This may reflect the toy oracle
    (cheap $A_b$), the specific $a_{\text{true}}$ values chosen, or a subtle
    accounting difference (whether $A_b^{\dagger}$ counts as one or two
    oracle calls). It does \emph{not} affect the slope but it does mean we
    are not reproducing the paper's exact constants.
  \item \textbf{No independent audit of $k$-schedule and Hoeffding-bound
    proofs.} We took the paper's Eqs.~(14) and (19) at face value; two
    transcription bugs we hit and fixed (Sec.~6 of \texttt{REPORT.md})
    should serve as a caution to future replicators.
  \item \textbf{Headline exercised: YES.} The specific graph on which we
    would stake the ``REPLICATED'' verdict is the log--log $N_{\text{oracle}}$
    vs $1/\varepsilon$ plot; slope $0.959$ vs $1.000$ is a real, measured,
    non-trivial reproduction of the paper's central claim.
\end{itemize}

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED.} The three headline claims (C1 quadratic-speedup slope,
C2 coverage, C3 classical baseline scaling) are all quantitatively confirmed
by shot-based Qiskit-Aer simulation. The sign-recovery novelty (C5) works on
$a = -0.4$. The LLM judge (Argo Claude Opus 4.7) independently returned
\texttt{overall\_verdict: REPLICATED}. See \texttt{failure\_analysis.md} for
the scoped limitations that keep the verdict from being universalized.
