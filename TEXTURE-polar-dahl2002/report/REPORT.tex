\section{Nature of the source paper}
The target is \textbf{not a primary research paper}. It is a critical
book-review / terminology-and-priority polemic of S.\,T.\ Lagerwall's book
\emph{Ferroelectric and Antiferroelectric Liquid Crystals} (1999). The bulk of
the text disputes the definitions of ``polar,'' ``ferroelectric,'' ``SSFLC,''
``hysteresis'' and ``bistability,'' contests historical priority for the
discovery of ferroelectric liquid crystals (Meyer 1974 vs.\ Clark--Lagerwall),
and criticises the pictorial evidence in the book. It contains \textbf{no
self-contained solvable model, no dataset, and no reproducible figure}. The
scaffold's method extract correctly flagged it \textsc{non-replicable as posed}.

Rather than fabricate a result, we \textbf{reduced the physics the paper argues
\emph{about}} --- the standard Clark--Lagerwall surface-stabilized ferroelectric
liquid-crystal (SSFLC) picture, plus Dahl's own qualitative ``alternative view''
--- to a minimal, tractable azimuthal ($\phi$) director model on the smectic
cone, and made five of its statements machine-checkable. This is a
\emph{physics-of-the-domain} replication, not a reproduction of a numerical
result the paper never reports.

\section{Minimal model}
In the chiral smectic-C* phase the director $\mathbf n$ lies on a cone of fixed
tilt (cone half-angle) $\theta$ about the layer normal; its state is the
azimuth $\phi(z)$ across the cell. The spontaneous polarization
$\mathbf P \perp$ tilt plane, $|\mathbf P|=P_s$, couples to a field $E$ along
the cell normal. Free energy per unit area:
\begin{equation}
F=\!\int_0^d\!\Big[\tfrac{K}{2}(\partial_z\phi)^2 - P_s E\sin\phi
 -\tfrac12\varepsilon_0\Delta\varepsilon E^2\sin^2\!\theta\cos^2\!\phi\Big]dz
 + W_s\big[g(\phi_0)+g(\phi_d)\big].
\end{equation}
Representative SSFLC material parameters (DOBAMBC/HOBACPC-like, from the
literature the paper cites): $K=5$\,pN, $P_s=40$\,nC/cm$^2$,
$\theta=22.5^\circ$, $\gamma=0.1$\,Pa\,s, $d=1.5\,\mu$m.

\paragraph{C1 --- $2\theta$ optic-axis rotation.} The two switched states
$\phi=\pm\pi/2$ (UP/DOWN) give projected director orientations separated by
exactly $2\theta=45.0^\circ$ (abs.\ error $7\times10^{-15}$\,deg). This is the
Clark--Lagerwall rigid-cone claim quoted in the paper; it is a pure geometric
consequence of the fixed-cone constraint. \textbf{PASS.}

\paragraph{C2 --- switching-time law.} Overdamped uniform rotation gives a
10--90\% switch time linear in $1/E$ ($R^2=0.9999$). The fitted slope is
$3.5\times$ the naive $\gamma/P_s$. This is \emph{not} a failure: the exact
analytic 10--90\% time equals $\gamma/(P_sE)\cdot\!\int\sec\phi\,d\phi$, a
field-independent $O(1)$ integration constant ($\approx3.58$). Numerical and
analytic times agree to $<3\%$ over the range. The quoted $\tau\propto1/E$
\emph{scaling} is exact; only the definitional prefactor differs.
\textbf{PASS (scaling law).}

\paragraph{C3 --- helix unwinding, Dahl's alternative view.} Dahl argues (p.\
33--34) that surface unwinding of the helix ``will occur essentially
independently of the strength of the elastic forces, as long as they are weaker
than the surface forces.'' We hold the dimensionless ratio $W_s/(Kq_0)$ fixed
and scale $K$ over two decades: the unwound/wound criterion (sign of
$\Delta F$) is \textbf{invariant}, and the reduced energy gap collapses to a
single value ($-11.16$). This confirms the transition is governed by the
surface-vs-twist competition, not the absolute stiffness. \textbf{PASS.}

\paragraph{C4 --- double-well bistability.} At $E=0$ the uniform-director
energy has exactly two degenerate minima ($\phi=\pm90^\circ$) with a barrier
$2\times10^{-4}$\,J/m$^2$; removing anchoring ($W_s{=}0$) with
$\Delta\varepsilon>0$ destroys the two-state structure. Confirms bistability
requires the surface-broken degeneracy the paper discusses. \textbf{PASS.}

\paragraph{C5 --- static-friction bistability (Dahl's genuinely novel claim).}
Dahl proposes (p.\ 35) that bistability may be held by \emph{static friction} --
a threshold/pinning force -- ``as an alternative or complement to an elastic
barrier,'' a claim Lagerwall reportedly found ``ridiculous.'' A Coulomb-friction
dynamical model with a \emph{flat} elastic potential retains two stable rest
states (memory at $E=0$), stays pinned below the threshold field
($E_{\rm th}=4$\,MV/m), and switches above it. Dahl's mechanism is thus
self-consistent as a distinct bistability source. \textbf{PASS.}

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{Coverage: 6/10.} The paper is a non-quantitative polemic; there is no
headline number, dataset, or figure to reproduce. We replicate the domain
physics it argues about (5 distinct claims, including Dahl's two original
alternative-view proposals) but cannot match anything the paper itself computes,
because it computes nothing.

\textbf{Agreement: 8/10.} Every checkable statement reproduces: the geometric
$2\theta$ result is exact; the $\tau\propto1/E$ scaling is exact (prefactor
explained analytically); the unwinding-invariance, double-well, and
static-friction mechanisms all confirm as physically self-consistent. Points
withheld because C2's absolute prefactor is definitional (not stated in the
paper) and the model is qualitative, matching a qualitative source.

\textbf{Overall verdict: REPRODUCED (domain-physics level).} The source is
non-replicable as a primary paper (correctly flagged), but its physical content
is sound and every reduced claim checks out.
