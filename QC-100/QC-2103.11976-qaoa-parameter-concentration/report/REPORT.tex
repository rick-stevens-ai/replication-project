\begin{abstract}
We independently reproduce the parameter-concentration phenomenon claimed in
arXiv:2103.11976 for QAOA state preparation with target
$|t\rangle = |0\ldots0\rangle$ at depths $p=1,2$. The analytical
overlap formula (paper eq.~5) is verified against a real Qiskit statevector
circuit to $10^{-16}$ precision; the derived relation $\gamma = \pi - 2\beta$
matches a numerical fit to 0.3\%; the approximate optimum
$\beta^{\star} \approx \pi/(n+2)$ agrees to six digits at $n=40$; and the
squared parameter drift $\Delta^{2}=|\theta_{n+1}-\theta_{n}|^{2}$ collapses
by a factor of $\sim1100$ over $n=4\ldots40$, unambiguously confirming
concentration. The one quantitative nuance is the concentration exponent
$l$: the paper claims $O(1/n^{4})$ asymptotically, but the finite-$n$
window $n \le 40$ yields $l \approx 3.5$, converging toward $l=4$ only
slowly. \textbf{Verdict: REPLICATED} (headline exercised; parameter
concentration was regenerated, not quoted).
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper claims}
The paper studies QAOA with cost Hamiltonian $H_{z} = 1 - |t\rangle\langle t|$,
mixer $H_{x} = \sum_{i} X_{i}$, and initial state $|+\rangle^{\otimes n}$.
Central testable claims:
\begin{description}
  \item[C1] Closed-form overlap $F_{1}(\gamma,\beta)$ at $p=1$ (eq.~5).
  \item[C2] Analytical optimum satisfies $\gamma = \pi - 2\beta$ (eq.~8).
  \item[C3] Leading-order optimum $\beta^{\star} \approx \pi/(n+2)$,
            $\gamma^{\star} \approx \pi(n+2)/(n+4)$ (Fig.~1).
  \item[C4] Concentration: $\Delta^{2} = O(1/n^{4})$ (eq.~11).
  \item[C5] Depth-2 amplitude eq.~13 with scaling structure eqs.~15--18.
  \item[C6] Numerical extension up to $n=17$, $p=5$ (Table~I) --- out of scope.
\end{description}

\section{Method}
CPU-only, seconds per run. Qiskit 2.5.0, qiskit-aer 0.17.2, NumPy 2.4.3,
SciPy 1.18.0, Python 3.13. No paid endpoints.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Implement $F_{1}$ (eq.~5) and $F_{2}$ (eq.~13) in NumPy;
        no sampling, no shot noise.
  \item Optimize with SciPy L-BFGS-B from many seeds, folding the
        $(\beta \to \pi-\beta, \gamma \to 2\pi-\gamma)$ symmetric branch.
  \item Cross-verify at $n=4,6,8$ by building the real QAOA
        state-preparation circuit in Qiskit
        (Hadamards $\to$ \texttt{Diagonal}($e^{-i\gamma|t\rangle\langle t|}$)
        $\to$ RX($2\beta$)) and computing overlap directly from the
        \texttt{Statevector}.
  \item Fit $\beta = \pi/(a_{1}n+a_{2})$ and $\gamma = b_{1}\pi - b_{2}\beta$.
  \item Concentration diagnostic: $\Delta^{2} \equiv
        (\gamma_{n+1}-\gamma_{n})^{2}+(\beta_{n+1}-\beta_{n})^{2}$, power-law fit.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Optimal-parameter scaling (C3)}
$\beta^{\star}$ matches $\pi/(n+2)$ to six digits at $n=40$; fit

\subsection{Concentration exponent (C4)}
Power-law fit of $\Delta^{2} = C/n^{l}$:
$l \approx 3.03$ ($n=4\ldots19$), $l \approx 3.50$ ($n=20\ldots39$),
$l \approx 3.58$ ($n=30\ldots39$). Absolute drop of $\sim1100\times$
confirms concentration. $\Rightarrow$ C4 \textbf{qualitatively REPLICATED};
asymptotic $l=4$ approached but not reached in $n\le40$.

\subsection{$p=2$ amplitude (C5)}

and $\gamma_{1}\to\pi$, $\gamma_{2}\to\pi-2\pi/n$ (eqs.~15--18).
$\Rightarrow$ C5 \textbf{REPLICATED}.

\section{Critique (honest, HARD-REQ)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Was the headline actually exercised?} \emph{Yes.} The concentration
        phenomenon was independently regenerated: I ran QAOA optimizations across
        17 sizes at $p=1$ (extended to 37 sizes at $n\le40$) and 12 sizes at $p=2$,
        recovered the optima from many seeds, folded the symmetry branch, and
        measured $\Delta^{2}$ directly from the recovered optima. This is
        \emph{not} a quotation of the paper's numbers.
  \item \textbf{Was concentration quantitatively verified for the paper's specific
        instance class?} \emph{Yes and partially no.} The instance class is a
        \emph{single} target state $|t\rangle = |0\ldots0\rangle$ (not a random
        ensemble). Concentration on that class was verified quantitatively: (i)
        $\beta^{\star}\to\pi/(n+2)$ matches at $n=40$ to six digits; (ii)
        $\Delta^{2}$ drops $\sim1100\times$. The one quantitative gap is the
        \emph{exponent} of concentration: the paper's asymptotic $l=4$ is
        approached at $l\approx3.5$--$3.6$ in the $n\le40$ window rather than
        cleanly recovered. This is a finite-$n$ artifact of the sub-leading
        $O(1/n^{2})$ correction in eq.~9, not a contradiction.
  \item \textbf{Comparison against a non-concentrated baseline?} \emph{No, and this is
        a gap.} A random-target or heterogeneous instance-class baseline
        (where concentration should fail or weaken) was not run. Only the
        paper's own single-target class was tested. A cleaner test would sweep
        target Hamming-weight or Pauli-weight $\ne 0$ and show $\Delta^{2}$ does
        \emph{not} concentrate, isolating the mechanism.
  \item \textbf{Scope limitation:} $p=1,2$ only. Claim C6 (numerical extension
        to $p=5$, Table~I) was declared out of scope --- so the depth-scaling
        aspect of concentration is untested here. The paper's own numerics at
        $p=3,4,5$ are unchecked.
  \item \textbf{Optimization risk:} L-BFGS-B on a non-convex landscape with the
        known $(\beta\to\pi-\beta)$ symmetry can trap in the reflected branch.
        Multi-seeding + explicit folding mitigates but does not eliminate this;
        a global optimizer (basin-hopping / grid + refine) would harden.
  \item \textbf{No noise model.} Everything is exact statevector. The paper is a
        theory paper so this is faithful, but any statement about NISQ-relevance
        of parameter transfer would require a shot/Depolarizing/T1--T2 sweep
        that was not done.
\end{itemize}
Net: the headline (parameter concentration) was independently regenerated on
the paper's specific instance class using a real quantum-circuit simulation;
verdict is upheld.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED.} The analytical machinery (C1, C2, C5), the leading-order
scaling (C3), and the qualitative concentration itself (C4) all reproduce.
The asymptotic exponent $l=4$ is approached but not exactly reached in the
finite-$n$ window explored, which the paper's own asymptotic expansion
predicts.
