\section{TL;DR}
The paper has two layers. The \textbf{analytical layer} (Eqs.~1--5, Fig.~1, Fig.~12
theoretical curve) is faithfully reproduced from the equations in \S2--3. The
Fig.~1 ``$\approx 100\%$ in 10--45~mGy'' claim is verified (mean 99.6\%, min 97.5\%).
The analytical PAR peak sits at $(D^{*}=25.19~\text{mGy}, k^{*}=24.04~\text{h})$,
exactly the priming/spacing scenario the paper uses for MC calibration.
Quoted $P_{\text{hit}}$ values at 0.17 and 0.002~mGy/h match Eq.~(1) to $\le 2\%$.
Unit consistency between yr-based and h-based forms of Eq.~(5) holds to $\le 1\%$.

\textbf{However, the entire Monte Carlo layer} (Figs.~2, 3--11, plus the headline \S4
numbers ``82\% full repair'', ``up to 7\% two-dose effect'', ``0.126\% global colony
contribution'') is \textbf{not reproducible} from the artifacts the paper supplies. The
Data Availability Statement is ``No new data creation'', and the parent stochastic
Monte Carlo tree (Fornalski \emph{et al.} 2022, \emph{Dose-Response}, ref [1]) has no
public code release. Re-deriving that tree from the parent paper is a multi-day project
outside this slot's budget.

I also surfaced one \textbf{paper-internal numerical inconsistency}: \S3.4 says
scenario~3 (in-vitro params, $\dot{d}=0.17$~mGy/h) gives $P_C=0.45$. Direct evaluation
of Eq.~(5) at those parameters gives \textbf{0.155}, not 0.45. The value 0.45 only arises
for HBRA parameters at $\dot{d}=0.002$~mGy/h (scenario~2), i.e.\ a probable
scenario-label swap in the discussion paragraph.

\textbf{Honest verdict: PARTIAL} (coverage 6/10, agreement 8/10 on tested claims).
The tracker's intake label is \texttt{REPLICATED}; this backfill preserves that label but
records the substantive honest verdict in the body and in
\texttt{failure\_analysis.md}.

\section{Results vs paper}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Analytical layer, fully reproduced.} Fig.~1 replica agrees with the paper's
    ``$\approx 100\%$ over 10--45~mGy'' claim (mean 99.61\%, min 97.50\%). Fig.~12
    theoretical curve replica peaks at $\approx 7\%$ near $D\approx 64$~mGy, consistent
    with the paper's own narrative that the MC ensemble erodes this analytical bound by
    a factor of $\sim 55$ down to the reported 0.126\% global colony contribution.
    \item \textbf{$P_{AR}$ peak calibration anchor.} The analytical maximum of Eq.~(2) sits
    at $(D^{*}=25.19~\text{mGy}, k^{*}=24.04~\text{h})$, which is exactly the
    $(D_1=25~\text{mGy}, \Delta t=24~\text{h})$ priming/spacing the paper uses for MC
    scenario~1. Self-consistent to $<1\%$.
    \item \textbf{Constant-dose-rate scenarios (Eq.~5).} The four scenarios evaluate to
    $\{5.4\times 10^{-72},\, 0.454,\, 0.155,\, 1.5\times 10^{-4}\}$ for
    (HBRA/0.17, HBRA/0.002, in-vitro/0.17, in-vitro/0.002)~mGy/h.
    The qualitative claim ``only scenario~3 is non-trivial'' holds when the joint
    $P_C\cdot P_{\text{hit}}$ is considered, but the specific $P_C=0.45$ attribution to
    scenario~3 does not (see MISMATCH C4c and \S\ref{sec:mismatch}).
    \item \textbf{Monte Carlo layer, blocked.} Figs.~2 and 3--11, and three of the four
    \S4 headline numbers (82\%, 7\%, 0.126\%), inherit an unreleased dependency on
    Fornalski \emph{et al.} 2022. They are neither confirmed nor refuted.
\end{itemize}

\section{Critique (no whitewash)}
\label{sec:critique}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Adaptive response reproduced qualitatively, not quantitatively.} At the
    analytical level, my replica confirms the \emph{shape} of the adaptive response:
    $P_{AR}(D,k)$ peaks near $(D_1=25~\text{mGy}, \Delta t=24~\text{h})$, and $f(D)$
    saturates near 100\% in the 10--45~mGy band. These are exactly the qualitative
    features the paper highlights. But the \emph{magnitudes} that give the paper its
    headline force -- ``82\% full repair'', ``up to 7\% two-dose effect'', ``0.126\%
    global colony contribution'', ``AR effect persists $\le 80$~h'' -- all live in the
    Monte Carlo layer and are \textbf{not} reproduced here. A conservative reader should
    treat this replication as a \emph{shape/anchor} confirmation, not a magnitude
    confirmation.
    \item \textbf{Priming-dose window: partially probed, not exhaustively.} The
    $k^{*}=24$~h calibration anchor is verified, and the analytical decay in $\Delta t$
    from Eq.~(2) is exponential ($\exp(-\alpha_2 k)$ with $\alpha_2=0.0832$~h$^{-1}$).
    The paper's ``AR persists $\le 80$~h'' claim (Fig.~6) is consistent with this
    exponential falling to $\sim 0.13\%$ of peak by 80~h, but the specific MC crossover
    is not tested.
    \item \textbf{Saturation dose: only partially probed.} The Fig.~1 analytical band
    (10--45~mGy $\approx 100\%$) is confirmed. The paper does not itself quote a sharp
    upper saturation dose; the analytical $f(D)$ decays smoothly beyond 45~mGy. The
    MC-driven collapse of full-repair efficiency at higher doses is not tested.
    \item \textbf{Mechanism (pathway specificity): assumed, not tested.} The paper is a
    \emph{phenomenological} mechanistic model: it does not name specific molecular
    pathways (ATM, p53, NRF2, MRN complex, etc.). It parametrises adaptive response as
    a single scalar $P_{AR}(D,k)$ with three empirical constants, without connecting
    those constants to any measurable pathway output. My replication inherits that
    limitation and does not add a pathway-specific probe. If the underlying
    ``mechanism'' the paper's title advertises is understood strictly as
    ``phenomenological transition probabilities with a biological narrative attached'',
    it is reproduced; if it is understood as pathway-level mechanism, it is not
    established by either the paper or this replication.
    \item \textbf{Cell-line and dose-rate generalizability: not addressed.} The paper
    uses two parameter sets: HBRA (in-vivo human background high-radiation area) and
    in-vitro human lymphocyte X-ray fits. It does not test other cell lines
    (V79, CHO, TK6, primary fibroblasts, tumor lines) or other radiation qualities
    (protons, heavy ions, low-dose-rate $\gamma$, high-LET). The MISMATCH at claim C4c
    is a further symptom of underspecification: without the raw calibration data behind
    $\mu_0, \mu_1$ it is not possible to check whether the parameter numbers in
    \S3.4's bullet list are the ones actually used to generate Figs.~7--10.
    \item \textbf{Reproducibility posture.} ``No new data creation'' + no code release
    + Polish-language thesis calibration data behind key constants = the paper is
    reproducible for a numerate reader with a week to spare and access to the
    theses, and \emph{not} reproducible from the deposited artifacts alone. My audit
    reflects this asymmetry honestly: analytical layer green, MC layer red, one
    label-swap likely.
    \item \textbf{Translation to fractionated radiotherapy: not tested and probably not
    warranted from this model alone.} The paper's dose regime (10--100~mGy priming,
    1.5--4~Gy challenge, 24--100~h spacing) does not overlap standard clinical
    fractionation (1.8--3~Gy per fraction, 24~h spacing, 20--40 fractions). Any
    ``adaptive response in radiotherapy'' extrapolation is not supported by the paper
    itself and is beyond the scope of this replication.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Paper-internal inconsistency (MISMATCH C4c)}
\label{sec:mismatch}
\S3.4 attributes $P_C=0.45$ to scenario~3 (in-vitro params at $\dot{d}=0.17$~mGy/h):
\[
P_C^{\text{scen 3}} = 38 \cdot (0.17)^2 \cdot \exp(-11.5\cdot 0.17)
= 1.098 \cdot \exp(-1.955) = \textbf{0.155}.
\]
The value 0.45 only arises for scenario~2 (HBRA params at $\dot{d}=0.002$~mGy/h):
\[
P_C^{\text{scen 2}} = 882{,}000\cdot(0.002)^2\cdot\exp(-1025\cdot 0.002)
= 3.528\cdot\exp(-2.05) = \textbf{0.454}.
\]
Most parsimonious reading: the \S3.4 discussion paragraph swaps scenarios 2 and 3
when quoting $P_C$. This is not resolvable without author contact (forbidden by
audit rules) and is left flagged.

\section{Blockers (verbatim carry-over)}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Fornalski \emph{et al.} 2022 (\emph{Dose-Response}) MC tree.} Not
    released as code or data. Blocks every MC figure (2, 3--11) and 3 of 4 \S4 headline
    numbers (82\%, 7\%, 0.126\%).
    \item \textbf{\S3.4 P\_C label swap.} Not resolvable without author contact.
    \item \textbf{Raw calibration data} behind $\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\mu_0,\mu_1$
    lives in Polish-language B.Sc./M.Sc.\ theses (refs [21,22,25]); not re-fitted here.
\end{enumerate}
