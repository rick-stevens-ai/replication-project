\section{Citation}
A.-K.~Kassam and L.~N.~Trefethen, ``Fourth-Order Time-Stepping for Stiff PDEs,''
\emph{SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing}, Vol.~26, No.~4, pp.~1214--1233, 2005.
DOI: \href{https://doi.org/10.1137/S1064827502410633}{10.1137/S1064827502410633}.

\section{Paper Summary}
The paper studies the fourth-order exponential time differencing Runge--Kutta
scheme (ETDRK4) in the Cox--Matthews formulation for stiff semilinear PDEs
$u_t = L u + N(u)$ discretized in space by Fourier (or Chebyshev) spectral
methods. Its central technical contribution is that the na\"ive floating-point
evaluation of the ETDRK4 $\varphi$-function coefficients such as
\[
  f_1 \;=\; h\,\frac{-4 - hL + e^{hL}(4 - 3hL + (hL)^2)}{(hL)^3}
\]
suffers catastrophic cancellation for small $|hL|$. This is cured to
machine precision by evaluating each coefficient as the mean of the
integrand over a circular contour in the complex plane around each
eigenvalue $hL$. With this fix, ETDRK4 is globally fourth-order in time
and outperforms other fourth-order exponential-type integrators (in
particular integrating-factor RK4, IFRK4) on the paper's four benchmark
stiff PDEs.

\section{Method}
Environment: local CPU (macOS, host \texttt{CherryRd}), Python 3.13,
NumPy 2.4.3, SciPy 1.18.0, mpmath 1.3.0, Matplotlib 3.10.8. No paid
endpoints. Analytic initial conditions only.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{ETDRK4 core} (\texttt{work/etdrk4\_core.py}). Function
  \texttt{etdrk4\_coeffs(L, h, M=32, r=1)} averages the $\varphi$-integrand
  over $M{=}32$ points on the FULL unit circle around each $hL$; coefficients
  remain complex. \texttt{etdrk4\_coeffs\_direct(L, h)} is retained solely
  for the cancellation demo.
\item \textbf{Fourier-spectral PDEs} (\texttt{work/pdes.py}): KS
  ($N{=}128,\;L_x{=}32\pi$; Trefethen \texttt{kursiv.m} IC),
  Burgers ($N{=}128,\;\nu{=}0.03$; IC $\sin x$),
  Allen--Cahn ($N{=}256,\;\varepsilon{=}0.01$; $L{=}-\varepsilon k^2 + 1$),
  KdV ($N{=}512,\;L_x{=}40$; exact 1-soliton, $c{=}1$).
\item \textbf{Integrators} (\texttt{work/integrators.py}): ETDRK4 and IFRK4.
\item \textbf{C1--C2 experiment} (\texttt{run\_cancellation.py}).
  Sweep $L{=}-|hL|$ for $|hL|\in[10^{-3},10^{1}]$ (60 log-spaced points, $h{=}1$),
  50-digit mpmath reference.
\item \textbf{C3--C4 experiment} (\texttt{run\_convergence.py}). Fine ETDRK4
  reference at $h_{\text{ref}}=h_{\min}/8$, $M{=}64$; five halved $h$s;
  $\max|u-u_{\text{ref}}|$ log--log slope; mean $\mathrm{err}_{\text{IF}}/\mathrm{err}_{\text{ETD}}$.
\item \textbf{C3 clean order for KdV} (\texttt{run\_kdv\_selfconv.py}):
  Cauchy self-convergence log$_2$ ratios.
\item \textbf{C5 KdV soliton + invariants} (\texttt{run\_kdv\_soliton.py}):
  three step sizes at $T{=}2$; mass and $\int u^2\,dx$ drift.
\item \textbf{C6 long-time KS} (\texttt{run\_ks\_figure.py}): $h{=}0.25$, $T{=}150$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Genuine Critique}
Below are the honest concerns and limitations of \emph{this replication}
(not of the paper); they are what a careful reviewer would legitimately
push back on.

\subsection*{1. KdV order fit against a reference is misleading}
For KdV the log--log slope against the fine ETDRK4 reference came out at
$2.65$, well below~4. Only after switching to Cauchy self-convergence do
we recover $\log_2$ ratios of $4.09$ and $3.85$ in the mid-range. Both
diagnostics are honest, but a reader looking only at the reference-based
table could reasonably worry that ETDRK4 is sub-fourth-order on
imaginary-spectrum problems. The root cause here is spatial spectral
floor saturation at $\sim 4\times10^{-11}$, not a defect in the
temporal scheme; but this deserves explicit narrative rather than a
footnote.

\subsection*{2. Complex-coefficient trap discovered mid-run}
An early implementation used a half-circle contour and took \texttt{real()}
of the average. On dissipative-spectrum PDEs (KS, Burgers, Allen--Cahn)
this looked fine because the true coefficients on the negative real axis
are real; but on KdV (pure-imaginary spectrum) it silently reduced ETDRK4
to first order. We caught this by cross-checking KdV order and rebuilt
the coefficient routine on the full unit circle with complex arithmetic
retained. It is worth flagging that any future extension --- to
non-normal $L$, to boundary-forced problems, to Chebyshev spectra with
non-real eigenvalues --- inherits this same trap and needs the same
sanity check.

\subsection*{3. KS ``match'' is not pixel-level}
Kassam and Trefethen's Figure~4 is a space-time contour of a chaotic
solution; a chaotic trajectory diverges exponentially from any nearby
trajectory, so pixel identity is impossible. We match on: (i) solution
bounded, (ii) $\max|u|\approx 3.4$ (paper: $\sim 3$), (iii) mean drift
$\sim 10^{-17}$, (iv) qualitative banded ``chaotic soliton'' pattern.
This is a legitimate qualitative match, but it is not equivalent to
verifying a fixed reference solution.

\subsection*{4. Krogstad variant (C7) not tested}
The paper compares ETDRK4 (Cox--Matthews) against ETDRK4-B
(Krogstad). We only implemented Cox--Matthews. Whether Krogstad
improves accuracy or stability on our benchmarks is an open item
in this replication, though the paper's headline claim is not
about Krogstad.

\subsection*{5. Initial conditions are underspecified in the paper}
We used Trefethen's \texttt{kursiv.m} KS IC and a KdV single-soliton
(instead of the two-soliton $A{=}25$ case, which needs dealiasing and
smaller steps). Both choices are defensible and cited, but they are
choices we made. If a future reader wanted to match the paper's KdV
plot pixel-for-pixel they would need to add the two-soliton setup.

\subsection*{6. Only fp64; only Fourier}
The paper also studies Chebyshev-spectral discretizations. We tested
only Fourier. Chebyshev raises additional issues (non-normal $L$,
non-uniformly spaced eigenvalues) that our contour routine has not
been stressed against. Additionally, all runs are in fp64. The
$\sim 10^{-15}$ contour floor is at the fp64 wall; whether the
scheme continues to shine at fp128/mpmath precision is untested.

\subsection*{7. No wall-clock competitiveness numbers}
We show ETDRK4 wins vs IFRK4 at equal $h$ (1.4$\times$ to 5.1$\times$
error ratio). We do not report wall-clock cost per step, so we do not
close the loop on ``which is faster to a given tolerance.'' The paper
argues ETDRK4 is competitive in cost as well as accuracy; our
replication only supports the accuracy half.

\subsection*{8. Judge signal is single-source}
\texttt{report/judge\_result.txt} is a single Argo LLM judge on the
initial draft. It is a useful sanity check, not a peer review. The
strong-REPLICATED verdict here rests on the numerics, not on the judge.

\section{Reproducibility Notes and Caveats}
\begin{itemize}
\item Full unit circle + complex coefficients are essential; the
  half-circle-plus-\texttt{real()} shortcut reduces ETDRK4 to first
  order on dispersive problems.
\item Reference-solution comparisons saturate at the spectral spatial
  floor at the smallest $h$; use Cauchy self-convergence for a clean
  temporal-only order estimate.
\item Comparison to K\&T figures is on slopes / magnitudes / gaps /
  qualitative pattern, not pixel matches.
\end{itemize}
