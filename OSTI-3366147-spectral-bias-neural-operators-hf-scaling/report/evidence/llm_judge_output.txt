## Assessment

### (1) Mechanism Fidelity

The HFS module implementation faithfully follows paper Eqs. 4-6: non-overlapping patch decomposition, DC = mean patch, HFC = per-patch deviation, rescaling X̂ = X + λ_DC·DC + λ_HFC·HFC with per-channel learnable scalars initialized to 1.0. The spectral error metric follows Eq. B.3 (RMS over band). Band boundaries (2% / 8.2% / 93.8%) are correctly applied. The primary fidelity gap is the task domain: synthetic 2D power-law fields replace BubbleML/Kolmogorov flow, which may alter whether HFS's inductive bias is activated (the paper's datasets have specific high-frequency structures that may be necessary to trigger the mechanism). Parameter counts (~493k vs paper's ~16M) and training scale also differ substantially. Within these constraints, the implementation appears mechanistically correct.

---

### (2) Per-Claim Verdicts

**C1 — Spectral bias exists in baseline:** **REPLICATED**
Fnorm_high (~0.40) is consistently 1.6–2.3× larger than Fnorm_low (~0.17–0.25) across all three seeds, confirming that the baseline ResUNet underlearns high-wavenumber modes.

**C2 — HFS reduces error at ALL frequency bands (especially high):** **NOT-REPLICATED**
F_high shows no improvement (Δ = −0.4%, −0.4%, −0.5% across seeds — i.e., marginally *worse* in all three), directly contradicting the paper's headline claim that HFS specifically reduces high-frequency spectral error; only low/mid bands show modest, inconsistent gains.

**C3 — HFS is architecturally cheap:** **REPLICATED**
Δparams = +1,440 (+0.29%) across all seeds, confirming negligible parameter overhead consistent with the paper's claim.

**C4 — HFS reduces overall RelL2/RMSE:** **PARTIAL**
Two of three seeds show improvement (Δ = +3.0%, +1.5%), but seed 2 shows slight degradation (−0.6%); mean improvement is ~+1.3%, directionally consistent with the paper but substantially smaller than the reported ~5% reduction, and not statistically robust across seeds.

---

### (3) Overall Verdict

**PARTIAL**

The core architectural claim (C3) and the existence of spectral bias (C1) are confirmed. The overall accuracy improvement (C4) is directionally present but weak and inconsistent. Critically, the paper's central mechanistic claim — that HFS specifically corrects *high-frequency* spectral error (C2) — is not reproduced; the high-frequency band shows no benefit in any seed. This is the most important claim and it fails cleanly. The task-domain gap is a legitimate caveat but does not excuse the sign reversal on the primary claimed effect.

---

### (4) One-Line Summary

> HFS overhead confirmed (C3) and spectral bias observed (C1), but high-freq error reduction (C2, core claim) not reproduced; overall gain weak/inconsistent (C4). PARTIAL.
