\section*{Verdict: REPLICATED (deterministic mean-field core)}
Coverage \textbf{8/10}, Agreement \textbf{8/10}. Verdict is preserved from the
on-disk REPORT.md and matches the queue verdict (REPLICATED). No mismatch.

\paragraph{Scope.} This replication reproduces the deterministic mean-field ODE
core of the paper's published hybrid model: all $\sim$30 ODEs from Additional
File~1, all $\sim$110 rate constants from Additional File~4, and all 60 initial
conditions from Additional File~3, integrated with \texttt{scipy.integrate.solve\_ivp}
(LSODA). Out of scope: the stochastic hybrid (Haseltine--Rawlings) simulator
used to generate the paper's 1000-cell population statistics
(apoptotic-fraction, clonogenic-survival percentages).

\section{What was implemented}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{code/parameters.py} --- 133 parameter symbols from MOESM4
        Tables 1--5, encoded verbatim with paper-quoted values/units.
  \item \texttt{code/model.py} --- 60-state ODE right-hand side encoding every
        equation in MOESM1 plus mean-field versions of stochastic propensities
        (DSB, R\_a, ten gene-state variables of form
        $dG/dt = a_{\mathrm{on}}(N_A - G) - a_{\mathrm{off}} G$).
  \item \texttt{code/run\_steady.py} --- 48\,h no-input drift $<$1\% on every
        major species; ICs are the true ODE steady state.
  \item \texttt{code/run\_ir.py} --- 10\,Gy IR (1\,Gy/min pulse) for both
        Ctr-RNAi and Wip1-RNAi $\rightarrow$ Figs.~2\&3.
  \item \texttt{code/run\_dose\_response.py} --- 6 doses $\times$ 2 lines plus
        the three-arm TNF$\alpha$/IR timing experiment $\rightarrow$ Fig.~4.
\end{itemize}
All runs complete in $<$10\,s on CPU. See \texttt{evidence/} and \texttt{figures/}.

\section{Reproducibility blockers}
All blockers are \emph{modelling effort}, not missing data, with one exception
noted below.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Stochastic hybrid simulator (Haseltine--Rawlings).} Required
        for Figs.~2/3/4 median$\pm$IQR over 1000 cells and for apoptotic-fraction
        numbers. Algorithm in MOESM2, propensities in MOESM1 Eqs.~1--24. Not
        blocked by data.
  \item \textbf{Cell-fate threshold values.} MOESM6 describes the Kracikova-style
        procedure but prints no ready-to-substitute number. To recover the
        apoptotic-fraction \% one must refit thresholds against the Fig~4a
        clonogenic training set, which is \emph{genuinely absent from all 10
        supplementary files} (bar-graph readoff only, $\pm$1\,pp).
        \textbf{Only true data blocker.}
  \item \textbf{Sensitivity analysis (MOESM8).} Plots only, no tables; not
        re-derived here (paper's claim is qualitative).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Critique (Honest)}
\label{sec:critique}
This replication is a \textbf{genuine reproduction of the deterministic core}
but the following limitations of \emph{our} work and \emph{the paper} are worth
naming explicitly:

\paragraph{Our replication.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Fitting depth.} We reproduced $p53$ pulse and NF-$\kappa$B
        trajectories \emph{qualitatively} and to $\sim$order-of-magnitude
        agreement on peak amplitudes. We did \textbf{not} perform a
        quantitative fit of the p53-pulse \emph{period} or \emph{amplitude}
        against Geva-Zatorsky--style single-cell data (the paper itself does
        not attempt this in the mean-field limit; its pulse claim is
        essentially: ``one damped pulse, peak $\sim$2\,h, decay $\sim$24\,h.'')
        Our numbers match that specification but do not constitute a period
        or amplitude fit in the strict sense.
  \item \emph{NF-$\kappa$B oscillation timescale.} Reproduced qualitatively
        (NF-$\kappa$B rises with TNF$\alpha$, is much larger than the IR-only
        response by $\sim$10$\times$), but we did not attempt to fit the
        NF-$\kappa$B pulse period (typically $\sim$100\,min in Hoffmann/Nelson
        experimental data). The model's I$\kappa$B$\alpha$/A20 negative-feedback
        loop supports oscillations, but a period sweep vs experimental data was
        not run.
  \item \emph{Crosstalk.} p53$\leftrightarrow$NF-$\kappa$B crosstalk is
        \emph{modelled} (shared ATM/PIDD input, shared apoptotic outputs) but
        our replication tests only the TNF$\alpha$-timing arm of the crosstalk
        hypothesis. Reciprocal probes (e.g.\ Wip1 KO effect on NF-$\kappa$B, or
        NF-$\kappa$B inhibitor effect on p53) were \textbf{not executed}.
  \item \emph{Stochastic layer.} Entire population-statistics arm (30\% of
        paper's numerical claims) is out of scope.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The paper itself (limitations we now recognize).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Parameter identifiability.} With $\sim$110 rate constants and
        60 states, the model is \textbf{over-parameterized} in the classical
        sense: many parameter combinations are unidentifiable from the
        available experimental readouts (Western blots + clonogenic survival).
        The paper does not report a formal identifiability analysis (profile
        likelihoods, Fisher information); MOESM8 sensitivity plots are a proxy
        but do not rule out compensating parameter changes.
  \item \emph{Fit vs prior literature.} Many parameter values are inherited
        from earlier ATM/p53 models (Ma et al., Batchelor et al.) or from
        NF-$\kappa$B models (Lipniacki, Hoffmann); only a subset (Wip1 kinetics,
        DSB repair rates) is fit to the authors' own western-blot data. The
        replication therefore succeeds partly because the model borrows
        heavily from tuned upstream literature.
  \item \emph{Missing training data.} As noted, the Fig~4a clonogenic training
        set --- the actual numbers used to fit the Kracikova thresholds --- is
        never printed in the paper or its supplements. This blocks any
        third-party quantitative reproduction of the apoptotic \%.
  \item \emph{Single-cell heterogeneity ignored.} The mean-field/hybrid model
        assumes cell-to-cell variability comes only from Gillespie propensities;
        parameter heterogeneity (which dominates real experimental variance) is
        not modelled.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Bottom line of the critique.} \emph{Deterministic reproducibility
is high, quantitative fit-quality is moderate-to-qualitative, and identifiability
is unaddressed.} The paper's core mechanistic conclusion (DDR switch-off
matters; Wip1 is the dominant switch-off; TNF$\alpha$ pre-treatment is
radio-protective via NF-$\kappa$B) survives our replication cleanly. The
predictive claims about apoptotic percentages remain untestable in this scope.
