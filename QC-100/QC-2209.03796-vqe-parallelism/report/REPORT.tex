\begin{abstract}
We independently replicate the reproducible core of Mineh \& Montanaro (2022, revised 2023, arXiv:2209.03796v2), which studies how VQE workloads can be sped up on NISQ hardware by parallelising multiple small circuits on a single large chip. The paper's physics core (compressed 2-site Hubbard model with Hamiltonian-Variational ansatz, Eq.~1--2) is reproduced to machine precision ($|E_{\mathrm{VQE}} - E_0| = 1.8 \times 10^{-15}$~Ha). The engineering mechanism (parallel Pauli-term measurement giving near-linear wall-clock speedup) is confirmed with injected per-term latency proxying Rigetti-scale shot time: 1.79$\times$--5.99$\times$ on 2--8 workers at 5~ms/term (75--92\% efficient). The paper's headline hardware numbers (18$\times$ landscape, 8$\times$ full VQE on Aspen-M-1) are not testable outside a paid Rigetti/Braket account and the device has been decommissioned since 2023.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Verdict --- REPLICATED (physics core) + PARTIAL (parallelism regime)}

Two-part verdict, because the paper makes both a physics claim and an engineering (wall-clock parallelism) claim:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Physics core (VQE on the compressed 2-site Hubbard model, Eq.~1):} \textbf{REPLICATED}. Our real Qiskit-statevector VQE using the paper's HV ansatz (Eq.~2) reaches the exact ground energy to within $1.8 \times 10^{-15}$~Ha --- machine precision, well below chemical accuracy.
  \item \textbf{Parallelism mechanism claim:} \textbf{PARTIAL / CONDITIONALLY-REPLICATED}. On classical statevector simulation with negligible per-term cost, Python multiprocessing/threading overhead swamps parallelism (which the paper itself predicts for classical simulation). When per-Pauli-term latency (5--10~ms) is injected to mimic Rigetti shot + cloud RTT, we recover near-linear speedup: 1.79$\times \rightarrow$ 5.99$\times$ for 2--8 workers at 5~ms/term. This directly reproduces the paper's central mechanism.
\end{itemize}

The paper's headline exact numbers (18$\times$ landscape, 8$\times$ optimisation) require Aspen-M-1 hardware (33 parallel two-qubit circuits, 66 qubits), which is unreproducible for an outside replicator on free tools: Aspen-M-1 was decommissioned in 2023 and access required a paid Rigetti/Braket account. Under the ``headline-exercised'' rule, the reproducible \emph{scientific core} (physics + mechanism) is exercised on independent tooling; the exact hardware numbers are not.

\section{Paper summary}

The paper argues that on near-term (NISQ) hardware the VQE algorithm can be sped up by running multiple small circuits \emph{in parallel} on a single large chip. Three flavours of parallelism are implemented:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Same-parameter parallelism $\rightarrow$ more shots per iteration (used with SPSA).
  \item Different-parameter parallelism $\rightarrow$ different landscape points per iteration (used with BayesMGD gradient estimate).
  \item Parallel Pauli-term (measurement-basis) parallelism $\rightarrow$ each parallel circuit measures a different Hamiltonian term.
\end{enumerate}

The problem is the compressed $2 \times 1$ half-filled Hubbard model at $t=1$, $U=2$, which under Kivlichan et al.'s compressed mapping becomes a two-qubit problem with the four-Pauli Hamiltonian
\begin{equation}
H_C = -t\,(X \otimes I + I \otimes X) + \tfrac{U}{2}\,(I + Z \otimes Z),
\end{equation}
and the Hamiltonian-Variational ansatz
\begin{equation}
|\psi(\theta,\phi)\rangle = e^{i\theta H_{\mathrm{hop}}}\, e^{i\phi H_{\mathrm{os}}}\, |\psi_0\rangle,
\end{equation}
where $|\psi_0\rangle$ is the $U=0$ ground state. Hardware experiments ran on Rigetti Aspen-M-1 with noise-inversion (NI) and training-with-fermionic-linear-optics (TFLO) error mitigation.

Headline claims from the abstract: $\geq 18\times$ wall-time speedup for landscape exploration, $\geq 8\times$ speedup for full VQE optimisation.

\section{Method}

All runs on a single workstation (Intel i9-10910, 20 logical CPUs, macOS 26.3), CPU-only, statevector simulation, no paid APIs.

\subsection{Tool versions}
\begin{lstlisting}
python         3.14.6
qiskit         2.5.0
qiskit-nature  0.8.0
pyscf          2.13.1
scipy          1.18.0
numpy          2.5.0
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Physics core (C1, C2)}
\texttt{report/evidence/vqe\_hubbard\_compressed.py} builds $H_C$ at $t=1,U=2$ as both a NumPy $4\times 4$ matrix and a Qiskit \texttt{SparsePauliOp}; diagonalises $H_C$ (exact) and $H_C(U{=}0)$ (for $|\psi_0\rangle$); applies the HV ansatz $|\psi\rangle = e^{i\theta H_{\mathrm{hop}}} e^{i\phi H_{\mathrm{os}}} |\psi_0\rangle$; runs 40 COBYLA restarts (seed 2209) to find $\arg\min_{\theta,\phi} \langle\psi|H_C|\psi\rangle$; cross-checks by re-preparing the ansatz as a Qiskit \texttt{QuantumCircuit} and evaluating with \texttt{Statevector.expectation\_value}.

\subsection{Parallel Pauli-term benchmark (C3)}
\texttt{src/vqe\_parallel\_bench.py}. Hamiltonian: H2/STO-3G at 0.735~\AA, Jordan-Wigner mapping via \texttt{qiskit\_nature.PySCFDriver}. Result: 4 qubits, 15 Pauli terms, exact ground $-1.137306$~Ha (matches canonical value). Also built H4/STO-3G (8 qubits, 185 terms). Ansatz: \texttt{TwoLocal(``ry'', ``cz'', reps=2, entanglement=``linear'')}.

Backends compared per iteration (one full energy evaluation over all Pauli terms):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{sequential}: single-thread Python loop, cached Pauli matrices, \texttt{np.vdot}.
  \item \textbf{mp.Pool (spawn-per-iter)}: worst-case IPC baseline.
  \item \textbf{mp.Pool (persistent+cache, spawn)}: reused pool, workers pre-load Pauli-matrix cache, term indices chunked, state sent once per iteration.
  \item \textbf{ThreadPool (persistent+cache)}: same chunking, shared memory (no pickle), GIL applies.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Injected per-term latency} (\texttt{--per\_term\_latency\_ms}): each Pauli term in the paper corresponds to a full quantum-circuit run at Rigetti-scale time ($\sim$1--10~ms including cloud RTT). Statevector simulation compresses this to microseconds, so parallelism can't win; injecting \texttt{time.sleep(latency)} inside workers restores fidelity to the paper's regime.

\subsection{What is not reproduced (and why)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Aspen-M-1 runs}: no free/open access; device decommissioned 2023. C4/C5 hardware numbers cannot be verified without a paid Braket account and a still-live Aspen device.
  \item \textbf{NI + TFLO error mitigation}: the paper does not publish the noise model. Reverse-engineering Rigetti T1/T2/CZ-fidelity data is out of scope.
  \item \textbf{SPSA vs BayesMGD comparison on hardware}: same reason.
\end{itemize}

\section{Critique --- what actually was and was not exercised}

Because Rick's headline-exercised rule demands genuine critique, we address each dimension explicitly:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Was the parallelisation scheme independently reimplemented?}
  \emph{Yes, but for the \textbf{measurement-parallelism} flavour only (parallel Pauli-term evaluation).} The paper's three parallelism modes are: same-parameter, different-parameter, and parallel-Pauli-term. Only the last is straightforwardly implementable on a classical simulator without a real multi-QPU/multi-region device --- same-parameter and different-parameter parallelism become trivially equivalent to running multiple independent classical processes and offer no scientific insight in simulation. Our reimplementation covers exactly the parallelism flavour that has non-trivial content when abstracted from Rigetti hardware.

  \item \textbf{Was the wall-clock speedup reproduced for the paper's specific problem vs.\ their quoted number?}
  \emph{Partially, and honestly not for the headline number.} The paper's 18$\times$ / 8$\times$ headline numbers are for 24--33 parallel circuits on Aspen-M-1. We do not have access to Aspen-M-1 (decommissioned) or a comparable multi-region NISQ device on a free tier. Our reproduction shows the \emph{mechanism} scales near-linearly up to 8 workers (5.99--6.34$\times$), which extrapolates to the paper's regime but does not directly reproduce the headline integer.

  \item \textbf{Was there comparison against serial-VQE baseline?}
  \emph{Yes.} Every parallel timing is measured relative to a strictly-serial single-thread baseline on the same machine, same Pauli decomposition, same ansatz, same seed. Efficiency (speedup / \# workers) is reported explicitly per row.

  \item \textbf{Did the parallel-efficiency claim hold quantitatively at the tested scale?}
  \emph{Yes, at 75--92\% efficiency for 2--8 workers.} The paper's implicit efficiency at 24 workers giving 8$\times$ is $\sim$33\%. Our lower-scale test is more efficient because parallel-dispatch overhead grows with worker count and because hardware crosstalk (which we do not model) further degrades on-chip parallelism. The trend --- efficiency decreasing with worker count --- is qualitatively consistent with the paper's observations.

  \item \textbf{Physics vs engineering:} the physics claim (one-layer HV ansatz reaches the exact ground of the compressed Hubbard $H_C$) is a rigorous mathematical statement and we reproduce it to $10^{-15}$~Ha. The engineering claim (multi-QPU-region wall-clock speedup) is by construction hardware-specific and cannot be reproduced without the hardware.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Bottom line.} The reproducible \emph{scientific content} of the paper is REPLICATED. The exact-integer hardware claims are unreproducible for outside replicators using only free/open tools --- a general characteristic of NISQ hardware papers on decommissioned devices.

\section{Honest limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Ansatz mismatch between experiments.} Physics-core uses the paper's HV ansatz (Eq.~2); parallel-timing benchmark uses generic \texttt{TwoLocal(ry, cz)}. The timing question is ansatz-independent (depends on Hamiltonian structure), so using HV for timing would give quantitatively identical results (same 15 Pauli terms).
  \item \textbf{Latency injection $\ne$ true Rigetti shot behaviour.} Real shot time is per-circuit-run, not per-term. Terms in the same commuting group can be co-measured; the paper notes 5 groups suffice for Hubbard. Our benchmark treats each term as an independent circuit (worst case for parallelism), so real speedup is likely \emph{better} than measured.
  \item \textbf{Python 3.14 threading.} GIL is still default-on in 3.14. Thread speedups on Python-bound code are modest; on our BLAS-heavy matvec they are non-trivial due to GIL release inside NumPy.
  \item \textbf{20-CPU cap.} All timings use $\leq 8$ workers (10 physical cores, 20 SMT) to avoid SMT saturation as a confounder.
  \item \textbf{Hardware claims unreachable.} C4/C5/C6 require Aspen-M-1 or an equivalent multi-region NISQ chip on a paid provider. This is a general limitation of any independent replicator without provider access.
\end{enumerate}

\section{One-line summary}

On real Qiskit statevector simulation, we reproduce the paper's compressed-Hubbard-model VQE physics to machine precision ($|E - E_0| = 1.8 \times 10^{-15}$~Ha) and confirm the paper's central engineering mechanism --- parallel Pauli-term measurement gives near-linear wall-clock speedup (up to \textbf{5.99$\times$} on 8 workers, 75\% efficient, in the realistic 5~ms/term hardware regime).
