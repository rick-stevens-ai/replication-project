\begin{abstract}
Murray et al.\ (2016) construct a three-state Markov chemistry model of $\gamma$H2AX--pATM
dynamics at a single DNA double-strand break (DSB) site, derive two moment-closure ODE
approximations (equations 2.5 and 2.8), fit six rate constants against $\gamma$H2AX foci /
neutral-comet time series for MCF7 and MDA-MB-468, and extend the framework to two
case studies: (i) antibody-based imaging of $\gamma$H2AX with an anti-$\gamma$H2AX-TAT
conjugate (Section 4.1), and (ii) $^{111}$In-Auger DSB induction (Section 4.2).
We reimplement every equation from Tables 1--2 alone (six rate constants and three
scaling constants); we verify eight of eleven testable claims quantitatively; we surface one
paper-side reproducibility issue (the printed \emph{bare} eq (2.5) is dynamically unstable at
the published MCF7 fit); and we report three data-side blockers (Figures 4, 7e, 8b are all
figure-only, blocking the Nelder--Mead refit of eq (3.3)). Verdict: \textbf{PARTIAL} at
coverage 8/10 and agreement 7/10.
This paper is best described as a \emph{theoretical framework paper} in which the majority of
formal ``claims'' are definitional (equations and closures) rather than empirically testable
predictions; we therefore separate framework-level tautologies from testable predictions
throughout.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper summary}

Murray, Cornelissen, Vallis, and Chapman (2016) is a theory-methods paper in the
\emph{Journal of the Royal Society Interface} that:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Models a single DSB site as a three-state stochastic system: a binary marker $X$
      (0 = repaired, 1 = unrepaired), a discrete count $Y$ of active pATM molecules bound near
      the site, and a discrete count $Z$ of $\gamma$H2AX chromatin phosphorylations
      surrounding the break (eq 2.1 master equation).
    \item Derives \emph{two} moment closures: an ``ad-hoc'' closure (eq 2.5) that closes on
      $\langle X \rangle$, $\langle Y \rangle$, $\langle Z \rangle$; and a
      \emph{conditional-mean} closure (eq 2.8) that additionally tracks
      $\langle Y \mid X{=}1 \rangle$ and $\langle Z \mid X{=}1 \rangle$.
    \item Fits six rate constants $k_1,\ldots,k_6$ for MCF7 and MDA-MB-468 separately (Table 1) using
      Nelder--Mead minimisation of a $\chi^2$-style cost (eq 3.3) against foci/comet time series.
    \item Extends the model to two experimental settings: an antibody state $Q$ that binds
      $\gamma$H2AX (eq 4.1--4.2) and a Poisson DSB-induction source $k_9 Q(1-X)$ driven by
      an $^{111}$In-Auger emitter carried by the antibody (eq 4.3--4.4).
    \item Introduces a scalar persistence measure AUC $= \int_0^\infty \langle X \rangle \mathrm{d}t$
      (eq 4.5).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Nature of the paper: theory vs.\ test}

This is a theory-forward paper. Of the model constructs the paper puts forward, most are
\textbf{definitional}: the master equation (2.1) is a modelling choice, the two closures (2.5,
2.8) are derivations from that choice, the antibody and Auger extensions (4.1--4.4) are
further modelling choices, and the AUC persistence measure (4.5) is a definition. These
constructs cannot be ``verified'' or ``falsified'' by re-running the same equations locally --
they can only be checked for internal consistency and for numerical implementability. That
subset we call \emph{framework-level}.

A smaller subset of the paper's content is \emph{testable} in the standard sense --- it makes
a quantitative prediction that either matches an external number or does not. We enumerate
those below (Section~\ref{sec:claims}) and audit them.

This distinction matters for the verdict: an honest ``REPLICATED'' judgement should require
agreement on the \emph{testable} claims, not merely on the definitional ones. See
Section~\ref{sec:critique}.

\section{Methods}

Full working artifacts are on disk (see \texttt{artifacts\_summary.md}); the flow was:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Retrieved paper PDF (EuropePMC \texttt{PMC4759787}, CC BY 4.0, md5
      \texttt{888349ba9763ec27c7185f6e3c8648e8}) and JATS XML.
    \item Implemented eq (2.1) master equation as a vectorised tau-leap SSA
      (\texttt{scripts/scripts\_ssa.py}) with $\tau = 5\!\times\!10^{-4}$ h for MDA468 and
      $5\!\times\!10^{-5}$ h for MCF7. Cross-checked against an \emph{exact} Gillespie SSA
      (\texttt{scripts/ssa\_exact.py}) on the MDA468 line for $t\le 1$ h, $n{=}100$; agreement
      within $5\!-\!20$\%.
    \item Implemented ad-hoc closure eq (2.5) in two variants: (a) \emph{bare} paper form; (b)
      with logistic saturation $(1-Y/Y_{\max})$, $(1-Z/Z_{\max})$ enforcing the Table 2 caps.
      Variant (a) is what the paper prints; variant (b) is required for MCF7 to remain bounded
      past $\sim 1$ h (see \S\ref{sec:critique}, item on the eq (2.5) instability).
    \item Implemented the 5-state conditional-mean closure eq (2.8) bare form
      (\texttt{scripts/closure\_validation.py}).
    \item Implemented antibody extension (4.1) with nominal $k_7{=}1$, $k_8{=}2$ (paper does
      not report them; only ratio $k_8[TAT]_0/k_7$ is inferable from Fig 7e); Auger extension
      (4.3--4.4) with nominal $k_9$ per $R$ set to $0.05$ h$^{-1}$ (paper reports only
      $k_9 \propto R$).
    \item Did \emph{not} reimplement the Nelder--Mead fit (eq 3.3) because the underlying
      foci/comet time series (Figures 4, 7e, 8b) are figure-only in the paper and in cited
      references [12, 17]. Table 1 values used verbatim.
\end{enumerate}

Software stack: pure CPython 3.13 + numpy + scipy. Total runtime for the full audit is
$\sim 2$ minutes on a single CherryRd CPU core. \textbf{No HPC, no GPU, no paid endpoints,
no network access required after the one-time PDF/XML pull.}

\section{Results vs paper}

Sanity numbers from the audit (from \texttt{artifacts/smoke\_results.json},
\texttt{closure\_validation.json}, and \texttt{claim\_audit.json}):

\begin{itemize}
    \item MDA-MB-468 24 h endpoint: $\langle X\rangle = 0.39$, peak $\langle Z\rangle = 73$ mol at $t=0.49$ h.
    \item MCF7 24 h endpoint (SSA / capped ODE): $\langle X\rangle = 0.040$, peak $\langle Z\rangle = 49$ mol at $t=0.10$ h.
    \item Auger AUC monotonicity: $R = 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 \Rightarrow$ AUC $=$ 5.4, 21.8, 29.3, 33.4, 36.0.
    \item MDA468 exact Gillespie at $t=1.0$ h ($n=100$): $\langle X\rangle=0.900, \langle Y\rangle=75.4, \langle Z\rangle=369.5$.
      Bare eq (2.5) at same point: $X=0.862, Y=80.7, Z=403$. Agreement $\sim 5\!-\!10\%$ ---
      confirms the paper's bare ODE form on the stable-fit line.
    \item MCF7 stability ratio $k_3 k_5/(k_4 k_6) = 1.000374$ (unstable).
    \item MDA468 stability ratio $= 0.986850$ (stable, barely).
    \item $k_5{=}0$ slowdown: MDA468 $8.45\times$, MCF7 $11.2\times$ (paper: ``approx.\ 10$\times$'').
    \item MDA468 SSA correlation of $(Z\ge Z^*)$ fraction vs $\langle Z\rangle$: Pearson $r = 0.994$.
\end{itemize}

Every qualitative prediction the paper makes is reproduced. The two quantitative disagreements are:
(1) the ad-hoc closure vs SSA RMS is $\sim 13\!-\!25\%$ on the dynamic range of $\langle Z\rangle$,
noticeably looser than the paper's Figure 3 plotted overlay would suggest (likely a mix of
tau-leap bias and true closure error); and (2) the MCF7 bare ODE (2.5) diverges past
$\sim 1\!-\!2$ h --- see below.

\section{Critique --- honest reading}\label{sec:critique}

\paragraph{This is a framework paper; ``replication'' means implementing the equations.}
Roughly two-thirds of what the paper claims is definitional: it defines a stochastic model, two
closures, two extensions, and a persistence measure. Those constructs are internally consistent
and locally reimplementable in $\sim$300 lines of Python; that is a real result, but it is not the
same thing as an empirical validation. Any ``REPLICATED'' verdict on this paper should be read as
\emph{the equations are correctly transcribed and simulate as advertised}, \emph{not} as \emph{the
paper's predictions have been re-tested against fresh data}.

\paragraph{Only a subset of testable claims can actually be tested from the paper alone.}
Of the 11 testable claims we enumerated, 2 (C-CLONOGENIC-R$^2$ and C-FIG4-FIT) cannot be tested at
all because the underlying data are figure-only in this paper and in the two cited experimental
papers. Even for the claims we \emph{did} test, the ``approx. 10$\times$'' k$_5{=}0$ slowdown claim
is verified only to $\sim$15--20\%, and the closure-vs-SSA agreement is looser than the paper's
plotted Fig 3 suggests. This does not falsify the paper, but it does mean that a fully independent
quantitative confirmation of the paper's core numerical claims is not currently possible from the
public record.

\paragraph{The bare eq (2.5) as printed is dynamically unstable at the published MCF7 fit --- a
paper-side reproducibility bug.} At MCF7's Table 1 constants, $k_3 k_5/(k_4 k_6) = 1.000374 > 1$;
the post-repair fixed point $(Y,Z) = (0,0)$ is a saddle with $\det J = -145$; and bare integration
of eq (2.5) to 24 h yields $\langle Y\rangle = 3622$ (vs $Y_{\max}=300$) and
$\langle Z\rangle = 11313$ (vs $Z_{\max}=1000$). The Z$_{\max}$ and Y$_{\max}$ caps of Table 2 are
enforced by the SSA \emph{by construction}, but they are \emph{absent} from the printed ODE. Either
the paper's Fig 4b was plotted to a horizon short enough to hide this, or the cap was applied
silently. We do not know which; either way this is a bug in the paper as printed. MDA-MB-468 sits
just on the stable side of the same bifurcation ($k_3 k_5/(k_4 k_6) = 0.987$), which suggests the
fitted parameter regime is generically very close to the saddle-node bifurcation --- a fragile
place to be doing quantitative predictions.

\paragraph{Only a subset of the frameworks was implemented.} Concretely: we implemented the
master equation, both moment closures, the antibody extension, and the Auger extension. We did
\emph{not} implement the Nelder--Mead fit against foci/comet time series (blocked by
figure-only data), and we did not implement the experimental-statistic tests (paired NCA OTM
$p$-value, clonogenic R$^2$). The AUC persistence measure was implemented but the surviving-fraction
correlation could not be tested because the surviving-fraction data are figure-only.

\paragraph{Framework predictivity vs descriptive fit.} The paper's core empirical claim is that
its 6-parameter fit reproduces MCF7 and MDA-MB-468 $\gamma$H2AX foci time series (Fig 4). With 6
free parameters and $\sim 14$ data points per line, this is likely to be descriptively good; the
paper does not report cross-validation, held-out data, or a comparison against a simpler baseline
(e.g.\ a two-timescale exponential decay). Whether the closure ODEs have \emph{genuinely}
predictive power beyond descriptive fitting is not resolved by the paper's own analysis and cannot
be resolved from the public record.

\paragraph{Antibody and Auger extensions are illustrative, not quantitatively pinned.} The paper
reports only $k_8[TAT]_0/k_7$ as a group (from Fig 7e slope) and $k_9 \propto R$ without a constant
of proportionality. So the extensions run and are internally consistent, but any absolute
antibody/Auger prediction rests on nominal choices we (or the paper's authors) had to make.

\paragraph{No author code.} No MATLAB script, no Gillespie SSA source is released. The SSA
language is not stated. This is not a hard blocker (equations are fully specified) but means we
cannot verify integrator tolerances, Nelder--Mead starting points, tau-leap step choices, or any
undisclosed regularisation.
