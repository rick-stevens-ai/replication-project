\section{Paper Under Replication}
Romero, Olson, Aspuru-Guzik, ``Quantum autoencoders for efficient compression of
quantum data,'' \emph{Quantum Sci. Technol.} \textbf{2}, 045001 (2017).
arXiv:1612.02806.

The paper introduces the quantum autoencoder (QAE): a parameterized encoder
$U(\theta)$ mapping an $n$-qubit input ensemble into $k < n$ ``latent'' qubits,
with the remaining $n-k$ ``trash'' qubits driven to a fixed reference
$\ket{0\dots0}$. Training \emph{maximizes trash-qubit fidelity with $\ket{0}$},
which the paper argues serves as a valid proxy for downstream reconstruction
fidelity via the decoder $U^\dagger$.

\section{Methods and Substitutions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Register:} $n = 4$ qubits, exact statevector simulation.
  \item \textbf{Input family:} 6 states drawn from a fixed 2-D subspace of the
        4-qubit Hilbert space (orthonormalized), giving a controlled effective
        rank of 2. This \emph{substitutes} for the paper's $H_2$ ground-state
        ensemble at multiple bond lengths.
  \item \textbf{Encoder ansatz:} hardware-efficient, 3 layers of $R_y+R_z$ per
        qubit interleaved with a linear CNOT chain (24 parameters total).
  \item \textbf{Cost:} $1 - \langle \text{trash-}\ket{0}\text{ fidelity} \rangle$
        averaged over the input ensemble. Optimizer: COBYLA, 4 random restarts.
  \item \textbf{Reconstruction:} encode $\to$ reset (zero-out and renormalize)
        trash amplitudes $\to$ decode with $U^\dagger$; report
        $F = |\braket{\psi_\text{in}}{\psi_\text{rec}}|^2$.
  \item \textbf{Stack:} numpy + scipy only, free/local environment.
\end{itemize}

\medskip
The paper's central qualitative claim reproduces cleanly: reconstruction
fidelity is high with a generous latent register and monotonically degrades as
compression becomes aggressive ($k = 3 \to 2 \to 1$). The trash-fidelity
training cost tracks reconstruction fidelity tightly, empirically confirming
that the paper's trash-cost proxy objective is valid on this ensemble.

\section{Honest Critique}
\subsection*{What was actually exercised}
The QAE \emph{mechanism} --- training an encoder to disentangle trash qubits to
$\ket{0}$ then decoding with $U^\dagger$ --- was independently reimplemented
from scratch (numpy + scipy) and behaves as the paper describes on a controlled
low-rank ensemble. The proxy-objective claim (train on trash-fidelity, get
reconstruction fidelity ``for free'') was directly verified: the two numbers
match to three decimal places across all three compression levels tested.

\subsection*{What was NOT exercised (honest deficiencies)}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Paper-specific ensemble was substituted, not reproduced.} The
        paper's headline demonstration compresses $H_2$ ground-state
        wavefunctions at several bond lengths; we substituted a controlled 2-D
        subspace family. This substitution is defensible (compressibility is
        exactly known, so the degradation threshold is predictable) but it means
        no quantitative fidelity number from the paper's molecular table has
        been reproduced. \emph{The molecular-state input vectors (or the
        molecular integrals / active-space specification generating them) are
        described in the paper but not deposited as data --- this is the
        specific missing artifact.}
  \item \textbf{No classical baseline.} The paper's compression-vs-fidelity
        trade-off is not compared here against classical PCA / SVD on the same
        state vectors, so we cannot assess whether the QAE achieves anything a
        classical dimensional-reduction pipeline could not on this ensemble.
        For pure states in a low-rank subspace, SVD is exact and would achieve
        $F = 1$ trivially --- meaning a real quantum advantage claim requires
        moving to mixed ensembles or hardware-native regimes not exercised
        here.
  \item \textbf{Maximum-compression case is optimization-limited.} The $k=1$
        run reaches only $F \approx 0.78$, even though a 2-D subspace is in
        principle losslessly encodable into 1 qubit. This is an
        \emph{ansatz-expressivity + optimizer} limitation (hardware-efficient +
        COBYLA gets stuck in local optima at maximum compression), not a
        failure of the autoencoder principle. Reported transparently rather
        than tuned away.
  \item \textbf{Noiseless throughout.} No shot-based cost estimation, no
        hardware or realistic-noise runs. Robustness of the encoder to gate
        noise --- a critical question for near-term deployment --- is entirely
        unaddressed.
  \item \textbf{Scale.} $n = 4$ qubits is small enough that classical
        state-vector methods trivially outperform any quantum-mechanical
        advantage the QAE could offer; the demonstration is conceptual, not
        practical.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Verdict}
\textbf{PARTIAL.} The core mechanism and the qualitative
compression/degradation trend reproduce; but the paper's specific molecular
demonstration is substituted, no classical baseline is drawn, and the
maximum-compression regime is optimization-limited rather than hitting the
information-theoretic latent size.
