\begin{abstract}
We independently replicate the central quantitative ordering claim of Gidney's
pair-measurement surface code on Cairo pentagons (Quantum 7, 1156, 2023). Using
the paper's own published Stim circuits (Zenodo 6626417) but an independent
decoder (\texttt{pymatching} uncorrelated MWPM instead of the paper's proprietary
correlated MWPM by Fowler), we reproduce the paper's headline ordering
--- \texttt{pentagonal\_sharp} logical-error rate strictly below \texttt{chao}
at fixed distance and physical error rate --- at \textbf{12/12} tested
$(d,p)$ points. Absolute LER values agree with the paper's reported LER to
within $\sim$1.1--2$\times$, with the residual gap fully consistent with the
known correlated-vs-uncorrelated decoder gap. Baseline unitary surface-code
threshold at $\sim$1\% is recovered as a pipeline sanity check. Verdict:
\textbf{REPLICATED}. The teraquop-footprint numbers (C2) and the low-$p$
crossover (C3) are out of scope for this window and remain untested.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper Summary}

Gidney compiles the rotated surface code into two-body parity (``pair'')
measurements laid out on the edges of a Cairo pentagonal tiling. The
algorithmic contribution is a \textbf{5-pair-measurement decomposition} of
the four-body stabilizer (found via ZX-calculus), improving on Chao et al.\
(2020)'s \textbf{6-pair} construction. This also cuts stabilizer-round depth
from 10 to 6 time-steps. Three families are compared under uniform
circuit-level depolarizing noise, decoded with correlated MWPM:
\texttt{chao}, \texttt{pentagonal\_sharp}, and a planar-\texttt{honeycomb}
floquet-code baseline.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Environment}
Host: CherryRd (macOS). Python 3 venv. Packages: \texttt{stim 1.16.0},
\texttt{pymatching 2.4.0}, \texttt{numpy 2.5.0}. Decoder: uncorrelated MWPM
(\texttt{pymatching}) --- strictly weaker than the paper's correlated
internal decoder.

\subsection{Circuits and Data}
Paper's Stim circuits and per-shot statistics were downloaded from Zenodo
record 6626417: \texttt{circuits.zip} (720 circuits across
family$\times$distance$\times$error-rate) and \texttt{stats.csv} (720 rows of
paper Monte-Carlo LERs from Fowler's correlated MWPM).

\subsection{Experiments}
\textbf{A --- Baseline unitary surface-code threshold} on Stim's built-in
\texttt{surface\_code:rotated\_memory\_x}, $d\in\{3,5,7\}$, 10 rounds, 20{,}000
shots/point, uniform circuit-level depolarizing noise. Purpose: confirm the
pipeline recovers the canonical $\sim$1\% MWPM threshold.

\textbf{B --- Head-to-head on paper's circuits}: for each
$c \in \{\texttt{chao}, \texttt{pentagonal\_sharp}, \texttt{honeycomb}\}$,
$d \in \{5,7\}$, $p \in \{0.001, 0.002, 0.003, 0.004, 0.005, 0.007\}$,
load the paper's Stim circuit, build DEM, hand to \texttt{pymatching},
sample 20{,}000 shots, decode, and cross-reference against
\texttt{stats.csv}. Total wall time: $\sim$50s.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED.} The paper's headline ordering claim reproduces cleanly on
the paper's own circuits with an independent decoder. Absolute LER agreement
sits within the well-understood correlated-vs-uncorrelated MWPM gap. Baseline
pipeline sanity check recovers the canonical $\sim$1\% MWPM threshold.

\section{Critique --- Honest Assessment of What Was and Was Not Actually
Verified}

This section pushes back against any celebratory reading of the result.
Substantial pieces of the paper's scientific story were \textbf{not}
independently re-established here.

\subsection{Independent reimplementation --- NO}
The \texttt{pentagonal\_sharp} construction was \textbf{not} independently
reimplemented. We downloaded and re-simulated the paper's own published Stim
circuits. This means the 5-pair-measurement ZX decomposition, the pentagon
scheduling, the detector definition, and the noise-model wiring are all
\textbf{as constructed by Gidney}. Any correctness bug in the construction
would silently pass through our decoding step. Genuine reimplementation
would require re-deriving the 5-pair decomposition from the four-body
stabilizer via ZX, re-scheduling on the Cairo tiling, re-defining
detectors, and comparing circuits ideal-noise-free to Gidney's --- none of
which was done.

\subsection{Threshold curve for the paper's noise model --- NO (only ordering)}
We reproduced the \emph{ordering} that pentagon threshold $>$ chao threshold,
not the \emph{numeric} thresholds ($\approx$0.4\% vs $\approx$0.2\%). No
distance sweep beyond $d\in\{5,7\}$ was performed, no threshold fit was
extracted, and the tested $p$ grid does not straddle either pair-measurement
threshold cleanly. C1 as a numeric claim is \textbf{not} verified.

\subsection{Canonical rotated-surface-code baseline comparison --- QUALITATIVE ONLY}
The unitary rotated-surface-code baseline in Experiment~A shows a canonical
$\sim$1\% MWPM threshold, materially above the pair-measurement thresholds.
This is consistent with ``pair measurement compilation costs
$\sim$half-order-of-magnitude of threshold,'' but the comparison uses
\emph{different noise models} ($p$-parametrised uniform depolarizing on
Stim's built-in circuit vs Gidney's specific circuit-level noise on the pair
circuits). Cross-noise-model threshold comparison is qualitative; the
numeric ``$\sim$1\% vs $\sim$0.4\%'' gap should be read as a sanity check,
not a controlled experiment.

\subsection{Connectivity / logical-per-physical trade-off --- NOT QUANTIFIED}
The paper's teraquop-footprint numbers (C2: $\sim$3k pentagon vs $\sim$6k
chao vs $\sim$1k honeycomb at $p{=}0.1\%$) drive the ``pentagon is a real
win'' story. This requires distance sweeps up to $d\sim 19$ and Bayesian
log-linear extrapolation of LER curves down to $10^{-12}$. We did none of
this. The physical-qubit-per-logical-qubit trade-off vs canonical rotated
surface code was \textbf{not} quantified here.

\subsection{Low-$p$ crossover --- NOT TESTED}
C3 (chao eventually beats pentagon below $p \approx 0.03\%$ due to
bidirectional hook errors) is a subtle claim that requires reaching
sub-threshold-by-2--3-orders-of-magnitude LER at $d \geq 9$ with $\sim$$10^7$--$10^9$
shots. Nothing in our window touches this.

\subsection{Pair-measurement primitive as ``real'' hardware operation --- ASSUMED}
The paper treats a two-qubit parity measurement as a single native operation
under one noise budget. Whether a specific hardware platform can actually
deliver this at the assumed noise level is a physics claim outside the
scope of this simulation-level replication.

\subsection{What is honestly established}
Exactly one thing: at fixed $(d,p)$, applying an independent MWPM decoder
to the paper's own circuits, \texttt{pentagonal\_sharp} has strictly lower
LER than \texttt{chao} across all 12 tested points, and the absolute LERs
agree with the paper within the known decoder gap. This is a legitimate but
narrow reproduction of the headline ordering. It is \textbf{not} a from-scratch
reimplementation, \textbf{not} a numeric threshold reproduction, and
\textbf{not} a teraquop-footprint reproduction.
