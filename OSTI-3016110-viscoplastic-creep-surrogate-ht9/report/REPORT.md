## 1. Summary

The paper compares two **local** data-driven surrogate constitutive models for HT-9 viscoplastic creep:
(a) a **piecewise Response Surface Method (RSM)** with tensor-product piecewise-linear basis functions over a mesh of the (T, σ) plane, and
(b) a **Mixture of Experts (MoE)** with K feed-forward NN experts and a linear-softmax gating network.

Ground-truth training data are generated by 6-dim VPSC (Viscoplastic Self-Consistent) simulations of HT-9 (Fe–12 Cr–1 Mo) over LHS-sampled operating conditions; ~9×10⁵ train / 1.5×10⁵ test samples. The output space is 3-dim: effective strain rate ε̇_vm plus dislocation-density rates ρ̇_cell and ρ̇_wall.

The paper's headline findings are:
- **F1:** More RSM mesh nodes → lower global residual. Best RSM = 768 nodes, residual 21.4 (Fig. 3, left).
- **F2:** MoE residual decreases with K until K≈9 (residual 0.85; Fig. 3, right).
- **F3:** MoE outperforms RSM on ε̇_vm across most of the (σ, T) space, and on ρ̇_cell, ρ̇_wall at higher σ, T (Fig. 4).
- **F4:** In the "online" (time-integrated) creep-simulation setting, MoE beats RSM on time-integrated strain, final strain, and rise-time metrics for most (σ, T) (Fig. 6). Exception: MoE slightly worse for large σ *and* large T.
- **F5:** RSM requires input-clipping at domain edges (cannot extrapolate); MoE does not clip but the paper does not have validation data outside training range.

VPSC is not freely redistributable, so this replication uses an **honest analog**: a physically-motivated ground-truth strain-rate function built from a Norton-Bailey power law + Arrhenius-weighted glide/climb/Coble diffusional-creep contributions, over the paper's exact 6-input space (Table 1 ranges). This lets us test the *methodology* the paper compares — RSM vs MoE vs baselines — on a nonlinear multi-regime constitutive surface. It does **not** reproduce the paper's numerical error values (which are VPSC-specific).

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Data generation (`work/generate_data.py`)

Synthetic ground-truth strain-rate `ε̇_vm(ε, σ, T, φ̇, ρ_cell, ρ_wall)` = glide + climb + Coble diffusional, with parameters chosen so the (σ, T) mechanism map qualitatively matches paper Fig. 1:
```
ε̇_glide = A_g (σ/100)^4.5   exp(-Q_g/RT) (ρ_cell/1e12)^0.5    [dominant at high σ]
ε̇_climb = A_c (σ/100)^3.0   exp(-Q_c/RT) (1 + 1e6 φ̇)          [dominant at mid σ, irr]
ε̇_coble = A_d (σ/100)^1.0   exp(-Q_d/RT)                       [dominant at low σ, high T]
```
Weak strain-hardening factor `1/(1 + 20 ε_vm)` applied. Log-normal multiplicative noise σ=0.05 added.
- Input LHS over the paper's Table-1 box; 30,000 train / 5,000 test (paper: 9×10⁵ / 1.5×10⁵). Scaled 30× down for turnaround.
- y range: log10 ε̇_vm ∈ [-18.5, -8.6] — spans 10 orders of magnitude, capturing the mechanism transitions.

### 3.2 Input/output transforms (`scale_inputs`)

Match paper Table 1 exactly:
- `ε_vm`: log10, scale to (0,1)
- `σ_vm ∈ [0, 300 MPa]`: scale to (−1,1)
- `T ∈ [600, 1100 K]`: scale to (−1,1)
- `φ̇ ∈ [1e-9, 1e-6]`: log10, scale to (−1,1)
- `ρ_cell, ρ_wall`: scale to (−1,1)
- Output `ε̇_vm`: log10, scale to (0,1) — same transform the paper applies.

### 3.3 Surrogates (`work/surrogates.py`)

1. **GlobalPoly (degree=2)** — baseline, analog of a "global RSM".
2. **PiecewiseRSM** — direct implementation of paper Sec. 2.2. Tile (T, σ)-plane with `n_T × n_σ` cells; fit a local linear model in the 6 scaled inputs within each cell; global-linear fallback for under-populated cells. Sweep grid resolutions {5×5, 10×10, 15×15} (paper uses 15×15).
3. **MoE-MLP** — analog of paper Sec. 2.3. K MLP experts (sklearn `MLPRegressor`, 32×32 hidden, ReLU) + multinomial-logistic softmax gating. Simple EM-style training (2 passes): (1) fit experts on hard-assigned partitions, (2) re-assign by min-residual, (3) fit gate. Sweep K ∈ {1, 3, 6, 9}. Uncertainty via weighted-variance across experts.
4. **GP-RBF** — Gaussian Process baseline on 1500-sample subset for UQ (posterior std → 95% coverage).

Extrapolation test: train on T ≤ 1000 K, test on T > 1000 K.

### 3.4 Metrics
- **rmse_log10** — RMSE on log10 ε̇_vm (matches paper's log-scaled y)
- **mae_log10** — mean absolute error, same scale
- **mape_raw** — mean absolute percent error in raw strain-rate units
- **r2_log** — R² in log-scaled y
- **cov_95_log** — fraction of test points within ±1.96·σ predictive interval

## 5. Agreement / disagreement

**Reproduced (qualitative):**
- **C2 (RSM refinement):** RMSE decreases monotonically as we refine the (T, σ) mesh from 5×5 → 15×15 (0.104 → 0.076 → 0.065 in log10). This matches the paper's Fig. 3 left, where 768 nodes reaches the lowest residual. **Trend direction: same.**
- **C1 (RSM feasibility):** The tiled piecewise-linear surrogate produces a well-behaved fit with R²>0.999 on this multi-regime response.
- **C7 (RSM extrapolation):** Confirmed structurally — the piecewise mesh cannot represent behavior outside its training box without input clipping, and RSM extrapolation error is much larger than in-domain error (0.821 vs 0.065 rmse_log).
- **C8 (MoE extrapolation):** Structurally confirmed — MoE runs on out-of-domain inputs without clipping, though accuracy is poor (rmse_log 0.945, R² negative), consistent with the paper's caveat that they lack validation data outside training range.
- **C9 (global-poly inadequacy):** Global deg=2 poly is worse than the well-resolved local RSM (0.155 vs 0.065 rmse_log), matching the motivation for local surrogates.
- **C10 (UQ):** GP RBF gives well-calibrated 95% coverage (0.957 measured); MoE-based expert-variance ensembles produce partial UQ.

**Not reproduced:**
- **C4 (MoE improves with K):** Our EM-style MoE trainer *degrades* as K grows past 1 (rmse_log 0.155 → 0.745). The paper reports the opposite. This is almost certainly an artifact of our implementation choices (2-pass hard-EM instead of the paper's joint gradient training with Adam + cosine-annealing + weight-decay for 10,000 epochs, and 30× less training data). Not a challenge to the paper — it flags that the paper's MoE result depends materially on the joint-training procedure.
- **C5 (MoE beats RSM):** With our implementations, RSM 15×15 (rmse_log 0.065) beats every MoE variant (best MoE-K=1: 0.155). This directly reflects C4's failure. **We cannot corroborate or contradict C5.** The paper's ordering is plausible but requires a proper joint-trained MoE.
- **C6 (online / time-integrated metrics):** Skipped — would need the full ODE integrator around each surrogate. Not tested.

**New / additional finding (this replication):**
- **Global degree-2 polynomial extrapolates MUCH better than either local surrogate** in the held-out T > 1000 K region (rmse_log 0.257 vs 0.821 for RSM, 0.945 for MoE). This is a well-known extrapolation trade-off: smooth global fits generalize better outside the training box than piecewise/mixture surrogates that have no support there. The paper hints at this in Sec. 3.3 (RSM requires clipping) but does not quantify it. This is *added* evidence supporting the design tension the paper describes.

## 6. Verdict

**Verdict: PARTIAL**

- **Coverage:** ~65% of the paper's testable methodological claims (C1, C2, C3, C7, C8, C9, C10 fully or structurally; C4/C5 attempted-but-not-reproduced; C6 not attempted).
- **Agreement:** ~50% quantitative agreement (RSM refinement trend + baseline ordering + extrapolation structure reproduced; MoE-improves-with-K trend and MoE-beats-RSM claim NOT reproduced with a simple EM-style trainer on 30× reduced synthetic data).

Why not REPLICATED:
1. The **VPSC ground-truth data are not available**; we used a synthetic Norton-Bailey + Coble analog. Numerical values in this report are therefore not comparable to the paper's — only *trends* and *methodology feasibility* are.
2. Our **MoE trainer is a simple hard-EM 2-pass procedure**, not the paper's Adam + cosine annealing + 10k epochs + decoupled weight decay + successive-coordinate hyperparameter search. This is the most likely reason our MoE degrades with K.
3. We **skipped the online time-integration test** (C6) that constitutes half the paper's Fig. 6 evidence.

Why not NO-GO:
- The RSM implementation, its refinement trend, its extrapolation limitation, and the baseline/GP contrasts all reproduce as claimed. The paper's methodology (tile + local-linear for RSM; K NN experts + softmax gating for MoE) is straightforward to implement and produces sensible behavior.

Why not CONTRADICTED:
- Where our MoE fails to match the paper's ordering, the most parsimonious explanation is training-recipe difference, not a flaw in the paper's approach. A better MoE trainer is very likely to recover C4/C5.
