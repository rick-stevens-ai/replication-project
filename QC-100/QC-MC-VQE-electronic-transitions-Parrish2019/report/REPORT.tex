\begin{abstract}
We independently re-implement, from scratch in NumPy/SciPy, the
Multi-Configurational Variational Quantum Eigensolver (MC-VQE) of Parrish
\emph{et al.}\ (PRL 122, 230401 (2019); arXiv:1901.01234) and reproduce its
central classical-simulator claims: (i) the exciton $\to$ spin-lattice
isomorphism (Eq.~8) and its FCI diagonalization (C1); (ii) MC-VQE excitation
energies within \SI{9.7}{\micro\electronvolt} of FCI for an N=12 LH2-type ring
(C2, ``tens of \si{\micro\electronvolt}''); (iii) oscillator strengths within
$0.09\%$ (MC-VQE) vs $2.5\%$ (CIS) --- MC-VQE $\ll$ CIS in every case (C3);
(iv) the state-averaged energy $=$ mean-diagonal contracted-H trace identity
(Eq.~6) exactly to residual $0.0$ (C5); (v) the N=8 linear H-aggregate stress
test where CIS is qualitatively wrong ($\sim 0.1$~eV / $66\%$ oscillator error)
while MC-VQE matches FCI ($\le 2$~meV / $\le 3\%$) (C7). C4 magnitude is
reproduced (sign is geometry-dependent). C6 (14 L-BFGS iterations to convergence
at N=18) and the full N=18 scale are \emph{not} completed within budget --- an
optimizer-efficiency / wall-time issue, not a physics disagreement. Verdict:
\textbf{REPLICATED}. No paid endpoints used.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper summary}

MC-VQE is a hybrid quantum/classical algorithm that computes the ground state
\emph{and} several low-lying excited states of a molecule on the same footing,
together with their oscillator strengths (hence absorption spectra). The
demonstration system is an \emph{ab-initio exciton model}: a photoactive
complex of $N$ chromophores, each reduced to two electronic states
(ground/excited), which maps exactly onto an $N$-qubit spin-1/2 lattice
Hamiltonian (Eq.~8):
\[
H = E\cdot I + \sum_A ( Z_A \hat{Z}_A + X_A \hat{X}_A )
   + \sum_{A>B} ( XX_{AB}\hat{X}_A\hat{X}_B + XZ_{AB}\hat{X}_A\hat{Z}_B
                + ZX_{AB}\hat{Z}_A\hat{X}_B + ZZ_{AB}\hat{Z}_A\hat{Z}_B).
\]
The four-stage algorithm is: (1) classically solve CIS for contracted
reference states $|\Phi_\Theta\rangle$; (2) optimize a state-averaged VQE
entangler $\hat{U}$ by minimizing the mean diagonal
$\langle\Phi_\Theta|\hat{U}^\dagger \hat{H}\hat{U}|\Phi_\Theta\rangle$;
(3) measure off-diagonal contracted-H elements via interference states
$(|\Phi_\Theta\rangle \pm |\Phi_{\Theta'}\rangle)/\sqrt{2}$;
(4) classically diagonalize the contracted Hamiltonian for Ritz
eigenvalues/eigenvectors. The paper simulates this classically (in-house
\emph{Quasar} simulator; no hardware, no noise) for an N=18 LH2 B850 ring
(Hilbert dim $2^{18}=262\,144$) and, in the supplement, an N=8 linear BChl-a
H-aggregate stack.

\section{Method (independent, from scratch)}

Everything is re-implemented in NumPy/SciPy
(\texttt{work/QC-MC-VQE-exciton.py}); no reuse of the paper's Quasar code.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Exciton model} from BChl-a monomer parameters (Qy $S_0\to S_1$
        gap $\approx 1.6$~eV, transition dipole $\approx 6.1$~D, difference
        dipole $\approx 1$~D) using the paper's supplemental dipole /
        transition-dipole two-body formula
        $V_{AB} = [\mu_A\!\cdot\!\mu_B - 3(\mu_A\!\cdot\! n)(\mu_B\!\cdot\! n)]/r^3$.
        Sign convention fixed so that $|0\cdots 0\rangle$ is lowest.
  \item \textbf{FCI:} full $2^N$ sparse Hamiltonian (SciPy CSR Pauli kron),
        lowest-$k$ via \texttt{eigsh} (N=18) or dense \texttt{eigh} (N$\le$12).
  \item \textbf{CIS:} $(N+1)$-dim single-excitation manifold
        $\{|0\cdots 0\rangle, |1_A\rangle\}$; classical diagonalization gives
        reference coefficients.
  \item \textbf{MC-VQE:} Matryoshka $R_y/F_y$ state-prep of contracted CIS
        references (statevector), SO(4) two-body entanglers (6 Givens angles
        each) on Hamiltonian-connectivity bonds (NN + ring closure);
        state-averaged L-BFGS from a zero-entanglement guess (paper's
        protocol); contracted-H built from diagonal expectations (Eq.~4) +
        interference-state differences (Eq.~5); classical \texttt{eigh} for
        Ritz eigenstates (Eq.~2).
  \item \textbf{Oscillator strengths:}
        $\hat{\mu} = \sum_A \mu_I I + \mu_Z \hat{Z} + \mu_X \hat{X}$;
        $O_{0\Theta} = (2/3)(E_\Theta-E_0)|\langle 0|\hat{\mu}|\Theta\rangle|^2$.
  \item \textbf{Rigorous alignment.} Each MC-VQE/CIS eigenstate matched to its
        maximum-$|$overlap$|$ FCI state; comparison restricted to FCI states
        with $>50\%$ single-excitation character. Double-excitation-dominated
        FCI states are reported separately (fundamentally outside the singles
        ansatz).
\end{enumerate}

Systems: N=8 linear H-aggregate stack (2 entangler layers) and N=12 cyclic
LH2-type ring (1 layer). An N=18 ring run was launched on uicgpu but did not
converge inside the wall-time budget --- see \S\ref{sec:limits}.

\section{Claim-by-claim verdict}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{C1 REPLICATED.} Exciton $\to$ spin-lattice built exactly as
        Eq.~8, diagonalized in full $2^N$ space for N=8, N=12.
  \item \textbf{C2 REPLICATED (ring).} N=12: max \SI{9.7}{\micro\electronvolt}
        --- squarely in the paper's ``tens of \si{\micro\electronvolt}'' claim.
        N=18 not converged in budget; N=12 same regime.
  \item \textbf{C3 REPLICATED (ring), supported (stack).} Ring MC-VQE oscillator
        error $0.09\%$ ($\ll 1\%$); stack CIS $65.8\%$ (qualitatively wrong),
        stack MC-VQE $\le 2.9\%$.
  \item \textbf{C4 magnitude reproduced.} CIS shifts up to $\sim 0.1$--$0.19$~eV
        in the H-aggregate. Sign is geometry-dependent (paper's supplement
        contrasts ring vs H-aggregate behavior).
  \item \textbf{C5 REPLICATED (exact).} Residual $0.0$ in both systems.
  \item \textbf{C6 PARTIAL.} MC-VQE converges from the zero-entangle guess but
        in 81 (N=12) / 163 (N=8) L-BFGS iterations vs the paper's 14 (N=18).
        Optimizer-conditioning issue (finite-difference gradient), not
        correctness --- the converged answer matches FCI to
        \si{\micro\electronvolt}.
  \item \textbf{C7 REPLICATED.} N=8 stack: CIS off by $\sim 0.1$~eV / $66\%$;
        MC-VQE within $\le 2$~meV / $\le 3\%$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Genuine critique and honest limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
This replication is honest but bounded. The following gaps are important:

\paragraph{(1) Headline N=18 not exercised, only N=12 surrogate.}
The paper's headline system is the N=18 LH2 B850 ring
($2^{18}=262\,144$-dim Hilbert space). Our N=18 uicgpu run did not converge
within the wall-time budget; we substitute an N=12 ring in the same regime.
Because MC-VQE accuracy is dominated by the ansatz expressivity (which is a
per-bond property, not a system-size one), the N=12 result is scientifically
informative --- but it is \emph{not} a bit-for-bit reproduction of the paper's
banner number. This is the single largest gap between what we report and what
the paper reports. We flag it here explicitly rather than silently substituting.

\paragraph{(2) Molecule-specific TeraChem monomer numbers not public.}
The paper's exact BChl-a $\omega$PBE/6-31G* monomer energies, transition
dipoles, and coordinates live in a supplemental data packet that is not in the
arXiv source. We use a physically-faithful BChl-a parametrization + the
paper's dipole model. This means we reproduced the \emph{method-accuracy}
claims (MC-VQE vs FCI vs CIS), not the paper's exact reported eigenvalues.
Someone with the paper's data packet could tighten this to
molecule-for-molecule identity.

\paragraph{(3) Comparison against classical CASCI / SA-CASSCF baselines not
performed.} A stronger scientific critique would compare MC-VQE energies not
only to CIS (a weak singles baseline) but also to state-averaged CASSCF or
CASCI, which are the classical excited-state gold standards. The paper focuses
on FCI-in-the-exciton-model, which we reproduce, but the broader question
``does MC-VQE beat classical multi-reference methods at comparable cost?'' is
not addressed by this replication and remains an open scientific critique of
the paper itself. (This is part of Q1 in \texttt{open\_questions.json}.)

\paragraph{(4) Cost-vs-accuracy trade-off not quantitatively swept.}
We report the paper's regime (1--2 entangler layers) and confirm
\si{\micro\electronvolt} accuracy, but we did not sweep circuit depth,
parameter count, or measurement count vs achieved accuracy. This would be
needed to independently verify the paper's implicit claim that MC-VQE is
\emph{efficient} (few parameters, few iterations), not just \emph{accurate}.
Our C6 partial verdict is one facet of this gap.

\paragraph{(5) No noise / no hardware.} Like the paper's own demonstration,
this is a noiseless statevector simulation. MC-VQE's robustness to shot noise,
readout error, and depolarizing noise is neither claimed by the paper nor
verified here.

\paragraph{(6) Double-excitation states out of scope.} By construction of a
singles ansatz, doubly-excited FCI states are unreachable. We flag them
explicitly and exclude them from matched metrics rather than letting them
inflate the reported error.
