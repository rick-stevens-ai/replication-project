\section{Paper summary}
The paper introduces \emph{block-encoding circuits} for
arithmetically-structured matrices --- sparse matrices whose
non-zero pattern and repeated-value structure can be captured by short
arithmetic formulas. Block encodings are the input model for the
quantum singular value transform (QSVT) and downstream algorithms
(HHL / matrix inversion, Hamiltonian simulation, phase estimation).

A block encoding of a matrix $A$ is a unitary $U$ on a larger Hilbert
space such that
\[
    U = \begin{pmatrix} A/\alpha & \cdot \\ \cdot & \cdot \end{pmatrix},
\]
where $\alpha$ is the \emph{subnormalisation}. The paper's ``base
scheme'' (Sec.~2.1) uses three oracles $O_c$, $O_r$, $O_{\mathrm{data}}$
plus a Hadamard-diffusion register, and gives subnormalisation
$\alpha = S \cdot \|A\|_{\max}$ with a flag-qubit count of
$1 + \log_2 S$ --- \emph{constant in $N$} for any matrix family of
fixed row/column sparsity $S$.

\section{Method}
Full construction in \texttt{src/block\_encode\_tridiagonal.py}. In
brief:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Build the tridiagonal $(d,m) \leftrightarrow (i,j)$ labelling
        (paper Eqs.~57--60) with padding $D_{\mathrm{pad}} = 2N$,
        $M = 2$, $S = 4$.
  \item Realise $O_c$ and $O_r$ as \emph{explicit permutations} of a
        $4N$-dimensional Hilbert space (since $MD = NS = 4N$).
  \item Build $R_{\mathrm{data}}$ as a multiplexed $R_y$ loading
        $\alpha_{dm} = A_d / \|A\|_{\max}$ per in-range pair, $0$
        otherwise.
  \item Assemble
        $U = H_S^{\dagger} \, O_r \, R_{\mathrm{data}} \, O_c^{\dagger} \, H_S$
        on registers \texttt{data (1q) $\otimes$ s (2q) $\otimes$ out ($\log_2 N$ q)}
        for a total of $3 + \log_2 N$ qubits.
  \item Verify unitarity ($\|U^\dagger U - I\|_\infty < 10^{-14}$) and
        the block-encoding property by reading matrix elements
        $\langle 0,0,i|\,U\,|0,0,j\rangle$ and by an independent
        Qiskit \texttt{Statevector} evolution.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Critique (what was actually done vs what the paper claims)}

\textbf{What this replication verifies.}
The block-encoding \emph{unitary} was independently reimplemented from
scratch (not vendored) for the tridiagonal family, and the two headline
\emph{numerical} claims --- (a) the encoded top-left block equals
$A/\alpha$ to machine precision, and (b) subnormalisation
$\alpha = S \|A\|_{\max}$ --- reproduce exactly. The
\emph{structural} flag-qubit claim ($1 + \log_2 S$ vs
$3 + \log_2 N$) is verified from the actual constructed circuits, not
copied from the paper.

\textbf{What this replication does \emph{not} verify.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Toffoli / T-count comparisons.} The paper's dominant
        practical claim is a Toffoli cost of $O(D)$ per data-loading
        (via QROM, ref.~[17]). We verified $D = 2N$ non-trivial
        rotations for the tridiagonal family but did \emph{not}
        re-derive or measure a compiled Toffoli count. Any claim of
        the form ``fewer T-gates than a generic block encoding'' is
        \emph{not} independently substantiated by this run.
  \item \emph{Baseline comparison.} We tabulated Gily\'en et al.'s
        flag-qubit count from its closed form ($3 + \log_2 N$) rather
        than implementing that scheme. A true head-to-head against a
        generic LCU or select/swap block encoding on the same
        tridiagonal instance is missing --- we do not have gate-count
        or subnormalisation numbers for the baseline circuit as
        actually compiled.
  \item \emph{Preamplified / PREP-UNPREP schemes} (Secs.~2.2, 2.3).
        These are where the paper claims stronger subnormalisation
        wins for certain matrices. Not exercised at all here.
  \item \emph{Other matrix families} (2D Laplacian, Toeplitz,
        checkerboard, extended binary tree; Sec.~3). Only the
        tridiagonal family was replicated.
  \item \emph{Scaling beyond $N=16$.} Statevector verification stops at
        $N=16$ because a full unitary is $O(N^2 S^2)$ complex entries;
        the flag-qubit-count table (up to $N=4096$) is a
        \emph{structural} check, not a full simulation.
  \item \emph{Downstream algorithmic gain.} No QSVT / HHL /
        Hamiltonian-simulation end-to-end was run. The claim that this
        block-encoding advantage carries through to a downstream
        query-complexity advantage is inherited from the paper, not
        tested.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Structure-vs-cost trade-off.}
Quantitatively, the trade-off we \emph{did} exercise is
$(N,\alpha,\text{flag qubits})$: for the tridiagonal family the paper
predicts $\alpha \to S \|A\|_{\max} \le 4$ (independent of $N$) with a
constant flag-qubit count, and we observe $\alpha \in [3.81, 3.90]$
and $3$ flag qubits at all $N$ tested. The paper's promise ``keep the
matrix structure narrow $\Rightarrow$ keep the block-encoding cost
low'' is verified for this narrow structure. It is \emph{not} verified
that the same story survives when the structure is relaxed
(nearly-tridiagonal / banded-with-noise / general-sparse). That is one
of the open questions.

\textbf{Honest headline: exercised.} The single headline that got
exercised is the flag-qubit / subnormalisation claim for the
tridiagonal instance (Sec.~3.3 of the paper), which reproduces exactly.
The broader claim that the construction \emph{generalises} across all
four matrix families in Sec.~3 was \emph{not} directly retested.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED} on the exercised headline (block-encoding property
$+$ flag-qubit / subnormalisation for the tridiagonal family). Broader
claims (other families, preamplified schemes, Toffoli / T-count,
downstream QSVT) remain untested and are captured as open questions.
