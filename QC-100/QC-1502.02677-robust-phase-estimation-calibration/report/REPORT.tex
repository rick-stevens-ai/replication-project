\begin{abstract}
We independently replicate the headline scaling claim of Kimmel, Low, and
Yoder (KLY, arXiv:1502.02677v3, ``Robust Calibration of a Universal
Single-Qubit Gate-Set via Robust Phase Estimation''): that the RPE
protocol on a single-qubit over-rotated gate $R_x(\pi/2 + \varepsilon)$
achieves Heisenberg-limited precision, $\sigma(\hat A) \propto N^{-1}$,
versus the shot-noise baseline $\sigma \propto N^{-1/2}$. A Qiskit
\texttt{Statevector} cross-check confirms the analytic cos/sin experiment
probabilities to $1.8\times 10^{-14}$; a 14-generation, 500-trial
Monte-Carlo sweep fits slope $-0.98$ (R$^2 = 0.997$) for RPE and $-0.50$
(R$^2 = 0.9997$) for shot noise --- both within tolerance of theory
($-1.0$ and $-0.5$ respectively). Verdict: \textbf{REPLICATED} for the
headline Heisenberg-scaling claim (C1--C3). The SPAM-robustness
experiment (C4) and multi-parameter gate-set nesting (C5) are
acknowledged extensions and were not run.
\end{abstract}

\section{One-line verdict}

\textbf{REPLICATED.} Single-qubit RPE on $R_x(\pi/2 + 0.037)$ reproduces
Heisenberg scaling $\sigma \propto N^{-0.98}$ (R$^2 = 0.997$); shot-noise
control reproduces $\sigma \propto N^{-0.50}$ (R$^2 = 0.9997$).

\section{Paper summary}

Kimmel--Low--Yoder show that a non-adaptive variant of the Higgins
\textit{et al.} phase estimation procedure can calibrate the amplitude
and off-resonance systematic errors of a universal single-qubit gate
set with \emph{provable Heisenberg scaling} and \emph{without perfect
state preparation, measurement, or entanglement}. Two experiment
families are defined at each generation $j = 1,\dots,K$ with
$k_j = 2^{j-1}$ gate applications:
\begin{align}
p_0(A,k) &= \tfrac{1}{2}(1 + \cos(kA)),\\
p_+(A,k) &= \tfrac{1}{2}(1 + \sin(kA)),\\
\widehat{kA} &= \operatorname{atan2}(\hat a_+ - M/2,\ \hat a_0 - M/2) \in (-\pi,\pi].
\end{align}
Estimates are unwrapped by range restriction to
$(\hat A_j - \pi/2^j, \hat A_j + \pi/2^j]$. Theorem~I.1 / V.1 gives
$\sigma(\hat A) = O(1/N)$ for $N = \sum_j 2 M k_j$. The 2021 erratum
tightens the analytic constant in Sec.~V.5 but explicitly confirms the
Heisenberg scaling result.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Environment}
Python 3.14.6, qiskit 2.5.0, numpy 2.5.0, scipy 1.18.0, matplotlib
3.11.0, macOS 25.3.0 on CherryRd (single laptop CPU, no accelerator, no
paid endpoint).

\subsection{Circuits}
Cos: $|0\rangle \to R_x(A)^k \to \text{measure } Z$. Sin: same followed
by $S$ then $H$ before measurement. Qiskit exact statevector
probabilities match the analytic $\tfrac{1}{2}(1\pm\cos(kA))$ and
$\tfrac{1}{2}(1\pm\sin(kA))$ identities to $1.8\times 10^{-14}$ across
$k \in \{1,2,4,\dots,256\}$
(\texttt{data/qiskit\_verify.json}, verdict MATCH).

\subsection{RPE core}
\texttt{code/rpe\_sim.py}: per-generation Binomial sampling
$n_0 \sim \operatorname{Bin}(M,p_0)$, $n_+ \sim \operatorname{Bin}(M,p_+)$
(equivalent to \texttt{qc.measure()} but faster); local estimate
$\widehat{kA} = \operatorname{atan2}(n_+/M-1/2,\ n_0/M-1/2)$; range unwrap by
picking the multiple of $2\pi/k$ closest to the previous-generation
estimate. Query budget $N = \sum_j 2 M k_j$.

\subsection{Baseline}
$k=1$ only, $M=N/2$ shots on each of cos/sin, same atan2 estimator ---
the ``no ladder'' control called out in the discussion around Eq.~V.4.

\subsection{Sweep}
$A = \pi/2 + 0.037$; $K \in [1,14]$ (so $k_{\max} = 8192$); $M=30$; 500
trials per point; seed 20260703. RPE slope fit uses $K \ge 4$ (once the
ladder is well past the range-unwrap ambiguity region); baseline slope
fit uses all points.

\section{Critique --- honest assessment of what this replication does and does not establish}

This section is deliberately less flattering than the summary. The
replication is real, but the scope is narrow.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Scope: single parameter, no SPAM, no comparison to RB.}
  We only test the RPE scaling exponent on a single amplitude
  over-rotation $\varepsilon$ in a noiseless simulator with a uniform
  binomial sampler. The KLY paper's stronger and more novel claims are
  (a) robustness to SPAM errors --- \emph{not tested here}; (b) fewer
  samples than randomized benchmarking (RB) for the same precision ---
  \emph{no side-by-side RB comparison run}. Both would substantially
  strengthen the story.
\item \textbf{Heisenberg scaling was verified qualitatively/quantitatively
  but not proved.} The fitted slope $-0.98$ is within 2\% of $-1.0$,
  which is close enough to be visually indistinguishable on log--log,
  but we did not verify the analytic \emph{constant} in front of the
  $1/N$, nor did we probe the crossover $N$ where the ladder actually
  starts to beat shot noise (small-$N$ region is dominated by
  range-unwrap noise; we bypassed it by fitting $K \ge 4$).
\item \textbf{The 2021 erratum was noted but not exploited.} The erratum
  tightens the analytic constant in Sec.~V.5 using the correct
  $\pi/(3k_j)$ bound. We did not re-derive or test the tightened bound
  --- only the scaling exponent, which the erratum confirms still holds.
  If a stricter replication is wanted, the tightened constant should be
  plotted as an overlay on Fig.~\ref{fig:precision}.
\item \textbf{Range-unwrap heuristic differs slightly from the paper's
  literal prescription.} We use the ``pick the multiple of $2\pi/k$
  closest to the previous estimate'' heuristic, which is algorithmically
  equivalent to KLY's range restriction
  $\hat A_{j+1} \in (\hat A_j - \pi/2^j, \hat A_j + \pi/2^j]$ under the
  assumption that consecutive generations do not wrap by more than one
  period. This is fine for the noiseless / low-noise regime tested here
  but was not stress-tested at higher $\varepsilon$ or with adversarial
  noise.
\item \textbf{Binomial sampler is a valid but non-obvious shortcut.} We
  use \texttt{np.random.binomial} rather than executing
  \texttt{qc.measure()} loops on the Qiskit simulator, on the grounds
  that the two are mathematically identical (proven by the
  \texttt{qiskit\_verify.py} $1.8\times 10^{-14}$ agreement). This saves
  minutes of wall-clock at the cost of one layer of indirection between
  the report and the ``real'' Qiskit backend.
\item \textbf{No hardware / no realistic device noise model.} A stronger
  replication would run RPE against a Qiskit \texttt{FakeBackend} with
  T1/T2 + readout error, or better yet on IBM Quantum hardware, to
  demonstrate the SPAM-robustness claim empirically. That is the
  natural next step.
\item \textbf{No two-qubit or multi-qubit extension.} The paper is
  explicit that RPE generalizes; we tested only the single-qubit core.
\end{enumerate}

Bottom line: the \emph{scaling law} claim (C1--C3) is genuinely
reproduced. The \emph{robustness} and \emph{comparison-to-RB} claims
that motivate the paper's practical relevance are \emph{not} tested
here, and reasonable follow-up work is captured in the Open Questions
section.

\section{Verdict}

\textbf{REPLICATED} for the headline Heisenberg-scaling claim
(C1--C3). C4 (SPAM robustness) and C5 (multi-parameter nesting) are
scoped-out extensions that would require additional simulation runs but
sit on top of the same estimator core; no evidence in this replication
contradicts them.
