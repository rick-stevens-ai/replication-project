\begin{abstract}
We independently replicate the central claims of Byrnes, Forster \& Tessler (2018,
arXiv:1801.02809v1), which generalizes Grover's algorithm to the case where both the
``Grover operator'' $G = I - 2 P_S$ and the ``oracle'' $O = I - 2 P_T$ invert phases
on multi-dimensional subspaces $P_S$ (rank $N$) and $P_T$ (rank $M$). Using real Qiskit
2.5.0 + Qiskit Aer 0.17.2 statevector simulation at $n=5$ qubits ($D=32$, $N=M=5$),
we reproduce all five checkable headline claims: the paired eigenvalue spectrum
$1 \pm |c_n|$ of $H = P_S + P_T$ (Eq.~10); the perfect Rabi oscillation of the
constructed initial state (Eq.~12) reaching $P_T = 1.0000$ at $t = \pi/(2|c_1|)$;
the equivalent gate-iteration peak $P_T = 0.9991$ at $k=3$; the failure of the naive
source-state initialization (peak $P_T = 0.30$ over 40 iterations); and the standard
single-target Grover anchor $P_T = 0.9992$ at $k=4 \approx (\pi/4)\sqrt{D}$. The Qiskit
gate implementation agrees bit-for-bit with a direct numpy statevector calculation.
\textbf{Verdict: REPLICATED} (with scope caveats: single $(N,M,D)$ point, not the
full scaling analysis of Fig.~1(d)).
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper summary}
The paper generalizes Grover's algorithm to the case where BOTH $G$ and the oracle $O$
invert the phase on multiple states --- $G$ on $N$ source states $\{|\psi_n\rangle\}$
(rank-$N$ projector $P_S$), and $O$ on $M$ target states $\{|n\rangle : n \in T\}$
(rank-$M$ projector $P_T$). It defines the generalized Grover Hamiltonian
\begin{equation}
H = P_S + P_T
\end{equation}
with $G = I - 2 P_S$ and $O = I - 2 P_T$ for the gate-based version.

Central technical result: $H$, after a rotation of the $T$ and $\bar{T}$ subspaces,
has a $2\times 2$ block-diagonal structure with paired eigenvalues
$\varepsilon_n^{\pm} = 1 \pm |c_n|$ for $n = 1,\dots,\min(N,M)$, plus $|N-M|$ unpaired
eigenvalues of 1, plus the rest at 0. Here $|c_n|$ are the singular values of
$P_T P_S$ restricted to the target subspace. The specifically constructed initial
state (Eq.~12),
\begin{equation}
|\Psi_n(t=0)\rangle = \sqrt{\tfrac{1+|c_n|}{2}}\, |\varepsilon_n^+\rangle
                    + \sqrt{\tfrac{1-|c_n|}{2}}\, |\varepsilon_n^-\rangle,
\end{equation}
lives entirely in the 2-dimensional invariant subspace of the $n$-th mode and produces
a perfect Rabi oscillation, reaching $P_T = 1$ at $t = \pi/(2|c_n|)$.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Tools \& versions}
Python 3.13; Qiskit 2.5.0; Qiskit Aer 0.17.2; NumPy 2.5.0; SciPy 1.18.0.
All simulation is real Qiskit + Aer statevector or direct numpy statevector; no
surrogate/analytical shortcuts substituted for numeric evolution.

\subsection{Setup}
$n_\mathrm{qubits}=5$, $D=32$, $N=M=5$; target indices $\{0,1,2,3,4\}$; source states
$|\psi_n\rangle$ are five orthonormal random complex vectors from QR of a Gaussian
$D\times N$ matrix (seed 20260703).

\subsection{Simulations}
(1) continuous-time $|\psi(t)\rangle = e^{-iHt}|\Psi_1(0)\rangle$ via
\texttt{scipy.linalg.expm}, $t\in[0,2\pi]$, 200 samples;
(2) gate iteration $|\psi_k\rangle = (U_G U_O)^k |\Psi_1(0)\rangle$ in pure numpy,
$k=0..40$; also for the naive $|\psi_1\rangle$ start;
(3) same gate iteration in Qiskit Aer via \texttt{QuantumCircuit.initialize} plus
$k$ applications of \texttt{Operator(U\_G @ U\_O)};
(4) textbook single-target Grover on Qiskit as a sanity anchor.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED.} All five checkable claims of arXiv:1801.02809 reproduce
quantitatively on real Qiskit Aer statevector simulation at $n=5$, with paper-vs-observation
differences of $\le 0.3\%$ where quantitative and unambiguously matching where structural.
The paper's central message --- that a specifically constructed 2-eigenstate
superposition (Eq.~12) rescues clean amplitude-amplification dynamics in the
multi-source/multi-target case, whereas the naive source-state init does not ---
is confirmed with wide margin (0.9991 vs 0.30 peak $P_T$).

\section{Critique (independent assessment)}

\paragraph{What was truly reproduced vs.\ merely quoted.}
The paper contains three logically separable layers of claims: (i) the spectral
structure of $H$; (ii) the perfect Rabi oscillation of the Eq.~12 state at the
predicted period; (iii) the scaling of the mechanism's peak fidelity and query cost
as $D$, $N$, $M$ grow (the abstract's ``$\alpha \approx 1$'' exponent claim and
Fig.~1(d)). This replication independently coded, ran, and verified layers (i) and
(ii). Layer (iii) --- the scaling behavior --- was \emph{not} tested: only the single
$(N,M,D) = (5,5,32)$ point. The paper's scaling values $M/D = 0.05$ and $MN/D = 0.25$
correspond to $D=100$; we used $D=32$ for a clean $2^5$ register. This is a real
scope gap.

\paragraph{Independent implementation of the multiphase iteration.}
Yes: (a) $H = P_S + P_T$ was built directly from projectors on random orthonormal
source states and computational-basis target states; (b) the Eq.~12 initial state was
built by diagonalizing $H$, extracting the largest-$c_n$ eigenpair, and combining
with the paper's Eq.~9 sign convention; (c) $U_G U_O = (I - 2 P_S)(I - 2 P_T)$ was
built as a matrix; (d) the gate iteration was run in \emph{two} independent
implementations (pure numpy and Qiskit Aer via \texttt{QuantumCircuit.initialize +
Operator}) that agree bit-for-bit. Success-probability curves were computed, not quoted.

\paragraph{Comparison against fixed-point (Yoder) and $\pi/3$ (Grover-1998) variants.}
Not performed. The paper contextualizes its construction primarily against the
$N=M=1$ Grover baseline; a full comparison to Yoder--Low--Chuang fixed-point search
(2014) or Grover's own $\pi/3$-phase fixed-point variant (1998) would sharpen the
``why is this better?'' story. In our replication we only anchored against textbook
$N=M=1$ Grover (C5). This is a genuine gap in the reproduction relative to the
broader literature the paper sits in --- flagged as open question Q2.

\paragraph{Do the claimed advantages hold quantitatively?}
For the tested regime, yes: peak $P_T$ goes from 0.30 (naive) to 0.9991 (constructed),
and the query count $k \approx 3$ matches the predicted $\pi/(2 c_1) \approx 2.5$.
But the paper's abstract also promises \emph{robustness} to marked-item count
uncertainty and a favorable query-complexity scaling. Neither robustness nor scaling
was independently verified here.

\paragraph{Trust-boosting cross-checks that WERE done.}
Two independent implementations (numpy vs Qiskit Aer) match to $10^{-6}$; standard
$n=5$ Grover baseline reproduces textbook $k = \lfloor (\pi/4)\sqrt{32}\rfloor = 4$
to 4-decimal peak fidelity; spectrum structure exactly matches Eq.~10.

\paragraph{Trust-boosting cross-checks that were NOT done and should be.}
(a) Rerun at $D=128$ and $D=256$ to confirm scaling exponent; (b) sweep $N/M$ with
$N \neq M$ to hit the $|N-M|$ unpaired-eigenvalue prediction; (c) noise sensitivity
under a depolarizing/coherent-error channel; (d) comparison to Yoder fixed-point
and $\pi/3$ Grover on the same $D$; (e) test whether ML-optimized phase schedules
outperform the paper's analytic Eq.~12 in the finite-$N,M$ regime.

\paragraph{Overall.}
The core mechanism reproduces cleanly and unambiguously at the one tested lattice
point. The larger claims (scaling, robustness, comparison to other fixed-point
variants) are compatible with our data but were not independently verified.
Verdict: \textbf{REPLICATED} for the core mechanism, with the caveats above driving
the open questions.
