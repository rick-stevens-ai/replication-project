# Brief — BVBRC-114

**Paper:** Khajanchi BK, Hasan NA, Choi SY, Han J, Zhao S, Colwell RR, Cerniglia CE, Foley SL. *"Comparative genomic analysis and characterization of incompatibility group FIB plasmid encoded virulence factors of Salmonella enterica isolated from food sources."* BMC Genomics 18:570 (2017). DOI: 10.1186/s12864-017-3954-5 · PMID: 28768482 · PMC5541697. Open access (CC BY 4.0).

**What/Why (1 paragraph):** The paper characterises IncFIB(K)-family virulence plasmids in seven *S. enterica* isolates from food/animal sources (six *S.* Typhimurium — SE163A/SE397/SE452/SE478/SE696A/SE710A — and one *S.* Heidelberg — SE819, deliberately IncFIB-deficient) and quantifies the contribution of the plasmid-encoded **SitABCD** (Fe²⁺/Mn²⁺ ABC transporter) and **aerobactin (iucABCD-iutA)** iron-acquisition systems to virulence. Bioinformatic claims (in silico serotype prediction, SNP phylogeny of a monophyletic 5-strain Typhimurium subclade, IncFIB rep gene diversity, conservation of Sit/aerobactin sequences across host bacterial species) are all reproducible from the public WGS records under NCBI BioProject **PRJNA312617**. Wet-lab claims (Caco-2 persistence of SE819::IncFIB transconjugant; qRT-PCR of sit/iuc under iron-limitation) are not testable in silico. This replication executes the bioinformatic pipeline end-to-end on the actual public assemblies and reaches a **PARTIAL** verdict (all four in-silico core claims independently reproduced; the two wet-lab claims marked "not testable" — not "failed").
