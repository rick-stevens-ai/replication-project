\begin{abstract}
We independently replicate the three headline claims of Gottlieb \& Shu,
\emph{Math.\ Comp.} 67(221):73--85, 1998 (DOI
\href{https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-98-00913-2}{10.1090/S0025-5718-98-00913-2}):
(C1) formal orders of accuracy 2 and 3 for the two- and three-stage
strong-stability-preserving (SSP; originally TVD) Runge--Kutta schemes;
(C2) their TVD property under CFL $\le \Delta t_{\mathrm{FE}}$, with the
predicted failure both past that bound and for a negative-$\beta$
counter-example RK method; and (C3) the optimal SSP coefficient
$c^{\ast} = 1$ for both schemes. All three claims are reproduced.
\textbf{Verdict: REPLICATED.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper summary}

Gottlieb \& Shu (1998) characterizes explicit Runge--Kutta time integrators
that preserve any strong-stability property already enjoyed by the
forward-Euler discretization of the same spatial operator $L$. If
$\|u + \Delta t\,L(u)\| \le \|u\|$ holds for $\Delta t \le \Delta
t_{\mathrm{FE}}$, then an SSP-RK scheme with SSP coefficient $c$ preserves
the same bound for $\Delta t \le c\,\Delta t_{\mathrm{FE}}$. The paper's
headline objects are:

\paragraph{SSP-RK2 (Heun, Shu--Osher form).}
\begin{align*}
u^{(1)} &= u^{n} + \Delta t\, L(u^{n})\\
u^{n+1} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\,u^{n} + \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(u^{(1)} + \Delta t\,L(u^{(1)})\bigr).
\end{align*}

\paragraph{SSP-RK3 (Shu--Osher).}
\begin{align*}
u^{(1)} &= u^{n} + \Delta t\, L(u^{n})\\
u^{(2)} &= \tfrac{3}{4}\,u^{n} + \tfrac{1}{4}\bigl(u^{(1)} + \Delta t\,L(u^{(1)})\bigr)\\
u^{n+1} &= \tfrac{1}{3}\,u^{n} + \tfrac{2}{3}\bigl(u^{(2)} + \Delta t\,L(u^{(2)})\bigr).
\end{align*}
Both attain the optimal SSP coefficient $c^{\ast} = 1$ among schemes of
their stage count; equivalently they preserve the forward-Euler CFL
exactly. The paper further shows that non-SSP RK schemes containing a
negative $\beta$ (i.e.\ a downwind Euler sub-step) can violate TVD even at
arbitrarily small CFL.

\subsection{Experiment design}

\paragraph{C1 --- Temporal order.} Scalar ODE $u' = -u$, $u(0)=1$,
$t_{\mathrm{final}}=1$, exact $\exp(-1)$. $\Delta t = 1/N$ for
$N \in \{8,16,32,64,128,256,512\}$. Report $|u_{\mathrm{num}} - \exp(-1)|$
and $\log_{2}$ rates; observed order = mean of last three rates. This is
the standard temporal-order test used by Gottlieb--Shu; it avoids the
purely-imaginary-eigenvalue issue that would otherwise contaminate an
SSP-RK2 order study.

\paragraph{C2 --- TVD.} Periodic 1D linear advection $u_{t} + u_{x} = 0$
on $[0,1]$, $N=200$ cells, step IC ($u=1$ on $(0.3,0.7)$, else $0$).
Spatial op = 1st-order upwind (which is forward-Euler-TVD for CFL $\le
1$). Report initial TV, maximum TV, and fractional increase
$(\mathrm{TV}_{\max}-\mathrm{TV}_{0})/\mathrm{TV}_{0}$. Five cases:
SSP-RK2/3 at CFL $= 1$ (should be TVD); SSP-RK2/3 at CFL $= 1.05$
(should fail); non-SSP negative-$\beta$ RK2 at CFL $= 0.5$ (should fail
even at low CFL).

\paragraph{C3 --- Empirical $c^{\ast}$.} Binary search for the largest
CFL such that step IC + upwind + the given SSP-RK integrator produces
zero fractional TV increase (tolerance $10^{-10}$) at
$t_{\mathrm{final}}=0.2$, $N=400$. Interval $[0.05,\,1.5]$ bisected 40
times; final gap $< 10^{-4}$.

\section{Genuine critique}
\label{sec:critique}

This is a genuine, self-adversarial critique of \emph{our own replication},
not a paper review. The paper's mathematics is water-tight; the honest
concerns below are about the scope and rigor of \emph{our} tests.

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{Scope is intentionally narrow.} We tested C1--C3 only: order,
TVD at/past $c^{\ast}$, and empirical $c^{\ast}=1$. We did not touch the
paper's 4-stage results, the non-existence result for a 4-stage
order-4 all-positive SSP-RK, or any of the paper's larger-stage-count
optimal-coefficient constructions. A ``REPLICATED'' verdict here means
``the three headline claims we tested were reproduced,'' not ``the paper
is entirely reproduced.''

\item \textbf{C3 is empirical, not a proof of optimality.} The binary
search demonstrates $c \ge 0.99997$ preserves TVD on \emph{this} problem
(step IC + upwind + $N=400$). It does not prove $c^{\ast} = 1$ is
\emph{optimal}, which is a separate order-barrier argument in the paper.
An adversarial IC or a different limiter could in principle expose a
smaller usable CFL than our binary search finds; we did not stress-test
across problems.

\item \textbf{The C2 CFL $= 1.05$ mismatch between SSP-RK2 ($3.3\!\times\!10^{2}$
blow-up) and SSP-RK3 ($5.5\!\times\!10^{-2}$ mild growth) deserves a
sentence.} The paper predicts both fail past $c^{\ast}$; both do.
Quantitative asymmetry (RK3 fails ``gently'' at 1.05) is consistent with
RK3 having more dissipation margin near the SSP boundary, but we did not
sweep CFL to characterize how sharply each scheme transitions. A denser
sweep would strengthen the demonstration.

\item \textbf{Tolerance and search resolution matter.} C3 uses tolerance
$10^{-10}$ on fractional TV increase and 40 bisections
($\text{gap} \approx 9\!\times\!10^{-5}$). Both are engineering choices.
Tightening the tolerance below round-off would be meaningless; loosening
it would let sub-percent overshoots masquerade as ``TVD.''

\item \textbf{Spatial discretization is fixed at first-order upwind.} That
is exactly forward-Euler-TVD, which is the correct baseline for testing
the SSP property. But a limited high-resolution spatial op (MUSCL, WENO)
would exercise SSP-RK in the regime where practitioners actually use it,
and would provide a stronger test than a step IC + first-order upwind.
Not required by the paper, and not in our scope.

\item \textbf{LLM-judge is a courtesy check, not evidence.} The judge sees
only our numeric summary; it cannot audit the code. Its ``REPLICATED''
verdict adds independent-eyes value but is not the reason the paper
replicates. The reason is the tables in \S4.

\item \textbf{No cross-implementation check.} We wrote a single NumPy
implementation and compared it to the paper. A second, independently
written implementation (e.g.\ a Julia or C port) would harden the result
against subtle bugs that happen to give the ``right'' answer.

\item \textbf{Reproducibility of exact numbers.} All three experiments are
deterministic (fixed grid, fixed initial condition, no RNG). Any modern
NumPy + IEEE-754 should reproduce our numbers to full precision. We did
not, however, pin exact package versions in a lock-file; a future NumPy
that changes accumulation order could shift the last digit.

\end{enumerate}

\emph{None of these caveats change the verdict.} They set honest limits on
what the verdict covers.

\section{Verdict}

\textbf{REPLICATED.} All three tested claims of Gottlieb \& Shu (1998) ---
formal 2nd/3rd order (C1), TVD/SSP with directional confirmation (C2), and
