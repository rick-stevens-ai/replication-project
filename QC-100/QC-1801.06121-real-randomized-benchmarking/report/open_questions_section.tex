\section{Open Questions}
\label{sec:open-questions}

The following five questions remain genuinely open after this replication.
Each is accompanied by the specific basis for why the current replication
did not answer it, and a concrete next-step probe that can be executed on
free endpoints (local Qiskit-Aer venv or the Argo proxy).

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{Chirality sensitivity under matched-infidelity coherent
vs.\ incoherent noise.} Does real RB actually distinguish a chiral coherent
error (e.g.\ small $\exp(-i\theta Y)$ rotation) from an achiral Pauli-mixture
of matched infidelity, and by how much given a fixed sequence budget?

\emph{Basis.} The paper's central practical selling point beyond group-size
economy is sensitivity to real-vs-complex error components. Our replication
only injected a real-diagonal ($X, Z$-only) channel, so the
chirality-distinguishing sensitivity was never directly exercised.

\emph{Next steps.} Extend \texttt{src/real\_rb.py} to inject two
matched-infidelity channels: a coherent $\exp(-i\theta Y)$ rotation with
$\theta$ chosen so $1 - \bar F = 0.005$, and an incoherent Pauli mixture with
the same $1 - \bar F$. Run both standard RB and real RB on each; report the
fitted $(b, c)$ pair from the two-exponential real-RB fit and the effective
standard-RB decay. Sensitivity metric:
$|b_{\text{chiral}} - b_{\text{achiral}}| / \sigma_{\text{joint}}$.

\item \textbf{Two-branch fit identifiability at $n \geq 2$.} Does
$F(m) = A + b^m B + c^m C$ hold for two-qubit real Clifford RB with a generic
(non-diagonal) noise channel, and can $b$ and $c$ be reliably separated at
realistic sequence budgets?

\emph{Basis.} For $n = 1$ with real initial state, only the $b$-branch
survives. For $n \geq 2$ or mixed initial states, the $c$-branch reactivates
and the fit becomes bi-exponential, which is notoriously ill-conditioned.
We did not test $n = 2$ nor a mixed-branch scenario at all.

\emph{Next steps.} Enumerate the $n = 2$ real Clifford group generated by
$\{Z_1, Z_2, H_1, H_2, CZ_{12}\}$. Build a two-qubit Qiskit circuit with a
generic 2-qubit Pauli-plus-coherent noise channel. Fit both branches with
\texttt{scipy.optimize.curve\_fit} plus a Bayesian bi-exponential prior (or
a bootstrap). Report parameter identifiability as a function of $M$ and
$m_{\max}$.

\item \textbf{Interleaved-real-RB behavior on out-of-group targets.} How
does an interleaved real-RB variant behave when the interleaved gate is
itself outside the real Clifford group (e.g.\ an $S$ or $T$ gate)?

\emph{Basis.} Standard interleaved RB requires the target to be a Clifford.
For complex-Clifford ($S$) or non-Clifford ($T$) targets, the mixed-group
sequence is not a real 2-design, and the residual decay may confound gate
infidelity with the reference-decay ratio.

\emph{Next steps.} Formulate $R\, g\, R\, g\, R\, g\, \dots$ where $R$ is a
random real Clifford and $g \in \{S, \sqrt{X}, T\}$ is fixed. Simulate;
measure whether decay remains exponential and whether a meaningful
$g$-fidelity estimator can be extracted. Compare against standard
interleaved RB on the same $g$.

\item \textbf{Extension to matchgate circuits.} Can real RB be extended to
matchgate circuits (nearest-neighbor free-fermion circuits), which naturally
live in an orthogonal subgroup, and does it give sharper matchgate-error
estimation than standard Clifford RB?

\emph{Basis.} Matchgates form a subgroup isomorphic to $\mathrm{SO}(2n)$
acting on $2n$ Majorana modes. Real Cliffords and matchgates share an
orthogonal-group structure; the intersection would suggest a native fault
diagnostic for free-fermion / measurement-based architectures.

\emph{Next steps.} Identify matchgate $\cap$ real Clifford via
Jozsa--Miyake structure. Simulate a matchgate circuit under matchgate-native
noise (nearest-neighbor $XX + YY$ errors) and under generic noise; compare
real-RB decay to matchgate-tomography fidelity. Requires a matchgate
simulator (free: OpenFermion or hand-rolled Pfaffian simulator).

\item \textbf{Character-RB and ML-estimator variants for real RB.} Can a
modern character-RB / cross-entropy-benchmarking (XEB) or ML-based
estimator reduce the sequence budget of real RB by $3$--$10\times$ while
preserving unbiased $b$-estimation on realistic non-Markovian noise?

\emph{Basis.} Character RB (Helsen \emph{et al.}) generalizes RB to
arbitrary compact groups via irrep projection; XEB uses Porter--Thomas
statistics; recent neural estimators have shown wall-clock improvements
over \texttt{curve\_fit} for standard RB. None have been benchmarked
head-to-head on real RB specifically.

\emph{Next steps.} Implement a character-RB estimator over the orthogonal
group irreps (trivial, adjoint, $\mathrm{sym}^2$). Compare to (a)
\texttt{curve\_fit}, (b) a small transformer or gradient-boosted regressor
trained on simulated (survival-curve $\to b$) pairs. Metric: RMSE$(b)$ at
fixed shot budget. Free endpoints: local venv plus Argo Opus for code
review.

\end{enumerate}
