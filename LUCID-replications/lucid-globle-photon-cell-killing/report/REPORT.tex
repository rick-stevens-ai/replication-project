\section{Paper}
Herr L, Friedrich T, Durante M, Scholz M. \emph{A Model of Photon Cell Killing
Based on the Spatio-Temporal Clustering of DNA Damage in Higher Order Chromatin
Structures.} \textit{PLoS ONE} 9(1): e83923.
DOI: \href{https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0083923}{10.1371/journal.pone.0083923}.

The GLOBLE (\emph{GiantLOop Binary LEsion}) model treats photon cell killing as
the spatio-temporal clustering of DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs) inside
higher-order chromatin loop domains ($\sim$2\,Mbp giant loops). A five-level ODE
system tracks isolated versus clustered DSBs and their conversion into lethal
lesions, reproducing dose-rate and split-dose survival behaviour and reducing to
the linear-quadratic (LQ) plus Lea--Catcheside form in the appropriate limit.

\section{Summary of the replication}
This reproduction is equation- and parameter-level: the paper distributes no
author code and no raw digitized experimental survival points. Using the paper's
equations and the Table~2 cell-line parameters (17 cell lines / 22 parameter
sets), we implemented the GLOBLE ODE (\texttt{code/globle.py}) and a re-pass
driver (\texttt{code/repass/repass\_globle.py}) that produces six independent
claim batches (A--F) plus the pass-1 figure reproductions.

\section{Method}
Tools: Python 3.11, NumPy, SciPy (ODE integration), Matplotlib. The ODE is the
same \texttt{globle.py} implementation written in pass~1; the re-pass driver
\texttt{code/repass/repass\_globle.py} adds claims A--F. All numbers below are
regenerated by that driver; full JSON is under \texttt{results/repass/}. Source
text used: \texttt{paper.md} (a Marker/Nougat-style extraction of the PLoS PDF,
sha \texttt{cb54cfea\dots}), cross-checked against
\texttt{pdftotext -layout artifacts/paper.pdf}; Tables 2 and 3 and all numeric
values agree between the two parses.

\item \textbf{Claim C (split-dose, all 5 lines).} 5/5 show $S(t_1{=}10\,\text{h}) > S(t_1{=}0)$.
\item \textbf{Claim D (Table 3).} 11/11 dose-rate-column and 11/11

\item \textbf{Claim E (analytical limits).} High-DR ($10^6$ Gy/h) vs static GLOBLE:
$\max|\Delta\ln L| = 1.46\times10^{-2}$. Low-DR ($10^{-3}$ Gy/h) vs Eq. 38:

\item \textbf{Claim F (Eq. 8 identity).} $\max$ relative error $2.80\times10^{-3}$.
\item \textbf{Pass-1 carry-over.} Fig. 4 GLOBLE/LQ equivalence

\section{Critique of the replication}
This section is deliberately not a whitewash.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Equation-level, not data-level.} Every ``REPLICATED'' here is a
reproduction of the paper's own \emph{equations and tabulated parameters} against
the paper's own \emph{stated} numbers. That is genuinely valuable (it confirms the
model is internally consistent and correctly transcribed), but it is a weaker form
of replication than reproducing the underlying \emph{experimental} survival curves.
Claim 7 --- the only test that touches measured biology --- is BLOCKED.
\item \textbf{Several ``new'' claims are near-self-consistency checks.} Claim D
(Table 3 $=$ Table 2) and Claim A ($\varepsilon_i<\varepsilon_c$) largely verify
that our transcription of the tables is faithful and that the ODE reproduces the
closed forms the paper derives from the same parameters. They confirm correctness
of implementation but do not independently corroborate the model against nature.
\item \textbf{Median-HLT$_i$ is column-ambiguous.} The paper's ``0.458\,h'' is only
matched by the split-dose column (5 lines); the dose-rate column median differs
(0.487\,h). We report both honestly rather than cherry-picking the matching column.
\item \textbf{Convergence tolerance is regime-dependent.} The high-dose-rate limit
only hits the 0.05 log tolerance at $\ge10^6$ Gy/h; at $10^4$ Gy/h it fails for the
shortest-HLT$_i$ lines (e.g. CHO~K1). This is expected physics, not a defect, but it
means the ``instantaneous limit'' claim is only reproduced at unphysically high dose
rates.
\item \textbf{Fig.-4 caption inconsistency (F8).} The paper's Fig.-4 caption gives
$\varepsilon_i = 0.002$ for $\alpha/\beta = 5.26$\,Gy, internally inconsistent with
Eq.~8; we used $\varepsilon_i = 0.005$ (carried from pass~1). This is a genuine
ambiguity in the source that we resolved by choosing the Eq.-8-consistent value.
\item \textbf{Deterministic-effect claim (Claim 5) is model-level only.} We
reproduce the dose-rate dependence of the model's deterministic-effect predictions
but do not validate against clinical pneumonitis / bone-marrow endpoints.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED} (equation/parameter level, all 17 Table-2 cell lines, both
Table-3 columns, analytical limits, and text statistics), with the single empirical
overlay claim (Claim 7) \textbf{BLOCKED} on the authors' undistributed digitized
experimental points (friction tag F2). Coverage 9/10, Agreement 9/10. The verdict
label is unchanged from pass~1 but its strength is materially increased.
