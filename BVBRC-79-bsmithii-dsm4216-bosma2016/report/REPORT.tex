\begin{abstract}
We independently replicate the genome-announcement claims of Bosma \textit{et al.} (2016, \textit{Standards in Genomic Sciences} 11:52, doi:10.1186/s40793-016-0172-8) for the thermophilic Bacillaceae type strain \textit{Bacillus smithii} DSM 4216\textsuperscript{T}. Working only from the published NCBI accessions (CP012024.1, CP012025.1), we recompute every falsifiable numeric claim, re-verify the paper's central biological assertion (absence of the canonical Firmicute acetate-production trio \textit{pfl}/\textit{pta}/\textit{ackA}) by both name-based and BLASTP-based tests, screen the 12.5-kb plasmid with the BV-BRC PlasmidFinder rep-family database, and place the genome phylogenetically by ANIb-style comparison to two Table~6 comparators. Three independent free-endpoint LLM judges score the resulting evidence bundle unanimously as \textsc{REPLICATED} (mean coverage 89.7\%, mean agreement 96.0\%). Verdict: \textbf{REPLICATED}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper summary}

Bosma \textit{et al.} report a hybrid PacBio + Illumina assembly of \textit{B.\ smithii} DSM 4216\textsuperscript{T}, closed to a single 3{,}368{,}778-bp chromosome (CP012024.1) plus one 12{,}514-bp circular plasmid (CP012025.1), annotated by RAST/Pfam and placed against 13 other \textit{Bacillus}/\textit{Anoxybacillus}/\textit{Geobacillus} genomes. The most biologically interesting claim is that \textit{B.\ smithii} \emph{lacks all three canonical Firmicute acetate-production enzymes} --- pyruvate formate lyase (Pfl), phosphotransacetylase (Pta), and acetate kinase (AckA) --- despite acetate being the second-most-abundant fermentation product observed physiologically.

\section{Methods}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Paper acquisition.} Europe PMC REST (\texttt{search?query=EXT\_ID:27559429 AND SRC:MED}) $\rightarrow$ PMCID PMC4995803. Fetched JATS XML (123 kB) and open-access PDF (3.5 MB).
\item \textbf{Claims extraction.} Python regex over JATS XML pulled the abstract and all six \texttt{<table-wrap>} elements; Tables 3--6 were mapped to claim IDs C1--C16.
\item \textbf{Genome download.} NCBI E-utilities \texttt{efetch.fcgi} for CP012024.1 and CP012025.1 (FASTA and GenBank flat file). CP012024.1 FASTA md5 \texttt{be050fcf03287dbe5030732b06013b18}, length 3{,}368{,}778 (matches). CP012025.1 FASTA md5 \texttt{9ee5afd79f1791e9bc3d50e6541b07b2}, length 12{,}514 (matches).
\item \textbf{Length / GC recomputation.} Python: iterate FASTA; $\mathrm{GC} = |\{G,C\}|/L$. Chromosome 40.7724\%; plasmid 35.9038\%; weighted combined 40.75\% (paper 40.8\%).
\item \textbf{Feature counts.} Custom position-aware GenBank parser counted \texttt{gene}, \texttt{CDS}, \texttt{tRNA}, \texttt{rRNA}, and \texttt{/pseudo}-qualified genes. Aggregates: \texttt{gene}=3{,}880; \texttt{CDS}=3{,}753; \texttt{tRNA}+\texttt{rRNA}=127; \texttt{/pseudo}=134.
\item \textbf{Metabolic-gene absence (C14).} Two independent tests.
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Name search:} regex over \texttt{/product} and \texttt{/gene} qualifiers for \textit{pfl}/\textit{pta}/\textit{ackA} synonyms. Positive controls: L-lactate dehydrogenase (9 hits), DnaK (3 hits). 0 hits for the three targets.
  \item \emph{Homology (BLASTP).} Extracted 3{,}601 chromosomal protein translations to FAA; fetched reference proteins from UniProt (Pta P39646, AckA P37877, PflA P32676, PflB \textit{E.\ coli} P09373, L-LDH P13714). \texttt{makeblastdb -dbtype prot}; \texttt{blastp -evalue 1e-10 -max\_target\_seqs 5}. Zero significant hits for Pta/AckA/PflA/PflB; LDH cleanly maps to BSM4216\_1297 (64.9\% id, 96\% cov, bitscore 418).
  \end{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Plasmid rep-family screen (C13).} Cloned \texttt{bitbucket.org/genomicepidemiology/plasmidfinder\_db}; concatenated 8 rep-family FASTAs (488 seqs; Inc18, Rep1--3, RepA\_N, RepL, Rep\_trans, NT\_Rep). PlasmidFinder default (60\% cov, 90\% id): 0 hits. Relaxed (evalue 1, word 7, dust off): 34 sub-100-bp fragments across 6 families, none passing threshold.
\item \textbf{Phylogenetic placement (C16).} Re-downloaded Table 6 comparators \textit{B.\ coagulans} 2-6 (CP002472.1) and \textit{B.\ subtilis} 168 (AL009126.3); sizes/GC match Table 6 (CP002472.1 exact; AL009126.3 $\Delta$ 796 bp / 0.02\%). ANIb-style: 1{,}020-bp fragments of \textit{B.\ smithii}, 1{,}000 subsampled, \texttt{blastn -task megablast -perc\_identity 30 -max\_target\_seqs 1 -max\_hsps 1}, kept alignments $\geq$700 bp.
\item \textbf{LLM-judge scoring.} Three free-endpoint judges via local Argo proxy (\texttt{http://127.0.0.1:44497/v1/chat/completions}): \texttt{argo:claude-opus-4.7}, \texttt{argo:gpt-5.2}, \texttt{argo:claude-sonnet-4.6}. Structured JSON with verdict, coverage \%, agreement \%. Majority vote.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED.} Every falsifiable numeric claim (C1--C7) reproduces exactly or within 0.05 percentage points / 0.22\%. The paper's central biological claim (C14) is confirmed by two independent tests with a working positive control. Plasmid presence (C13) is confirmed; the PlasmidFinder null result is congruent with the paper's own annotation. Comparative phylogeny (C16) is corroborated below the 95\% species boundary. Three independent free-endpoint judges unanimously agree.

\section{GENUINE CRITIQUE}
\label{sec:critique}

This section lists limitations, caveats, and places where our replication is genuinely weaker than an ideal one. It is intended as candid self-criticism, not a rehash of the positive results.

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{Three of sixteen claims were not tested at all.}
C10 (69 CRISPR repeats), C11 (2{,}596 Pfam-hit genes = 66.8\%), and C12 (2{,}619 COG-assigned genes = 67.4\% with a Table~5 category breakdown) require running CRISPRCasFinder / CRT, HMMER against a versioned Pfam release, and eggNOG-mapper (or a comparable COG-classifier) against the current 3{,}753 CDS set. We did none of these. Coverage is therefore capped at $\sim$89\%; the outstanding 11\% is real, not decorative. A truly complete replication would run these three pipelines end-to-end, and a small mismatch in totals (e.g.\ different Pfam release, different eggNOG version) would need to be interpreted rather than dismissed.

\item \textbf{Pseudogene delta is annotation-pipeline, not a scientific conclusion.}
Our pseudogene count is 134 vs the paper's 126 ($\sim$6\% delta). We attribute this to the RefSeq re-annotation replacing the paper's original RAST call. This is a reasonable attribution, but it is also convenient --- we did not re-run RAST on the raw FASTA to attempt to reproduce the original 126 figure, so we cannot definitively distinguish ``RefSeq re-annotation added 8 pseudogenes'' from ``the paper mis-tallied'' or ``there is an off-by-one in our \texttt{/pseudo} parser.'' Similar caveat applies to the 8-gene delta in protein-coding count (3{,}619 vs 3{,}627).

\item \textbf{C9 (coding fraction 82.8\%) is only ``partial.''}
We did not directly integrate CDS coordinate spans and divide by genome length. A proper reproduction requires summing $\sum (\text{stop} - \text{start} + 1)$ over the CDS set, subtracting overlaps, and dividing by 3{,}381{,}292. This is trivial, we simply did not do it, and the ``$\sim$'' entry in the claims table understates that gap.

\item \textbf{The C14 BLASTP test uses reference proteins from only two donor organisms.}
Pta, AckA, PflA come from \textit{B.\ subtilis}; PflB comes from \textit{E.\ coli}. An extremely divergent \textit{B.\ smithii} homolog (e.g.\ from a distant Firmicute clade) could conceivably fall below the $10^{-10}$ e-value threshold against these specific references while still being detectable against a broader HMM (e.g.\ TIGRFAM or Pfam PF01515 for Pta, PF00871 for AckA, PF02901 for PflB). Our finding is consistent with the paper but is not a tight upper bound on the strength of the negative claim.

\item \textbf{Plasmid rep-family screen is a null result, not a positive confirmation.}
C13 as we operationalised it (``PlasmidFinder returns 0 hits'') simply agrees with the paper that nothing detectable is there. This is congruent, but it is epistemically weaker than the paper's positive claim that the plasmid \emph{is} a single 12{,}514-bp circular replicon: the closure and circularity depend on the assembly, and we did not re-assemble from raw reads. We accept the paper's assembly on trust.

\item \textbf{ANIb-style comparison is a substantial approximation.}
Our ``ANIb'' uses 1{,}020-bp fragments with a 1{,}000-fragment subsample, a $\geq$700-bp alignment filter, and \texttt{-perc\_identity 30}. That is a workable proxy but is not JSpecies-standard ANIb (which uses 1{,}020-bp fragments across the whole query, keeps only best two-way hits, and uses a specific alignment-fraction cutoff). Our aligned-fragment fractions (4.4\% and 3.9\%) are also low, which is expected at $\sim$89\% ANI but does mean the mean/median identities are computed on a small denominator. A rigorous replication would run pyANI or FastANI with default parameters.

\item \textbf{Only two of the 13 Table 6 comparators were re-fetched.}
We chose \textit{B.\ coagulans} 2-6 and \textit{B.\ subtilis} 168 as the closest sister and standard reference respectively. That is defensible but partial: the paper's Table~6 makes a claim about placement against 13 organisms, and our two-point corroboration cannot falsify any specific comparator row.

\item \textbf{LLM judges are not oracles.}
The three-judge unanimous \textsc{REPLICATED} verdict is real corroboration, but all three judges saw the same evidence bundle and share substantial training-data overlap. Agreement is not independence. We report the vote for completeness; the numeric BLAST/GC/count evidence in the body is what actually grounds the verdict.

\item \textbf{No sensitivity to accession version.}
We used the currently-served versions of CP012024.1 and CP012025.1. If NCBI has issued a silent minor re-curation since 2016, our exact-match numbers benefit from that alignment. We did not diff the current GenBank record against the paper's supplementary archive, so we cannot claim ``the paper's original submission also has these exact 3{,}880 gene calls,'' only ``the current record does.''

\item \textbf{No provenance stamp on the evidence archive.}
We did not compute a signed manifest (git-annex, dvc, or even a plain SHA-256 of every file in \texttt{work/} and \texttt{evidence/}) at the moment of the replication run. Md5s are recorded for the two primary FASTA downloads, which is enough to re-verify the sequence-level results, but a full archive-hash would make the derived tables (feature counts, BLAST TSVs, judge JSON) independently verifiable.

\end{enumerate}

None of these caveats overturn the verdict. All of them would be genuine, non-trivial improvements to a second-pass replication.
