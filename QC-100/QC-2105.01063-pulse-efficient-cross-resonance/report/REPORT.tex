\section{Paper summary}
The paper introduces a \emph{pulse-efficient} Qiskit transpiler
pipeline that (a) exposes cross-resonance-based $R_{ZX}(\theta)$ as a
native continuous-angle two-qubit gate whose calibrated pulse is
obtained by linearly scaling the pulse area of the already-calibrated
$\mathrm{CX} = R_{ZX}(\pi/2)$, and (b) inserts a template-substitution
pass that rewrites $\mathrm{CX}\text{-}R_z(\theta)\text{-}\mathrm{CX}$
($=R_{ZZ}(\theta)$) and other common two-qubit blocks into echoed
pairs of scaled $R_{ZX}(\theta/2)$ pulses. Because the total
cross-resonance time is $|\theta|$ (vs.\ $\pi$ for the full-CX
version), the circuit spends less time in the noisy CR interaction
and, empirically, achieves higher fidelity \emph{without any extra
calibration}. Reported gains: up to $\sim 50\%$ process error
reduction for $R_{ZZ}(\theta)$ and arbitrary SU(4) blocks, and
$42\text{--}52\%$ schedule-duration and $38\%$ max-avg-cut-error
reduction for depth-1 QAOA MAX-CUT on an 11-node non-native graph on
\texttt{ibmq\_mumbai}.

\subsection{C1 --- $R_{ZZ}(\theta)$ sweep (12 pts on $[0.1,\pi]$)}
Baseline: $\mathrm{CX}\,R_z(\theta)\,\mathrm{CX}$, CR-time $= \pi$.
Pulse-efficient: echoed $(I\!\otimes\! H)\,R_{ZX}(\theta/2)\,(X\!\otimes\!
I)\,R_{ZX}(-\theta/2)\,(X\!\otimes\! I)\,(I\!\otimes\! H)$, CR-time $=
|\theta|$. Sanity: $|\mathrm{Tr}(U_{\mathrm{PE}}\cdot
R_{ZZ}(\theta)^\dagger)|/4>0.999$ for every $\theta$.
Noise: per-gate depolarising rate $p_g = 0.02\cdot|\theta_g|/(\pi/2)$
(the paper's App-C coherence-limited model). Simulated with
\texttt{AerSimulator(method="density\_matrix")}; fidelity is
$\langle\psi_{\mathrm{ideal}}|\rho_{\mathrm{noisy}}|\psi_{\mathrm{ideal}}\rangle$.

\subsection{C2 --- Depth-1 QAOA MAX-CUT on K4}
Linear coupling map $0\text{-}1\text{-}2\text{-}3$ forces SWAP
insertion (non-hardware-native, matching paper's intent). Ideal
statevector $\to \langle H_C\rangle$ on a $5\times 5$ $(\gamma,\beta)$
grid. CNOT-basis transpile at optimization level 3; PE transpile
walks the DAG and replaces $\mathrm{CX}\text{-}R_z(\theta)\text{-}
\mathrm{CX}$ triples with echoed $2R_{ZX}(\theta/2)$ blocks
(equivalent to the paper's \texttt{zz2} template; done manually
because Qiskit's \texttt{TemplateOptimization} is $O(|C|^7)$ and
prohibitively slow for the sweep). Same noise model, 8192 shots.

\section{Critique --- limits of this replication (honest)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{No real hardware.} The paper's principal claim is that
  the improvement survives on real IBM Q hardware
  \emph{without additional calibration}. This replication only
  operates in simulation, so C4 is untested. Reproducing the
  hardware curves would require IBM Q Network access to
  \texttt{ibmq\_mumbai}-class devices and pulse-level scheduling
  privileges.
  \item \textbf{No independent Qiskit-Pulse re-implementation of the
  pulse-shape scaling.} We assumed the paper's model that
  scaling the pulse area of the calibrated $\mathrm{CX}=R_{ZX}(\pi/2)$
  produces a valid $R_{ZX}(\theta)$; we did not re-derive that
  from a Hamiltonian tomography of a physical device. The paper's
  fidelity-vs-duration curves reported at the pulse level (Fig.\ 2
  region) are therefore \emph{not} independently reproduced here.
  \item \textbf{No comparison against the default compiled CR
  baseline at pulse level.} Our ``CNOT baseline'' is a
  gate-level CX pair; the paper's baseline is IBM's default
  compiled CR schedule. Our comparison is a fair simulation-level
  analog, not a bit-exact reproduction of the paper's default-CR
  reference.
  \item \textbf{Hamiltonian tomography and hardware calibration were
  not addressed}, because they are not testable in simulation.
  This is the largest ``hardware-only'' scope gap.
  \item \textbf{Process tomography vs.\ state fidelity.} We use state
  fidelity of the noisy channel on $|00\rangle$ as the scalar;
  average gate fidelity via Chi-matrix would be a stricter reproduction.
  \item \textbf{SU(4) generalisation (Sec.\ III) not attempted.} A
  full Cartan-decomposition + Weyl-chamber sweep is beyond the
  compute budget and adds no new information about the headline claim.
  \item \textbf{11-node $\to$ 4-node scaling.} K4 preserves the
  \emph{shape} of the reduction curve but the absolute
  max-deviation numbers are not directly comparable to the
  paper's 11-node values (only the \emph{percentage} reduction
  is).
  \item \textbf{Template-optimisation pass replaced by DAG walk.} We
  emulate rather than invoke \texttt{TemplateOptimization} because
  the built-in pass has $O(|C|^7)$ cost. The unitary equivalence
  is asserted in-line, so the replacement is semantically
  faithful, but not identical code path.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Headline exercised?} Yes. Both quantitative headlines
(``$\sim 50\%$ RZZ error reduction under coherence-limited noise''
and ``$\sim 38\text{--}52\%$ QAOA cut-error / schedule-time
reduction on a non-native graph'') are directly numerically exercised
and matched within a few percentage points. The pulse-level and
hardware-calibration claims (C4 and the Fig.\ 2 pulse curves) are
\emph{not} exercised.

\section{Reproducibility}
Single script \texttt{code/replicate.py} ($\sim 330$ lines), no
external data. Deterministic \texttt{seed\_transpiler=42}; only
randomness is 8192-shot sampling. Evidence JSON in
\texttt{report/evidence/}: \texttt{summary.json},
\texttt{C1\_rzz\_sweep.json}, \texttt{C2\_qaoa\_sweep.json}. In-line
unit test asserts $\mathrm{Operator}(U_{\mathrm{PE}})\equiv
R_{ZZ}(\theta)$ up to global phase.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED.} The two quantitative headline claims are
reproduced within tolerance under the paper's own coherence-limited
noise model. Pulse-level (Fig.\ 2) and hardware-only claims are
inherently untestable on a laptop and remain unexercised. See
\S Critique above and \texttt{failure\_analysis.md} for the honest
scope caveats.
