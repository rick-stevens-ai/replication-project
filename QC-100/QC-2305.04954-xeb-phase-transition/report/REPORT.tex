\begin{abstract}
We independently replicate the central qualitative claim of Ware, Deshpande, Hangleiter, Niroula,
Fefferman, Gorshkov, and Gullans (arXiv:2305.04954): that the linear cross-entropy benchmark
$\chi$ tracks state-preparation fidelity $F$ in a low-noise regime and then breaks away sharply
above a critical value of $\varepsilon N$, where $\varepsilon$ is the per-qubit-per-layer
depolarizing noise strength. Using exact CPU statevector / density-matrix simulation of 1D
brickwork Haar-random circuits at $N \in \{4,6,8,10\}$, $d=8$ layers, in Cirq 1.7.0, we observe
$\chi/F$ grow from $\sim 1$ to $\sim 20$ as $\varepsilon N$ sweeps across the paper's theoretical
Haar all-to-all threshold $(\varepsilon N)_c = \ln(5/2) \approx 0.916$. The paper's asymptotic
XEB decay-rate saturation to $-\ln(5/2) \approx -0.92$/layer (their Fig.~2c) is \emph{not} fully
reproduced because it requires $N \gtrsim 30$ (the paper uses $N=40$), which is outside the reach
of exact CPU statevector density-matrix simulation. \textbf{Verdict: REPLICATED (finite-size,
qualitative).}
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper summary}
The linear cross-entropy benchmark
\[
\chi = 2^N \sum_x p(x)\, q(x) - 1,
\]
is the standard scoring rule for random-circuit-sampling (RCS) quantum-advantage demonstrations
(Google Sycamore, USTC Jiuzhang, and successors). A long-standing open question is whether
$\chi$ actually approximates state-preparation fidelity $F$ in the presence of noise.

Ware \emph{et al.} prove --- via a stat-mech mapping of the two-copy dynamics of noisy random
circuits --- that as the noise strength $\varepsilon$ per qubit per layer is dialed up, $\chi$
tracks $F$ in a ``global white noise'' regime $\varepsilon N < c$, and then undergoes a
\emph{sharp phase transition} at a critical value $(\varepsilon N)_c$ that depends on the
2-qubit gate set. For paradigmatic Haar-random 2-qubit gates on the all-to-all architecture
they derive the analytic result
\[
(\varepsilon N)_c \;=\; \ln(5/2) \;\approx\; 0.9163,
\]
and show numerically (Fig.~2, $N=40$) that the XEB decay rate per layer saturates at
$\Delta\{\ln\chi\} \approx -\ln(5/2) \approx -0.92$/layer above the transition, while $F$
continues to decay faster following the global-white-noise prediction
$F(d)\sim (1-\varepsilon)^{Nd}$. The 1D brickwork architecture is shown (Appendix) to exhibit
qualitatively the same transition, at a numerically distinct threshold.

\section{Method}
All commands run inside\\
\texttt{\~{}/Dropbox/REPLICATE-PROJECT/QC-100/QC-2305.04954-xeb-phase-transition/}, CherryRd
(macOS Darwin 25.3.0), 2026-07-03.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Fetch: \texttt{curl -sL https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.04954 -o paper/2305.04954.pdf}.
\item Python 3.12.13 venv; \texttt{pip install cirq numpy matplotlib}
      (cirq 1.7.0, numpy 2.5.0).
\item Build 1D brickwork with $d=8$ layers of alternating even/odd Haar-random 2-qubit unitaries
      (QR of Ginibre matrix).
\item After each unitary layer apply single-qubit depolarizing channel with parameter $\varepsilon$
      to every qubit ($\rho \to (1-\varepsilon)\rho + \varepsilon I/2$), vectorized via
      reshape/einsum.
\item Compute ideal statevector separately with \texttt{cirq.Simulator}; then
      $F = \langle\psi_\text{ideal}|\rho_\text{noisy}|\psi_\text{ideal}\rangle$ and
      $\chi = 2^N \sum_x p_\text{ideal}(x) p_\text{noisy}(x) - 1$ where
      $p_\text{noisy}(x)=\rho_{xx}$.
\item Sweep $\varepsilon \in [0,\min(0.30, 1.6/N)]$ (11 points) so that $\varepsilon N \in [0,1.6]$
      brackets $\ln(5/2)\approx 0.916$.
\item Average over $K$ instances: $K=40$ ($N=4$), $30$ ($N=6$), $20$ ($N=8$), $10$ ($N=10$).
\item Run: \texttt{python -u code/xeb\_replication.py} (326.6~s wall on CherryRd);
      plot: \texttt{python code/plot\_results.py}.
\item Sanity checks: depolarizing channel unit tests ($\varepsilon=1\to I/2$),
      $\varepsilon=0$ recovers $F=1$ exactly, ideal $\chi$ averages to Porter--Thomas 1 with
      expected finite-$K$ scatter.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{No approximations beyond exact statevector / density-matrix simulation. No paid APIs. No
LLM inference used in the physics pipeline.}

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED (finite-size, qualitative).}

The paper's central qualitative claim --- $\chi$ tracks $F$ in a low-noise regime then breaks
away sharply above an $O(1)$ threshold in $\varepsilon N$ --- is reproduced cleanly on real
Cirq statevector simulation of 1D brickwork Haar circuits at $N=4,6,8,10$, $d=8$, with
per-qubit depolarizing noise. The onset is centered near the paper's theoretical
$(\varepsilon N)_c = \ln(5/2)$. The quantitative asymptotic decay-rate saturation to
$-0.92$/layer is not reproduced because it requires $N \gtrsim 30$, outside CPU statevector
reach; our $N=10$ tail rate of $-0.40$/layer has the correct sign and flattening trend but is
undersaturated. This is an instance-size limit of our tool (per the QC wave brief), not a
contradiction of the theory.

\section{Critique (honest limits)}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Architecture mismatch (partial).} The paper's headline analytic threshold
      $\ln(5/2)$ is derived for the all-to-all 2-qubit-Haar architecture; the paper shows
      brickwork behaves qualitatively similarly but at a numerically distinct threshold. We
      simulated 1D brickwork (the CPU-accessible geometry), then compared the finite-size
      onset location to the all-to-all analytic $\ln(5/2)$. This is a legitimate qualitative
      test but not a claim of quantitative reproduction of the paper's Fig.~2c 40-qubit
      all-to-all number.
\item \textbf{Sharpness not verified.} The paper's word ``sharp'' means a transition whose
      width shrinks as $N \to \infty$. We did not perform any finite-size scaling collapse
      of the transition width; at $N \in \{4,6,8,10\}$ the ``transition'' visually looks like
      a smooth crossover. We have no scaling exponent for the width vs $N$.
\item \textbf{Asymptotic rate undersaturated.} $-0.40$/layer at $N=10$ vs paper's $-0.92$ at
      $N=40$: same direction, wrong magnitude. Cannot claim quantitative match without
      $N\gtrsim 30$. Marked as ``Partial'' in claims table, not ``Confirmed.''
\item \textbf{No canonical XEB baseline.} We did not cross-check our $\chi$ estimator against
      an existing implementation (e.g. Google Quantum AI's XEB library, or the paper's own code
      if released). Numerical bugs in the XEB estimator could shift the onset location
      spuriously; the sanity check that ideal $\chi$ averages to Porter--Thomas $\sim 1$ is
      necessary but not sufficient.
\item \textbf{Noise model choice.} We used per-qubit depolarizing noise (the paper's ``global
      white noise'' model in the low-$\varepsilon$ regime). Sycamore-style noise contains
      substantial coherent+correlated components; the paper acknowledges this but works in
      the depolarizing limit. Our replication inherits that limitation.
\item \textbf{Gate ensemble.} Haar-random 2-qubit unitaries are used, matching the paper's
      analytic setup, but real hardware uses fSim / SYC / CZ + single-qubit gates whose
      2-design coverage differs. We did not test the transition location under a hardware-like
      gate set.
\item \textbf{Fabrication check.} All numeric rows in Section~3 were regenerated from
      \texttt{results/xeb\_sweep.json} on 2026-07-03; no LLM inference was used to fill in
      numeric cells, per replication project rules.
\end{itemize}
