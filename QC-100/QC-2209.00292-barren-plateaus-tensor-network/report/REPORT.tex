\begin{abstract}
We independently reproduce the headline barren-plateau claim of Cervero Mart\'in et al.\ (2023, arXiv:2209.00292) for the qMPS ansatz. Using PennyLane 0.45.1 (\texttt{default.qubit}), 1000 random parameter samples per qubit count $N \in \{3,\dots,9\}$, we recover an unambiguous exponential decay of $\mathrm{Var}[\partial_{1,1}\langle X_N\rangle]$ with fitted base $b_\mathrm{MC} = 0.282$ vs.\ the paper's Theorem~3 prediction $b_\mathrm{th} = 3/8 = 0.375$. Verdict: \textbf{REPLICATED} for the headline C1+C2 claim. Auxiliary claims (qTTN/qMERA polynomial scaling, sum-of-local avoidance, complexity argument) were not exercised.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper Summary}
The paper analyses the barren-plateau phenomenon for three tensor-network-inspired variational ansatze: qMPS, qTTN, qMERA. Its central conclusion is a hierarchy of gradient-variance scalings driven by the observable's distance from the network's canonical centre. For qMPS with far single-site observable $X_N$ and top-left parameter $\theta_{1,1}$, Theorem~3 (Eq.~13, $j<i=N$ branch) gives:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Var}\left[\partial_{1,1}\langle X_N\rangle_{qMPS}\right] = 11 \cdot \left(\tfrac{1}{8}\right)^2 \cdot \left(\tfrac{3}{8}\right)^{N-1}.
\label{eq:thm3}
\end{equation}

\section{Method}
Tool versions (venv, real): Python 3.14.6; PennyLane 0.45.1 (\texttt{default.qubit}); NumPy 2.5.0; SciPy; Matplotlib.

\subsection{Ansatz}
qMPS staircase per Fig.~3/Eq.~9. 2-qubit blocks $(j,j{+}1)$ with $U_1$ (canonical centre) applied first, boundary $U_{N-1}$ (Eq.~11) last. Block structure: $RX{\cdot}RZ$ on both qubits, then CNOT (top$\to$bottom), then $RX{\cdot}RZ$ on both --- an 8-parameter (10 for the boundary block) universal 2-qubit block with the same causal-cone structure as Eq.~10.

\subsection{Monte-Carlo estimator}
For each $N \in \{3,\dots,9\}$: sample 1000 $\theta \sim \mathrm{Unif}[-\pi,\pi]^M$, compute parameter-shift $\partial_{1,1}f(\theta)=\tfrac{1}{2}[f(\theta+\tfrac{\pi}{2}e_{1,1}) - f(\theta-\tfrac{\pi}{2}e_{1,1})]$, take sample variance. Seed \texttt{20260703} for full reproducibility.

Log-linear fit: MC-fitted base $b_\mathrm{MC}=0.282$; paper base $b_\mathrm{th}=3/8=0.375$; ratio 0.75. Var drops by $\sim1900\times$ from $N=3$ to $N=9$, i.e.\ $\sim 3.3$ orders of magnitude on 6 qubits --- unambiguous barren-plateau signature. For reference McClean~2018 unstructured is $\sim 2^{-N}$ (base 0.5); both our MC and the paper's Thm~3 are below this (\emph{more severe} plateau), consistent with far-observable qMPS being worst-case.

\section{Critique / Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Scaling was independently reproduced, not just quoted.} We ran 7,000 real state-vector circuit evaluations and log-linearly fit the exponential; the negative slope and its order of magnitude are our own numerical result, not a re-plot from the paper.
\item \textbf{Base mismatch (0.282 vs.\ 0.375) is a real 25\% deviation}, not zero. We attribute it to using a standard $RX{\cdot}RZ{\cdot}CX{\cdot}RX{\cdot}RZ$ 8-parameter block instead of the paper's literal Eq.~10 block. Both are locally 2-design-equivalent at each stage, but the exact Haar-measure prefactor that produces the $3/8$ constant is block-realisation-specific. We did not re-derive the constant symbolically for our block --- the reproduction is of the \emph{phenomenology} (exp.\ decay in $N$), not of the numeric constant.
\item \textbf{No generic-HEA baseline comparison.} We did not simulate a Hardware-Efficient Ansatz on the same $N$ range and compare its Var$[\partial]$ decay against qMPS. The paper's structural claim that qMPS's far-observable plateau is comparable-or-worse than generic HEA would be strengthened by a side-by-side.
\item \textbf{No qTTN / qMERA reproduction.} Claim C4 (polynomial-vs-exponential dichotomy) is the paper's most \emph{practically consequential} finding and it is \emph{not} exercised here. A REPLICATED verdict on C1+C2 alone does not vouch for the polynomial-scaling story.
\item \textbf{No local-vs-global cost quantitative check.} Claim C3 (sum-of-local avoidance) is untested; only the far-observable global-like case was probed.
\item \textbf{Eq.~13 piecewise coverage.} We tested only the $j<i=N$ branch; the $j=i$ and $j=i+1$ analytic branches of Eq.~13 were not exhaustively verified.
\item \textbf{Sample-size caveat.} 1000 samples/$N$ is enough to resolve the exponential trend cleanly but at $N=9$ Var$\sim10^{-5}$ and the MC standard error is not negligible; a more careful uncertainty envelope would require $\gtrsim 10^4$ samples/$N$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED} (headline exercised: qMPS exp.\ decay + base-order-of-magnitude comparison, C1+C2). Verdict is \emph{scoped} to the headline claim; auxiliary claims C3, C4, C5 are unverified and any downstream use should not conflate ``qMPS barren plateau reproduced'' with ``qTTN/qMERA polynomial-scaling reproduced.''
