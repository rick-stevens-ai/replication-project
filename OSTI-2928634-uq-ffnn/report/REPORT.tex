\begin{abstract}
We independently replicate the analytical uncertainty-propagation framework of Diamzon and Venturi (2026), \emph{``Uncertainty propagation in feed-forward neural network models''} (Neural Networks 194:108178, DOI 10.1016/j.neunet.2025.108178). Our re-implementation of the closed-form mean (Eq.\ 37), variance/covariance (Eqs.\ 41--42), and one-point PDF (Eq.\ A.4) --- built independently from the paper text, without consulting the authors' code repository --- reproduces the paper's tight analytic-vs-Monte-Carlo agreement in the small-perturbation regime ($\beta{=}0.1$: variance median-rel-err $\approx 2\%$, correlation off-diagonal RMSE $\approx 0.03$--$0.06$) and confirms the large speedup ($45\times$--$460\times$ vs $10^5$-sample MC on an A100). It does \emph{not} reproduce the paper's headline Table~2 tight agreement at $\beta=1.5$ on our independently trained 5-layer network (within-run $|\Delta| \le 0.05$ vs our max $|\Delta| = 0.888$) and does \emph{not} confirm Fig.~10's claim that linearisation error decreases with depth (our $L{=}20$ result is worse than $L{=}5$). We assess the overall verdict as \textbf{PARTIAL}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Method (independent re-implementation)}
\subsection{Discretisation and operator}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Gauss--Legendre--Lobatto nodes on $[-1,1]$ with 31 nodes computed via the roots of $P_{30}'$ (\texttt{numpy.polynomial.legendre.Legendre.deriv().roots}).
  \item Quadrature weights $w_j = 2/[N(N{+}1)P_N(x_j)^2]$ and standard differentiation matrix $D$ (Hesthaven, \emph{Nodal DG}, App.\ A).
  \item Operator (Eq.\ 63) implemented as batched dense matrix ops.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{MLP}
Width $N{=}64$, depth $L \in \{1, 5, 20\}$, leaky-ReLU $\alpha=0.01$, linear output layer without bias to match the paper's $A$ matrix (Eq.\ 10).

\subsection{Training}
Adam, dataset of $5\times10^5$ i.i.d.\ Gaussian input samples ($\sigma{=}0.5$, chosen because the paper does not print $\sigma$ but this value gives output range $[-1.4,1.4]$). For $L{=}1, 5$: 60 epochs, batch 2000, lr $10^{-3}$, default PyTorch init. For $L{=}20$: 150 epochs, Kaiming-normal init with \texttt{nonlinearity='leaky\_relu'}, lr warmup $10^{-6}\to 10^{-4}$ over 10 epochs then StepLR halving every 25 epochs. (Default init at $L{=}20$ collapsed to a constant.)

\subsection{Analytic moments}
Since leaky-ReLU is piecewise linear, ``linearisation at $\mathbf{h}$'' equals the exact Jacobian at $\boldsymbol{\mu}$. We build
\[
  J = D_L W_L\, D_{L-1} W_{L-1} \cdots D_1 W_1, \qquad D_k = \operatorname{diag}(\phi'(\mathbf{h}_k))
\]
with $\mathbf{h}_k = W_k \mathbf{r}_{k-1} + \mathbf{b}_k$, $\mathbf{r}_0 = \boldsymbol{\mu}$. Then $\mathbf{q}_j = \mathbf{A}_j J$ and
\[
  m_j = \mathbf{A}_j\,\phi(\mathbf{h}_L), \quad
  \operatorname{Var}(g_j) = \tfrac{\beta^2}{3}\|\mathbf{q}_j\|^2, \quad
  \operatorname{Cov}(g_i,g_j) = \tfrac{\beta^2}{3}\, \mathbf{q}_i \cdot \mathbf{q}_j.
\]

\subsection{Analytic PDF}
Fourier integral of the sinc-product (Eq.\ A.4) on a 4096-point $a$-grid with $a_{\max}=40/(\beta \min |q_{jn}|)$. Sign-plus-log-magnitude representation of $\prod_n \operatorname{sinc}(a\beta q_{jn}/\pi)$ to avoid over/underflow. (Note the numpy \texttt{sinc} convention: $\operatorname{sinc}(x) = \sin(\pi x)/(\pi x)$.)

\subsection{Monte Carlo benchmark}
$10^5$ uniform $\mathbf{z} \sim U[-\beta,\beta]^{N_x}$ samples on GPU, batched at $10^4$, giving empirical mean, covariance, and correlation matrix over the $g$ outputs.

\subsection{Test conditions}
Fixed $\mu_i = 0.5\sin(\pi x_i) + 0.3\cos(2\pi x_i)$. Perturbation amplitudes $\beta \in \{0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5\}$. Depths $L \in \{1, 5, 20\}$.

\subsection{Depth trend (C5)}
Contrary to Fig.~10 of the paper, our $L{=}20$ network shows a \emph{larger} analytic-vs-MC gap than $L{=}5$ at every $\beta$. However, our $L{=}20$ is less well-fit (nRMSE 0.078 vs 0.028 for $L{=}5$), which we consider a probable confound. See Figure \texttt{fig\_corr\_rmse\_vs\_beta.png}.

\subsection{Compute speedup (C6)}
Median analytic-vs-MC wall-clock speedup: $L{=}1$: $45$--$83\times$; $L{=}5$: $66$--$112\times$; $L{=}20$: $182$--$462\times$. Absolute times: MC 3--32\,ms per case, analytic 30--100\,$\mu$s.

\section{Final verdict: PARTIAL}
The method is faithfully implemented and works in the small-perturbation regime with the promised speedup. The paper's headline large-$\beta$ tight agreement (Table~2) and depth-improvement claim (Fig.~10) do not reproduce on our independently trained networks. The gap plausibly reflects unspecified training details (exact $\mu$, exact optimiser schedule, exact input variance $\sigma$, exact initialisation) rather than an error in the analytic formulas.

  \item[Q2.] Does the paper's ``linearisation error decreases with depth'' claim hold once training nRMSE is matched across depths (e.g., with ResNet connections)?
  \item[Q3.] What are the a-priori truncation-error bounds for the sinc-product Fourier PDF integral as $N_x$ grows?
  \item[Q4.] What is the closed-form marginal PDF under correlated Gaussian $\mathbf{z}$ (not uniform $U[-\beta,\beta]^{N_x}$), and does depth-monotonicity survive?
  \item[Q5.] How does the analytic-vs-MC gap scale with operator nonlinearity? Is there a phase-transition-like collapse for strongly nonlinear operators?
\end{enumerate}
