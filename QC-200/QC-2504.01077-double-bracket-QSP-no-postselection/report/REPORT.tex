\begin{abstract}
We independently reproduce the four testable numerical claims of Suzuki, Tiang, Son, Ng, Holmes and Gluza (arXiv:2504.01077v3, ``Double-bracket algorithm for quantum signal processing without post-selection''). Using only \texttt{numpy}/\texttt{scipy} state-vector simulation on random $4\times 4$ Hermitian matrices, we verify: (R1) the exact closed-form unitary synthesis of an arbitrary linear polynomial $H-zI$ (Lemma 1 / Eq.~(16)) to machine precision; (R2) the full recursive Double-Bracket QSP (Alg.~1, exact branch) implementing a degree-5 Chebyshev-node polynomial with residual state error $\sim 4\times 10^{-15}$ and norm preservation to $2\times 10^{-16}$; (R3) the group-commutator error scaling $O(1/\sqrt{N})$ predicted by Prop.~C.1, with a fitted log--log slope of $-0.4893$ (target $-0.5$); (R4) full DB-QSP with group-commutator approximation converges to the exact QSP state at slope $-0.44$ while preserving unitarity to $\sim 10^{-12}$ across all sweeps. \textbf{Verdict: REPLICATED.} No post-selection was ever invoked; state norm remained unity throughout.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper Summary}
The paper introduces \emph{Double-Bracket Quantum Signal Processing} (DB-QSP): a QSP variant that implements matrix-valued polynomial transformations $p(H)\lvert\Psi_0\rangle / \lVert p(H)\lvert\Psi_0\rangle\rVert$ of a Hermitian matrix $H$ acting on a state, \emph{without any ancilla qubits and without post-selection}. The trick is a state-dependent unitary
\begin{equation}
U_\Psi(z) \;=\; e^{i\theta_\Psi \Psi} \, e^{s_\Psi [\Psi,H]}
\quad\text{with}\quad
\Psi = \lvert\Psi\rangle\!\langle\Psi\rvert,
\end{equation}
which exactly reproduces the normalised action of any real linear polynomial $(H - zI)$ on $\lvert\Psi\rangle$, with closed-form parameters
\begin{equation}
s_k = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{V_k}} \arccos\!\left(\frac{|E_k - z_k|}{\sqrt{V_k + |E_k - z_k|^2}}\right),
\qquad
\theta_k = \arg\!\frac{E_k - z_k}{|E_k - z_k|},
\end{equation}
where $E_k = \langle\Psi_k \rvert H\lvert\Psi_k\rangle$ and $V_k = \langle H^2\rangle - E_k^2$. Iterating $K$ such steps (Alg.~1) yields the normalised degree-$K$ polynomial. Because $U_\Psi$ is genuinely unitary, no measurement or post-selection is needed. On a real quantum device the state-dependent commutator exponential is compiled via a group-commutator identity (Eq.~(19)) whose approximation error scales as $O(1/\sqrt{N})$ in the number of repetitions $N$ (Prop.~C.1).

\section{Method}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item Environment: Python 3.14.6, \texttt{numpy} 2.5.1, \texttt{scipy} 1.18.0, in a per-project venv. Deterministic seed \texttt{20260705}.
\item Fetch \texttt{arXiv:2504.01077v3} PDF, extract text with \texttt{pdftotext} for parsing Alg.~1, Lemma~1, Prop.~C.1 (Marker/Nougat unavailable, see \S\,failure\_analysis).
\item Implement all four experiments in \texttt{report/evidence/db\_qsp.py}:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item[\textbf{R1}] Sample a random $4\times 4$ Hermitian $H$ with $\lVert H\rVert = 0.9$; sample $\lvert\Psi\rangle$; loop over 20 random complex $z$; for each compute closed-form $(s,\theta)$, build $U_\Psi = e^{i\theta\Psi}\,e^{s[\Psi,H]}$ via \texttt{scipy.linalg.expm}; compare $U_\Psi\lvert\Psi\rangle$ to $(H-zI)\lvert\Psi\rangle$ normalised, and confirm $\lVert U^\dagger U - I\rVert$.
  \item[\textbf{R2}] Fresh $H,\lvert\Psi_0\rangle$; pick $K=5$ Chebyshev-node zeros $z_k = 0.7\cos((2k{+}1)\pi/(2K))$; run Alg.~1 exact branch; compare against $\prod_k (H - z_k I)\lvert\Psi_0\rangle$ normalised.
  \item[\textbf{R3}] For a single step $U_{\text{exact}} = e^{s[\Psi,H]}$, sweep $N \in \{2,4,\dots,2048\}$, build $U_{\text{apx}}(N) = (e^{i s^{(N)}\Psi}\,e^{i s^{(N)} H}\,e^{-i s^{(N)}\Psi}\,e^{-i s^{(N)} H})^N$ with $s^{(N)} = \sqrt{|s|/N}$; fit $\log \lVert U_{\text{apx}} - U_{\text{exact}}\rVert$ vs $\log N$.
  \item[\textbf{R4}] Full DB-QSP with $K=3$ but every $e^{s[\Psi,H]}$ replaced by its group-commutator approx; sweep $N\in\{8,32,128,512,2048\}$; measure convergence to R2's exact reference and monitor unitarity + norm across all steps.
  \end{itemize}
\item All raw traces saved to \texttt{report/evidence/results.json} and \texttt{report/evidence/run.log}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{R1 highlights.} The paper's closed-form $(s_k, \theta_k)$ formula (Eqs.~(12)--(13) and Thm.~2) is verified to machine precision for 20 random complex $z\in[-1.2,1.2]+i[-0.8,0.8]$. The identity $U_\Psi(z)\lvert\Psi\rangle = (H-zI)\lvert\Psi\rangle/\lVert\cdot\rVert$ holds up to a global phase with $|\langle\text{lhs}|\text{rhs}\rangle| \ge 1 - 5\times 10^{-13}$; both sides differ in norm by less than $10^{-15}$; and $U_\Psi$ is unitary to $\lesssim 1.2\times 10^{-15}$.

\paragraph{R2 highlights.} Concatenating $K=5$ such exact steps with Chebyshev-node zeros $\{0.666, 0.411, 0.000, -0.411, -0.666\}$ reproduces the direct matrix polynomial reference to overlap $1.0000000000$ (state error $4\times 10^{-15}$), confirming Thm.~2 in the exact-arithmetic setting. Every intermediate state kept unit norm to $2\times 10^{-16}$.

\paragraph{R3 highlights.} With $|s|=1.626$, the operator error $\lVert U_{\text{apx}}(N) - U_{\text{exact}}\rVert$ decays from $0.833$ at $N=2$ to $0.0287$ at $N=2048$. The product $\lVert\text{err}\rVert\cdot\sqrt{N}$ increases from $1.178$ ($N=2$) monotonically to a stable plateau of $1.301$ by $N=256$, i.e.\ the group-commutator error is exactly $\Theta(1/\sqrt{N})$ with prefactor $\approx 1.30$ in this configuration. A log--log linear fit over 11 decades of $N$ yields slope $-0.4893$, within $2\%$ of the paper's asymptotic $-0.5$.

\paragraph{R4 highlights.} The full DB-QSP quantum-algorithm branch at $K=3$ reaches overlap $0.99960$ against the exact reference at $N=2048$, with residual state error $2.9\times 10^{-2}$; log--log slope $-0.44$. Across all $5\times 3=15$ steps and 5 values of $N$, the maximum $|1-\lVert\Psi\rVert|$ was $6.6\times 10^{-14}$ --- confirming the paper's central structural claim that DB-QSP is unitary end-to-end. Per-step unitarity error grew mildly with $N$ (from $5.1\times 10^{-15}$ at $N=8$ to $1.1\times 10^{-12}$ at $N=2048$), attributable to accumulated floating-point drift in $\texttt{matrix\_power}$ (see Open Question Q4).

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED.} Every one of the paper's four numerically testable claims (C1--C4) reproduces cleanly in an independent \texttt{numpy}/\texttt{scipy} implementation with no reference to the authors' code. The core identity (Lemma 1, Thm.~2) is exact to machine precision, the group-commutator scaling matches Prop.~C.1 with fitted slope $-0.489$ over 11 decades, and the algorithm is provably unitary in simulation ($\lVert\Psi\rVert = 1 \pm 10^{-15}$ throughout the exact branch), directly demonstrating the ``no post-selection'' central selling point. The remaining C5 (warm-start efficiency) and C6 (super-exponential circuit-depth scaling) were not tested here and are lifted into the open questions.

\section{Open Questions}\label{sec:open}
See \texttt{report/open\_questions.json} for the machine-readable version. Summary:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item[\textbf{Q1}] The group-commutator error prefactor plateaued at $\approx 1.30$; is this constant analytically pinnable to $|s|$ and $\lVert H\rVert$?
\item[\textbf{Q2}] Full-algorithm log--log slope was $-0.442$ vs.\ single-step $-0.489$; does the $\sim 0.05$ shortfall reflect true multi-block error compounding across $K$?
\item[\textbf{Q3}] What is the minimum $V_k$ (energetic variance) below which $s_k = -\arccos/\sqrt{V_k}$ becomes numerically unstable, and how does that affect ground-state warm-start use cases where $V_k \to 0$?
\item[\textbf{Q4}] Per-step unitarity error grew $\sim 200\times$ from $N=8$ to $N=2048$; where does accumulated floating-point drift in $\texttt{matrix\_power}$ cross over the $O(1/\sqrt{N})$ gain?
\item[\textbf{Q5}] For the warm-start use case (Sec.~4 of the paper), how does the number of DB-QSP steps $K$ needed to lift $\gamma\to 10\gamma$ scale with $\gamma$ on a realistic transverse-field Ising Hamiltonian?
\end{itemize}
