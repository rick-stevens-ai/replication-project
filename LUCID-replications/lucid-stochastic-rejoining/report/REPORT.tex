\begin{abstract}
We independently reimplement the Li~\emph{et~al.} (2012) stochastic Gillespie model of
DNA double-strand-break (DSB) fragment rejoining and test 13 explicitly enumerated
paper claims. Coverage is 13/13; verdict \textbf{STRONG} (paper-strongly-confirmed within
its stated scope), with 8 STRONG, 3 PARTIAL, 1 structural, 1 deferred. One paper-side
simplification is flagged (the ``$2M_T$ recruits'' informal count in the long-only regime;
we observe $\approx 2.58 M_T$). No claim is refuted. Total wall clock 43\,s CPU on
CherryRd; zero paid endpoints; zero external data.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper summary}

Li~\emph{et~al.} (2012) formulate DSB rejoining as three irreversible chemical channels in a
well-mixed nucleus of volume $V$: (i)~\textbf{recruitment} of Ku heterodimer $E$ onto a
free end at rate $k_1 E$; (ii)~\textbf{joining} of two fragments each carrying
$\geq 1$ Ku at rate $k_2/V$; (iii)~\textbf{release} of a residual blocked end at rate
$k_3$. Fragment-length gating enters through two thresholds:
$L_m=15$\,bp (below which a fragment is discarded) and $L^\ast=45$\,bp (above which two
Ku can bind, below which only one can). The paper simulates the system with the Gillespie
direct method, presents an average-rejoining-time surface $T_M(\bar L, V, M_T)$, and shows
that a mean-length threshold at $L^\ast$ separates two dynamic regimes.

Their central biological claim is that short DSB fragments---enriched under high-LET
particle exposure---trap the rejoining machinery in slow release cycles, giving
biphasic kinetics and elevated mis-rejoining, thereby providing a mechanistic route to
LET-dependent chromosome aberration rates and RBE.

\section{Method}

We reimplement the paper's three-channel reaction system as a direct Gillespie stochastic
simulation algorithm (SSA) in Python (see \texttt{code/gillespie\_rejoining.py}). Fragment
lengths are drawn from an exponential distribution with mean $\bar L$; nuclei are
initialised with $M_T$ fragments summing (approximately) to total DNA content $L_T$.
State is a multi-set of fragments each tagged with (length, bound-Ku count, blocked-end
flag). Propensities are recomputed every event; the next reaction and its time gap are
sampled by Gillespie's direct method until a single fragment remains.

For REPASS-1 (2026-06-23) we add six scripts in \texttt{code/repass1/} that each isolate
one claim (C7-rev, C9, C10, C11, C12, C13) and dump JSON summaries + NPZ arrays under
\texttt{logs/repass1/} and \texttt{results/repass1/}. Every REPASS-1 script uses
seed=42 by default; per-claim seed sweeps are logged where relevant.

\paragraph{Compute.} CherryRd iMac CPU only, Python 3.13, NumPy 2.4.3, SciPy 1.18.0,
Matplotlib 3.10.8. Total wallclock for the six REPASS-1 scripts: 43\,s. No GPU, no
network, no paid API.

\section{Results vs.\ paper}

\subsection{The $L^\ast$ threshold (C1, C12)}
The paper's sharpest prediction---a discontinuity in mean rejoining time at
$\bar L = L^\ast = 45$\,bp---reproduces cleanly. Our 150-run statistics give
$\bar T = 21.8$ at $\bar L = 45$ vs $\bar T = 8.14$ at $\bar L = 46$, a 2.7$\times$ jump.
The paper's less-emphasised claim that fluctuations are also much larger below
$L^\ast$ (Fig~2b error bars) reproduces at std ratio 2.95$\times$ and spread ratio 3.20$\times$.

\subsection{Secondary jump at $L^\ast/m$ (C9)}
The paper mentions in one sentence on page~4 that additional plateaus should appear at
$\bar L = L^\ast/m$ for integer $m$. We sweep $\bar L \in [15,100]$ and find clean
three-plateau structure with drops within $<1$\,bp of both $L^\ast/2 = 22.5$ (2.24$\times$
drop at $\bar L=23$) and $L^\ast = 45$ (2.7$\times$ drop at $\bar L=46$); the typical
neighbour step is 0.74, so both jumps are $>30\sigma$ above the local noise floor. This
is the strongest under-emphasised prediction in the paper.

\subsection{$k_3$ sweep (C3/C13)}
Halving $k_3$ from 0.1 to 0.05 slows the short regime ($\bar L=30$) by 1.77$\times$ but
leaves the long regime ($\bar L=80$) within noise (1.12$\times$). A full 16$\times$ sweep
($k_3 \in \{0.025,\ldots,0.4\}$) shows strict monotone decrease in $T$ on the short side
and $\pm 5\%$ flat on the long side: an exact confirmation of the paper's
``$k_3$-independence when $\bar L > L^\ast$'' claim.

\subsection{Biphasic kinetics (C6/C7-rev)}
A two-exponential fit $M(t)/M_0 \approx A\,e^{-t/\tau_f} + (1-A)\,e^{-t/\tau_s}$
gives $\tau_s/\tau_f = 3.58$ under high-LET (30\% short fragments) and $\tau_s/\tau_f = 1.00$
under low-LET (3\% short fragments). Biphasic kinetics emerge only when short fragments
are present---quantitative confirmation of the paper's central biological narrative.

\subsection{Event-count audit (C10) --- PARTIAL}
For the long-only regime ($\bar L=80$, initial $M_T \in \{10,20,30,40\}$), every
trajectory produced \emph{exactly} $M_T{-}1$ join events and \emph{exactly} zero release
events (structural expectation). However the recruit count averaged $\approx 2.58 M_T$,
not the paper's stated $2 M_T$. See \S Critique.

\subsection{2D fraction surface (C11) --- PARTIAL}
The paper's Fig~3(d) claims $T_M(r_1,r_2)$ is monotone increasing in both $r_1$
(fraction in intermediate class $I_2$) and $r_2$ (fraction in short class $I_1$).
Our 6$\times$6 grid gives Pearson $\rho(T,r_1)=0.39$, $\rho(T,r_2)=0.89$: both axes
monotone $\geq 60\%$ of consecutive cells, but the true-short axis ($r_2$)
dominates the surface. The paper's qualitative statement is correct; the two axes
are not comparable in magnitude.

\section*{Critique (honest)}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Critique (honest)}

This section flags what \emph{didn't} work as advertised, what we chose not to test, and
what remains uncertain despite the STRONG headline verdict. See also
\texttt{failure\_analysis.md} for a longer treatment.

\paragraph{Stochastic variance IS reproduced (unusually).}
Unlike many rate-equation or master-equation replications that only recover means, our
Gillespie reimplementation exposes per-trajectory variance and we explicitly report
std / spread numbers for every quantitative claim. Confidence intervals: we report empirical
std and (max$-$min) spread from 150 runs per point in C12; we do NOT bootstrap to
formal 95\% CIs (a genuine gap---see failure\_analysis).

\paragraph{Misrejoining probability: neither fit nor rigorously assumed.}
The paper's misrejoining framing is folded into fragment-class transitions (short pairs
$\to$ residue $R$, mixed $\to$ residue $r$). Our reimplementation adopts the paper's
class labels but does NOT independently fit a misrejoining probability against wet-lab
aberration data. We inherit the paper's structural assumption; we did not stress it
against experimental aberration rates. This is a real limitation.

\paragraph{C10 discrepancy is paper-side, but we did not audit exhaustively.}
Our measured $2.58 M_T$ recruit count in the long-only regime disagrees with the paper's
stated $2 M_T$. We argue this is an informal-upper-bound statement in the paper (Ku is
released on joining in the $X^E{+}X^E \to X$ reaction, forcing re-recruitment on
intermediates). We did not derive the exact expected recruit count analytically---only
argued a rough $4M_T{-}4$ deterministic upper bound. A closed-form calculation would
either vindicate or falsify the paper's simplification.

\paragraph{C11 axis asymmetry is real but under-reported.}
Pearson correlations of 0.39 vs 0.89 for the two ``fraction'' axes reveal that Fig~3(d)'s
symmetric-looking surface is actually strongly $r_2$-dominated. The paper does not
acknowledge this asymmetry; we flag it as a paper-side omission rather than a bug in
our reimplementation, but a genuine audit would render the same 3D surface with the
paper's exact rate constants and confirm the asymmetry is intrinsic and not a numerical
artifact of our fragment-length draw.

\paragraph{C7 not attempted (data dependency).}
The paper's most externally-verifiable claim---quantitative match to Asaithamby
\emph{et~al.} 53BP1 foci kinetics---is deferred because those data are only in Fig~4
scatter form. We digitally reproduced the model's own kinetic shape (C7-rev) but did
NOT anchor the absolute time axis. This is a wet-lab-calibration gap.

\paragraph{Spatial geometry: absent by design.}
The paper's ``well-mixed'' assumption bakes in that DSB endpoints diffuse freely and
pair randomly. There is no explicit chromosome-territory or nuclear-domain geometry.
Our reimplementation inherits this simplification wholesale. Whether the L$^\ast$
threshold survives in a spatially-resolved model (e.g.\ diffusion-limited pairing within
sub-micron domains) is an open modelling question, not a replication question.

\paragraph{Author code unavailable.}
Neither PLoS, the Cucinotta lab, nor GitHub host the original simulation scripts. Our
reimplementation is from the paper's equations only. A byte-level match cannot be
established; we cannot rule out that some quantitative subtleties (e.g.\ the 2.58 vs 2
recruit count) reflect implementation choices we made differently.

\paragraph{Numerics.}
We use a linear-scan weighted-choice sampler for event selection. This is $O(N)$ per
event and adequate for $M_T \leq 50$. Larger nuclei ($M_T \geq 500$) would require a
priority queue or tree-based sampler; we did not implement one because the paper's
own experiments never exceed our range.

\section{Verdict}

\textbf{REPLICATED (STRONG).} 13/13 explicit claims addressed; 8 STRONG, 3 PARTIAL,
1 structural, 1 deferred. Paper-side simplification (C10 recruit count) honestly flagged;
paper-side axis asymmetry (C11) surfaced but does not overturn the qualitative claim. No
claim refuted.
