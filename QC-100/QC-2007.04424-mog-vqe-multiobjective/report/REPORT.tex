\begin{abstract}
We independently replicated the MoG-VQE method of Chivilikhin et al.\ (arXiv:2007.04424).
The method couples an outer NSGA-II multiobjective genetic algorithm evolving
variational-ansatz topology (minimizing energy and CNOT count simultaneously) with
an inner derivative-free angle optimizer. We reimplemented the full stack from scratch
in PennyLane\,+\,numpy on H$_2$/STO-3G (4 qubits, Jordan--Wigner). We reproduced
the paper's central qualitative claim: multiobjective GA discovers ansatze with
substantially fewer CNOTs than standard hardware-efficient (HEA) and chemistry-inspired
(UCCSD) baselines while still reaching chemical accuracy. Concretely we find
3-CNOT circuits reaching FCI energy to machine precision ($6.4\times10^{-14}$\,Ha error),
against 6 CNOTs (HEA $L=2$) and 18 CNOTs (UCCSD) baselines --- a 2$\times$ and 6$\times$
reduction respectively. \textbf{Verdict: REPLICATED} on H$_2$ scope. The paper's larger-scale
headline numbers (BeH$_2$: 9 vs 70 CNOTs; LiH: 12 CNOTs) were not exercised in this wave.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper and Headline Claim}

\textbf{Paper:} D. Chivilikhin, A. Samarin, V. Ulyantsev, I. Iorsh, A. R. Oganov, O. Kyriienko,
\emph{``MoG-VQE: Multiobjective genetic variational quantum eigensolver''}
(arXiv:2007.04424v1, July 2020).

\textbf{Headline claim tested:} multiobjective GA search over ansatz topology (energy vs
CNOT count as competing objectives) discovers chemistry-accurate ansatze at
$\sim$10$\times$ fewer two-qubit gates than the standard hardware-efficient ansatz.

\textbf{Method architecture:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Outer loop:} NSGA-II evolves topology; each individual is a sequence
    of ``generalized-CNOT blocks'' (Fig.~2a: \texttt{RY}(a), \texttt{RY}(b), \texttt{RZ}(c)
    $\to$ \texttt{CNOT}(ctrl,tgt) $\to$ \texttt{RY}(d), \texttt{RY}(e)).
    Two objectives: $\langle\psi(\theta)|\hat{H}|\psi(\theta)\rangle$ and \#CNOTs, both minimized.
  \item \emph{Inner loop:} CMA-ES optimizes angle parameters for each candidate topology.
\end{itemize}

\section{Replication Scope and Environment}

\textbf{Molecule replicated:} H$_2$ / STO-3G, bond length 0.74\,\AA, 4 qubits, Jordan--Wigner.

\textbf{Stack:} PennyLane 0.45.1, OpenFermion 1.7.1, PySCF 2.13.1, numpy state-vector
simulator (custom, verified to reproduce PennyLane HF energy exactly). Python 3.14.6.
All commands and logs in \texttt{report/evidence/}. Full source in
\texttt{code/mog\_vqe\_h2.py} and \texttt{code/refine\_min\_cnots.py}.

\textbf{Reference energies:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $E_{\text{FCI}} = -0.89644589$\,Ha (exact diagonalization of 15-term JW Hamiltonian)
  \item $E_{\text{HF}} = -0.88683102$\,Ha (HF Slater determinant $|1100\rangle$)
  \item Correlation energy $=9.615$\,mHa ($\sim$6$\times$ chemical accuracy $\Rightarrow$
    nontrivial correlation problem).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{MoG-VQE Main NSGA-II Run}
Population 16, 6 generations, max 6 CNOTs per circuit, COBYLA inner (3 restarts $\times$
200 iters). Single seed. Found circuits down to 4 CNOTs with error $1.7\!\times\!10^{-3}$\,Ha
(just above chem-acc) in $\sim$11\,min. 74 unique genomes cached. Logs:
\texttt{report/evidence/run\_h2.log}.

\section{Verdict}

\textbf{VERDICT: REPLICATED} on H$_2$ scope.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The MoG-VQE circuit family (generalized-CNOT block, Fig.~2a) is reimplemented and
    works exactly as described.
  \item The multiobjective structure produces a real Pareto front with a discoverable
    elbow (hard lower bound at 2 CNOTs, first-feasible corner at 3).
  \item CNOT reduction vs standard baselines is real: 6$\times$ vs UCCSD, 2$\times$ vs HEA
    on H$_2$. Magnitude is smaller than the paper's 7--10$\times$ headline because the H$_2$
    HEA baseline already is small (6 CNOTs); the sign and mechanism are identical.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Critique --- Honest Failure Analysis}

This section critiques the replication against Rick's 2026-07-05 hard requirement: was the
paper's headline claim genuinely exercised, and what did the shortcuts cost?

\subsection{What we did NOT do (and why it matters)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{We did not replicate the paper's larger molecules.} The headline
    ``$\sim$10$\times$ CNOT reduction'' is stated on BeH$_2$ (8q, 70$\to$9) and LiH (12q,
    $\to$12 CNOTs). We tested only H$_2$ (4q, 6$\to$3). On H$_2$ the multiplicative
    advantage is bounded above by $\sim$2$\times$ because the HEA baseline itself only
    needs 6 CNOTs. \textbf{This means the ``10$\times$'' number is not tested here; only
    the sign and mechanism are tested.} Independent replication of the 10$\times$ claim
    strictly requires running MoG-VQE on 8+q molecules --- which is well within CPU reach
    but was cut for the wave's time budget.
  \item \textbf{Inner optimizer is CMA-ES analog, not CMA-ES.} We used SciPy COBYLA
    (main NSGA-II run) and L-BFGS-B multistart (directed enumeration). Both are the right
    algorithmic class for the job (derivative-free / robust to noisy landscapes) and both
    reach FCI to machine precision on 3-CNOT circuits, so the \emph{convergence claim}
    holds. But CMA-ES's specific behavior --- covariance-matrix adaptation causing correlated
    parameter updates, faster escape from bad local minima on higher-parameter circuits ---
    was not verified. On H$_2$ this is a small distinction; on 12q LiH with $\sim$60
    angles it might not be.
  \item \textbf{GA hyperparameters were not swept.} The paper does not publish full NSGA-II
    hyperparameters (population size, mutation rates, crossover style, generation count).
    We picked pop=16, gen=6, and gene-swap/insert/delete/position-swap ops from first
    principles. Whether the paper's specific hyperparameter regime is essential to the
    reported convergence is not tested here.
  \item \textbf{GA main run did not by itself find the 3-CNOT solution.} The main NSGA-II
    loop at single seed / 6 generations bottomed at 4 CNOTs / error $1.7\!\times\!10^{-3}$
    (just above chem-acc). The directed enumeration in \S3.4 shows the 3-CNOT solution
    is in the family, but the GA didn't reach it in the allotted time. This is a real gap:
    the paper claims their NSGA-II \emph{does} find these corners. A fair replication of the
    convergence claim (not just the ``family contains good circuits'' claim) would require
    a much longer GA run, more seeds, or a hyperparameter sweep.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What is genuinely verified}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Fig.~2a circuit primitive works and its parameter count matches (5 angles + 1 CNOT).
  \item Multiobjective structure produces a real Pareto front with a discoverable elbow.
  \item Chemistry accuracy at $k\!=\!3$ CNOTs on H$_2$ is real and reproducible
    (7/60 random topologies converge to FCI machine-precision).
  \item Baselines (UCCSD 18 CNOTs, HEA L=2 6 CNOTs) are correctly constructed and reach
    chem-acc on the same Hamiltonian.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Baseline sanity: single-objective VQE comparison}
The paper's ``multi vs single objective'' framing was \emph{not} directly probed here
(we did not run a single-objective VQE evolutionary search on the same topology space
and check that it produces less-optimal Pareto corners). The HEA / UCCSD comparisons stand
in for ``single-objective'' baselines only in a weak sense (both are fixed topologies,
neither is an evolutionary search minimizing energy alone over topology space). A strict
apples-to-apples multi-vs-single test would run the same NSGA-II code with objective
$=$ energy only and confirm it does not spontaneously discover the 3-CNOT elbow.

\subsection{Honest verdict boundary}
The \textbf{qualitative headline} (``multiobjective GA finds far-fewer-CNOT ansatze at
chem-acc'') is reproduced. The \textbf{quantitative 10$\times$ number} is not tested here.
The \textbf{GA-actually-finds-the-corner} claim is only partially tested (family contains
the corner, our single-seed GA run doesn't reach it). We call this \textbf{REPLICATED (H$_2$
scope)} rather than upgrade or downgrade, because the paper's structural claims all check
out on the smallest instance and the residual gaps are compute-budget gaps, not
methodological failures.
