\section{Paper Summary}
Griffiths \& Niu (1996) show that the inverse Quantum Fourier Transform
(iQFT) used in Kitaev-style phase estimation can be replaced by a
\emph{semiclassical} procedure: the $k$ counting qubits are measured
one at a time (least-significant first), and each measured classical bit
is fed forward as a $Z$-rotation phase correction to the not-yet-measured
qubits. This removes all two-qubit gates from the iQFT block --- only
single-qubit operations plus classical control remain --- while producing
\emph{exactly the same output distribution} as the coherent iQFT. The
result is the mathematical foundation of iterative / measurement-based
phase estimation.

\section{Methods and Substitutions}
\textbf{Setup.} A single-qubit phase gate $U$ with eigenvalue $e^{2\pi i \varphi}$
on eigenstate $|1\rangle$. After the controlled-$U^{2^j}$ ladder,
counting qubit $j$ carries phase $e^{2\pi i \cdot 2^j \varphi}$; the
pre-iQFT register is the product state
$\prod_j (|0\rangle + e^{2\pi i \cdot 2^j \varphi}|1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}$.

\textbf{Method A (coherent).} Apply the full $k$-qubit inverse QFT matrix
to the statevector; output distribution equals $|\text{amplitudes}|^2$.

\textbf{Method B (semiclassical).} Iterative phase estimation: measure the
qubit carrying $2^{k-1}\varphi$ first (yielding the LSB of the estimate);
feed each measured bit forward as a phase correction to subsequent
(higher-significance) qubits. All measurement branches are enumerated to
obtain the \emph{exact} distribution (no Monte-Carlo sampling), so the
comparison is distribution-vs-distribution at machine precision.

\textbf{Metric.} Total-variation (TV) distance between the two
distributions.

\textbf{No quantum framework required.} Pure numpy; artifacts: \texttt{replicate.py},
\texttt{results.json}.

\section{Debugging Provenance (honest)}
The first two independent implementations (the subagent's, and Ollie's
first cut) reported disagreement (TV $\approx 1.0$) --- a bit-ordering /
convention bug, not a real contradiction. Diagnosed via an
exactly-representable phase $\varphi = 0.375 = 0.011_2$, which must return
$y=3$ at $k=3$: a spurious bit-reversal on the iQFT output and an inverted
feed-forward order were the culprits. After fixing the convention so both
methods use the identical index mapping, the equivalence is exact. The
buggy subagent version is preserved as \texttt{replicate\_subagent\_buggy.py}.

\section{Critique --- What Was and Was Not Established}
\textbf{Independently reimplemented (headline exercised).} Yes. Both
methods are coded from scratch in $\sim$100 lines of numpy: (A) explicit
$k$-qubit iQFT unitary applied to the constructed pre-iQFT state, (B) a
branch-enumerated iterative measure-and-feed-forward loop that returns the
exact probability of every classical outcome string. The two are compared
distribution-to-distribution --- \emph{not} amplitude-to-amplitude nor
sample-to-sample. This is the strongest form of algorithmic equivalence
test available for this claim.

\textbf{Distribution reproduced for the paper's specific test input?}
Partially. The paper's core claim is convention-independent
(the equivalence theorem holds for \emph{all} $\varphi$ and all $k$),
and we exercise 8 $(\varphi, k)$ points including exactly-representable
phases ($0.375, 0.0625, 0.5, 0.8125, 0.46875$) and non-representable ones
($0.1, 0.7, 1/3$). The paper does not present a single canonical test
distribution to compare against; it presents the equivalence itself, which
is what we verify. A specific quoted numerical example from the paper was
not lifted verbatim.

\textbf{Comparison against canonical (all-quantum) QFT baseline?}
This IS the comparison. Method A is the canonical all-quantum inverse-QFT
applied coherently to the state vector; Method B is the semiclassical
replacement. The TV distance of $6.2\times 10^{-15}$ across 8 experiments
directly quantifies the ``baseline vs. replacement'' agreement.

\textbf{Qubit / depth reduction \emph{quantitatively} verified?}
\textbf{No.} This is the honest gap. Griffiths--Niu note that the
semiclassical iQFT eliminates all controlled-phase gates (which cost
$\mathcal{O}(k^2)$ two-qubit operations in the coherent version) and
requires only $k$ single-qubit rotations plus $k$ mid-circuit
measurements. We did \emph{not} construct a gate-count / circuit-depth
table comparing the two methods; the qubit-count reduction (the ancilla
register is unchanged --- the ``reduction'' is in gates and coherence
requirement, not counting qubits per se) was also not tabulated. Adding
a gate-inventory comparison is one of the open questions below.

\textbf{Noise / hardware realism.} All experiments are ideal
(noiseless, exact distributions). Measurement noise and finite-shot
sampling behaviour --- which are exactly the settings where the
semiclassical version's classical-control overhead becomes relevant ---
were not exercised.

\section{Verdict}
The semiclassical Fourier transform exactly reproduces the coherent
inverse-QFT phase-estimation distribution using only single-qubit
operations and classical feed-forward, verified to machine precision
across 8 eigenphases and bit counts $k=3$--$5$. The paper's central
equivalence claim (the actual theorem) is reproduced exactly and
completely.

\textbf{VERDICT: REPLICATED} --- Coverage $9/10$, Agreement $10/10$.
Coverage is $9$ rather than $10$ only because the explicit gate-count /
circuit-depth reduction and noisy/sampled regimes were not separately
quantified.
