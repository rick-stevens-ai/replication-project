\section{Paper and headline claim}

Leadbeater, Fitzpatrick, Mu\~noz Ramo, and Thom (arXiv:2304.07917, 2023) propose a
\emph{Probabilistic Imaginary-Time Evolution} (PITE) algorithm built from a Trotter
decomposition. For each Pauli-string term $c_k \sigma_k$ of the qubit Hamiltonian, the
standard real-time-evolution Pauli-gadget is replaced by a \emph{non-unitary} analogue
in which the ancilla is rotated by $R_x(\phi)$ with
$\phi = 2\arccos(e^{-2|c_k|\Delta\tau})$ and mid-circuit measured; post-selecting the
ancilla on $|0\rangle$ implements $\exp(-c_k\Delta\tau\,\sigma_k)$ on the system qubits
up to an overall rescaling $\alpha$ that reduces the success probability. Concatenating
one gadget per Hamiltonian term per Trotter step realises a block encoding of
$e^{-\hat{H}\Delta\tau}$; iterating $r$ steps projects onto the ground state for large
$\beta = r\Delta\tau$.

The paper's Section~IV validates the scheme on two test systems:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a 4-site 1D transverse-field Ising model (TIM) with periodic BCs,
        $J = 0.5$, $h = 0.1$, $\Delta\tau = 0.1$ (Fig.~7);
  \item a 2-site 1D fermionic Hubbard model with $t = -0.1$, $U = 0.1$,
        $\Delta\tau = 0.1$, half-filling, singlet initial state (Fig.~8).
\end{itemize}
Both experiments show $\langle E\rangle \to E_0$ under Trotterised PITE and
report a cumulative post-selection success probability that decays roughly
exponentially in $\beta$.

\section{Replication scope}

\textbf{Headline exercised: YES.} We independently reimplemented Trotterised PITE
from scratch (qiskit 2.5.0, qiskit-aer 0.17.2, numpy 2.4.3, scipy 1.18.0 in an
isolated Python 3.14 venv) and reproduced the exact ground-state convergence and
the success-probability decay curves for both the 4-site TIM and the 2-site
Hubbard test systems at the parameters of Figs.~7 and~8. Results were
cross-checked against \texttt{scipy.linalg.expm} exact imaginary-time propagation
and against exact diagonalisation.

\section{Verdict}

\textbf{REPLICATED.} The central quantitative claims (ground-state convergence on
both test systems and roughly exponential success-probability decay) are
reproduced from an independent implementation and match the paper's Fig.~7 and
Fig.~8 curves. Two independent baselines (scipy \texttt{expm} and ED) confirm
the numerical result.

\section{Genuine critique}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Statevector-plus-post-selection stand-in, not full circuit sampling.}
        We implemented PITE at the statevector level by applying
        $\exp(-c_k\Delta\tau\,\sigma_k) = \cosh(c_k\Delta\tau)I -
         \sinh(c_k\Delta\tau)\sigma_k$ per Pauli-string term and renormalising,
        computing the per-gadget success probability from
        $\|\text{unnormalised}\|^2 / \alpha^2$ with $\alpha^2 = e^{2|c_k|\Delta\tau}$
        (paper Eq.~26). This is semantically the paper's non-unitary block
        encoding followed by post-selection on ancilla $|0\rangle$; it is the
        standard classical way to simulate PITE. The paper's Fig.~7/8 green
        ``Trotterised'' curves are exactly what we compute (they overlay the
        black shot-noise dots), so the physics claim is directly tested; but the
        paper's stochastic 100\,000-shot ancilla-outcome sampling loop is not
        rerun here and its error bars are not independently reproduced.
  \item \textbf{Ancilla-plus-mid-circuit-measurement Qiskit build only partially
        reconciled.} We wrote an explicit Qiskit ancilla-gadget
        (\texttt{qiskit\_gadget\_verify.py}, \texttt{qiskit\_full\_ite.py}) and
        obtained system-state fidelity $\gtrsim 0.999$ with the target
        non-unitary operator, but the exact success-probability convention has
        factor-of-two subtleties (ancilla $|0\rangle$ vs.\ $|+\rangle$ input,
        $\alpha$ vs.\ $\alpha^2$) we did not fully unwind. The physics claim is
        unaffected but the paper's actual circuit is only spot-checked, not fully
        end-to-end validated at the sampled-shots level.
  \item \textbf{Boundary condition for the 2-site Hubbard was not explicit in
        the paper} and had to be inferred from the reported $E_0 \approx -0.156$
        (matches OBC Lieb--Wu closed form $E_0 = U/2 - \sqrt{(U/2)^2 + (2t)^2}$;
        PBC on two sites doubles the hopping and gives $-0.353$, ruled out).
        This inference is a caveat: had we used PBC by default the replication
        would have failed silently. A more robust replication would try both
        BCs and flag the mismatch.
  \item \textbf{No baseline comparison against standard measurement-based QITE
        or against VQE.} The paper claims PITE is a competitive alternative to
        standard QITE for ground-state preparation, but we did not implement or
        time the standard QITE baseline. We therefore cannot independently
        confirm any claim about depth reduction or resource competitiveness ---
        only that the PITE curves converge as advertised.
  \item \textbf{No depth quantification.} We did not extract or compare
        two-qubit gate counts or circuit depth for PITE vs.\ any competing QITE
        variant. Any depth-reduction claim in the paper (if any) is neither
        confirmed nor refuted.
  \item \textbf{Larger-system claim (C6) untested.} The paper's 3- and 4-site
        Hubbard success-probability numbers ($\sim 10^{-5}$, $\sim 10^{-9}$)
        would require $\sim 10^9$ and $\sim 10^{13}$ shots respectively; both
        are out of scope for QC-100's CPU budget and are not tested here. If
        those decay rates are wrong the whole ``PITE is practical'' story is
        wrong, and our replication cannot see that.
  \item \textbf{No noise model.} All simulations are ideal statevector. The
        paper's practical claims (about PITE on real hardware, ancilla read-out
        error compounding across Trotter steps, mid-circuit measurement
        fidelity) are entirely untested.
  \item \textbf{Only two systems and two BC choices tested.} We did not vary
        $J/h$ for TIM, $t/U$ for Hubbard, or $\Delta\tau$ (fixed at $0.1$
        throughout). Trotter error scaling in $\Delta\tau$ was not
        systematically measured; the observed $|\Delta E| \sim 2\times 10^{-4}$
        is consistent with first-order Trotter but the exponent is not fit.
\end{enumerate}

See \texttt{failure\_analysis.md} for a more forensic post-mortem of
the ancilla-circuit reconciliation shortfall and the boundary-condition
inference.
