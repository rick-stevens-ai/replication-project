\section{Open Questions}

The following five questions were surfaced by this replication and remain
open. Each is a concrete, executable follow-up that would extend the paper
without requiring paid compute.

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{Does the fully-implicit parity QAOA advantage over standard
unencoded QAOA survive on weighted-MaxCut or higher-order ($k$-body,
$k\ge 3$) Ising problems, or is it specific to the paper's uniform
$U[-1,1]$ all-to-all 2-body class?}\\
\textit{Basis.} This replication only tested $J_{ij}\sim U[-1,1]$ on $K_6$,
exactly as in the paper. The parity code is defined for pair couplings;
$k$-body terms require extra ancillas or additional constraint cycles
that were not touched here.\\
\textit{Next steps.} Extend the numpy simulator to accept (i) weighted
distributions (log-normal, portfolio-covariance-like) and (ii) 3-/4-body
$H_Z$ via additional parity ancillas. Rerun the four-$n_r$ sweep at $N=6$,
$p=3$, $\ge 24$ instances per family; compare vs standard-QAOA baseline
with matched depth budget. Local CPU only.

\item \textbf{How does parity QAOA compose with error mitigation (ZNE,
PEC, symmetry verification) --- is virtual distillation essentially free
because of the code's built-in constraints, or does the constraint-preserving
driver break the mitigation ansatz?}\\
\textit{Basis.} A measured bitstring that violates a plaquette
is a detected error. The paper does not exploit this. ZNE/PEC assume
homogeneous noise; the mixed explicit/implicit driver has structurally
different gate footprints per $n_r$.\\
\textit{Next steps.} Aer noise-model rerun of the same 24 instances at
CNOT rates $\{10^{-3},3{\times}10^{-3},10^{-2},3{\times}10^{-2}\}$;
report raw $E_{\mathrm{res}}$, post-selected on constraints, and after
Richardson ZNE (noise factors $[1,2,3]$) per $n_r$. Local Aer, $\sim 2$
CPU-hours.

\item \textbf{On a concrete near-term device with limited native connectivity,
does parity QAOA give a real depth advantage over SWAP-network-compiled
standard QAOA on a specific optimisation benchmark (portfolio, TSP)?}\\
\textit{Basis.} The paper's C4 statement is in terms of driver-line
lengths, not compiled physical CNOT depth on a given coupling map.
On IBM heavy-hex or IonQ linear, standard QAOA's SWAP overhead can
close (or reopen) the gap.\\
\textit{Next steps.} Take $N=6$ complete-graph Ising plus one benchmark
(6-asset Markowitz, or 6-city TSP-as-Ising). Compile (a) standard QAOA
via Qiskit SABRE to heavy-hex + linear; (b) parity QAOA to the LHZ
square-lattice. Report 2-qubit gate count and depth per layer, and the
break-even $N$ above which parity wins physical depth. Local Qiskit
transpiler; no hardware.

\item \textbf{How sensitive is the parity-QAOA advantage to the
constraint penalty strength $c$ in $H_C=(c/2)\sum(1-\prod\sigma_z)$,
and does an adaptive schedule $c(t)$ or $c(n_r)$ tighten both the
fully-explicit and hybrid limits?}\\
\textit{Basis.} We fixed $c=3.0$ throughout. The fully-explicit
$E_{\mathrm{res}}=0.479$ may partly reflect a suboptimal $c$: too small
under-enforces the code space; too large creates a barren-plateau-like
QAOA landscape.\\
\textit{Next steps.} Sweep $c\in\{0.5,1,2,3,5,10\}$ for $n_r\in\{0.4,0.6,1.0\}$
on 12 instances at $N=6$, $p=3$. Then try $c_l=c_0(1+l/p)$ across QAOA
layers. If adaptive $c$ closes $>30\%$ of the implicit-vs-explicit gap,
the paper's ordering claim needs a caveat. Same numpy simulator,
$\sim 30$ CPU-min per $c$.

\item \textbf{Is the modular ``system-size-independent depth $\sim l_{\max}$''
of Sec.\ IV--V asymptotic-only, or does it hold cleanly at moderate
sizes $N\in\{8,10,12,15\}$ where NISQ hardware actually lives?}\\
\textit{Basis.} Fig.~5's modularisation was confirmed only structurally
at $N=6$ here. The pattern-of-cycles overhead may keep depth growing
sub-linearly with $N$ before the asymptotic flatness kicks in.\\
\textit{Next steps.} Extend to $N\in\{8,10,12,15\}$ using
\texttt{scipy.sparse} Krylov evolution (full statevector at $2^{29}$
is impractical). For each $N$, count number of driver lines, max line
length, and total 2-qubit depth per QAOA layer after linear-NN
compilation, with $l_{\max}=5$ fixed. Plot depth vs $N$; compare vs
standard-QAOA compiled to the same LHZ grid. Local CPU only,
$\sim 4$ CPU-hours.

\end{enumerate}
