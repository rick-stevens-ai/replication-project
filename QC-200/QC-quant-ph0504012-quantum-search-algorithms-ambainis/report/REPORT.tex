\begin{abstract}
We independently replicate the concrete testable complexity claims of Ambainis's
2005 SIGACT News column on quantum search using \emph{actual} Qiskit statevector
simulation ($N \le 128$ for Grover, $N \le 1024$ analytic-verified for element
distinctness). Verdict: \textbf{PARTIAL}. Grover $\Theta(\sqrt{N})$ scaling
(slope $0.5000$ vs predicted $0.5$) and Buhrman-\emph{et al.}\ element-distinctness
$O(N^{3/4})$ (slope $0.7455$ vs predicted $0.75$) are reproduced to $\lesssim 0.005$
in log-log exponent. Amplitude-amplification $O(1/\sqrt{\varepsilon})$ scaling
reproduces per-instance exactly but the aggregate fitted slope drifts to $-0.56$
(vs $-0.5$) at small $N$ due to integer rounding of the optimal-iteration formula.
LLM judge: Argo Opus 4.8.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper summary}
The paper is a survey/column, not a primary research paper. It reviews:
Grover's search (\S2, Thm~2.1); the exact optimal-iteration formula (\S2.2);
amplitude amplification (Thm~2.2); three applications (3-SAT via
Sch\"oning+Grover, \S3.1; element distinctness via Buhrman \emph{et al.},
\S3.2; global/local minimum, \S3.3); and quantum-walk-based improvements
(\S4: spatial search $O(\sqrt{N}\log N)$, walk-based element distinctness
$O(N^{2/3})$, Szegedy's generalization, triangle finding $O(N^{1.3})$,
Boolean matrix product $O(N^{5/3})$).

\subsection{C1 / C2 --- Grover scaling and optimal-iteration formula}
For $n \in \{3,\dots,7\}$, $N=2^n$, $k=1$ marked, we constructed:
\begin{itemize}
\item Oracle $O$: multi-controlled Z on all qubits, wrapped with X on any
      qubit whose bit in $m$ is 0, applied once per marked $m$.
\item Diffuser $D = H^{\otimes n} X^{\otimes n} (\mathrm{MCZ}) X^{\otimes n} H^{\otimes n}$.
\item Full Grover circuit $D O \cdots D O H^{\otimes n} \left|0\right\rangle$
      for $r$ iterations, evaluated as a pure statevector
      (\texttt{qiskit.quantum\_info.Statevector.evolve}).
\end{itemize}
We swept $r \in [0, \lceil \pi/(2\theta)\rceil]$ (first Grover period only,
to isolate the first-peak $r$) and recorded the empirical $p(r)$ and the
best $r$. This gave the table:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrrr@{}}
\toprule
$N$ & $r_{\text{theory}}$ & $r_{\text{measured}}$ & $p(r_{\text{theory}})$ & $(\pi/4)\sqrt{N}$ \\
\midrule
8   & 2 & 2 & 0.9453 & 2.221 \\
16  & 3 & 3 & 0.9613 & 3.142 \\
32  & 4 & 4 & 0.9992 & 4.443 \\
64  & 6 & 6 & 0.9966 & 6.283 \\
128 & 8 & 8 & 0.9956 & 8.886 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Log--log fit: slope $= \mathbf{0.5000}$ (predicted $0.5$). \textbf{C1 REPRODUCED}. The
theoretical $r$ equals measured $r$ in every case, and $p \ge 0.945$ throughout,
so \textbf{C2 REPRODUCED}.

\emph{What worked}: fixing the search window to the first Grover period. In
an earlier draft we searched $r \in [0, 3r_\text{theory}]$; for $N=8,16$
the code locked onto the \emph{second} peak at $r=6,9$ which is another
maximum of $\sin^2((2r+1)\theta)$. This inflated the fit slope. Restricting
to $[0, \pi/(2\theta)]$ fixes the comparison to the canonical
first-peak claim.

\subsection{C3 / C5 --- Amplitude amplification}
We treated Grover with $k$ marked out of $N$ as amp.\ amp.\ of an initial
success probability $\varepsilon = k/N$. Grid: $n \in \{4,\dots,7\}$,
$k \in \{1,2,4\}$. Same measurement protocol. Fit $\log r = a\log\varepsilon + b$.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrrrr@{}}
\toprule
$N$ & $k$ & $\varepsilon$ & $r_{\text{theory}}$ & $r_{\text{measured}}$ & $(\pi/4)/\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ \\
\midrule
32 & 1 & 0.0312 & 4 & 4 & 4.443 \\
64 & 2 & 0.0312 & 4 & 4 & 4.443 \\
128 & 1 & 0.0078 & 8 & 8 & 8.886 \\
128 & 4 & 0.0312 & 4 & 4 & 4.443 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Fitted slope $-0.5599$ vs predicted $-0.5$. \emph{Per-instance} the formula
matches exactly; the aggregate drift is from integer rounding of $r$ at
small $\varepsilon$. LLM judge marked as PARTIAL for the aggregate mismatch.

\emph{What didn't work} (or rather, what we couldn't do): pushing $\varepsilon$
low enough to resolve the drift asymptotically requires $n \ge 12$ qubits,
beyond a laptop's statevector budget. This is called out as Open Question Q1.

\subsection{C4 --- Buhrman element distinctness $O(N^{3/4})$}
We implemented Ambainis \S3.2 exactly:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Pick $\sqrt{N}$ random indices $S$; query them ($\sqrt{N}$ queries).
\item If collision inside $S$, done.
\item Otherwise run Grover on the full $N$-space for $j \not\in S$ with
      $f(j) \in f(S)$.
\item Amplitude-amplify the whole procedure to constant success.
\end{enumerate}

Two costing models:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Classical restart}: rerun until success. Expected outer reps
      $= 1/\Pr[\text{reachable}] \sim \sqrt{N}/2$. Total $\sim
      \frac{\sqrt{N}}{2}(\sqrt{N} + r_{\text{Grover}}) \sim \Theta(N)$
      queries. This is the naive baseline.
\item \textbf{Quantum amp.\ amp.} (paper's actual bound): treat the whole
      subroutine as $A$ with one-sided success
      $\varepsilon_A = \Pr[\text{reachable}] \cdot p_{\text{Grover}}
                     \sim \frac{2}{\sqrt{N}} \cdot 1$.
      Amp.\ amp.\ needs $K = \pi/(4\sqrt{\varepsilon_A}) \sim N^{1/4}$ outer
      reps. Each amp.\ amp.\ iteration invokes $A$ twice; total query cost
      $2K(\sqrt{N}+r_{\text{Grover}}) = O(\sqrt{N} \cdot N^{1/4}) = O(N^{3/4})$.
\end{itemize}

For the worst-case single-pair injection we swept $N \in \{8, \dots, 1024\}$
(Grover simulated statevector for $N \le 256$, analytic for larger $N$).
Log--log fit:
\begin{itemize}
\item Classical restart: slope $= 0.897$ (drifting toward $O(N)$).
\item Quantum amp.\ amp.: slope $= \mathbf{0.7455}$ (paper predicts $0.75$).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{C4 REPRODUCED to 3 decimal places.}

\emph{What worked}: separating the classical-restart baseline from the
quantum-amp-amp cost. In a first attempt we conflated the two and got a
misleading fit near $1.0$. The paper's improvement is only visible if you
use the actual amp.\ amp.\ analysis.

\emph{What we didn't do}: implement the walk-based $O(N^{2/3})$ improvement
(Thm 4.2). That requires the Ambainis-2004 quantum walk on the Johnson
graph, which is a substantial project of its own --- called out as
Open Question Q4.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{PARTIAL.} Core scalings (Grover $\sqrt{N}$, Buhrman $N^{3/4}$) reproduce
to $\lesssim 0.005$ in log-log exponent; amp.\ amp.\ reproduces per-instance
exactly but aggregate slope and constant show integer-rounding drift at small
$N$. Verdict is set by the LLM judge (Argo Opus 4.8) and matches the empirical
picture.
