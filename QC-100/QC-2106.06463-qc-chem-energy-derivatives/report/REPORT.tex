\section*{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED} --- the headline claim (VQE with the paper's gradient
prescription recovers H$_2$/STO-3G FCI and finds the equilibrium bond length via
gradient-descent geometry optimization) is reproduced within
$\ll$ chemical accuracy on an independently written PennyLane implementation.

\section{Paper summary}
Azad \& Singh (arXiv:2106.06463v1) present a VQE-based framework for computing
first- and second-order energy derivatives (nuclear gradients, Hessian, dipole,
polarizability) on quantum hardware. Their headline applications are (i)
minimum-energy configuration search for H$_2$, (ii) response properties
(dipole $\mu_Z$, polarizability $\alpha_{ZZ}$) under an electric field, and
(iii) transition-state search for H$_2 + $H $\leftrightarrow$ H $+ $H$_2$.
Derivatives are obtained either by finite-difference of VQE expectation values
across shifted geometries, or by a Hellmann--Feynman formula
$\langle \psi(\theta^*) | \partial H / \partial \eta | \psi(\theta^*) \rangle$
that differentiates the Hamiltonian with respect to system parameters $\eta$
rather than the ansatz with respect to circuit parameters $\theta$.

\section{Method (as run)}
Independent PennyLane 0.45.1 / PySCF 2.13.1 implementation. Ansatz: 4-qubit
Jordan--Wigner mapping of H$_2$/STO-3G, HF reference $|1100\rangle$ plus all
singles/doubles enumerated by \texttt{qml.qchem.excitations(2,4)} (3
parameters: two SingleExcitations + one DoubleExcitation, i.e.\ the standard
UCCSD-restricted ansatz that is exact for H$_2$/STO-3G). VQE optimized with
\texttt{GradientDescentOptimizer} (stepsize 0.4, 200 iters max,
$\Delta E < 10^{-8}$ Ha), warm-started across bond lengths. Gradients: central
difference ($h = 0.005$\,\AA) on either $E_{\text{VQE}}(R)$ (VQE-FD) or on
$\langle H(R\pm h) \rangle$ at fixed $|\psi(\theta^*, R)\rangle$ (VQE-HF).
Classical reference: PySCF FCI + finite-difference gradient.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Energies (5 points)}
All five sampled bond lengths reproduce FCI to $<10^{-8}$ Ha residual --- the
3-parameter ansatz spans the exact ground-state manifold for this system.

\subsection{Gradients}
VQE-FD gradient matches classical FCI-FD gradient to $\sim 10^{-8}$ Ha/\AA{}
across all 5 points. Hellmann--Feynman gradient has $\sim 10^{-5}$ Ha/\AA{}
residual --- three orders of magnitude worse than VQE-FD but still four orders
below the chemical-force threshold ($\sim 10^{-3}$ Ha/\AA).

\subsection{Geometry optimization}
Gradient descent from $R_0 = 1.0$\,\AA{} converges in 7 iterations to
$R = 0.7349$\,\AA, $E = -1.137306$\,Ha. Compared to the paper's quoted
$(0.741\text{ \AA}, -1.137\text{ Ha})$: energy agreement $|\Delta E| = 0.306$\,mHa,
about $5\times$ under the paper's chemical-accuracy tolerance of 1.6 mHa. Our
converged $R$ in fact matches the analytical STO-3G FCI equilibrium more
tightly than the paper's quoted 0.741 \AA.

\section{Critique / honest assessment}
\subsection*{What was genuinely verified}
\begin{itemize}
\item An \emph{independent reimplementation} of the two derivative recipes (VQE-FD
      and Hellmann--Feynman) was written from scratch in PennyLane and produced
      values numerically consistent with a classical FCI-FD reference on the
      same molecule/basis. This is a real code-level replication, not just a
      re-run of paper-provided scripts.
\item Finite-difference agreement was checked at 5 bond lengths (headline: full
      curve straddles the equilibrium; sign flip of $dE/dR$ correctly localizes
      the minimum between $R = 0.70$ and $R = 0.735$\,\AA).
\item The geometry-optimization loop was closed end-to-end: gradient recipe
      $\to$ update rule $\to$ convergence within chemical accuracy of the
      paper's stated minimum. This is the paper's central methodological claim.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{What was NOT exercised / weaknesses of this replication}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Hessian / higher-order derivatives not tested.} The paper's C5 (Newton's
      method) and by extension its polarizability $\alpha_{ZZ}$ (a second
      derivative of $E$ w.r.t.\ electric field) were skipped. Since the Hessian
      recipe is the natural extension of the gradient recipe, the derivative
      machinery is not \emph{fully} verified --- only its first-order slice.
\item \textbf{Measurement-count / shot-noise scaling not addressed.} The paper's
      \S III.C discusses NISQ measurement overhead, but this reproduction uses
      the noise-free \texttt{default.qubit} statevector simulator with no shot
      sampling. The reproduced numbers therefore say nothing about the
      \emph{practical} feasibility claim (that these derivatives are estimable
      within realistic shot budgets on hardware).
\item \textbf{H$_2$ is the easiest possible test.} The 3-parameter UCCSD ansatz for
      H$_2$/STO-3G is provably exact --- \emph{any} correctly implemented VQE
      matches FCI here. This replication therefore verifies our code and
      confirms the paper's derivative formulas are correct on this system, but
      does not stress-test the method on ansatz-error-limited regimes (larger
      molecules, tighter basis sets, active-space truncations) where derivative
      quality would actually be a discriminating benchmark.
\item \textbf{Response properties ($\mu_Z$, $\alpha_{ZZ}$) not reproduced.} The
      dipole/polarizability curves of Fig.\ 5 --- which are the paper's most
      distinctive numerical contribution beyond geometry optimization --- were
      skipped. Without these, the ``derivatives for property calculations''
      framing of the paper is only partially covered.
\item \textbf{H+H$_2$ TS search not reproduced.} The transition-state exercise
      (Fig.\ 6b, symmetric TS at $(0.936, 0.936)$\,\AA) is the paper's most
      demanding geometric application. Skipping it means our verdict is
      conditional on the extra saddle-point search machinery not introducing
      new bugs beyond the minimum-search recipe we did verify.
\item \textbf{Ideal simulator only.} No noise model, no hardware run. All modern
      derivative-VQE hardware demos (real transmon runs) fight barren plateaus
      and measurement noise that are absent here.
\end{itemize}
