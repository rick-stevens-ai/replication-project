# Brief — OSTI 3374627

**Paper**: Kacmaz, Huerta & Haas 2025, "Resolving turbulent magnetohydrodynamics: a hybrid operator-diffusion framework" (Mach. Learn.: Sci. Technol. 6 035057; DOI 10.1088/2632-2153/ae054c).

**What/why**: The paper introduces DINOs — a two-stage surrogate for 2D incompressible resistive MHD that couples a physics-informed neural operator (PINO, a tensor-FNO trained with PDE-residual loss) to a score-based diffusion corrector. Central quantitative claim (Table 1): the diffusion corrector reduces relative-L2 error over the PINO-only baseline at every Re from 250 onward, and the PINO-only baseline error grows monotonically with Re (0.007 → 0.079 → 0.168 → 0.213 → 0.255 → 0.327 → 0.391 at Re = 100…10 000). This replication independently reproduces the paper's dataset-generation pipeline (2D pseudo-spectral MHD via a from-scratch RK4 spectral solver, GRF initial conditions, Prm=1, N=64), trains a small FNO from scratch as the paper's PINO-only baseline for Re ∈ {100, 500, 1000, 3000}, and evaluates 26-step rollout relative-L2 error on held-out simulations. Verdict: **PARTIAL** — we reproduce the qualitative core claims (monotone error growth with Re; magnetic vector potential A is the worst-hit channel; FNO spectral bias underestimates high-k energy in the velocity spectrum) and the reproduced numbers sit in the same regime as the paper's PINO baseline, but without training the diffusion corrector (which would require the paper's full 800-sim/Re dataset and multi-GPU multi-day training) we cannot replicate the paper's *headline* claim that diffusion halves the error.
