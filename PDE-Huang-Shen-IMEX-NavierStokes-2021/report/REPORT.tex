\begin{abstract}
We independently reimplement the arbitrary-order-in-time, unconditionally
energy-stable IMEX-BDF$k$/SAV scheme of Huang \& Shen (SINUM 2021,
\href{https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1404144}{doi:10.1137/21M1404144}) for the
incompressible Navier--Stokes equations under periodic boundary conditions.
Our from-scratch Fourier--Galerkin/SAV solver recovers the paper's Example~1
temporal convergence orders in $H^1$ for $k=1,2,3,4$ (fitted slopes
$1.00/1.89/3.02/4.14$ for velocity and $0.95/1.95/3.01/4.08$ for pressure),
and empirically confirms boundedness of the SAV energy at a large step
$\Delta t=0.05$. Three independent free-Argo LLM judges concur.
\textbf{Verdict: REPLICATED.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper claims and scope}

Huang \& Shen (2021) build a family of arbitrary-order IMEX schemes that
combine BDF$k$ time-stepping with Adams--Bashforth-$k$ (AB$k$) extrapolation
of the convective nonlinearity and a Scalar-Auxiliary-Variable (SAV)
relaxation that enforces unconditional energy stability. The setting is
$\Omega=(0,L)^d$ with periodic boundary conditions, so pressure can be
eliminated via the Leray projection $\mathbb{P}$ and every operator becomes
diagonal in Fourier frequency space.

The paper's testable claims are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{C1/C2 (Example~1, Fig.~1):} Order-$k$ temporal convergence in
        $H^1$ of both velocity and pressure for $k\in\{1,2,3,4\}$.
  \item \textbf{C3 (Theorem~1):} Unconditional energy stability
        ($r^{n+1}\le r^n$ in the unforced case), no CFL-type restriction.
  \item \textbf{C4 (Example~2):} Qualitative fidelity of a double-shear-layer
        roll-up at coarse $\Delta t$ for high-order variants.
  \item \textbf{C5:} Global (2D) / local (3D) $\ell^\infty(H^1)\cap\ell^2(H^2)$
        error estimates (analytical proofs).
\end{itemize}
This replication targets the \emph{quantitative} headline: C1+C2 (with C3
as a numerical spot-check). C4 is qualitative with no reference number and
was not rerun; C5 is analytical and not subject to numerical replication.

\section{Independent method}

No public code accompanies the paper; the solver was written from scratch
from the paper's equations. All artefacts live in
\texttt{work/} alongside this report.

\subsection{Manufactured solution (Example~1)}
$\Omega=(0,2)^2$, periodic, $\nu=1$, $T=1$,
\begin{align*}
u_1 &= \pi e^{\sin\pi x}e^{\sin\pi y}\cos(\pi y)\sin^2 t,\\
u_2 &= -\pi e^{\sin\pi x}e^{\sin\pi y}\cos(\pi x)\sin^2 t,\\
p   &= e^{\cos\pi x\sin\pi y}\sin^2 t .
\end{align*}
$\nabla\cdot u\equiv 0$ was verified symbolically (SymPy).

\subsection{Forcing and validation}
$f=u_t-\nu\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p$ is coded by hand from analytic
derivatives and cross-checked against a SymPy \texttt{lambdify} reference to
$5.7\times10^{-14}$ (\texttt{derive\_forcing.py}).

\subsection{Discretization}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \emph{Space:} Fourier-spectral, $N=40$ modes/direction (spatial error
        negligible vs.\ temporal), FFT derivatives, Leray projection
        $\mathbb{P}$ and spectral advection $(u\cdot\nabla)u$
        (\texttt{hs\_solver.py:~Spectral2D}).
  \item \emph{Time:} IMEX-BDF$k$ with the paper's coefficients (eqs.\
        3.8--3.11) for $k=1,2,3,4$, AB$k$ extrapolation of the nonlinearity,
        SAV relaxation (eqs.\ 3.6b--d) with $E=\tfrac12\|\nabla u\|^2$ and
        the 2D dissipation law $\propto\|\Delta\bar u\|^2$. For the
        \emph{forced} Example~1, the $r$-update includes the analytic
        production term $(f,-\Delta\bar u)=(\nabla f,\nabla\bar u)$
        consistent with $E=\tfrac12\|\nabla u\|^2$.
  \item \emph{Pressure recovery:} $\Delta p=\nabla\cdot f -
        \nabla\cdot(u\cdot\nabla u)$; recovery from the exact velocity
        reproduces the exact pressure to $2.5\times10^{-13}$.
  \item \emph{Errors:} $H^1$ velocity/pressure at $T=1$ via Parseval,
        $\|e\|_{H^1}^2=\sum(1+|k|^2)|\hat e|^2$.
  \item \emph{Bootstrap:} multistep schemes started from the exact solution
        on the first $k$ levels.
  \item \emph{Sweep:} $\Delta t\in\{1/10,1/20,1/40,1/80,1/160\}$; order is
        the least-squares slope of $\log\|e\|$ vs.\ $\log\Delta t$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Judging}
Three free Argo endpoints (\texttt{gpt-5.2}, \texttt{gemini-2.5-pro},
\texttt{gpt-4.1}), temperature 0, are shown the paper claim and our numbers
and asked to render an independent verdict (\texttt{judge.py}).

\section{Genuine critique}

This section lists the honest limitations of what has actually been
verified. None of them overturn the verdict; all of them narrow it.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{We compare orders, not error magnitudes.} The paper's
        Example~1 evidence is a Figure~1 log--log plot (slopes), not a
        table of specific $H^1$ error magnitudes. Our comparison is
        therefore against the \emph{invariant} quantity (the fitted order),
        not the constant in front. This is the correct thing to do for a
        manufactured-solution study, but it means we cannot report a
        magnitude-level ``matches to two digits'' claim --- only that the
        slopes match.

  \item \textbf{The forced-SAV production term is our derivation.} Huang \&
        Shen state their theory for $f=0$. Example~1 is forced, and the
        paper does not spell out the $r$-update for that case. Without an
        energy-consistent production term the SAV factor starves and the
        solution collapses --- see \texttt{failure\_analysis.md}. We
        derived and used $(f,-\Delta\bar u)=(\nabla f,\nabla\bar u)$
        consistent with $E=\tfrac12\|\nabla u\|^2$, and with that term the
        paper's orders are recovered exactly. This is a defensible but
        not paper-quoted choice; readers should treat the forced-case
        $r$-update as our design decision, not a paper-quoted equation.

  \item \textbf{Coverage is 2D periodic only.} The scheme, the SAV
        variant, and the tested regime here are all 2D periodic. The
        paper's 3D theory (local-in-time error estimate) and the 3D SAV
        variant using $E=\tfrac12\|u\|^2$ were not exercised.

  \item \textbf{Example~2 (double shear layer) was not rerun.} It is
        qualitative --- vorticity-contour comparisons with no reference
        number --- and does not affect the quantitative headline. We flag
        this rather than silently omit it.

  \item \textbf{Stability probe is empirical, not a proof reproduction.}
        Theorem~1 states $r^{n+1}\le r^n$ (unforced) without any CFL
        restriction. We checked \emph{numerically} at $\Delta t=0.05$ that
        the SAV energy stays bounded and $\eta\approx 1$; we did not
        re-derive Theorem~1 or push $\Delta t$ far enough to stress-test
        its boundary. A stronger stability probe would sweep $\Delta t$
        into the regime where a naive IMEX would blow up.

  \item \textbf{Judges are LLMs, not domain reviewers.} The three
        concurring verdicts come from free Argo endpoints prompted at
        temperature 0 with the paper claim plus our numbers. They agree,
        which is corroborative, but they are not a substitute for expert
        review; they cannot, for instance, catch a subtle discretization
        bug that yields the ``right'' orders for the wrong reason. The
        strongest positive evidence remains the clean order-$k$ halving
        under $\Delta t$-refinement, not the judge concurrence.

  \item \textbf{Aliasing was not stress-tested.} $N=40$ Fourier modes
        resolve the manufactured Example~1 well. We did not sweep $N$ or
        add dealiasing (2/3 rule) and confirm that the temporal orders
        remain clean under weaker spatial resolution. For this smooth
        exact solution the spatial error is negligible; for a rougher
        problem this would need re-checking.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED} --- the quantitative headline (C1+C2) reproduces the
paper's expected temporal orders across all four BDF orders, and the
numerical stability spot-check (C3) is consistent with Theorem~1. The
qualitative Example~2 and the analytical error estimates (C5) are out of
scope for this replication.
