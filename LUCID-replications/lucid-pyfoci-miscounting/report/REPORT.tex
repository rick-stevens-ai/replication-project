\section{Paper Summary}

Ingram \emph{et al.}, \emph{A computational approach to quantifying miscounting of
radiation-induced double-strand break immunofluorescent foci}, Communications
Biology \textbf{5}, 700 (2022), DOI \texttt{10.1038/s42003-022-03585-5}, introduces
the \textbf{PyFoci} pipeline: Geant4-DNA generates ground-truth DSB spatial
distributions from proton and photon irradiation, a bi-exponential repair model
attenuates DSBs over time, foci are convolved with microscope PSFs (24
microscope$\times$magnification configurations spanning wide-field, confocal, and
Airyscan optics), and a Laplacian-of-Gaussian counting algorithm quantifies the
observable foci counts. Comparing observable counts against the known ground-truth
DSB count yields a per-cell miscount error. The paper reports systematic and
substantial over- and under-counting biases that depend on microscope resolution,
voxel size, deconvolution, 2D-vs-3D analysis, LET, and DSB clustering. The
practical implication --- \emph{immunofluorescent focus counts are a
resolution-dependent proxy, not a direct readout of DSB burden} --- is what
motivated its inclusion in the LUCID low-dose radiation-biology corpus: any
downstream repair-kinetics inference built on counted foci inherits a
resolution-specific bias.

\section{Numerical Results vs Paper}

\textbf{Claim 7 --- Fig 1 Mann--Whitney (120 comparisons).} All 120 author-published
Bonferroni-adjusted two-sided p-values reproduced from
\texttt{080322\_dataframe\_zstack0\_airyscan\_63x.parquet}: 120/120 significance-
direction agreement, 120/120 within 1.5 orders of magnitude on $p$.

\textbf{Claim 8 --- Airyscan magnification (Fig 4, 2\,Gy / 15\,min).} Median \%
miscount (DSB): x10 $-82.2\%$, x20 $+2.1\%$, x40 $+45.1\%$, x63 $+59.4\%$, x100
$+61.0\%$. Only x10 under-counts; x20--x100 span narrows to $+2\%\to+61\%$, matching
the paper's ``significant under-counting at x10'' and ``\% miscount similar
between other magnifications'' claims.

\textbf{Claim 9 --- Voxel-size trend (Fig 5, 24 configs).} Spearman
$r_{\text{DSB}} = -0.78$ ($p=7.3\times10^{-6}$), $r_{\gamma\text{H2AX}} = -0.72$
($p=6.3\times10^{-5}$). Paper's negative-relationship claim holds at
$p<10^{-4}$.

\textbf{Claim 10 --- Deconvolution (Fig 6, Airyscan x63, 1\,Gy).} Deconv-DSB beats
raw-DSB in 7/8 groups; at 24\,h, deconv-DSB is the best-of-four in 4/4 subgroups.
The single exception is the high-LET 30-min case, exactly the clustering-heavy
regime the paper flags as pathological.

\textbf{Claim 11 --- 3D analysis (Fig 7).} 30-min DSB-3D under-counts in 4/4
radiations, monotone in LET ($-51,-54,-60,-131\%$). At 24\,h, low-LET recovers
to within $\pm30\%$; high-LET (P27.95) still under-counts ($-183\%$). Matches all
four Fig 7 sub-claims.

\textbf{Claim 12 --- Clustering CD\_200nm vs miscount (Fig 8).} Spearman
$r_{\text{DSB}}=-0.088$ ($p=6.9\times10^{-33}$),
$r_{\gamma\text{H2AX}}=+0.061$ ($p=1.6\times10^{-16}$). Clustering-bin medians of
DSB miscount: 1.0--1.5\,$+14$; 1.5--2.5\,$-10$; 2.5--5\,$-26$; 5--10\,$-16$.
Direction and asymmetry (H2AX weaker, sign-flipped) both match Fig 8.

\textbf{Claim 13 --- Bi-exponential repair (Fig 3a).} Constants
$a_1=0.711, a_2=0.289, \tau_1=1.54\,\text{h}, \tau_2=10\,\text{h}$; all four
radiation types deviate $\leq 0.0124$ from the same curve at every sampled
time point, confirming the paper's ``uniformly enforced simulation'' claim
to $\approx$1\% precision.

\section{Critique --- Honest Assessment}

The paper is unusually reproducible-friendly, and the re-pass verdict of
\textsc{REPLICATED} is defensible \emph{as scoped}. Several substantive caveats
prevent this from being a full end-to-end reproduction:

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{We validated the paper against its own cached intermediates, not
against a rerun of the pipeline.} The authors released the DataFrame outputs of
their PyFoci pipeline, and every quantitative claim we ``reproduced'' was
computed \emph{on those DataFrames}. If there were a bug upstream in Geant4-DNA
input parsing, in the LoG counter's parameter choices, or in the PSF convolution
step, we would not detect it --- our re-computed p-values and correlations would
merely be self-consistent with the released tables. Claim 5 (BLOCKED) is
therefore not a cosmetic blocker: it is the one test that would catch a
pipeline-level bug, and it remains untested.

\item \textbf{Environment friction was retired, not resolved.} F6 (Python
3.14/\texttt{numba} wheel gap) blocks the raw-image rerun. The workaround
(``spin up a Python 3.11 venv'') is trivial in principle, was not attempted here,
and represents real work that any downstream auditor still owes.

\item \textbf{The Mann--Whitney reproduction has a self-consistency ceiling.}
Because we used the authors' exact rows, any effect of DataFrame preprocessing
(row filtering, outlier handling, dose-column parsing) is baked in; our 100\%
p-value match confirms our test harness reads the same table the paper
described, not that the underlying statistics on the raw images are correct.

\item \textbf{Figure 6's single exception is not fully explained.} The 30-min
high-LET case where raw-DSB happens to match deconv-DSB is consistent with the
paper's narrative (dense clustering saturates both), but we did not quantify how
close ``coincidence'' is to ``systematic effect''; a bootstrap over cells would
be needed.

\item \textbf{Clustering Spearman is weak in magnitude.} $r_{\text{DSB}}=-0.088$
is highly significant ($p\sim10^{-33}$) purely because $n=18{,}168$, not because
the effect is large. The paper's phrasing (``clustering increases under-count'')
is directionally correct but the effect size in the cached-DataFrame regime is
modest --- the bin-median trend ($+14 \to -26$) is more compelling than the
Spearman. An auditor who took ``$|r|<0.1$'' at face value might reasonably
question whether the CD\_200nm operationalisation is the strongest possible
clustering metric.

\item \textbf{Figure regeneration is analog-only.} We reproduced ``the same
qualitative shape'' for Figs 3a, 4, 5 (see \texttt{figures/repass/*.png}); we
did not attempt pixel-exact replicas of the full 12-figure set and did not
reproduce the supplementary figures at all. Claim 6 is honestly graded
\textsc{PARTIAL}.

\end{enumerate}

None of these caveats overturn the verdict. They do bound what \textsc{REPLICATED}
means here: the paper's \emph{released numerical evidence} supports its
\emph{stated quantitative claims}, and its \emph{artifact release} is complete
enough for a determined auditor to close the remaining gaps in a day or two.

\section{Bottom Line}

PyFoci-miscounting is \textbf{REPLICATED} at the released-artifact level with
12/13 claims quantitatively reproduced. The one blocked claim is a raw-pipeline
rerun bounded by Python-environment friction, not by a scientific dispute. The
paper is a strong example of how to release computational biology work
reproducibly. Downstream users should nonetheless treat any focus-count-derived
DSB inference as microscope-configuration-conditional and, ideally, calibrate
their own imaging setup against PyFoci ground truth rather than adopting a
published bias correction verbatim.
