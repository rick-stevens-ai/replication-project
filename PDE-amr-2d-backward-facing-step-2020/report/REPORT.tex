\begin{abstract}
We attempt an independent replication of Li \& Li (2020, IJCM, DOI
\texttt{10.1142/S0219876220410121}), which verifies a velocity-driven adaptive-mesh-refinement
(VDAMR) method on the 2D backward-facing step (BFS) benchmark at low Reynolds numbers.
The paper is paywalled; we obtain the verbatim abstract via Semantic Scholar. We implement
an independent stream-function/vorticity BFS solver in Python (distinct from the sibling
Chorin/MAC replication), execute Navier--Stokes runs at $\mathrm{Re}\in\{50,100,200\}$ with
mesh refinement at $\mathrm{Re}=50$, curate Armaly-1983 and Erturk-2008 public benchmark data
in a common Reynolds convention, and---as a solver-independent test of the paper's
mathematical claim---verify the VDAMR divergence-flag indicator and vortex-centre-recovery
convergence on a manufactured analytical stream function with a KNOWN vortex-centre location.
An LLM judge (Argo GPT-5.2, cherryrd \texttt{:4000} aggregator) rates the outcome
\textbf{PARTIAL} (55\% agreement): the paper's methodological claims (C1: flag-fraction decay;
C2: vortex-centre self-convergence) are cleanly reproduced solver-independently, but the
paper's own benchmark comparisons (C3) and AMR-vs-uniform efficiency claim (C4) remain
untested because the full-text paper is paywalled and our full BFS-NS solve did not
robustly produce recirculation across the target Re-range.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper Summary}
Li \& Li (2020) verify a 2D adaptive-mesh-refinement (AMR) method by applying it to the
canonical backward-facing step (BFS) flow at low Reynolds numbers. The method sits atop the
open-source \textit{Navier2D} vertex-centred finite-volume Navier--Stokes code (Engwirda,
MATLAB Central) using median-dual control volumes. After each NS solve the algorithm
computes the discrete divergence residual per control volume, flags those with
$|\mathrm{div}|$ above a fraction of the maximum, and bisects them. Accuracy is demonstrated
by convergence of the recovered vortex-centre location (extracted from linearly-interpolated
numerical solutions) toward Armaly-1983 experimental and Erturk-2008 numerical benchmark
values.

\subsection{Independent BFS solver (\texttt{work/bfs\_psi\_omega.py}, $\sim$330 LOC)}
Stream-function/vorticity ($\psi$--$\omega$) formulation with:
\begin{align}
  \nabla^2 \psi &= -\omega, \\
  u &= \partial \psi / \partial y,\quad v = -\partial \psi / \partial x, \\
  \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial t} + u \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial x} + v \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial y}
  &= \frac{1}{\mathrm{Re}} \nabla^2 \omega.
\end{align}
Uniform Cartesian mesh; sparse LU factorisation of $\nabla^2$ once per grid via
\texttt{scipy.sparse.linalg.splu}; boundary contributions to the Poisson RHS assembled
into a one-shot sparse matrix $B$ so per-timestep BC handling is one sparse mat--vec.
Hybrid central/1st-order-upwind convection (central where cell-P\'eclet
$|u|\Delta x\,\mathrm{Re}<2$, upwind otherwise). Explicit RK2 in time, adaptive
$\Delta t$ (CFL 0.35). Steady-state detection: relative range of $x_r/S$ over a 40-sample
window below $10^{-4}$.

\subsection{Manufactured VDAMR verification (\texttt{work/vdamr\_synthetic.py})}
Bona-fide analytical streamfunction $\psi(x,y) = \psi_{\rm base}(x,y) + \psi_{\rm vortex}(x,y)$ where
$\psi_{\rm base}$ is analytic Poiseuille (upstream and downstream matched at the step) and
$\psi_{\rm vortex} = -A\exp\!\left(-\frac{(x-x_c)^2 + (y-y_c)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)$ with
KNOWN $(x_c, y_c) = (3.0, 0.35)$, $A=0.10$, $\sigma=0.25$. Because $\psi$ is a genuine
streamfunction, the ANALYTICAL divergence is identically zero; the DISCRETE divergence of
central-difference velocities is $O(\Delta x^2)$ round-off, which is what Li's VDAMR flag
is measuring on a real solution. Six uniformly refined grids
$\Delta x \in \{0.4, 0.2, 0.1, 0.05, 0.025, 0.0125\}$, finest $1201\times 161 = 193\,461$
cells; wall time $\sim$2\,s on uicgpu. Vortex-centre recovery by (a) $\arg\min_{\rm sub}\psi$
and (b) 2D quadratic sub-grid fit on a $3\times3$ stencil.

\subsection{Reference data curation (\texttt{work/reference\_data.py})}
Armaly-1983 (10 pts, $\mathrm{ER}=1.94$) and Erturk-2008 (10 pts, $\mathrm{ER}=2.00$)
tables of $x_r/S(\mathrm{Re})$, with Reynolds-convention conversions to Li's convention:
$\mathrm{Re}_{\rm Li} = \mathrm{Re}_D / 3 = \mathrm{Re}_e / 1.5$.

\textbf{What worked:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Flag fraction monotonically decays from 0.147 to 0.0005 (2.5 orders of magnitude
        across 1.6 decades of $\Delta x$). Claim C1 reproduced.
  \item Vortex-centre self-convergence with observed orders \textbf{1.03} (argmin) and
        \textbf{2.27} (quadratic sub-grid); the argmin order corresponds to first-order
        argmin discretisation error, the quadratic to true second-order sub-grid recovery.
        Claim C2 reproduced.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{What didn't work / caveats:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Argmin y-coordinate converges to 0.164, not to the geometric Gaussian centre 0.35.
        This is a consequence of adding a negative Gaussian to a Poiseuille background;
        the raw $\arg\min\psi$ metric picks up the wall-shifted minimum. See Open
        Question Q2.
\end{itemize}

\section{Open Questions}
Five heavy-duty grounded questions with concrete next steps live in
\texttt{report/open\_questions.json}. Summary:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Does the paper's median-dual FV divergence operator give the same flag-fraction
        decay rate we observed with central FD on a manufactured field?
  \item Which vortex-centre extraction convention does the paper use (raw
        $\arg\min\psi$ vs bulk-subtracted vs $|u|=0$)? This can shift $y_c$ by a factor
        of 2.
  \item Why does the $\psi$--$\omega$ formulation fail to develop recirculation where a
        Chorin projection succeeds on the same coarse meshes?
  \item Minimum $h/\Delta x$ (cells per step height) for VDAMR to correctly identify the
        primary vortex, as a function of Re.
  \item Explicit Reynolds-convention reconciliation across the Li / Armaly / Erturk
        conventions (factors of 3 and 1.5); does the paper document its choice?
\end{enumerate}
