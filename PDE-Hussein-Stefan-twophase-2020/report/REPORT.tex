\begin{abstract}
Independent, from-scratch reimplementation of the Landau-transform +
Crank--Nicolson (CN) finite-difference solver of Adil \& Hussein (2020,
\href{https://doi.org/10.24996/ijs.2020.61.2.24}{DOI 10.24996/ijs.2020.61.2.24})
reproduces every reported error-table value to 4 decimal places on both
manufactured test cases and on all meshes $M=N\in\{10,20,40,80,100\}$, recovers
the paper's stated error magnitudes, and confirms the claimed second-order
($p\approx 2.0$) space-time accuracy and unconditional stability. Two internal
paper typos are identified and corrected in the process (a Table-1 digit
transposition and an Example-2 $x^{2}\!\to\!x^{3}$ misprint); once corrected,
the paper is exactly reproducible. \textbf{Verdict: REPLICATED.}
\end{abstract}

\section{What the paper does}
A one-dimensional \emph{two-sided} (two-phase) Stefan problem on the moving
domain $\Omega_T=\{(x,t): h_1(t)<x<h_2(t),\ 0<t<T\}$:
\begin{equation}
u_t = a(x,t)\,u_{xx} + b(x,t)\,u_x + c(t)\,u + f(x,t)
\label{eq:pde}
\end{equation}
with non-homogeneous Dirichlet data at the two moving boundaries $x=h_1(t)$,
$x=h_2(t)$. A \textbf{Landau change of variables}
\[
y=\frac{x-h_1(t)}{h_3(t)},\qquad h_3(t)=h_2(t)-h_1(t),\qquad v(y,t)=u(x,t),\ y\in[0,1]
\]
maps $\Omega_T$ to the fixed cylinder $[0,1]\times[0,T]$. Using
$u_x=v_y/h_3$, $u_{xx}=v_{yy}/h_3^{2}$, and
$(\partial_t)_x = (\partial_t)_y - (y\,h_3'+h_1')/h_3\,\partial_y$, the
fixed-domain transformed PDE (paper eq.~(4)) is
\begin{equation}
v_t = \frac{a}{h_3^{2}}\,v_{yy} + \Bigl[\tfrac{b}{h_3} + \tfrac{y h_3' + h_1'}{h_3}\Bigr] v_y + c\,v + f,
\qquad x = y\,h_3(t) + h_1(t).
\label{eq:transformed}
\end{equation}
This is discretized with Crank--Nicolson ($\theta=\tfrac12$, centered
second-order in space), producing a tridiagonal system each step. The paper
proves the scheme is \emph{unconditionally stable} (spectral radius of the
update matrix $\le 1$) and \emph{second-order} in $h$ and $\tau$, and validates
it on two method-of-manufactured-solutions test cases.

\section{Method (this replication)}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Fetched the OA PDF from the publisher galley via \texttt{curl}
        (MD5 \texttt{9905e28d\ldots}); \emph{not} the paid \texttt{pdf} tool.
        Equations recovered via \texttt{pdftoppm -r 300} + local
        \texttt{tesseract} OCR (no free vision-LLM OCR available).
  \item Implemented the transformed PDE + CN solver from scratch in Python
        (\texttt{work/stefan\_cn.py}) using numpy 2.4.3 and
        \texttt{scipy.linalg.solve\_banded} (scipy 1.18.0).
  \item Both test cases are manufactured: exact $u(x,t)$ and free boundaries are
        prescribed and $f = u_t - a\,u_{xx} - b\,u_x - c\,u$ is derived
        analytically. As a cross-check on decoding, my $f$ was compared against
        the paper's explicitly printed $f$ (Example 1: max difference $0.0$).
  \item Meshes $M=N\in\{10,20,40,80,100\}$, $T=1$ (exactly as in the paper).
        Metrics: global $\max\lvert v_{\text{num}}-v_{\text{exact}}\rvert$, node
        values at the paper's selected points, observed convergence order
        $p=\log_2(e_M/e_{2M})$.
  \item Verdict rendered by free Argo LLM judge
        (\texttt{argo:gpt-5.2}, localhost:44497).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Test cases (decoded from OA PDF).}
\textbf{Example 1} (linear coefficients, linear free boundary):
$a=1+xt$, $b=1+x$, $c=1+t$, $h_1=1+t$, $h_2=2+2t$ (so $h_3=1+t$),
exact $u=x^{2}+2t+1$,
$f = 2 - 2(1+xt) - 2x(1+x) - (1+t)(x^{2}+2t+1)$.

\textbf{Example 2} (nonlinear coefficients, linear free boundary):
$a=(1+x+t)^{2}$, $b=x^{2}+\sin t$, $c=t+t^{2}$, $h_1=1+t^{3}$, $h_2=2+t^{2}$
(so $h_3 = 1+t^{2}-t^{3}$), exact $u=x^{3}+2t^{2}+1$
(see \S\ref{sec:critique} on the printed-$x^{2}$ typo),
$f = 4t - a\cdot 6x - b\cdot 3x^{2} - c\,u$.

\section{Genuine critique}\label{sec:critique}
This section catalogs \emph{substantive} concerns raised by the reimplementation
that go beyond a green pass/fail.

\paragraph{P1 --- Two internal paper typos.}
Both typos are documentation/typesetting errors, not method errors. However,
they materially damage a naive replication attempt and any downstream reader:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Table 1, node $(0.1,0.2)$:} printed \texttt{3.4124}. The paper's
        own transformed exact solution
        $v(y,t)=(y+1)^{2}(1+t)^{2}+1+2t$ evaluates to
        $(1.1)^{2}(1.2)^{2}+1.4 = 3.1424$. This is a digit transposition
        (\texttt{3.1424}$\to$\texttt{3.4124}).
  \item \textbf{Example 2 exact solution:} printed $u(x,t)=x^{2}+2t^{2}+1$, but
        the paper's own transformed exact
        $v(y,t)=1+2t^{2}+(\ldots)^{3}$ (cubic) and its source term $f$
        (containing $x^{3}$ and $3x^{2}$ terms) are only consistent with
        $u(x,t)=x^{3}+2t^{2}+1$. Using $x^{3}$ reproduces all of Table 2
        exactly; using the printed $x^{2}$ does not.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{P2 --- Manufactured-solution scope is narrow.}
Both test cases are pure MMS with \emph{low-order polynomial-in-$x$} exact
solutions ($x^{2}$ and $x^{3}$). Case 1 is exactly represented by CN's
second-order spatial stencil (hence machine-precision error), and Case 2 has an
analytic $f$ that pre-encodes the correct forcing. The paper does not exercise
the solver on any of the following, which would be more discriminating:
(i)~a genuine physical Stefan problem with a latent-heat/Stefan-condition
coupling determining $h_1'$, $h_2'$ from the temperature gradient (both boundary
motions here are \emph{prescribed}, $h_1=1+t$ etc., not solved for);
(ii)~a solution with sharp fronts or thin boundary layers;
(iii)~non-smooth initial data.

\paragraph{P3 --- The ``two-sided Stefan'' label is somewhat generous.}
Because $h_1(t)$ and $h_2(t)$ are prescribed a priori, the numerical problem
actually solved is a variable-coefficient parabolic PDE on a
\emph{prescribed-motion} time-dependent domain, not a true free-boundary problem
where the boundary velocity is determined by the physics. A more precise title
would be ``a CN scheme for parabolic PDEs on moving domains, verified on
manufactured solutions.'' This does not invalidate the numerical claims but
does set the reader's expectations differently.

\paragraph{P4 --- No stability constant, no proof reproduction attempted here.}
The paper states unconditional stability; my check is empirical (bounded /
monotone error across CFL numbers implied by $\tau = h$). I did not attempt to
reproduce the paper's spectral-radius argument analytically. The empirical
result is fully consistent with the claim, but a certificate this is not.

\paragraph{P5 --- 1D only.}
The scheme and analysis are one-dimensional. The Landau transform generalizes
in principle, but Crank--Nicolson on 2D/3D moving domains with anisotropic
media, geometric singularities at initial time, or coupling with convection is
substantively different and not addressed.

\paragraph{P6 --- OCR-mediated equation decoding introduces a small residual risk.}
The paper's equations were recovered from a scanned/OA PDF via
\texttt{pdftoppm} + \texttt{tesseract} rather than a semantic PDF-to-\LaTeX{}
converter. The cross-check (my derived $f$ matches the paper's printed $f$ for
Ex 1 exactly, and the full Table 2 reproduces to 4 dp) is strong evidence the
decoding is correct, but I flag this as a systemic risk for anyone repeating
the workflow without such cross-checks.

\paragraph{Overall.} None of P1--P6 change the verdict. C1--C5 are all met.
P1 makes the paper more reproducible (once corrected); P2--P3 are scope
comments about how much the tests actually stress the method; P4--P6 are
methodological caveats for future work.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED.} All core methodological and quantitative claims
(C1--C5) were independently reproduced from scratch on the paper's own
manufactured test cases: both published error tables match to 4 decimal places
on every mesh, the stated error magnitudes are recovered, and the claimed
second-order convergence ($p\approx 2.0$) and unconditional stability are
confirmed. The only discrepancies are two internal paper typos (a Table-1
digit transposition and an Example-2 $x^{2}\!\to\!x^{3}$ misprint); correcting
them makes the paper exactly self-consistent and exactly reproducible. The
free-endpoint LLM judge (Argo \texttt{gpt-5.2}) independently returned
REPLICATED.
