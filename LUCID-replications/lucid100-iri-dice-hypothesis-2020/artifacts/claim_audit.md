# IRI-DICE — Claim Audit Table

Paper: Langen B, Helou K, Forssell-Aronsson E (2020). *The IRI-DICE hypothesis: ionizing radiation-induced DSBs may have a functional role for non-deterministic responses at low doses.* Radiation and Environmental Biophysics 59:349–355. DOI: 10.1007/s00411-020-00854-x.

Article type per the journal: **"CONTROVERSIAL ISSUE"** — i.e., a position/hypothesis piece, not a primary-data paper.

## Verification budget summary

Counting only items that admit any kind of objective check at all:

- **External-citation checks (does the cited paper exist and does Langen et al. summarize its headline finding correctly?):** C1, C2, C3, C4, C5, C6, C8, C9 — **8 / 8 verified consistent** (with the standard-of-evidence available to a free-tier desk audit: title, journal, and abstract-level findings of each citation).
- **Internal arithmetic check:** C7 (1 in ~50) — **verified** (1/0.02 = 50.0).
- **Hypotheses H1–H4 and proposals P1, P2:** non-falsifiable at this time with current technology, as the paper itself states; classed as "internally consistent" rather than verified.
- **Original quantitative results re-computable from a model in the paper:** **zero.** There is no equation, no simulation output, no data table, no figure with numbers. Figure 1 is a labeled schematic. There are no supplementary materials of quantitative type referenced.

## Bottom line for the audit

This is a **conceptual / position paper in the journal's "Controversial Issue" track**. There is nothing to recompute. The replication is necessarily a logic-and-citation audit, and on that axis the paper is **internally coherent and accurately cites its sources**, with one piece of trivial arithmetic that checks out.
