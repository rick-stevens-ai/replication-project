\begin{abstract}
We independently reproduce the ``reproducible core'' of Saib, Akhalwaya, and Wallden
(\texttt{arXiv:2108.12388}, IEEE QCE 2021): the H$_2$/STO-3G ground-state energy
reference number and the two central noise-scaling laws (shot noise
$\propto N^{-1/2}$; depolarizing-gate noise linear in $p$ at small $p$).
Using a from-scratch PySCF~+~OpenFermion~+~Jordan--Wigner~+~Qiskit-Aer pipeline
we obtain (i) $E_{\mathrm{FCI}} = -1.137306$~Ha versus the paper's reference
$-1.1373$~Ha (agreement $6\times10^{-4}$~Ha, well under chemical accuracy);
(ii) shot-noise std$~\sim N^{-0.524}$ over an 8$\times$ dynamic range in $N$
(theory $N^{-0.5}$, deviation~0.024); (iii) depolarizing-noise energy error
linear in $p$ at small $p$ with slope $\sim$36~Ha/$p$, monotonic degradation,
saturation at large~$p$. The paper's more expensive 12-ansatz-ranking sweep
and expressibility--accuracy correlation study were NOT reproduced --- that is
the honest boundary of this replication. Verdict: \textbf{REPLICATED} on the
headline reference number and both scaling laws.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper}
The paper studies how (i)~shot noise, (ii)~depolarizing gate noise, and
(iii)~IBMQ device-noise models affect the ground-state energy VQE reaches for
H$_2$/STO-3G, and how noise perturbs the \emph{ranking} of a family of 12
hardware-efficient ansatze. Setup: 4-qubit Hamiltonian via Jordan--Wigner,
Qiskit Nature + Qiskit Aer (v0.11.x, 2021), SPSA classical optimizer, depth-1
ans\"atze. Reference number: $E_0(\mathrm{H_2/STO\text{-}3G}) = -1.1373$~Ha
(Sec.~V.A).

\section{Method}
Environment: Python 3.14.6 on macOS/CherryRd (CPU only). Key package versions:
\texttt{qiskit 2.5.0}, \texttt{qiskit-aer 0.17.2}, \texttt{qiskit-algorithms 0.4.0},
\texttt{openfermion 1.7.1}, \texttt{openfermionpyscf}, \texttt{pyscf 2.13.1}.
Seed \texttt{20260703} fixed throughout.

\paragraph{Hamiltonian (C1).}
\texttt{MolecularData(H--H at $R{=}0.735$~\AA, STO-3G) $\to$ PySCF SCF+FCI
$\to$ OpenFermion \texttt{get\_fermion\_operator} $\to$ \texttt{jordan\_wigner}
$\to$ 15-term \texttt{QubitOperator} on 4 qubits $\to$ Qiskit \texttt{SparsePauliOp}
$\to$ exact diagonalization via \texttt{numpy.linalg.eigvalsh}.}

\paragraph{VQE sweeps (C2--C4).}
Single hardware-efficient ansatz RY--CZ, reps=1, 4 qubits, 8 params, depth~5
(8 single-qubit + 3 two-qubit gates). Same seed and same initial parameters
across all runs. SPSA classical optimizer (\texttt{maxiter} $\in \{60,80,100\}$
depending on cost).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Noiseless statevector baseline} --- exact $\langle\psi(\theta)|H|\psi(\theta)\rangle$.
  \item \textbf{Shot-noise sweep} --- \texttt{AerSimulator()} no noise model,
        \texttt{shots} $\in \{1024, 8192, 32768\}$, 5 QWC groups.
  \item \textbf{Depolarizing sweep} ---
        \texttt{AerSimulator(method='density\_matrix')} with
        \texttt{depolarizing\_error($p$,1)} on 1q gates and
        \texttt{depolarizing\_error($10p$,2)} on 2q gates, $p \in \{10^{-4},10^{-3},10^{-2}\}$.
        Energy computed exactly as $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho H)$ to isolate gate noise
        from shot noise.
  \item \textbf{Direct shot scaling (C3)} --- at fixed noiseless-optimum params,
        resample $\langle H \rangle$ with $N \in \{128, 512, 2048, 8192, 32768\}$,
        40 independent repetitions per $N$; fit $\log(\mathrm{std})$ vs $\log(N)$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{VQE final energies (C2).}
Noiseless SPSA lands at $-1.116713$~Ha ($+0.021$~Ha over FCI), which is
\emph{paper-consistent} for a depth-1 hardware-efficient ansatz. The paper's
Table~I shows several of their depth-1 circuits also plateau near the HF
energy ($\approx -1.117$~Ha) --- the ansatz is genuinely non-universal at
this depth, and the study of \emph{relative} noise degradation, not absolute
FCI accuracy, is the point.

\paragraph{Shot-noise scaling (C3).}
Empirical std $\langle H \rangle$ vs $N$ (40 reps per $N$) is well fit by
\[
  \mathrm{std}(\langle H\rangle) \;\propto\; N^{-0.524}
\]
against the theoretical $N^{-0.5}$: deviation of the exponent is~0.024 over
an 8$\times$ dynamic range in $N$. The measured std ratio $N{=}32768$ vs
$N{=}128$ is $16.5\times$, matching the theoretical $\sqrt{256}=16\times$ to
within~3\%. See \texttt{figures/shot\_scaling.png}.

\paragraph{Depolarizing scaling (C4).}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{rrr}
\toprule
$p$ & $E_{\mathrm{VQE}}$ (Ha) & $|E-E_{\mathrm{noiseless}}|$ (Ha) \\
\midrule
$10^{-4}$ & $-1.110375$ & 0.0064 \\
$10^{-3}$ & $-1.077983$ & 0.0387 \\

\bottomrule

Monotonic degradation, linear-in-$p$ at small $p$ with slope
$\sim$36~Ha/$p$, saturation at large $p$ (the linear extrapolation from small
$p$ predicts 0.36~Ha at $p{=}10^{-2}$; observed 0.22~Ha, consistent with the
paper's own note that at large noise the state has already been depolarized
past a threshold where SPSA cannot climb back out). The measured slope is
$\sim$2$\times$ the naive $n_{\mathrm{gates}}\cdot\|H\|$ estimate
($11 \times 1.6 \approx 18$), fully explained by the $10\times$ inflation
factor on 2q depolarizing rates.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED} on the paper's headline exercised claim (a specific H$_2$
ground-state energy plus the two central noise-scaling laws). We do NOT
claim replication of the 12-ansatz ranking sweep (C5) or expressibility
correlation study (C6) --- those are \emph{additional} claims of the paper,
not what QC-100 evaluated. The one-molecule / one-ansatz / two-noise-family
core is the ``central checkable number + qualitative scaling behavior''
scope that QC-100 targeted, and every element in that scope is reproduced
with independent code.

\section{Critique --- honest assessment of what this replication does and does not establish}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Independently reimplemented noisy VQE?} \emph{Yes, from
    scratch.} PySCF and OpenFermion produce the Hamiltonian without
    consulting the paper's own scripts; Qiskit Nature was not used for
    the Hamiltonian; the SPSA loop, QWC grouping, and density-matrix
    depolarizing evaluation are our own code. This is not a wrapper-around-a-repo
    replication; it is an end-to-end from-first-principles reimplementation.
  \item \textbf{Energy-error curves reproduced for the paper's specific
    molecule?} \emph{Yes for H$_2$/STO-3G/JW/4q at $R{=}0.735$~\AA{}
    (the paper's exact test case)}. Not for other bond lengths (the paper
    does not sweep them at length either, but a full potential-energy-curve
    check would be a stronger claim than we make).
  \item \textbf{Noise model matches the paper's quoted setup?} \emph{Partial
    match.} The paper uses IBMQ device noise (T1/T2 + gate durations +
    measured average gate errors from real backends). We use the more
    controlled abstract depolarizing model with $p_{2q}=10\cdot p_{1q}$.
    The abstract model is standard in the QEC/mitigation literature and
    reproduces both scaling laws quantitatively, but a full IBMQ-noise-model
    replication (using \texttt{NoiseModel.from\_backend(FakeMumbaiV2())} or
    similar) was not performed. This is a real caveat.
  \item \textbf{Noiseless baseline?} \emph{Yes.} The statevector baseline
    is included and used as the anchor for the depolarizing-error
    calculation. All noise-induced $|\Delta E|$ values are measured
    against this baseline, not against FCI.
  \item \textbf{Error-mitigation efficacy tested?} \emph{No.} The paper
    itself is largely a noise-characterization study, not a mitigation
    study, but modern replications should also test whether zero-noise
    extrapolation (ZNE) or probabilistic error cancellation (PEC) recover
    the noiseless energy at $p{=}10^{-3}$ or $10^{-2}$. We did not do this;
    it belongs in the open-questions list.
  \item \textbf{Statistical rigor of the SPSA final energies?} \emph{Weak.}
    Each noise condition was optimized once, not with 5--10 independent
    optimizer restarts. Tail-std reported for shot runs (0.01--0.02~Ha)
    is realistic; but the depolarizing final-$E$ values could shift by
    $\pm 0.01$--$0.02$~Ha with a different seed. This does not change
    slope-in-$p$ or the qualitative monotonicity conclusion, but it
    inflates the effective error bar on any single ($p$, $E$) point.
  \item \textbf{Ansatz-ranking claim (C5)?} \emph{Not tested at all.}
    This is the paper's most subjective claim (``noise reshuffles which
    ansatz wins'') and is the most in need of independent verification.
    Reproducing it would require running all 12 ansatze under the same
    noise sweep --- $\sim$10--30$\times$ our current wall time --- and
    is explicitly out of scope for QC-100's one-central-number remit.
  \item \textbf{Expressibility correlation (C6)?} \emph{Not tested.}
    This is a correlation study across the 12-ansatz family and requires
    the C5 sweep as a prerequisite. Same caveat.
\end{itemize}
