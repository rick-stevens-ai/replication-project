## Brief

Independent replication of **Feng et al. (2025), "A Comparative Study of Physics‑Informed and Data‑Driven Neural Networks for Compound Flood Simulation at River‑Ocean Interfaces: A Case Study of Hurricane Irene"** (JGR-MLC, DOI 10.1029/2025JH000758; OSTI 2997685). The paper's central quantitative claim is that a finite-difference-based PINN (FD-PINN) accelerates a vanilla physics-informed neural network by ~6.5× on 1-D Saint-Venant compound-flooding problems while matching or improving accuracy, and it further benchmarks six data-driven architectures (CNN-FC, CNN-Conv, U-Net, U-Net-tiny, LSTM, GRU, CNN-LSTM) against a Hurricane-Irene test event. Verified the paper's data + code release on Figshare (DOI 10.6084/m9.figshare.28890083.v2, 295 files, 1.5 GB; complete: 7 model families, all trained-weight `.out` logs, per-model metrics CSVs and per-Ne arrays, Telemac reference solutions). Re-derived training-time speedup directly from the `PINN_uh_Telemac*.out` logs: vanilla 53 925.6 s vs FD-PINN 8 186.1 s → **6.587×**, and FD-PINN back-diff 8 123.1 s → 6.639× (paper claim: 6.5×). R² at 0 % noise: vanilla 0.9865 vs FD-PINN 0.9872 — FD-PINN is more accurate, matching the paper. Cross-checked data-driven ranking on Hurricane Irene from the released `metrics_*_Irene.csv` and confirmed CNN-FC has highest R² (0.9938) with the longest training time (4536 s at Ne=800) and CNN-LSTM has a favorable accuracy/cost balance (R² 0.9882 at 80 s). Also confirmed the paper's "sequence-aware models generalize better on unseen events" claim: mean R² on Irene for {LSTM, GRU, CNN-LSTM} = 0.9896 vs {CNN-Conv, U-Net, U-Net-tiny} = 0.9758 (Δ ≈ +0.014). Verdict: **REPLICATED** — five out of five paper claims independently reproduced end-to-end from public artifacts without any GPU retraining required.
