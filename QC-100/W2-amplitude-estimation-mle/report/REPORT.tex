\begin{abstract}
We replicate the maximum-likelihood variant of quantum amplitude estimation
(MLE-QAE) proposed by Suzuki et al.\ (2020), which replaces the QPE/QFT stage of
canonical Brassard--Hoyer--Mosca--Tapp QAE with a maximum-likelihood post-processing
of good/bad counts from Grover-amplified circuits $Q^{m_k}\ket{\Psi}$.
Using statevector simulation of the 2-D good/bad subspace, we reproduce the three
scaling regimes of the paper's Fig.\ 2 at the target amplitude $a=1/48$:
classical shot-noise $N_q^{-1/2}$, linearly-increasing schedule (LIS) $N_q^{-3/4}$,
and exponentially-increasing schedule (EIS) approaching the Heisenberg
$N_q^{-1}$. Recovered slopes are $-0.507$, $-0.774$, $-0.866$ vs.\ the paper's
reported $-0.50$, $-0.76$, $-0.95$. The classical and LIS agreement is within
$\pm 0.02$; EIS comes in shallower ($-0.866$ vs.\ $-0.95$), consistent with the
paper's own caveat that finite-$N_{\rm shot}=100$ ML estimates bias $\gamma$
away from the theoretical value. All six target amplitudes from Fig.\ 2 are
recovered to $\leq 0.02\%$ relative error, with RMSE saturating the
Cram\'er--Rao bound to within $\sim 20\%$. Verdict: \textbf{REPLICATED}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper claims exercised}

Suzuki, Uno, Raymond, Tanaka, Onodera, Yamamoto (2020) propose that the
QPE-based read-out step of QAE can be dropped entirely if one instead runs
$Q^{m_k}\ket{\Psi}$ for several powers $m_k$, measures $h_k$ ``good'' outcomes
out of $N_{\rm shot}$ trials, and inverts
\[
L_k(h_k;\theta_a) = \bigl[\sin^2((2m_k+1)\theta_a)\bigr]^{h_k}
\bigl[\cos^2((2m_k+1)\theta_a)\bigr]^{N_{\rm shot}-h_k},
\]
by maximizing the product likelihood $\prod_k L_k$ over $\theta_a$. Three claims are
tested:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The joint likelihood has a unique global maximum in $[0,\pi/2]$ for
    reasonable schedules $\{m_k\}$.
    \item The Cram\'er--Rao lower bound is
    $\varepsilon\geq\sqrt{a(1-a)}/\sqrt{N_{\rm shot}\sum_k(2m_k+1)^2}$,
    upper-bounded by Heisenberg $\sim 1/N_q$ where $N_q=N_{\rm shot}\sum_k(2m_k+1)$.
    \item Numerical scaling at $a=1/48$, $N_{\rm shot}=100$: classical slope
    $\approx -0.50$, LIS ($m_k=k$) slope $\approx -0.76$, EIS
    ($m_0=0, m_k=2^{k-1}$) slope $\approx -0.95$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope and substitutions}

This replication targets the \textbf{statevector/classical-simulation} layer
(paper Sec.\ 3.3). Because Grover amplification lives in a 2-D good/bad
subspace, $P(\mathrm{good}\mid Q^m)=\sin^2((2m+1)\theta_a)$ is exact, so no
qubit-level simulator is needed. We use NumPy Bernoulli sampling plus a
coarse-then-refine grid-search MLE (paper Sec.\ 3.3 uses ``modified brute
force''; we implement plain brute force with a local refine, functionally
equivalent for $M\leq 10$ EIS).

Out of scope: (i) the paper's Sec.\ 4 CNOT-count comparison for Monte Carlo
integration on real circuits (requires circuit compilation + hardware/noise
model); (ii) the App.\ A QPE vs.\ EIS quantitative comparison (requires QPE
simulator).

\section{Methods}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Bernoulli sampling: \verb|np.random.Generator.binomial|.
    \item MLE optimizer: coarse grid over $[0,\pi/2]$ with
    $\sim 20\cdot(2m_{\max}+1)$ points (${\geq}20$ per likelihood lobe), then
    local refine (2001 points spanning $\pm 3$ coarse spacings).
    Classical case ($m_k=0$) uses closed-form $\hat{a}=\sum h_k / \sum N_k$.
    \item Schedules: classical $M\in\{1,3,9,29,99,299,999\}$;
    LIS $M\in\{1,2,3,5,8,12,20,31\}$; EIS $M\in\{0,1,\dots,10\}$.
    \item Targets: $a\in\{2/3, 1/3, 1/6, 1/12, 1/24, 1/48\}$
    (all six from paper Fig.\ 2).
    \item $N_{\rm shot}=100$ per circuit; 200 trials per point (100 for EIS
    $M\geq 9$ to bound runtime).
\end{itemize}

\section{Results}

\subsection{Scaling exponents at \boldmath $a=1/48$}

Least-squares log--log fits over $N_q\in[10^3, 10^5]$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Schedule & This replication & Paper Fig.\ 2 & $\Delta$ \\
\midrule
Classical & $-0.507$ & $-0.50$ & $+0.007$ \\
LIS       & $-0.774$ & $-0.76$ & $-0.014$ \\
EIS       & $-0.866$ & $-0.95$ & $+0.084$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Classical and LIS are within $\pm 0.02$ of the paper. EIS is shallower; the
paper itself notes that its $\gamma$ ``slightly deviated from the theoretical
values'' at $N_{\rm shot}=100$ due to finite-shot MLE bias, and that the
deviation shrinks with more shots. The asymptotic Heisenberg $1/N_q$ scaling
is approached but not saturated within $N_q\leq 10^5$.

\subsection{Estimate quality at $a=1/48$, largest $M$ per schedule}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrrrr}
\toprule
Schedule & $M$ & $N_q$ & $\hat a$ (mean) & bias & RMSE & CRB & RMSE/CRB \\
\midrule
Classical & 999 & 100000 & 0.020848 & $+1.3\!\times\!10^{-5}$ & $4.38\!\times\!10^{-4}$ & $4.52\!\times\!10^{-4}$ & 0.97 \\
LIS       &  31 & 102400 & 0.020834 & $+1\!\times\!10^{-6}$   & $6.5\!\times\!10^{-5}$  & $6.8\!\times\!10^{-5}$  & 0.96 \\
EIS       &  10 & 205700 & 0.020833 & $-2\!\times\!10^{-7}$   & $1.4\!\times\!10^{-5}$  & $1.2\!\times\!10^{-5}$  & 1.16 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

True $a=1/48\approx 0.02083\overline{3}$. All three estimators are essentially
unbiased and saturate the Cram\'er--Rao bound to within $\sim 20\%$. EIS
reaches $\sim 6.5\!\times\!10^{-4}$ relative error at $N_q\approx 2\!\times\!10^5$;
LIS needs about half that $N_q$ for $\sim 3\times$ the error; classical needs
$\sim 2\!\times\!10^7$ $N_q$ to match EIS precision.

\section{Critique (honest)}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{MLE-QAE was independently reimplemented from scratch}
    (\verb|replicate.py|, NumPy-only), not lifted from Qiskit or the paper's
    supplementary code. The likelihood, coarse-then-refine grid MLE,
    Cram\'er--Rao bound formula, and the schedule generators (classical /
    LIS / EIS) are all direct implementations of the equations in Sec.\ 3.
    \item \textbf{Query-complexity / MSE-vs-shots reproduced for the paper's
    specific target amplitude ($a=1/48$)} vs.\ Fig.\ 2 quoted slopes:
    classical $-0.507$ vs.\ $-0.50$ (\emph{match}), LIS $-0.774$ vs.\ $-0.76$
    (\emph{match}), EIS $-0.866$ vs.\ $-0.95$ (\emph{partial}; shallower than
    paper by 0.084). The EIS gap is quantitatively consistent with the paper's
    own reported finite-$N_{\rm shot}$ bias, but we did not run the
    $N_{\rm shot}=1000$ or $N_{\rm shot}=10^4$ sweeps that would definitively
    close the gap.
    \item \textbf{No comparison against canonical QFT-based QAE baseline was
    made} (paper App.\ A). We did not implement a QPE simulator, so the
    paper's central pitch --- ``MLE-QAE achieves near-Heisenberg without
    the QFT'' --- is only \emph{indirectly} validated: we confirmed the
    near-Heisenberg part, but did not head-to-head compare against QFT-QAE
    at matched $N_q$ on the same target amplitude. A fuller replication
    would include a Qiskit-based QPE-QAE baseline sweep.
    \item \textbf{Heisenberg-scaling claim: partially quantitative.} EIS shows
    an asymptotic \emph{trend} toward Heisenberg (slope more negative than
    $-3/4$, RMSE saturating CRB), but at $N_q\leq 10^5$ the fitted slope is
    $-0.866$, not $-1.0$. The paper claims the exponent ``approaches'' $-1$
    and provides Fig.\ 2 as evidence at slope $-0.95$; our lower-shot run
    lands at $-0.866$. So the qualitative claim (Heisenberg scaling) holds,
    but the quantitative slope match is 0.084 off, which we attribute to
    finite-shot bias but did not empirically confirm by re-running at higher
    $N_{\rm shot}$.
    \item \textbf{App.\ A QPE-vs-EIS} is not reproduced. Same reason as above.
    \item \textbf{Paper omits RNG seeds and exact per-point trial count}
    (``1000 times''). We used 200 trials (100 for EIS $M\geq 9$), which is
    already sufficient to expose the slope trends; the paper's stated
    uncertainty on slopes is $\pm 0.1$, so our 0.084 EIS gap is within that
    envelope.
\end{itemize}

\section{Verdict}

\textbf{REPLICATED} (headline exercised): the paper's three-slope Fig.\ 2
result --- $N_q^{-1/2}$, $N_q^{-3/4}$, approaching $N_q^{-1}$ --- is
independently reproduced with our own implementation, at the paper's own
target amplitudes, with slopes within the paper's stated $\pm 0.1$ envelope.
The MLE is unbiased and saturates the Cram\'er--Rao bound. What is \emph{not}
independently replicated: the direct QPE-QAE comparison (App.\ A) and the
CNOT-count numbers (Sec.\ 4).

Coverage: 8/10. Agreement: 9/10.
