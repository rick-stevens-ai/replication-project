# Brief — BVBRC-121 Neisseria WGS-based species identification (Khoder et al. 2022)

**What:** Independent replication of Khoder et al. 2022 (PMID 36362240, doi 10.3390/ijms232113456) — a whole-genome sequencing (WGS) study that claims OrthoANI + isDDH + pangenome analysis reclassifies four Lebanese Neisseria clinical isolates (R19/R20/R21/R23), originally misidentified as *N. gonorrhoeae* by the API-NH biochemical panel, as *N. flavescens* (R19/R21/R23) and *N. mucosa* (R20), and further claims that NCBI's Neisseria genome database contains taxonomic errors.

**Why:** Complete-genome-announcement + phylogenomic-taxonomy paper on BV-BRC-121 replication track; tests whether a modern, fully-free workflow (skani ANI + UPGMA tree + LLM-judge) can independently reproduce a species-level identification claim from raw NCBI accessions with no manual database access.

**Result (one line):** PARTIAL — the "not *N. gonorrhoeae*" claim replicates cleanly for all four isolates (ANI 83.6–85.2% to FA1090, far below the 95% species cutoff), R20 = *N. mucosa* is confirmed (96.06% to a mucosa RefSeq reference), and the "NCBI contains misclassifications" claim is independently corroborated (deposited "N. sicca VK64" is 96.83% ANI to *N. macacae*); but the specific *N. flavescens* label for R19/R21/R23 does NOT survive a strict 95% ANI test — R19 is 98.0% to *N. subflava*/perflava references vs 94.1% to a *N. flavescens* type-strain, and the R19–R23 and R21–R23 pairwise ANIs sit at 94.32% / 94.58%, both below the paper's own stated species boundary. The paper's *overarching* claim of taxonomic ambiguity in the flavescens/perflava/subflava/mucosa complex is therefore strongly reinforced by our independent rerun.
