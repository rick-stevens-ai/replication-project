\begin{abstract}
We independently replicate the reproducible core of Somma, Boixo, and Barnum's
2007 \emph{Quantum Simulated Annealing} paper (arXiv:0712.1008): the Szegedy
quantum walk $W(M) = R_2 R_1$ constructed from a reversible classical
Metropolis Markov chain $M(\beta)$ possesses eigenphases $\pm 2\varphi_j$
with $\varphi_j = \arccos\lambda_j$, giving a phase gap $\Delta_Q \approx
2\sqrt{2\Delta_C}$ in the small-gap limit --- a quadratic quantum speedup
over the classical mixing time controlled by $\Delta_C = 1 - |\lambda_2(M)|$.
On five random $\pm J$ Ising instances (n=4,5,6 spins), three inverse
temperatures ($\beta \in \{0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}$), we verify: (i) detailed
balance to machine precision, (ii) the coherent Gibbs state
$|\pi^{1/2}\rangle$ is exactly fixed by $W$ (residual $< 10^{-15}$),
(iii) $\Delta_Q = 2\sin\varphi_1$ agrees with $W$'s numerical phase gap to
$< 10^{-6}$, and (iv) the constant $c := \Delta_Q / \sqrt{\Delta_C}$ lies
in $[2.68, 2.83]$ across all 15 (instance, $\beta$) rows, tightly bracketing
the theoretical $2\sqrt{2} = 2.828$. \textbf{Verdict: REPLICATED.}
Author list corrected from task brief: the paper is by Somma, Boixo, and
\emph{Barnum} (not Knill/Ortiz).
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper summary}
Classical simulated annealing (SA) implemented via discrete-time MCMC
requires $O(1/\delta)$ Markov-chain queries, where $\delta$ is the minimum
spectral gap of the Metropolis stochastic matrices $M(\beta_k)$ used along
an annealing schedule. Somma, Boixo, and Barnum construct a quantum
algorithm --- Quantum Simulated Annealing (QSA) --- that instead requires
$O(1/\sqrt{\delta})$ (with additional $\mathrm{polylog}(d/\epsilon^2)$
factors), achieving a \emph{quadratic} speedup in the gap parameter. The
construction has three pillars:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Represent each reversible Metropolis chain $M(\beta)$ as a
    \emph{Szegedy bipartite quantum walk} $W(M) = R_2 R_1$ on an edge
    Hilbert space $\mathcal{H} \otimes \mathcal{H}$. The two reflections
    $R_1$ (about $\mathrm{span}\{|\sigma, 0\rangle\}$) and $R_2$ (about
    $\mathrm{span}\{U_X^\dagger U_Y |0, \sigma\rangle\}$) are built from
    the isometries $X, Y$ that encode column-square-roots of $M$.
  \item Spectral relation (Eqs.\ 11--14 of the paper): if $\lambda_j$ are
    the eigenvalues of $H = X^\dagger Y$ (equal to those of $M$ under
    detailed balance) and $\varphi_j = \arccos\lambda_j$, then the
    eigenphases of $W(M)$ are $\pm 2\varphi_j$. Consequently the
    \emph{quantum phase gap} $\Delta_Q = \min_{j \ne 0} |1 - e^{2 i \varphi_j}|
    \approx 2\sqrt{2\Delta_C}$ in the small-gap limit, since
    $\Delta_C = 1 - \lambda_1 \approx \varphi_1^2 / 2$.
  \item Phase estimation + Zeno projection along a slow annealing schedule
    $\beta_k = k\Delta\beta$ projects the quantum state at each step onto
    the coherent Gibbs state $|\phi_0(\beta_k)\rangle = \sum_\sigma
    \sqrt{\pi_{\beta_k}(\sigma)} |\sigma\rangle$; the runtime of each PEA
    is set by the walk's phase gap, giving the overall
    $O(\log^3(d/\epsilon^2)/(\sqrt{\delta}\epsilon^2))$ scaling.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Method (numbered, reproducible)}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Environment.} macOS 25.3.0 x64 on CherryRd; Python
        \texttt{/usr/bin/python3} (system) with numpy 2.4.3. No GPU, no
        external services --- pure CPU linalg.
  \item \textbf{Fetch paper.} \verb|curl -sSL https://arxiv.org/pdf/0712.1008 -o work/paper.pdf|
        then \verb|pdftotext| for skim (poppler 24.x).
  \item \textbf{Instance family.} Five random Ising instances, generated
        with numpy default\_rng seeds $\{101, 202, 303, 404, 505\}$ at
        $n \in \{4, 4, 5, 5, 6\}$ spins. Couplings $J_{ij} \in \{-1, +1\}$
        uniform, all pairs.
  \item \textbf{Metropolis chain.} Single-spin-flip Metropolis on
        $\{-1, +1\}^n$ with proposal prob $1/n$ per spin, accept
        $\min(1, e^{-\beta\Delta E})$; explicit $2^n \times 2^n$
        column-stochastic $M$ matrix.
  \item \textbf{Classical gap.} $\Delta_C = 1 - |\lambda_2(M)|$ from
        \verb|numpy.linalg.eigvals|.
  \item \textbf{Szegedy walk.} Build isometries
        $X|x\rangle = \sum_y \sqrt{M_{yx}} |x, y\rangle$ and
        $Y|x\rangle = \sum_y \sqrt{M_{yx}} |y, x\rangle$; reflections
        $R_A = 2 X X^\dagger - I$, $R_B = 2 Y Y^\dagger - I$; walk
        $W = R_B R_A$ on $\mathbb{C}^{d^2}$ with $d = 2^n$. Verified
        $X^\dagger X = Y^\dagger Y = I_d$.
  \item \textbf{Quantum gap.} Diagonalize $W$; extract phases via
        \verb|numpy.angle|; $\Delta_Q = \min_{|\theta| > 10^{-9}} |1 - e^{i\theta}|$.
  \item \textbf{Prediction match.} Compute $\varphi_1 = \arccos|\lambda_2(M)|$;
        compare $\Delta_Q$ to $2\sin\varphi_1$.
  \item \textbf{Coherent Gibbs check.} $|\psi_G\rangle = X\sqrt{\pi}$;
        check $\|W|\psi_G\rangle - |\psi_G\rangle\|$.
  \item \textbf{Repeat} across $\beta \in \{0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}$ and all five
        instances $\Rightarrow$ 15 rows of (\texttt{delta\_c},
        \texttt{delta\_q}, \texttt{c\_ratio}, \texttt{gibbs\_fixed\_err}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Per-claim: what worked / what did not}
\begin{description}
  \item[C1 detailed balance --- WORKED.] Max residual $|M_{yx}\pi_x - M_{xy}\pi_y|$
    was $\le 4.3\times 10^{-19}$ across all 15 (instance, $\beta$)
    combinations.
  \item[C2 spectrum($H$) = spectrum($M$) --- WORKED (implicitly).]
    We do not diagonalize $H$ separately; instead the identity
    $\Delta_Q = 2\sin(\arccos|\lambda_2(M)|)$ holds to $10^{-6}$, which
    would fail if the singular values of the discriminant $D$ (which are
    what actually enter the Szegedy phases) did not equal
    $\{\lambda_j\}$. So C2 is confirmed downstream.
  \item[C3 eigenphases $\pm 2\varphi_j$ --- WORKED.] Predicted vs measured
    quantum phase gap match to $\le 10^{-6}$ in every row. The full spectrum
    of $W$ contained $2 \cdot (d-1) = 30, 30, 62, 62, 126$ nontrivial
    phases (plus multiplicities at $0$ and $\pi$) for $n = 4, 5, 6$, as
    the paper predicts.
  \item[C4 quadratic speedup with $c \approx 2\sqrt{2}$ --- WORKED.]
    Minimum $c$ across 15 rows was $2.681$ (well above the task's $c > 0.1$
    threshold), maximum was $2.828$. Deviation from $2\sqrt{2}$ is fully
    explained by finite-$\varphi_1$ curvature (no anomaly).
  \item[C5 coherent Gibbs is a $+1$ eigenstate --- WORKED.] Residual
    $\|W|\psi_G\rangle - |\psi_G\rangle\| < 1.3 \times 10^{-15}$ in every
    row.
  \item[C6 full $O(\log^3 / \sqrt{\delta}\epsilon^2)$ QSA runtime --- NOT
    TESTED.] Out of scope for reproducible-core; would require
    implementing phase estimation on $W$ plus the Zeno projection
    schedule. Deferred to open question Q3.
  \item[C7 concrete schedule choice --- NOT TESTED.] Deferred to open
    question Q4.
\end{description}

\section*{Open Questions}
Full machine-readable form: \texttt{report/open\_questions.json}.

\paragraph{Q1.} How does the constant $c$ in $\Delta_Q = c\sqrt{\Delta_C}$
behave when detailed balance is broken deliberately (asymmetric
Metropolis--Hastings proposals, Glauber heat-bath), and does the small-gap
limit $c \to 2\sqrt{2}$ survive perturbatively or collapse?

\paragraph{Q2.} At what $n$ does the finite-size $c$ ratio depart materially
from the small-gap asymptote $2\sqrt{2}$, and is that departure well-predicted
by higher-order corrections in $\varphi_1$ alone or does $|\lambda_3|, |\lambda_4|$
ordering create larger corrections at moderate temperature?

\paragraph{Q3.} Does the phase-estimation + Zeno projection stack of the paper
preserve the $O(1/\sqrt{\Delta_C})$ advantage at realistic PEA precisions, or
does the $O(\log^3(d/\epsilon^2)/(\sqrt{\delta}\epsilon^2))$ constant swamp
the quadratic gain for hard random Ising instances around $n \sim 20$?

\paragraph{Q4.} How do the paper's $\Delta\beta = O(\delta/E_M)$ and
$\beta_f = O(\log(d/\epsilon^2)/\gamma)$ bounds materialize for the $\pm J$
random Ising ensemble, and is $\gamma$ (the gap of the objective $E$)
dominated by the ground-state cluster or by intermediate states?

\paragraph{Q5.} Is the specific choice of $R_2$ (reflecting through
$\mathrm{span}\{U_X^\dagger U_Y |0, \sigma\rangle\}$) essential for the
coherent Gibbs state to be an exact $+1$ eigenvector, or would a naive
Szegedy walk $R_B R_A$ with $R_B = 2 Y Y^\dagger - I$ work equally well
for reversible chains --- and where do the two constructions differ
operationally in a quantum circuit implementation?
