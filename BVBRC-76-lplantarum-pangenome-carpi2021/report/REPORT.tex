\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Verdict: PARTIAL REPLICATION.} Independent rerun on 124 of the paper's 127
\emph{L.\ plantarum} complete RefSeq genomes (delta $-3$, consistent with NCBI curation drift)
using a version-drifted but faithful Prokka $+$ Roary reimplementation reproduces
(i) total pan-genome size within 2.3\% (16{,}522 vs 16{,}911),
(ii) combined core $+$ soft-core within 2.1\% (1{,}888 vs 1{,}850),
(iii) shell and cloud counts within $\sim$1--3\%,
(iv) the qualitative ``open pan-genome'' conclusion via an independently
computed Heaps'-Law exponent $\gamma = 0.385 < 1$, and
(v) the ``new genes still added past the 100-genome mark'' claim (mean 43.8
new genes at step 100; 44.4 at step 124).
The probiotic-marker-gene fraction claim (C4) could not be re-evaluated because
Wiley's supplementary Table S5 sits behind a Cloudflare wall, hence PARTIAL rather than full REPLICATED.
Three independent LLM judges (Argo gpt-5.2, gpt-5.4, gemini-2.5-pro) unanimously
scored PARTIAL (mean coverage 0.81, mean agreement 0.85, mean confidence 0.85).
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Citation.} Carpi FM, Coman MM, Silvi S, Picciolini M, Verdenelli MC, Napolioni V.
        \emph{J Appl Microbiol} 132(1):592--604 (2022, online 2021).
        DOI \href{https://doi.org/10.1111/jam.15199}{10.1111/jam.15199}. PMID 34216519. PMC9290807. Open access (CC-BY).
  \item \textbf{Aim.} Refine taxonomy and functional characterization of \emph{Lactiplantibacillus plantarum}
        (formerly \emph{Lactobacillus plantarum}) via pan-genome analysis of every complete public genome,
        with attention to probiotic potential.
  \item \textbf{Cohort.} Of 541 \emph{L.\ plantarum} NCBI Assembly entries available July 2020,
        130 were complete; three (CNEI-KCA5, KLDS1.0391, SN13T) were dropped for missing RefSeq annotation,
        leaving $N = 127$ for analysis.
  \item \textbf{Pipeline.} Prokka v1.14.5 (annotation) $\to$ OrthoFinder v2.4.0 (phylogeny sanity)
        $\to$ FastANI v1.31 (ANI sanity) $\to$ Roary v3.11.2 (pan-genome)
        $\to$ Parsnp v1.5.3 (core-SNP phylogeny). Roary thresholds:
        core 99--100\%, soft-core 95--99\%, shell 15--95\%, cloud 0--15\% of strains.
  \item \textbf{Headline numbers.} Pan-genome $=$ 16{,}911 gene families
        $=$ 1{,}436 core $+$ 414 soft-core $+$ 1{,}858 shell $+$ 13{,}203 cloud.
        Open pan-genome (new genes added after 100 strains). $\approx$70\% of the 75 probiotic marker genes fall in core/soft-core.
\end{itemize}

\section{Method}

\subsection{Data retrieval}
NCBI Datasets v2 REST query (no auth) for \emph{Lactiplantibacillus plantarum},
\texttt{assembly\_level=complete\_genome}, \texttt{exclude\_atypical=true}, page size 500.
Paged through 865 total complete-genome hits (2026-07-03), filtered
\texttt{release\_date $\le$ 2020-07-31} $\to$ 251 hits (125 GCA $+$ 126 GCF),
deduped by GCF accession $\to$ 125 assemblies, deduped by strain $\to$ 124 unique strains.
Downloaded 399\,MB via \texttt{datasets download genome accession \dots}.

\subsection{Annotation}
Fresh conda env \texttt{bvbrc76} on \texttt{uicgpu} (8$\times$A100, 255 cores, 2\,TB RAM):
Prokka 1.14.6 (paper: 1.14.5), Roary 3.13.0 (paper: 3.11.2), Panaroo 1.8.0, BLAST$+$, MAFFT.
Per-genome Prokka run with \texttt{--fast}, parallelized with \texttt{xargs -P 24};
wall time $\approx$20\,min for all 124 genomes.

\subsection{Pan-genome (Roary)}
\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\ttfamily\small,frame=single]
roary -e --mafft -p 48 -f <outdir> -i 95 -cd 99 gffs/*.gff
\end{lstlisting}
BLASTP identity 95\%, core cutoff 99\% of strains (same as paper).
Wall time: $\sim$15\,min for BLAST all-vs-all; MCL clustering continued in background.

\subsection{Rarefaction and Heaps' Law}
Roary emits \texttt{number\_of\_genes\_in\_pan\_genome.Rtab},
\texttt{number\_of\_conserved\_genes.Rtab},
\texttt{number\_of\_new\_genes.Rtab} (10 permutations $\times$ 124 genomes).
Heaps' Law fit by least squares: $\log \text{pan}(N) = \log \kappa + \gamma \log N$
over genomes 10--124. $\gamma < 1 \Rightarrow$ open.
Fitted $\gamma = 0.3854$, $\kappa = 2583.22$.

\subsection{LLM-judge scoring}
Three independent judges (temperature 0.0 where allowed) via Argo proxy
(\texttt{http://127.0.0.1:44497/v1}, free endpoint):
\texttt{argo:gpt-5.2} $\to$ PARTIAL (cov 0.75, agr 0.78, conf 0.72);
\texttt{argo:gpt-5.4} $\to$ PARTIAL (cov 0.82, agr 0.86, conf 0.88);
\texttt{argo:gemini-2.5-pro} $\to$ PARTIAL (cov 0.85, agr 0.90, conf 0.95).
Majority: PARTIAL (3/3). Argo Anthropic (opus-4.7 / opus-4.8) returned repeated 502 gateway errors;
substituted gpt-5.4 and gemini-2.5-pro to keep three independent families.

\section{Genuine Critique}

This section is the honest audit of \emph{what did and did not replicate}, and where our
own execution introduces uncertainty distinct from the paper's science.

\subsection{Strengths of the paper that survive independent scrutiny}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The headline finding is real.} The claim that \emph{L.\ plantarum} has an
        open pan-genome of $\sim$16k gene families dominated by a large cloud fraction
        is quantitatively reproduced on an independently assembled cohort with a
        completely re-executed toolchain. This is not a book-keeping artifact.
  \item \textbf{Roary threshold choices are defensible.} The 99/95/15 four-way partition
        is Roary's canonical scheme, and the total pan-genome size --- which is
        \emph{insensitive} to that cutoff --- lands within 2.3\%. The specific choice
        of 99\% (rather than 100\%) for the core threshold matters little for the joint
        core+soft-core budget.
  \item \textbf{Data is fully re-derivable.} 124 of 127 genomes were recovered from
        NCBI Datasets with a single filtered REST query and one \texttt{datasets download}.
        The paper's cohort is genuinely public and reproducible six years later.
  \item \textbf{Openness conclusion is robust.} $\gamma = 0.385$ is far enough below 1 that
        cohort or version drift is not going to flip the qualitative conclusion.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Weaknesses / gaps we cannot silently paper over}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Internal core/soft-core split reshuffled by $-20.3\%$ in soft-core.}
        This is threshold-boundary sensitivity from the $N=124$ vs $N=127$ cohort
        delta, not a scientific disagreement --- but it \emph{does} mean the paper's
        exact 1{,}436/414 split should not be treated as a hard reproducibility target
        the way total or joint counts are. Any downstream analysis (e.g.\ ``genes in
        soft-core'') inherits this instability.
  \item \textbf{PMG claim (C4) untested.} We could not independently verify that
        $\sim$70\% of the 75 probiotic-marker genes fall in core/soft-core, because
        Wiley Cloudflare-blocks the supplementary Table S5 ZIP. The paper claims
        open access; the supplements effectively are not. This is a real gap: the
        70\% number is the paper's central functional-annotation claim, and we
        cannot corroborate it from public artifacts.
  \item \textbf{Version drift.} Prokka 1.14.6 (us) vs 1.14.5 (paper), Roary 3.13.0 vs 3.11.2.
        Minor. But BLAST database versions and Prokka's HMM library have moved
        forward six years, which quietly changes annotation confidence for
        borderline ORFs and thus feeds into cluster boundaries.
  \item \textbf{Only headline claims tested.} Parsnp core-SNP phylogeny (Fig 4),
        OrthoFinder/FastANI sanity (Figs 1, 2), plasmid/prophage/CRISPR/bacteriocin
        counts (their downstream tool zoo) all remain untested. The paper's
        functional-genomics narrative rests on these; we replicated only the
        pan-genome skeleton.
  \item \textbf{Single-run permutation.} We used Roary's default 10-permutation
        rarefaction; the paper does the same, but neither of us bootstraps
        the Heaps' fit --- so the confidence interval on $\gamma$ is unreported.
        $\gamma = 0.385$ is comfortably $< 1$, but strictly we cannot quote a CI.
  \item \textbf{No cross-tool sanity check.} Panaroo is installed in the env but not
        run. A Panaroo pass at the same 95\% identity would test whether the
        16.5k pan-genome size is Roary-specific or a real property of the data
        (Panaroo tends to give smaller pan-genomes by aggressively de-noising
        annotation errors). This is the single biggest missing sanity check.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Where we might be wrong}
\begin{itemize}
  \item If NCBI has retroactively suppressed genomes for reasons \emph{correlated
        with pan-genome content} (e.g.\ all suppressed genomes were misclassified
        \emph{L.\ pentosus}), our 124 could be systematically less diverse than the
        paper's 127, biasing $\gamma$ downward. We have no way to check this
        without the paper's original accession list (not published in a
        machine-readable form in the main text).
  \item Prokka \texttt{--fast} mode skips some HMM libraries. Full Prokka would
        annotate more borderline ORFs and could add $\sim$1--3\% to total gene
        counts. This trades runtime for completeness; it does not affect the
        qualitative openness result.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Net assessment}
The paper's central computational result --- a $\sim$16k-gene open pan-genome of
\emph{L.\ plantarum} with a $\sim$1.4--1.6k core, dominated by cloud-genome
heterogeneity --- is a genuine, independently reproducible finding on real
public data with a standard FOSS toolchain. The exact numbers reproduce to
within a few percent when cohort and version drift are accounted for. The
one un-testable claim (PMG core fraction) is the paper's most functionally
interesting number, so ``PARTIAL'' is the honest verdict: not because the
paper is wrong, but because we cannot from public artifacts fully corroborate it.

\section{Verdict}

\textbf{PARTIAL REPLICATION.} Total pan-genome and joint core$+$soft-core
within 2--3\%; openness $\gamma = 0.385$; PMG fraction untested (Wiley
Cloudflare blocks supplementary Table S5). 3/3 LLM judges: PARTIAL.
