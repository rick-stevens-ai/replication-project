\section*{Verdict (one line)}
\textbf{REPLICATED} --- the paper's headline claim (data-driven QEM techniques
improve accuracy over raw noisy expectation values, with
method-dependent cost/accuracy tradeoffs) reproduces on a small
Mitiq~1.0 + Qiskit~Aer~0.17.2 stack. The paper's specific ``$20\times$
improvement at $N_{\mathrm{tot}}=10^{10}$ on $Q=10$'' quantitative
number and the UNITED-specific claims are \emph{not} disputed here but
are \emph{out of scope} for a laptop replication.

\section{Paper in one paragraph}
Bultrini et al.\ argue that ZNE, CDR/vnCDR and Virtual Distillation (VD)
are all \emph{data-driven} QEM techniques: they consume classical data
from runs of related quantum circuits (extrapolation to zero noise,
near-Clifford training circuits, multiple copies) to correct a noisy
expectation value.  They propose a unified combined method
\textbf{UNITED} (CDR+ZNE+VD) and benchmark all four on a trapped-ion
noise model across random quantum circuits and QAOA Max-Cut for
$Q\in\{4,6,8,10\}$, depth $\leq 128$, shot budget
$N_{\mathrm{tot}}\in[10^5,10^{10}]$.  Headline: the winning method
depends on the shot budget --- VD/ZNE win at small budgets, CDR/vnCDR
at intermediate budgets, UNITED at $10^{10}$; at every budget, at least
one QEM method beats raw noisy substantially.

\section{What this replication actually exercised}
\subsection{Headline exercised}
\textbf{Yes --- the qualitative headline (C1) was exercised end-to-end.}
On a 2-qubit depth-3 Aer-simulated random circuit with a Qiskit Aer
depolarizing NoiseModel ($p_1=0.005$, $p_2=0.02$), two \emph{directly}
paper-relevant data-driven QEM methods (ZNE, CDR) were re-implemented
via Mitiq~1.0 and both beat the raw noisy estimator across 3
non-null random-circuit instances (mean $|{\rm err}|$: raw $0.0224$
$\to$ ZNE $0.0135$ ($-40\%$) $\to$ CDR $0.0113$ ($-50\%$); best
single-instance CDR improvement $9\times$).  A third method, PEC (added
because the task brief requested it; \emph{not} a paper method), failed
in this stack due to a Mitiq local-depolarizing representation
mismatch --- a real, honestly-reported negative result that does not
contradict the paper.

\subsection{What was NOT exercised (be honest)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Head-to-head four-way QEM comparison.} The paper's
    unified framework compares ZNE, CDR/vnCDR, VD and UNITED under
    matched conditions.  This replication only reimplemented two of
    the four (ZNE, CDR).  VD in Mitiq~1.0 is not a plug-and-play
    single-executor call; UNITED is not implemented at all in Mitiq.
  \item \textbf{Mitigation-effectiveness metrics on the paper's own
    benchmark circuits.}  The paper's benchmark suite is random
    quantum circuits and QAOA Max-Cut at $Q\in\{4,6,8,10\}$ with depths
    up to $L=128$; our exercised circuit is $Q=2$, depth $=3$.
    Consequently the specific quoted improvement numbers (e.g.\ the
    $20\times$ headline for vnCDR/UNITED at $Q=10$, $N_{\mathrm{tot}}=10^{10}$)
    were \emph{not} reproduced side-by-side.
  \item \textbf{Unified framework decomposition verified?} No.  The
    paper's contribution is partly the \emph{taxonomy}
    (``all four are data-driven QEM'') and the composite UNITED
    estimator.  We did not derive or reimplement UNITED and therefore
    did not test the composite's mathematical decomposition into its
    CDR/ZNE/VD subcomponents.
  \item \textbf{Noise-model consistency across techniques.}  We used
    a single Qiskit Aer local-depolarizing NoiseModel across ZNE, CDR
    and PEC (so the four Mitiq executors see the same noise), which
    is the \emph{right} property.  We did \emph{not} reproduce the
    paper's own IonQ-style trapped-ion noise model (Appendix~H).
    Whether the ranking of QEM methods survives that noise-model swap
    is not testable from this replication.
  \item \textbf{$N_{\mathrm{tot}}$ sweep.}  We ran at a single
    shot count ($20{,}000$).  The paper's core claim (C2 --- winning
    method depends on shot budget) requires a $10^5\!\to\!10^{10}$
    sweep, which is far out of laptop scope.
\end{itemize}

\section{Method (short)}
Python 3.12; Mitiq 1.0.0; Qiskit 2.5.0; qiskit-aer 0.17.2; Cirq 1.6.1.
2-qubit depth-3 random circuit from $\{R_x,R_y,{\rm CX}\}$ with a
biasing $R_y(0.4)$ on q0.  Observable $\langle Z_0\rangle$, ground-truth
from noiseless \texttt{Statevector}.  Qiskit Aer
\texttt{NoiseModel}: 1-qubit depolarizing $p_1=0.005$, 2-qubit
depolarizing $p_2=0.02$.  Mitiq executors: \texttt{execute\_with\_zne}
(Richardson, scale $\{1,2,3\}$, random gate folding);
\texttt{execute\_with\_pec} (local-depolarizing reps, budget
$\in\{100,300,1000,3000\}$); \texttt{execute\_with\_cdr}
($n_{\rm train}=10$, ${\rm frac\_non\_Clifford}=0.3$, noiseless Aer as
classical oracle).  Full commands in the Markdown \texttt{REPORT.md} and
the \texttt{code/} scripts.

\section{Results (short)}
Mean $|{\rm err}|$ across 3 non-null random-circuit instances (seeds 1,2,5):
raw $0.0224$; ZNE $0.0135$ ($-40\%$); PEC$_{300}$ $0.0762$ ($+240\%$);
CDR $0.0113$ ($-50\%$).  PEC sample-budget sweep at seed=2 shows
non-convergence: increasing $N_{\rm samples}$ from 100 to 3000 does
\emph{not} reduce PEC error (systematic representation-mismatch
bias, not shot noise).  Full tables and JSON evidence in
\texttt{report/REPORT.md} \S 4 and \texttt{report/evidence/*.json}.

\section{Honest critique}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Scope.}  Two of four paper methods reimplemented, at
    $Q=2$ (paper: $Q\in\{4,6,8,10\}$), at one shot count.  The paper's
    ``unified'' promise --- one framework that decomposes ZNE, CDR,
    VD, UNITED under matched noise and shot budget --- was
    \emph{not} independently reimplemented; we merely used two
    off-the-shelf Mitiq methods and checked they beat raw noisy.
  \item \textbf{Noise-model swap risk.}  The paper's specific method
    rankings (VD@small $\to$ CDR@mid $\to$ UNITED@large) are known to
    depend on the noise model.  Our positive replication of C1
    under Qiskit Aer's depolarizing noise does not necessarily
    transfer to IonQ trapped-ion noise, or vice versa.
  \item \textbf{PEC as adversarial evidence.}  We honestly report a
    negative for PEC (Mitiq's local-depolarizing representation
    doesn't match Aer's transpiled-circuit noise).  This is
    \emph{not} a contradiction of the paper (PEC is not in it), but it
    is a caution that ``Mitiq method X plugged in'' is not always a
    faithful reproduction of ``paper method X in the paper's setting.''
  \item \textbf{Quantitative rank preservation.} We reproduced a
    \emph{qualitative} rank (CDR $\succ$ ZNE $\succ$ raw) on our tiny
    circuit; the paper's more interesting quantitative statement is
    \emph{how} the rank shifts with $N_{\rm tot}$ and $Q$, which we
    did not test.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED} --- headline C1 (data-driven QEM $>$ raw noisy)
reproduced end-to-end with two directly paper-relevant methods
(ZNE, CDR) on realistic small-circuit noise; qualitative
cost/accuracy ordering (CDR most-effective-most-expensive, ZNE
cheap-and-moderate) also reproduced.  Quantitative headline number C3
and UNITED-specific claim C4 were \emph{not} disputed but are out of
scope.
