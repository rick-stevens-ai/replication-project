\section{Target}
Malashevich, Souza, Coh \& Vanderbilt, \emph{Theory of orbital magnetoelectric
response}, New J. Phys. \textbf{12}, 053032 (2010); arXiv:1002.0300.
\textbf{Headline claim:} the linear orbital magnetoelectric (ME) susceptibility
$\alpha_{da}=\partial M_a/\partial E_d$ of a 3D tight-binding \emph{ordinary} ME
insulator, evaluated under periodic boundary conditions (PBC) via the k-space
Berry-phase / Chern--Simons ``axion'' orbital magnetoelectric polarizability
(OMP), agrees with bounded-sample (open-boundary, finite-field) calculations.

\section{Model (Appendix A, table A1)}
Spinless simple-cubic lattice, primitive cell doubled to $2\times2\times2=8$
sites/cell:
\[
H_0=\sum_i E_i\,c_i^\dagger c_i+\sum_{\langle ij\rangle} e^{i\phi_{j\to i}}\,c_i^\dagger c_j,\qquad |t|=1,
\]
with on-site energies $E_i=\{-6.5,0.9,1.4,1.2,-6.0,1.5,0.8,1.2\}$ and the complex
NN hopping phases of table A1; one $x$-phase is the scanned parameter
$\phi\in[0,2\pi]$. The two lowest bands are occupied; TR and inversion are broken
(required, else $\alpha\equiv0$). The position operator $r$ is diagonal in the TB basis.

\section{Methods implemented from scratch}
\paragraph{(A) Bounded sample (gauge-free).} Open-BC cubes of $L\times L\times L$
cells; apply $H=H_0+\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf r$; orbital magnetization
$M_z=-\tfrac{1}{2V}\,\mathrm{Tr}[P_{\rm occ}(x v_y - y v_x)]$ with $v=i[H,r]$;
$\alpha_{zz}=\partial M_z/\partial E_z$ by central finite differences
($E_z=\pm0.01$), extrapolated in $1/L$.
\paragraph{(B) k-space Chern--Simons axion OMP, eq.~(47a).}
\[
\theta_{\rm CS}=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\!\int\! d^3k\;\epsilon_{ijk}\,
\mathrm{tr}\!\Big[A_i\partial_j A_k-\tfrac{2i}{3}A_iA_jA_k\Big],
\]
with the non-Abelian Berry connection $A_{mn,b}=i\langle u_m|\partial_b u_n\rangle$
over the two occupied bands, in a smooth gauge built by projecting delta trial
orbitals at the two lowest-on-site sites (paper's prescription).
$\alpha^{\rm CS}_{\rm iso}=\theta_{\rm CS}\,e^2/(2\pi hc)$.

The Berry/orbital machinery ($v=i[H,r]$, occupied-projector traces, eigenbasis
Berry connections) was adapted from the \textbf{gobel2024} Kubo/Berry kernel
(\texttt{shared-kernels-cache/gobel2024\_sd\_skyrmion\_kubo\_Lz\_kernel.py}).

\section{Results}
Natural units $e=\hbar=c=1$ ($h=2\pi$), $\phi=0.5\pi$ unless swept.
\begin{itemize}
\item Model is insulating: min direct gap (valence$|$conduction) $=1.64$.
\item Bounded-sample $\alpha_{zz}$ (per $L$) and k-space $\alpha^{\rm CS}_{\rm iso}$
both fall at the numerical noise floor, $\sim10^{-3}$--$10^{-4}$, for the
accessible resolution.
\end{itemize}

\section{Assessment}
Both formalisms were built from scratch and run in $<12$\,s. Both return
$\alpha$ values sitting at the numerical noise floor for the accessible
resolution (open clusters $L\le5$; k-grid $8^3$). At this resolution the true
(small) $\alpha$ signal is \emph{not resolved above numerical noise}, and the two
estimators do not track each other across $\phi$ (correlation $\approx-0.07$).
Demonstrating the paper-level PBC-vs-bounded agreement (differences $\sim10^{-7}
e^2/\hbar c$) requires the paper's $L=4$--$7$ with $1/L,1/L^2,1/L^3$
extrapolation and an $80^3$ mesh, plus a globally smooth (max-localized-Wannier)
gauge for the gauge-sensitive Chern--Simons 3-form. Those precision ingredients
were outside the fast-budget scope.

\paragraph{Verdict: PARTIAL.} Correct model, correct formulas, both methods
executing on an insulating spectrum; quantitative cross-method agreement not
resolved at coarse resolution. Coverage 6/10, Agreement 3/10.
