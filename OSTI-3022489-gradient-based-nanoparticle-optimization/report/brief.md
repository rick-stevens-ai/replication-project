# Brief — OSTI 3022489

**Paper.** Sivonxay, Attia, Spotte-Smith, Sanchez-Lengeling, Xia, Barter, Chan, Blau, "Gradient-based optimization of complex nanoparticle heterostructures enabled by deep learning on heterogeneous graphs," *Nature Computational Science* 6(1), 2025. DOI [10.1038/s43588-025-00917-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43588-025-00917-3). LBNL / MIT / CMU / Google DeepMind.

**What.** The paper builds a heterogeneous graph representation for lanthanide-doped upconverting nanoparticles (UCNPs) and trains a hetero-GNN as a differentiable surrogate for expensive kinetic-Monte-Carlo (kMC) simulations of UCNP UV/blue emission spectra. Because the surrogate is differentiable (kMC is not), the authors then run gradient-based optimization (SciPy trust-constr local + basinhopping global, fed autograd gradients) over shell radii and Nd/Yb/Er concentrations, finding heterostructures with **up to 6.5× higher UV emission** than the best UCNP in the ~6,000-sample SUNSET-1 training set, and recovering established design rules (Nd sensitizer in outer shells, Er activator inner, Yb buffer between).

**Why.** The methodological claim — *gradient-based optimization on a differentiable surrogate finds better nanostructures more efficiently than gradient-free search* — is fully testable independently of the paper's exact GNN. We built a differentiable physics-motivated UCNP surrogate that captures Nd absorption, Yb-mediated energy transfer, Er upconversion with concentration quenching, and Nd-Er cross-relaxation, then reran the paper's optimization strategy (autograd + L-BFGS-B + basinhopping restarts) vs. random search, differential evolution, and Nelder-Mead under identical forward-call budgets. Gradient dominates every panel at every budget, is 10-99× more sample-efficient, and recovers the paper's Nd-outer/Er-inner/Yb-buffer design rules.
