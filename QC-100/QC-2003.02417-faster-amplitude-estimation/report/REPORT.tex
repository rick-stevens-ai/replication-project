\begin{abstract}
Nakaji's \emph{Faster Amplitude Estimation} (FAE, arXiv:2003.02417) proposes a QPE-free amplitude-estimation algorithm with a proven $N_{\mathrm{orac}} = O(1/\epsilon)$ upper bound whose prefactor $\approx 4.1\times 10^{3}$ is $\sim 10^{2}\times$ tighter than the previous best rigorous bound (Grinko IQAE). We independently reimplemented Algorithm~1 verbatim on a 2-qubit statevector simulator (a $4\times 4$ analytic simulator equivalent to Qiskit's \texttt{StatevectorSimulator}) together with a Suzuki-MLAE baseline, and reran the paper's Fig.~3 sweep for $a \in \{0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4\}$ with 100 FAE trials per $\ell$. All four fitted log--log slopes lie in $[0.85, 1.26]$, consistent with the Heisenberg slope of~1 within statistical fluctuation; the stage-2 trigger $j_0$ decreases monotonically with $a$ (5, 4, 3, 3) exactly as reported; and the empirical prefactor sits $\sim 30\times$ below the proven upper bound, matching the paper's own observation that the bound is not tight in practice. \textbf{Verdict: REPLICATED.} The proof of Theorem~1 was not re-derived, and a head-to-head prefactor race against Grinko IQAE was \emph{not} run --- these are the principal caveats.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper summary}

Quantum amplitude estimation targets $a$ in
$A|0\rangle = a|\tilde{\Psi}_{1}\rangle|1\rangle + \sqrt{1-a^{2}}|\tilde{\Psi}_{0}\rangle|0\rangle$.
Canonical QAE (Brassard et al., QPE on the Grover-like operator $Q$) reaches Heisenberg $N_{\mathrm{orac}} = O(1/\epsilon)$ but needs deep controlled-$Q$ circuits ill-suited to NISQ. Nakaji's FAE (Algorithm~1) is QPE-free and built on:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Attenuating the amplitude via $X = A \otimes R$ with $R|0\rangle = \tfrac{1}{4}|1\rangle + \tfrac{\sqrt{15}}{4}|0\rangle$, giving $\sin\theta = a/4$ with $\theta \in [0, 0.252]$.
    \item Iteratively estimating $\cos(2(2^{k}+1)\theta)$ for $k = 1,\dots,\ell$, refining $[\theta_{\min}, \theta_{\max}]$ each round.
    \item A \textbf{two-stage} structure: stage~1 uses $\arccos$ of the measured cosine until $2^{n+1}\theta_{\max} \geq 3\pi/8$; stage~2 additionally measures $\cos(2(2^{k}+2^{j_{0}}+1)\theta)$ and reconstructs $\sin$ via a trig-addition identity to resolve the $\bmod\,\pi$ ambiguity.
    \item Chernoff-bound-driven shot counts $N_{\mathrm{shot}}^{1\mathrm{st}} = 1944\ln(2/\delta_{c})$, $N_{\mathrm{shot}}^{2\mathrm{nd}} = 972\ln(2/\delta_{c})$.
\end{enumerate}
Theorem~1 proves $N_{\mathrm{orac}} \leq (4.1\times 10^{3}/\epsilon)\ln(4\log_{2}(2\pi/3\epsilon)/\delta)$. Section~3 (Fig.~3) numerically verifies near-Heisenberg scaling with 1000 trials per $(a,\ell)$, reporting the 95th-percentile amplitude error.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Environment}
Python 3.14.6, \texttt{qiskit 2.5.0}, \texttt{qiskit-aer}, \texttt{numpy 2.5.0}, \texttt{scipy}, \texttt{matplotlib}. The actual runtime uses a purely analytic $4\times 4$ statevector simulator (the entire system fits in 2 qubits); correctness is verified to machine precision against the analytic identity $\sin^{2}((2m+1)\theta)$ in \S4.1.

\subsection{Implementation}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{code/oracle.py} --- builds $A$, $R$, $X = A \otimes R$, and $Q = X S_{0} X^{\dagger} S_{\mathrm{good}}$ (eq.~5). \texttt{exact\_prob\_good\_after\_Qm(a, m)} is the honest statevector probability of $|11\rangle$ after $Q^{m}|\Psi'\rangle$.
    \item \texttt{code/fae.py} --- Algorithm~1 verbatim: two stages, Chernoff intervals (eq.~8), extended \texttt{atan2} (eq.~9), integer $n_{j}$ update (eq.~25), $N_{\mathrm{shot}}^{1\mathrm{st}} = 1944\ln(2/\delta_{c})$, $N_{\mathrm{shot}}^{2\mathrm{nd}} = 972\ln(2/\delta_{c})$; \texttt{COS(m, N\_shot)} samples $\mathrm{Binomial}(N_{\mathrm{shot}}, p_{\mathrm{good}})$ and returns $c_{m} = 1 - 2N_{11}/N_{\mathrm{shot}}$.
    \item \texttt{code/mlae.py} --- Suzuki MLAE baseline: exponential schedule $m_{k} = 2^{k-1}$ for $k = 1\dots M$ plus $m_{0} = 0$, likelihood optimised by grid + Brent over $\theta \in [0, 0.4]$.
    \item \texttt{code/experiment.py} --- full sweep: $a \in \{0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4\}$, FAE at $\ell \in \{3,4,5,6,7\}$ with 100 trials, MLAE at $M \in \{4,5,6,7,8,9\}$ with $N_{\mathrm{shot}} = 100$ and 200 trials.
    \item \texttt{code/make\_plots.py} --- produces \texttt{report/evidence/fig3\_replication.png}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Prefactor sanity (C2, weak)}
For $a = 0.3$ (best fit, $R^{2} = 0.99$), $N_{\mathrm{orac}} \approx 88.9/\epsilon^{1.26}$. Extrapolating to $\epsilon = 10^{-4}$ gives $\approx 9.8\times 10^{6}$. Paper's proven upper bound at $\epsilon = 10^{-4}, \delta = 0.05$: $\approx 2.9\times 10^{8}$. Empirical is $\sim 30\times$ below the proven bound --- consistent with the paper.

\subsection{MLAE baseline (context, not a paper claim)}
MLAE slopes: 0.79, 3.89, 1.07, 0.79. The $a = 0.2$ outlier is the well-known bimodal-likelihood failure Suzuki-MLAE suffers near boundary $\theta$'s --- the very ambiguity FAE's two-stage design fixes. Where MLAE's fit is clean ($a=0.3$, slope 1.07), its prefactor is~24.6 vs FAE's~88.9: FAE's per-shot count $N_{\mathrm{shot}}^{1\mathrm{st}} = 1944\ln(200) \approx 10\,300$ is very conservative to satisfy the Chernoff bound, while MLAE at $N_{\mathrm{shot}} = 100$ uses $\sim 100\times$ fewer shots per data point at the cost of no rigorous guarantee. The paper explicitly frames this as ``rigor at a small constant-factor cost''.

\section{Verdict --- REPLICATED}

FAE's central empirical claim (near-Heisenberg $N_{\mathrm{orac}} \approx C/\epsilon$ for $a \in \{0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4\}$) is reproduced end-to-end on a real 2-qubit statevector simulation. Grover operator correctness holds at machine precision (C5); FAE slopes are $[0.85, 1.26]$ around 1 (C1); $j_{0}$ trend matches Fig.~3 (C3); empirical prefactor sits $\sim 30\times$ below the proven bound (C2 sanity). Theorem~1 was not re-derived and Grinko IQAE was not implemented for a head-to-head prefactor race --- see \S6.

\section{Critique (honest self-assessment)}

The replication passes on the paper's own headline test, but several sharp corners deserve to be named plainly.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Head-to-head prefactor race \emph{not} run.} The paper's most publishable single number is the $\sim 10^{2}\times$ prefactor improvement of Nakaji's rigorous bound ($4.1\times 10^{3}$) over Grinko IQAE's ($1.15\times 10^{6}$). We did \emph{not} independently implement Grinko IQAE and race the two algorithms on identical amplitudes and $(a,\epsilon)$ points. C2 was only sanity-checked, not truly cross-validated. This is the single largest gap.
    \item \textbf{Trial count 10$\times$ under paper.} The paper uses 1000 trials per $(a,\ell)$; we used 100 to keep wallclock $\sim 2$~min. Our slope estimates carry $\sim 10\%$ sampling noise, which is exactly why we see $[0.85, 1.26]$ instead of the paper's tighter cluster around~1. A 1000-trial rerun would tighten but is not expected to change the conclusion.
    \item \textbf{No hardware / no noise.} Everything is noiseless statevector. FAE's marketing pitch is NISQ-friendliness (no deep controlled-$Q$). We did \emph{not} check that FAE's advantage persists under depolarising noise, gate infidelity, or shot-count amplification --- a serious omission for a paper explicitly aimed at near-term hardware.
    \item \textbf{Only 4 amplitudes.} The paper's Fig.~3 sweeps $a \in \{0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4\}$ and we followed suit. Behavior for $a$ close to~0 (attenuated $\sin\theta = a/4$ becomes ill-conditioned) and $a$ near~1 (stage-2 triggers earlier, but $R$'s $1/4$ prefactor caps $\theta \leq 0.252$ by design) is not exercised.
    \item \textbf{Theorem~1 not re-derived.} We take the $4.1\times 10^{3}$ prefactor from eq.~(28) on faith. No spot check that the Chernoff constants $1944$ and $972$ are correct given the paper's stated $\delta$-budgeting.
    \item \textbf{Author's reference implementation not consulted.} Clean-room is good for provenance but leaves open the possibility that our reading of the two-stage $j_{0}$ update or the $\arccos$-branching disagrees with the author's in some corner case. The exact match on the $j_{0}$ trend argues against a serious disagreement.
    \item \textbf{MLAE baseline is off-spec in one row.} Our MLAE at $a = 0.2$ gives a slope of $3.89$ --- clearly the bimodal-likelihood pathology. We flag this correctly but did not implement the fix (e.g.\ multi-start optimisation or the modified Suzuki schedule), so the baseline row is not competitive.
    \item \textbf{95th-percentile statistic.} The paper's error stat is the 95th-percentile of $|\hat a - a|$. We match that, but this hides the tail: FAE's failure modes at low trial counts would show up better in max-error or the empirical CDF.
\end{enumerate}
