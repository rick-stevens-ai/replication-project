\begin{abstract}
We attempt a from-scratch replication of the headline result of Nakazawa
\emph{et al.}: the orbital-magnetization suppression ratio
$R = -\Delta M_{orb}/M_{orb}^0$ can exceed $50\%$ upon introducing $\sim1\%$
impurities into a triple-$Q$ charge loop-current (cLC) kagome metal, and $R$ is
qualitatively \emph{insensitive} to the cLC order parameter $\eta$. We build the
kagome tight-binding model with purely imaginary NN hopping modulation
$\delta t_{ij}=\pm i\eta$, compute the uniform orbital magnetization with the
modern-theory itinerant-circulation operator on real-space flakes, and insert a
unitary-limit ($I=100$ eV) vacancy impurity. \textbf{Verdict: PARTIAL.} The
\emph{magnitude} of the giant effect is reproduced ($R$ from $\sim10\%$ up to
$\sim80\%$ near $1\%$ impurity, mean $\approx49\%$), confirming that dilute
impurities drastically suppress $M_{orb}$. The paper's more subtle claim --- that
$R$ is \emph{insensitive} to $\eta$ --- was \emph{not} reproduced: our simplified
open-flake model shows $R$ decreasing with $\eta$, closer to the naive
$R\propto\xi_J^2$ expectation the authors argue against.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model}
Kagome lattice, three sublattices A/B/C. Nearest-neighbor hopping $t=-0.5$ eV;
third-nearest intra-sublattice $t'=-0.02$ eV; Van Hove filling
$n=2.55$/3-site cell. The cLC order is a purely imaginary, odd-parity NN hopping
modulation $\delta t_{ij}=\pm i\eta$ with a fixed triangle chirality (triple-$Q$
staggered-flux state), breaking global time-reversal symmetry and yielding a
finite uniform orbital magnetization. A single unitary-limit impurity $I=100$ eV
(vacancy) is placed on an A site.

\section{Orbital magnetization (modern theory)}
Rather than the closed $k$-space bulk+edge formula (Eq.~4 of the paper), we use
the equivalent real-space \emph{itinerant} orbital operator (the ``nonlocal
itinerant circulation'' the paper credits as the origin of the giant effect):
\begin{equation}
M_{orb} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n\in\text{occ}}\langle\psi_n| L_z |\psi_n\rangle,
\qquad L_z = \tfrac12(X v_y - Y v_x),\quad v_a = i[H,R_a].
\end{equation}
This operator (and its implementation) is reused from the g\"obel2024 kernel.
The suppression ratio is $R=(M_{orb}^0-M_{orb}^{imp})/M_{orb}^0$, averaged over an
Imp-1-like (triangular-loop) and Imp-2-like (hexagonal-loop) A site.

The mean $R$ normalized to $1\%$ impurity is $\approx 49\%$ (individual values
span $10$--$83\%$), so the ``can exceed $50\%$ with $1\%$ impurities'' magnitude
claim is corroborated. However, $R$ falls monotonically with $\eta$
(relative spread $\sim0.6$ across the scanned $\eta$), so the paper's
\emph{$\eta$-insensitivity} is NOT reproduced. The clean $M_{orb}(\eta)$ scaling
exponent came out $\approx0.3$ rather than the paper's $\eta^3$; the small open
flake and coarse filling snap do not resolve the low-$\eta$ VHS structure.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{PARTIAL.} Giant suppression of $M_{orb}$ by dilute impurities
(magnitude, order $\gtrsim50\%$ at $\sim1\%$) is reproduced from an independent
implementation; the finer claims ($\eta$-insensitivity, clean $\eta^3$ law) are
not, due to deliberate simplifications (open flake vs.\ $512^2$ folded-BZ
supercell, single-orbital $b_{3g}$ sector, no self-consistency). See
\texttt{failure\_analysis.md}.
