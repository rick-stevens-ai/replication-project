\title{From-scratch replication of the S-matrix / bipartite-crystalline-lattice

\author{TEXTURES-100 REPLICATE-PROJECT (automated)}

\begin{abstract}
We reproduce the central computable claim of Jiang \emph{et al.}
[arXiv:2311.09290v2], namely the S-matrix / generalized bipartite-crystalline-lattice
(BCL) flat-band counting theorem used to explain the quasi-flat bands of kagome
1:1 (FeGe), 1:6:6 (MgFe$_6$Ge$_6$), and 1:3:5 (AV$_3$Sb$_5$) families. Building
Bloch Hamiltonians from scratch in \texttt{numpy}, we confirm (i) the s-orbital
kagome baseline flat band at $E=+2t$, (ii) the chiral bipartite counting rule
$N_L-N_{\tilde L}$ (and $N_L+N_{\tilde L}-2r_s$ for rank-deficient $S_k$) flat
bands pinned at $E=0$ across four configurations including the paper's
$d_{x^2-y^2}/p^t_{xy}$ ($3{+}2$) group, and (iii) the intra-sublattice
generalization producing a flat band at nonzero energy. All predictions match to
numerical tolerance ($<10^{-14}$ bandwidth). Verdict: \textbf{REPLICATED}
(theorem-level; DFT fitting and cRPA not attempted).
\end{abstract}

\section{Target claim}
The paper argues that treating kagome materials with a single s-orbital model is
qualitatively insufficient, and instead decomposes the Fe $d$ orbitals into three
groups forming generalized bipartite crystalline lattices. For a chiral bipartite
Bloch Hamiltonian
\begin{equation}
H(k)=\begin{pmatrix} 0_{N_L} & S(k)\\ S^\dagger(k) & 0_{N_{\tilde L}}\end{pmatrix},
\quad N_L\ge N_{\tilde L},
\end{equation}
chiral symmetry pins at least $N_L-N_{\tilde L}$ flat bands at $E=0$; if
$\mathrm{rank}(S_k)=r_s\le N_{\tilde L}$, there are $N_L+N_{\tilde L}-2r_s$ flat
bands (Eqs.~S10.1--S10.2). Adding an intra-sublattice block $A(k)$ with a
$k$-independent eigenvalue of multiplicity $n_a>N_{\tilde L}$ yields $n_a-N_{\tilde L}$
flat bands at nonzero energy (Eq.~S10.3). Concrete kagome instance: the
$d_{x^2-y^2}/p^t_{xy}$ group has $N_{d_2}=3$, $N_p=2\Rightarrow$ one flat band.

\section{Method}
All Hamiltonians were built from scratch. The s-orbital kagome baseline uses the
shared \texttt{loop\_current\_kagome\_kernel.py} \texttt{KagomeModel} (provenance
credit; originally built for Fernandes \emph{et al.}, arXiv:2502.16657). Bipartite
$S(k)$ blocks were constructed as $S(k)=P(k)\,\Sigma\,Q^\dagger(k)$ with controlled
BZ-wide rank and distinct nonzero singular values. Flat bands were identified as
those with BZ bandwidth $<10^{-6}$ (measured $<10^{-14}$). A coarse
$12\times12$--$24\times24$ BZ grid suffices since flatness is exact.

\section{Scope and honesty}
This is a \emph{theorem-level} replication of the flat-band counting mechanism —
the paper's core analytic engine. We did \emph{not} perform the DFT band fitting,
cRPA interaction extraction, or material-specific orbital assignments; those
require ab initio inputs outside this fast pass. The loop-current mean-field probe
(shared kernel) is included only for class provenance: this paper is a flat-band
framework, not a spontaneous loop-current paper, despite the corpus class label.
