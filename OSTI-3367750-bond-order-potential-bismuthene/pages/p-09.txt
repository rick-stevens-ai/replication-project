Two scenarios were examined: (i) one in which the
simulation cell was allowed to relax in the x and y directions
at each displacement step (Figure 6a), and (ii) one in which
the in-plane lattice parameters were constrained, preventing
relaxation (Figure 6b). In both cases, the potential energy
increases monotonically as the structure approaches the flat
configuration, reaching a maximum at zero buckling height.
This behavior is expected because flattening disrupts the
preferred hybridization environment in bismuthene and forces
Bi—Bi bonds into an energetically unfavorable geometry. As
the out-of-plane displacement is reversed and the system
transitions back to the low-buckled structure, the energy
decreases correspondingly, reflecting a return to the mechan-
ically stable ground state.

Across all three Tersoff potentials, the energy barrier
associated with flattening mirrors the stiffness hierarchy
observed in the elastic constants and phonon spectra.
Consistent with its deeper equilibrium well and stronger
angular penalty, ML-Tersoff-1 predicts the highest energy cost
for flattening, indicating a strong resistance to ZA-mode
distortions. ML-Tersoff produces an intermediate barrier,
while ML-Tersoff-2 which has the shallowest well and weakest
angular rigidity predicts the smallest energy penalty for
flattening. This ordering also matches the ZA phonon
frequencies in the Supporting Information (Figures S1b and
S2b), where ML-Tersoff-1 shows the stiffest ZA branch and
ML-Tersoff-2 the softest.

A consistent trend is observed when comparing relaxed and
unrelaxed cases. For all potentials, the energy is higher when
in-plane relaxation is constrained. Allowing in-plane relaxation
enables the lattice vectors to adjust and redistribute internal
stress as the buckling height decreases, thereby lowering the
overall energy. When this relaxation pathway is blocked, the
structure accumulates significantly larger internal stresses,
resulting in a higher potential energy at each displacement
step.

The DFT reference calculations exhibit a similar increase in
energy as the buckling height approaches zero, but with one
notable difference. In the relaxed DFT case, a distinct dip in
the potential-energy curve occurs precisely at the flat
configuration, suggesting the presence of a metastable
intermediate configuration.*° This dip indicates that the flat
structure may represent a shallow local minimum on the
energy landscape, even though it is not the true ground state.
Such metastability has been proposed in other heavy group-V
monolayers and is associated with subtle competition between
sp’-like and sp*-like bonding configurations. None of the three
ML-optimized Tersoff potentials reproduce this energy dip,
although ML-Tersoff-2 comes closest due to its lower
flattening barrier.

Overall, the modal analysis reveals that the three RL-
optimized potentials capture the qualitative shape of the
flattening energy pathway and reproduce the overall energetic
preference for the low-buckled structure. The differences
among ML-Tersoff, ML-Tersoff-1, and ML-Tersoff-2 in the
magnitude of the flattening barrier are fully consistent with the
trends established earlier in the pairwise, angular, and bond-
order contributions and the phonon spectra. This analysis also
highlights where the empirical potentials diverge from DFT: in
particular, the inability to capture the shallow metastable dip at
the flat configuration when in-plane relaxation is allowed.
