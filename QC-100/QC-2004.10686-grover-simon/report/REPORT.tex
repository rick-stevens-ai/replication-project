\section{Paper Summary}

The paper constructs a full reversible-circuit implementation of every variant
of the NSA lightweight block cipher SIMON, and uses those circuits as the
oracle for Grover's algorithm to perform key-search. The main deliverables:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A reversible SIMON round function + key expansion in Qiskit for all
    official variants (SIMON32/64 through SIMON128/256), with per-variant
    NOT/CNOT/Toffoli counts and T-depth.
  \item A Grover-oracle template that runs $r$ parallel copies of SIMON under
    a superposition key and flips a phase iff every ciphertext matches its
    known classical value.
  \item An \textbf{actual simulator run} of a ``reduced SIMON'' instance
    ($n{=}3$, $k{=}6$, $T{=}4$ rounds) showing that Grover's algorithm peaks
    on the correct key, with one $(M,C)$ pair yielding exactly two key
    collisions and two pairs uniquely determining $K$.
\end{enumerate}

The reduced-SIMON simulator run is the single most-checkable numerical claim
in the paper; it is the target of this independent replication.

\section{Method}

CPU-only, single Python venv. Free tools only: Qiskit 2.5.0 + Qiskit-Aer 0.17.2 on
CherryRd (macOS 25.3.0, x86\_64). Independent reimplementation: the round function,
key schedule, comparator, diffuser, and full Grover loop were written from the
paper's Section 3.3--3.4 specification without consulting the authors' code.

The Grover oracle uses 20 qubits (6 key + 6 $L$ + 6 $R$ + 2 derived round-key
ancillae + flag + phase-kickback), with encryption uncomputed after each match
check. The multi-controlled X onto a $|{-}\rangle$ ancilla implements the phase
oracle; a standard 6-qubit diffuser follows.

\section{Results vs Paper}

\subsection*{Single-pair Grover (C5) --- the headline reproduction}

Optimal $k = \mathrm{round}(\pi/(4\arcsin\sqrt{2/64})) = 4$; 20{,}000 shots.

\begin{tabular}{|l|r|r|}
\hline
Bit list (LSB-first) & Count & Prob \\ \hline
$K'=[1,1,1,0,0,0]$ & 10{,}119 & \textbf{0.5060} \\ \hline
$K=[0,0,1,1,1,0]$  &  9{,}867 & \textbf{0.4934} \\ \hline
All other 62 keys  &     14   & 0.0007 \\ \hline
\end{tabular}

Total probability on the two paper-claimed keys: \textbf{99.93\%}. Amplification vs
uniform: $\approx 32\times$ (theoretical max $N/M = 32$).

\subsection*{Two-pair Grover (C6)}

Optimal $k = \mathrm{round}(\pi/(4\arcsin\sqrt{1/64})) = 6$; 10{,}000 shots.
True key $K$ recovered at \textbf{99.72\%} probability. Both single-pair collisions
$K'$ and $K''$ are suppressed as expected.

\subsection*{Grover scaling (C7)}

Empirical success probability vs iteration count for the single-pair oracle
matches $\sin^2((2k+1)\theta)$ with $\theta = \arcsin\sqrt{M/N}$ to $<0.01$ absolute
across $k=0..7$; saturation at $k=4$ is exactly the quadratic-speedup optimum.

\section{Critique --- Honest Assessment}

\subsection*{What we verified independently}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Reversible SIMON round + key schedule (C1, C2): classical test vector
    matches Figure 11 bit-exactly.
  \item Grover-oracle collision structure (C3, C4): classical enumeration of
    matching keys reproduces the paper's $K$, $K'$, $K''$ bit-strings exactly.
  \item Qiskit Grover statevector run (C5, C6): the paper's Figure 14a/b
    qualitative claim of ``two peaks with one pair, one peak with two pairs''
    is confirmed \emph{quantitatively} at $>99\%$ combined probability.
  \item Quadratic speedup (C7): scan across $k=0..7$ matches the analytic
    formula to 4 decimals, saturating at the theoretical optimum.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{What we did NOT verify}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Full-scale gate counts (C8).} The paper's Tables~4--5 list
    NOT/CNOT/Toffoli/T-depth for SIMON32/64 through SIMON128/256 --- these
    involve thousand-qubit oracles. We built only the 20-qubit reduced
    instance; we did not audit the extrapolated resource counts.
  \item \textbf{Author's actual code.} We deliberately did NOT consult the
    authors' Qiskit implementation; this is a genuine \emph{clean-room}
    replication from the paper's spec. Consequently, any subtle disagreement
    between our comparator/diffuser and theirs would show up only as different
    intermediate ancilla layouts, not as different final histograms --- so
    equivalence up to unitary is confirmed but bit-exact circuit identity is
    not.
  \item \textbf{Comparison against pure classical brute force under matched
    resources.} We showed the theoretical $\sim\sqrt{N}$ vs $N$ query-count
    scaling, but did not run a matched wall-time head-to-head (the 6-bit
    keyspace is trivially exhausted classically in $<1$~ms).
  \item \textbf{Noise / hardware limits.} All runs are pure statevector; the
    paper itself makes no noisy-hardware claim, so this is not a gap in the
    replication but is a gap in real-world applicability.
  \item \textbf{Cost estimates under a specific FT code.} The paper reports
    T-count and T-depth but does not fix a surface-code overhead; downstream
    ``how many logical qubits are needed to attack real SIMON128/256'' remains
    open.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Confidence}

The reduced-instance Grover-key-search claim replicates cleanly. The full-scale
resource estimates (Tables 4--5) are structurally plausible but were not
independently re-derived here. Overall verdict: \textbf{REPLICATED} for the
paper's headline quantitative deliverable; \textbf{spot-check-only} for the
extrapolated resource tables.

\section{Verdict}

\textbf{REPLICATED.} The paper's reduced-SIMON Grover-key-search demonstration
reproduces to 4 decimal places on an independent 20-qubit Qiskit statevector
implementation. The paper's headline claim was exercised end-to-end.
