\begin{abstract}
We independently re-derive and numerically verify the exact, computable
Section-4 predictions of Bertozzi, Garnett \& Laurent
(\emph{SIAM J.\ Math.\ Anal.}, 2012; arXiv:1204.1095) for the multidimensional
nonlocal aggregation equation with a Newtonian interaction kernel. Because the
paper is an analysis paper with no accompanying code, ``replication'' here
means (i)~independent re-derivation of the radial-to-Burgers reduction and the
shock-time formula, and (ii)~from-scratch numerical verification via three
mutually independent implementations (closed-form shell ODE, $N$-particle
Lagrangian simulation, and inviscid Burgers characteristics). All five
computable claims (C1--C5) reproduce to machine precision or to
$\mathcal{O}(10^{-9})$ discretisation error. Three independent free-tier Argo
judges (gpt-5.2, gemini-2.5-pro, gpt-4.1) unanimously return
\textbf{REPLICATED}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper and setup}
The paper studies the nonlocal aggregation equation
\begin{equation}
\partial_t \rho \;-\; \operatorname{div}\!\bigl(\rho\,\nabla K\!\ast\!\rho\bigr) \;=\; 0,
\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^d,\ d\ge 2, \label{eq:agg}
\end{equation}
with power-law kernels $K(x)=|x|^\alpha/\alpha$, in the regime
$2-d\le \alpha <2$ where smooth densities develop finite-time singularities.
The main analytical results are: (i)~global-in-time existence of radially
symmetric monotone-decreasing measure solutions (continuation past blow-up);
(ii)~preservation of monotonicity for $\alpha<2$; and, most importantly for a
numerical replication, (iii)~the \emph{Newtonian} case $\alpha=2-d$, in which
the radial problem localises and reduces exactly to the inviscid Burgers
equation on a half-line, opening the door to classical conservation-law
theory.

\paragraph{Compute.}
Local host (CherryRd), \texttt{numpy 2.4.3}~/~\texttt{scipy 1.18.0}; no
library PDE solver was used for the dynamics. Judges via the free Argo proxy
(no paid endpoints).

\section{Method}
All physics was implemented from scratch in \texttt{numpy}; \texttt{scipy} is
present but unused for the actual PDE dynamics.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Radial reduction (C1).} For the Newtonian kernel
$-\Delta K=\delta$, the radial velocity is $v(r)=-m(r)/r^{d-1}$ with
$m(r)=\int_0^r s^{d-1}\rho(s)\,ds$. For monotone data the mass strictly
interior to a Lagrangian shell is conserved along its characteristic, giving
the closed-form shell ODE
\begin{equation}
r(t)^d \;=\; r_0^d - d\,m_0\,t, \qquad t_{\rm shell}=\frac{r_0^d}{d\,m_0}.
\end{equation}
\item \textbf{Uniform ball / C4.} $\rho\equiv c$ inside $R_0$, normalised so
$m(R_0)=1$ gives $c=d/R_0^d$. Then $m(r_0)=c\,r_0^d/d$ and
$t_{\rm shell}=R_0^d/d$, \emph{independent of $r_0$} $\Rightarrow$
simultaneous collapse. Checked over 20 shells for $d=2,3,4$.
\item \textbf{Direct $N$-particle radial simulation (C2).} $N\!=\!1500$
particles sampled from $r^{d-1}\rho(r)$; RK4 integration of
$dr_i/dt=-M(r_i)/r_i^{d-1}$ with $M(r_i)=(\text{\# inside})\times(\text{mass/particle})$.
No shell-conservation assumption is baked in. Ordering among active shells
is monitored.
\item \textbf{Inviscid Burgers by characteristics (C1/C3).} $z\!=\!r^d$
gives $dz/dt=-d\,m$, and $m$ is constant along $z$-characteristics: the
equation \emph{is} $m_t-m m_z=0$ up to a dimensional factor. Predicted shock
time $t_{\rm shock}=1/(d\sup_z m'_{\rm init}(z))$.
\item \textbf{Density blow-up (C5).} Along a collapsing uniform-ball shell,
$\rho(t)=m_0/[(1/d)r(t)^d]$; the numerical ratio $(d\rho/dt)/\rho^2$ is
measured.
\item \textbf{Non-uniform monotone tests (C3).} Gaussian $\rho=e^{-4r^2}$ and
parabolic cap $\rho=\max(1-(r/R)^2,0)$: $m_{\rm init}(z)$ built numerically,
$\sup_z m'_{\rm init}$ from a uniform-$z$ grid; formula compared against
observed first blow-up (interior char.-cross \emph{and} origin-reach).
\item \textbf{Multi-judge assessment.} Free Argo endpoints \texttt{gpt-5.2},
\texttt{gemini-2.5-pro}, \texttt{gpt-4.1} (opus deliberately avoided).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Results vs.\ paper}

\subsection*{C4 --- collapsing ball: simultaneous collapse}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}cccc@{}}
\toprule
$d$ & $t_*^{\rm theory}=R_0^d/d$ & shell-time mean & spread across 20 shells \\ \midrule
2 & $0.500000$ & $0.500000$ & $1.67\!\times\!10^{-16}$ \\
3 & $0.333333$ & $0.333333$ & $1.11\!\times\!10^{-16}$ \\
4 & $0.250000$ & $0.250000$ & $5.55\!\times\!10^{-17}$ \\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Spread is at machine precision: all characteristics collapse at the origin
simultaneously at exactly $R_0^d/d$. Independent particle simulation:
mean collapse $t=0.5014$ ($d\!=\!2$), $0.3342$ ($d\!=\!3$) vs.\ theory
$0.5$, $0.3333$ ($\lesssim 0.3\%$ time-step error).

\subsection*{C1 --- Burgers equivalence}
The $z$-characteristic speed $-d\,m$ gives $t_{\rm shock}=R_0^d/d$, matching
the closed-form shell ODE exactly (\texttt{matches\_methodA=True} for
$d=2,3,4$). The three independent routes agree.

\subsection*{C3 --- shock-time formula}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llccc@{}}
\toprule
data & $d$ & $t_{\rm shock}=1/(d\sup m'_{\rm init})$ & observed first blow-up & rel.\ err \\ \midrule
gaussian & 2 & $1.004508$ & $1.004508$ & $4.4\!\times\!10^{-10}$ \\
gaussian & 3 & $1.088751$ & $1.088751$ & $2.0\!\times\!10^{-16}$ \\
parabola & 2 & $1.000125$ & $1.000125$ & $1.8\!\times\!10^{-9}$ \\
parabola & 3 & $1.002387$ & $1.002387$ & $8.9\!\times\!10^{-16}$ \\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For monotone data the first interior characteristic-crossing time equals the
origin-reach time: the shock forms exactly at the origin, as
eq.~4.5 of the paper asserts.

\subsection*{C5 --- density blow-up $\rho_t=\rho^2$}
Median $(d\rho/dt)/\rho^2 = 1.00000$ and stable (self-similar) along the
collapsing-shell characteristic for $d=2,3$; $\rho$ grows from $\mathcal{O}(1)$
to $\gtrsim 2000$ as the shell approaches the origin.

\subsection*{C2 --- monotonicity / shocks only at origin}
Among shells with $r>0.05$ (away from origin) the worst ordering disorder is
\emph{exactly zero} for $d=2$. The residual disorder at the origin boundary
does not decrease with $\Delta t$
($0.94\!\times\!10^{-2}$ stable across $\Delta t = 4\!\times\!10^{-4}\to
5\!\times\!10^{-5}$), confirming it is the physical shock at the origin,
not a numerical scheme artefact.

\section{Genuine critique}
\label{sec:critique}
This section is deliberately adversarial. The verdict remains REPLICATED, but
the following limitations must be recorded honestly.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{We verified Section~4, not the whole paper.} The paper's
principal analytical contributions --- global existence of monotone-decreasing
measure solutions for the full range $2-d<\alpha<2$, Dirac-mass emergence at
the critical exponent, and loss of monotonicity for $\alpha>2$ --- are
proof-level statements about measure-valued solutions and are not amenable to
direct numerical checking. They are honestly \emph{not tested here} (this is
what makes C6 ``out of scope''), and we make no claim to have verified them.
Anyone reading this replication as covering the paper's Theorems 1--3 would
be over-reading it.
\item \textbf{Kernel narrowness.} All numerical verification is confined to the
Newtonian exponent $\alpha=2-d$. That is where the radial problem localises to
Burgers on the half-line, which is precisely why it is tractable --- and
precisely why the Newtonian case does \emph{not} exercise the more delicate
cases $2-d<\alpha<2$ or the borderline behaviour near $\alpha=2$ where
monotonicity is lost. Passing on Newtonian is necessary, but far from
sufficient, evidence for the general theory.
\item \textbf{The three ``independent'' methods share a common assumption.}
The closed-form shell ODE, the $N$-particle simulation, and the Burgers
characteristics solve all assume radial symmetry, monotone-decreasing initial
data, and shell-mass conservation along characteristics. They therefore
cross-check the numerics but do not independently \emph{stress-test} those
assumptions. A genuinely independent test would perturb radial symmetry or
monotonicity and see what breaks --- we did not do that.
\item \textbf{Simultaneous-collapse spread is not a physics test.} The
$\sim\!10^{-16}$ spread across shells in Table~C4 is machine epsilon
because $t_{\rm shell}=R_0^d/d$ is manifestly $r_0$-independent by
construction. The number is \emph{consistent with} the paper; it should not
be quoted as a strong empirical confirmation. The real content is the
particle-simulation cross-check at $\sim\!0.3\%$ discretisation error.
\item \textbf{C3 rel.\ err.\ pattern.} The $10^{-16}$ rows for $d=3$ are
essentially tautological (both sides use the same $\sup m'_{\rm init}$ from
the same uniform-$z$ grid). The $\sim\!10^{-9}$ rows for $d=2$ are the
genuinely informative ones: they encode the interpolation and root-finding
error in extracting the first blow-up time from the characteristic solve.
Reporting both without distinguishing them is misleadingly optimistic.
\item \textbf{Judge concordance is weak evidence.} Three LLM judges returning
REPLICATED is worth reporting but should not be over-weighted. They read
essentially the same evidence bundle; their agreement is closer to
inter-rater reliability on \emph{our narrative} than to independent scientific
verification. The load-bearing evidence is the numerics in
\S\S C1--C5, not the judge vote.
\item \textbf{Early $\texttt{np.gradient}$-on-nonuniform-grid slip.} A
transient $d=3$ Gaussian shock-time diagnostic was wrong before the
corrected \texttt{c3\_shock\_time.py} was written. The final numbers are
right, but the fact that the first attempt was silently incorrect is a
reminder that ``matches to $10^{-16}$'' can also be an artefact of using the
paper's own formula on both sides.
\item \textbf{Publisher-HTML unavailability.} SIAM/T\&F/MDPI were Cloudflare
-blocked; we used arXiv full text and LaTeX source. For pure mathematics
this is the canonical source, but a purist would want to diff the published
proof text against the arXiv preprint.
\end{itemize}
On balance, the paper's \emph{computable} Newtonian predictions are reproduced
to machine precision by three mutually consistent implementations, and no
internal inconsistency was found; the caveats above concern \emph{scope} of
the replication, not its correctness.
