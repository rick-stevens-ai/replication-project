\begin{abstract}
We independently reproduce the simulation-side headline claim of Meirom \& Frankel (2023, DOI \texttt{10.3389/frqst.2023.1273581}): that a Hardware-Efficient / pulse-parameterized ansatz reaches chemical accuracy (defined by the authors as $<1.6\,\text{mHa}$ vs FCI) in VQE for the 2-qubit parity-reduced STO-3G Hamiltonian of H$_2$ across all studied bond distances, and analogously for HeH$^+$. Using an independent path (Qiskit 2.5.0 + qiskit-nature 0.8 + PySCF 2.13 statevector, EfficientSU2 ansatz, COBYLA optimizer), we reproduce chemical accuracy at 7/7 H$_2$ and 5/5 HeH$^+$ distances, with the equilibrium H$_2$ energy matching FCI to $\sim 3\times 10^{-9}\,\text{Ha}$. The paper's distinctive pulse-level PANSATZ construction, its 7$\times$ schedule-duration claim, the LiH 4-qubit result, and the real-hardware (\texttt{ibm\_lagos}) run were not attempted here as they require Qiskit-Dynamics with a calibrated backend Hamiltonian and paid IBM Quantum access. Two independent Argo LLM judges (gpt-5.2, gpt-5.4) return verdict \textbf{PARTIAL} with consistent reasoning.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper summary}

PANSATZ is a hardware-native ansatz for VQAs on fixed-frequency transmons (IBM Falcon). Instead of parameterizing gate rotation angles, the authors expose \emph{pulse duration} (plus a per-layer driving phase) of pre-defined DRAG single-qubit pulses and cross-resonance (CR) two-qubit pulses. Because the "identity ansatz" corresponds to zero-duration CR pulses, the ansatz has \emph{structured adaptivity} --- schedule length grows with the entanglement required. Their headline benchmarks are VQE ground-state energies for H$_2$, HeH$^+$ (both 2-qubit reduced), and LiH (4-qubit reduced) in the STO-3G basis, reaching chemical accuracy across all distances in simulation, and reaching chemical accuracy on \texttt{ibm\_lagos} for H$_2$ with only readout-error mitigation.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Environment}
Python 3.14.6; Qiskit 2.5.0; qiskit-nature 0.8.0; PySCF 2.13.1; NumPy + SciPy (system-site). Venv at \texttt{work/venv/}. Homebrew macOS host \texttt{CherryRd}.

\subsection{Pipeline}
For each bond distance $d$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Build the electronic Hamiltonian via \texttt{PySCFDriver} (\texttt{qiskit-nature}) at basis \texttt{sto3g}.
\item Map to qubits with \texttt{ParityMapper(num\_particles=$\ldots$)} $\;\rightarrow\;$ 2-qubit operator (identical to the paper's setup, Sec.~3).
\item Reference FCI computed two independent ways: (a) NumPy eigendecomposition of the $4\times 4$ qubit Hamiltonian; (b) full-CI in the STO-3G active space via \texttt{pyscf.fci.FCI}. They agree to double precision.
\item VQE with \texttt{EfficientSU2(reps=2, linear)} (12 parameters), classical optimizer \texttt{COBYLA}, seed 42, \texttt{maxiter=500}. Same family as the paper's Real-Amplitudes HEA baseline (GANSATZ); pulse-level PANSATZ itself requires Qiskit-Dynamics + backend calibration and is out of scope for a pure statevector sanity replication.
\item Chemical-accuracy check: $|E_\text{VQE} - E_\text{FCI}| < 1.6\,\text{mHa}$ (paper's own threshold).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Verdict}

\noindent\textbf{PARTIAL.} The paper's simulation-side headline number (chemical accuracy across all H--H distances on the 2-qubit parity-reduced STO-3G H$_2$ Hamiltonian, plus HeH$^+$) was \emph{fully reproduced} to nano-Hartree precision at the equilibrium distance and to sub-milli-Hartree across the full curve, using an independent path (PySCF FCI + Qiskit statevector VQE). Two Argo judges concur. The paper's distinctive novelty --- pulse-level PANSATZ, 7$\times$ schedule reduction, hardware chemical accuracy on \texttt{ibm\_lagos}, and LiH 4-qubit --- was not attempted here (see \S\ref{sec:gaps}).
