\section*{Paper}
Benfenati, Mazzola, Capecci, Barkoutsos, Ollitrault, Tavernelli, Guidoni,
\emph{``Improved accuracy on noisy devices by non-unitary Variational Quantum
Eigensolver for chemistry applications''}, arXiv:2101.09316v1 (Jan 22 2021).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Wave:} QC-100 \quad \textbf{Verdict:} \textbf{REPLICATED}

\section{Paper summary}

The authors propose \textbf{non-unitary VQE (nu-VQE)}: instead of minimizing
$\langle \psi(\theta) | H | \psi(\theta) \rangle$, they minimize the Rayleigh
quotient of a modified Hamiltonian sandwiched between the ansatz state and a
\textbf{non-unitary operator} $J$ (a diagonal Jastrow-like operator
$J = \exp(\sum_k \alpha_k Z_k + \sum_{k<l} \alpha_{kl} Z_k Z_l)$). The energy
estimator is
\[
E_{\nu} \;=\; \frac{\langle \psi | J H J | \psi \rangle}{\langle \psi | J^2 | \psi \rangle}.
\]
The key selling point is that $J$ is diagonal in the computational basis, so
the \emph{quantum circuit depth is unchanged}; only $O(n^2)$ new
\emph{classical} variational parameters are added.

\textbf{Headline claim (C2, Sec.~V.B, Figs.~7--8):} on noisy simulations,
nu-VQE achieves an energy error \textbf{$\sim$one order of magnitude smaller}
than standard VQE at equal circuit depth. This is the headline we tested.

\section{Method (as executed here)}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Molecule:} H$_2$ at $R \approx 0.7414$~\AA, STO-3G, parity mapping
    + 2-qubit reduction (2-qubit Hamiltonian; coefficients from O'Malley
    et al., PRX 6, 031007 (2016)).
  \item \textbf{Exact reference:} $E_{\text{FCI}} = -1.85727498$~Ha (dense
    diagonalization of the 4$\times$4 Pauli Hamiltonian).
  \item \textbf{Ansatz (identical for both methods):}
    $R_y(\theta_0)@q_0 \; R_y(\theta_1)@q_1 \; \text{CNOT}(q_0{\to}q_1) \;
    R_y(\theta_2)@q_0 \; R_y(\theta_3)@q_1$. 4 angles, 1 CNOT.
  \item \textbf{nu-VQE Jastrow:} 3 classical parameters
    $(\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_{01})$, evaluated on the density matrix on the
    classical side.
  \item \textbf{Noise:} Qiskit Aer depolarizing model,
    (low) $p_1=10^{-3}, p_2=10^{-2}$; (high) $p_1=2{\cdot}10^{-3}, p_2=2{\cdot}10^{-2}$.
    Simulator: \texttt{AerSimulator(method="density\_matrix")} (no shot noise).
  \item \textbf{Optimization:} COBYLA, 10 (noiseless) / 8 (noisy) random restarts, best
    energy retained. Full implementation in \texttt{code/nu\_vqe\_h2.py}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Verdict: REPLICATED}

The paper's headline claim (C2) --- that nu-VQE with a diagonal Jastrow
operator achieves $\sim$order-of-magnitude lower energy error than standard
unitary VQE at equal circuit depth under realistic noise --- was reproduced
by an independent Qiskit 2.5 / Aer 0.17 implementation. We measured error
reduction ratios of 31.6$\times$ (low noise) and 33.7$\times$ (high noise),
firmly inside the ``order-of-magnitude'' regime the paper claims. Same
ansatz, same circuit depth, same estimator formula (Eq.~10); no code reused
from the paper. Both the mechanism and the direction of the effect agree
with Figs.~7--8 and Sec.~V.B of the original.

\section{Critique (honest)}

\paragraph{What we \emph{did} independently exercise.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Independent re-implementation of the nu-VQE energy estimator
    $\text{Tr}[JHJ\rho]/\text{Tr}[J^2\rho]$ from scratch.
  \item Fresh Hamiltonian build from O'Malley et al. coefficients, not from
    the paper's supplementary code.
  \item Direct same-depth VQE vs.\ nu-VQE energy convergence comparison for
    the paper's molecule (H$_2$), noisy simulator, both methods run to
    optimizer convergence with multi-start.
  \item Quantitative headline-error-ratio check against the paper's
    ``$\sim 10\times$'' figure --- reproduced, in fact somewhat exceeded.
  \item Ancilla / measurement overhead: since $J$ is diagonal, the extra
    quantum overhead is \emph{zero gates}; the additional expectation value
    $\text{Tr}[J^2 \rho]$ is one extra diagonal observable. We verified this
    concretely (no gate added, one extra classical trace).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What we \emph{did not} independently exercise (honest limitations).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{System size mismatch.} The paper's headline plot (Fig.~7b) is
    H$_2$ in the 6-31G basis (6-qubit); we used H$_2$/STO-3G (2-qubit).
    Our 1-block ansatz already saturates FCI noiselessly, so the C1
    ``shallow-depth advantage'' claim is only trivially exercised.
  \item \textbf{Noise-model realism.} We used a uniform depolarizing channel,
    not the full calibrated ibmq\_boeblingen noise (T1/T2, gate-specific
    error rates, readout error, crosstalk). The paper's noise realism is
    higher; a full ibmq-calibration replay was out of scope.
  \item \textbf{No shot noise.} We used the density-matrix backend, so
    Tr[$JHJ\rho$] and Tr[$J^2\rho$] are exact. In the paper the ratio
    Tr[$JHJ\rho$]/Tr[$J^2\rho$] is estimated from finite shots, which can
    amplify variance in the denominator. We did not stress-test this.
  \item \textbf{Molecule coverage.} Only H$_2$. LiH and H$_2$O (C5) were
    \emph{not} tested. Mapping robustness across JW / BK / parity (C3) not
    tested. Basis-set robustness (C4) only lightly touched.
  \item \textbf{Optimizer sensitivity.} COBYLA with modest restarts; no
    landscape analysis or comparison against SPSA / L-BFGS-B / natural
    gradient, which the paper touches on.
  \item \textbf{No hardware run.} All results are noisy simulator, not real
    device.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Bottom line of the critique.}
The headline result C2 was exercised on the paper's molecule (H$_2$) with a
faithful implementation of the paper's estimator formula, same ansatz, same
circuit depth, and a noise model of roughly the right order. The
$31$--$34\times$ error reduction we measured is stronger than the paper's
$\sim 10\times$; the gap is explained by (a) smaller circuit accumulates
less noise, (b) no shot noise in our setup, (c) the paper's own figure was
already an order-of-magnitude statement. C3--C5 (mapping / basis / molecule
robustness) remain \emph{not independently verified}; we relied on the
paper's authority for those. This is a REPLICATED verdict for the
headline (C2), not a full-scope re-audit of the paper.
