287 The errors in the crystal lattice structure and cohesive energy were
288 calculated with respect to reference DFT values. For the equation of
289 state (EOS), the error is evaluated by comparing the EOS curves
290 obtained from reference DFT data and from the fitted Tersoff
291 potential. Two metrics were used to quantify the EOS error. First, a
292 shape error measures how well the fitted potential reproduces the
293 overall curvature of the EOS curve after aligning the energy minima,
294 ensuring that the elastic response is captured correctly rather than
298 only the equilibrium point. Second, a shift error measures the
296 difference in the equilibrium (minimum) energy between the
297 reference and fitted curves, penalizing errors in cohesive energy.
298 The total EOS error is obtained as a weighted combination of these
299 two contributions.

300 Phonon dispersion errors are evaluated by directly comparing
301 phonon frequencies from the fitted potential with reference data along
302 identical high-symmetry paths in the Brillouin zone. Several terms
303 were used to quantify the error. A stability-related term penalizes the
304 presence of additional imaginary phonon modes, ensuring that the
308 fitted potential does not introduce unphysical lattice instabilities. A
306 frequency gap term compares characteristic gaps between acoustic
307 and optical branches. A band-shape term measures deviations
308 between predicted phonon branches with reference to DFT
309 calculation across the Brillouin zone, with greater emphasis on low-
310 frequency acoustic modes that dominate elastic and thermal behavior.
311 Additional terms control the maximum phonon frequencies to avoid
312 overstiffening, and explicitly evaluate errors at the I’ point to preserve
313 correct acoustic behavior and symmetry. The total phonon error is
314 computed as a weighted combination of all these contributions.

Properly defining rewards in a RL based search is essential for
guiding the agent toward optimal outcomes. A well-designed reward
function ensures that the agent’s actions are aligned with the desired
objectives by providing clear feedback on performance. This
alignment helps the agent focus on strategies that lead to better
results, minimizing the exploration of less relevant paths. Effective

rewards also facilitate a balance between exploration and exploitation, 3
enabling the agent to discover new solutions while refining known 35

effective strategies. In this case, the rewards an RL agent can earn in

the tree-based search depend on how well the properties predicted by 353

the RL-optimized Tersoff potential parameters align with respect to
the target properties.

In our workflow, the objectives are assessed hierarchically, with
errors for various properties evaluated at each stage according to an
increasing level of difficulty or as determined by the user’s discretion.

The prediction of the properties in any hierarchical class is carried out ;

through integration with the Large-scale Atomic/Molecular Massively
Parallel Simulator (LAMMPS).”” Prior to the calculations of these
properties, it is essential to ensure that the initial configurations are in
their minimum energy configuration. This is achieved using the
conjugate gradient method,” with the criteria of force and energy
convergence set around 107" eV Aq and 107 eV. In our
simulations, a barostat maintaining a pressure of P = 1 bar is
implemented. Additionally, a 3 X 3 X 1 supercell configuration is
selected to strike a balance between computational efficiency and the
accuracy required for the simulations.

Post optimization, we calculated the lattice dimensions, cohesive 3

energies, equations of state, elastic constants, and phonon dispersion
curves. The lattice parameters, indicative of the material’s structural
integrity, are derived directly from the dimensions of the optimized

unit cell. The cohesive energy was calculated on a per-atom basis. 375

This calculation involves dividing the potential energy of the

optimized structure by the number of atoms. We calculated the ;

equations of state by subjecting the material to incremental strain and
calculating the cohesive energies corresponding to those strains, For
calculating the material's elastic constants, we subjected the material

to deformations at various directions and calculated the elastic ;

constants as the ratio of the stress developed and the strain at any
particular direction. The phonon dispersion curve was calculated
using the phonopy“” tool which calculates the phonon using the finite
difference approach. For calculating the dynamical matrix necessary
for phonon we used a 6 X 6 X 1 supercell and a 4 X 4 X 1 k-point grid
and before changing the atomic displacement we ensured that the
interatomic forces are less than 0.02 eV/A. The interatomic forces for
MD simulations were calculated using LAMMPS with the optimized
Tersoff potential parameters. The properties of different parameter
sets sampled by the RL optimizer were predicted by these methods.
