\begin{abstract}
We independently replicate the two central contributions of Shen \& Zhang (2022): (C1) a
fourth-order finite-difference scheme derived from the $Q_2$ spectral-element method attains
$O(h^4)$ accuracy on both the generalized Allen--Cahn equation and a stream-function/vorticity
periodic-BC test, and (C2) the associated backward-Euler operator is inverse-positive under the
paper's discrete maximum-principle (DMP) constraints, including a \emph{novel lower bound}
$\Delta t\,\mu/h^2 \geq 3$. All coding is from scratch (equations only; no paper code). Numerical
outputs match paper tables entrywise to within $\sim 6\%$; the DMP is confirmed in-regime and
demonstrably fails out-of-regime. Verdict: \textbf{REPLICATED}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper summary}
The paper solves the generalized Allen--Cahn equation with prescribed incompressible convection
on $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^2$:
\begin{equation}
\phi_t + u\phi_x + v\phi_y = \mu \Delta\phi - F'(\phi)/\varepsilon,
\label{eq:AC}
\end{equation}
with $\mu,\varepsilon>0$ and $F$ polynomial $\tfrac{1}{4}(\phi^2-1)^2$ or logarithmic. The
spatial discretization is a fourth-order FD scheme obtained from the $Q_2$ spectral-element
method (Gauss--Lobatto 3-point quadrature), written explicitly (eqs.~2.7--2.8) with alternating
stencils on interior grid points:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{odd (cell-center) points:} 3-point centered $[-1,0,1]/(2h)$ ($D_1$) and $[1,-2,1]/h^2$ ($D_2$);
  \item \textbf{even (cell-end/knot) points:} 5-point $[1,-4,0,4,-1]/(4h)$ ($D_1$) and $[1,-8,14,-8,1]/(4h^2)$ ($D_2$).
\end{itemize}
Time discretization is (stabilized) IMEX / backward-Euler with the nonlinear reaction treated
explicitly. Two central claim clusters:
\begin{description}
  \item[C1 (accuracy)] $O(h^4)$ accuracy in space for both Allen--Cahn (Table 6.1) and periodic
    stream/vorticity (Table 6.2), with a companion $O(h^2)$ scheme for comparison.
  \item[C2 (DMP)] The operator is monotone / inverse-positive ($\bar L^{-1}\ge 0$) under a mesh
    + time-step constraint; the novelty is a \emph{lower} bound $\Delta t\,\mu/h^2\ge 3$ absent
    in classical 2nd-order schemes (Theorem 3.9; Theorem 4.1 adds the reaction constraint
    $\Delta t\cdot \max|F''|\le\varepsilon$ for the nonlinear Allen--Cahn case).
\end{description}

\section{Method}
Software: Python 3, \texttt{numpy}, \texttt{scipy} (\texttt{splu}, dense \texttt{inv}),
\texttt{sympy} (manufactured sources). No paper code used. Light time-stepping runs local; heavy
$320\times320$ Table-6.2 run on \texttt{uicgpu} ($8\times$A100). Free endpoints only.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{$D$ matrices} (\texttt{fdmats.py}): \texttt{build\_D1\_D2(n,h)} (4th) and
    \texttt{build\_D1\_D2\_2nd(n,h)} (2nd) as $n\times(n{+}2)$ matrices acting on
    $[\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_{n+1}]$, exactly per eqs.~(2.7--2.8). Sanity: raw truncation orders
    $\sim 2$ for both, matching Remark~1.
  \item \textbf{2D operator} (\texttt{solver.py}): Kronecker assembly of
    $[\mathrm{diag}(u)(D_{1x}\otimes I_y) + \mathrm{diag}(v)(I_x\otimes D_{1y})
     - \mu(D_{2x}\otimes I_y + I_x\otimes D_{2y})]$ on interior unknowns (column-major
    \texttt{vec}), with Dirichlet boundary data moved to the RHS via an interior/boundary
    column split.
  \item \textbf{Validation first} (\texttt{validate\_steady.py}): steady convection--diffusion
    $\phi + u\phi_x + v\phi_y - \mu\Delta\phi = f$, manufactured
    $\phi=\sin^2 x\,\sin y$ on $[0,2\pi]^2$. Confirms superconvergence before any
    time-dependent test.
  \item \textbf{Table 6.1} (\texttt{table61.py}): Allen--Cahn, $\mu=0.1$, $\varepsilon=0.05$,
    $u=v=\sin(y-x)$, $F=\tfrac14(\phi^2-1)^2$, manufactured
    $\phi=(0.75+0.25\sin t)\sin y\sin^2 x$ (vanishes on $\partial[0,2\pi]^2$
    $\Rightarrow$ homogeneous Dirichlet). BDF3 IMEX (conv-diff implicit; reaction/source
    explicit with 3rd-order extrapolation). \texttt{splu} prefactor. Errors at $T=0.2$;
    grids 9/19/79/159.
  \item \textbf{Table 6.2} (\texttt{table62.py}): stream/vorticity accuracy test, exact
    $\omega = -2e^{-2\mu t}\sin x \sin y$, $\mu=0.1$, periodic BC (periodic $D_1/D_2$, $N$
    even). BDF3 IMEX, \texttt{splu} prefactor. Errors at $T=0.2$; grids 40/80/160/320.
  \item \textbf{Discrete maximum principle} (\texttt{monotonicity.py}): form
    $\bar L = I/\Delta t + \mathrm{conv} - \mu\Delta_h$, take dense inverse, check entrywise
    non-negativity in-regime and out-of-regime (lower $\Delta t$ bound violated). An earlier
    direct nonlinear bound test at the paper's illustrative $239\times239$ / $\Delta t=\Delta x/6$
    setting (\texttt{maxprinciple.py}) shows overshoot; \S\ref{sec:critique} explains this is
    outside the theorem regime and is consistent with the paper.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Genuine Critique}\label{sec:critique}
This section is deliberately skeptical. The verdict remains REPLICATED, but honest boundaries
should be recorded.

\paragraph{1. Sec 6.2 figures are outside the DMP regime.}
Figures 6.1/6.2 in the paper use $\Delta t = \Delta x/6$ (or $/7$) on a $239\times239$ grid. For
$\mu=0.01$, $\varepsilon=0.05$, $F=\tfrac14(\phi^2-1)^2$ (so $\max|F''|=2$ on $[-1,1]$),
Theorem~4.1 requires
$h \le \min\!\bigl(0.216\,\mu/\|u\|,\ \sqrt{\mu\varepsilon/\max|F''|}\bigr)
    \approx \min(0.00216, 0.0158) = 0.00216$,
i.e.\ approximately \emph{2900} grid points --- far finer than 239. Our direct nonlinear run at
$239\times239$ (\texttt{maxprinciple.py}) does show modest overshoot beyond $\pm 1$ (run max
$\approx 1.03$ for the 4th-order scheme, $\approx 1.06$ for the 2nd-order companion). This is
\emph{consistent} with the paper --- which never claims bound preservation at that resolution ---
but a reader could be forgiven for expecting Sec 6.2 to display the DMP itself. It does not.

\paragraph{2. What we verified vs.\ what we did not.}
We verified the operator-level rigorous claim (Thm 3.9: $\bar L^{-1}\ge 0$) by direct dense
inversion and elementwise sign check. We did \emph{not} verify the fully nonlinear Theorem 4.1
inside its regime (that would require $\sim$2900$^2$ interior unknowns and dense inverses of a
$\sim 8\times 10^6$-vector operator, which is infeasible at reasonable cost). Our claim is
therefore: the linear-operator monotonicity underpinning the nonlinear theorem is confirmed on
achievable meshes, and the paper's out-of-regime figures are internally honest.

\paragraph{3. Time-integration coupling.}
The rigorous DMP result assumes backward Euler with explicit reaction. Our accuracy tables use
BDF3 IMEX to push the temporal error below the $O(h^4)$ spatial target; the DMP tests
independently reduce to backward Euler. There is no contradiction, but the reader should note
that BDF3 IMEX is used for accuracy, not for verifying the DMP itself.

\paragraph{4. Residual $\sim 10\%$ gaps at the $10^{-6}$--$10^{-8}$ error level.}
On the finest Allen--Cahn grid the $\ell^1$ error sits close to the temporal/round-off floor.
Small relative differences to the paper values there (up to $\sim 10\%$) reflect that floor, not
scheme discrepancy: paper and replication show identical convergence \emph{orders}. This is a
common but honest limitation of accuracy-only tables at low error magnitude.

\paragraph{5. Paper tables internally consistent.}
We found no internal contradictions in the paper's numeric tables; our numbers align with theirs
in both magnitude and order, on every listed grid.

\paragraph{6. Judges free but not model-diverse.}
The three judges are all Argo-hosted (gpt-5.2, gemini-2.5-pro, gpt-4.1). Opus was excluded per
wave rules. Consensus is real but is not evidence for cross-vendor coverage; readers should
weight the multi-judge result accordingly.

\section*{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED.} Convergence orders, table magnitudes, and the operator-level DMP (Thm 3.9)
all reproduce; the novel lower-$\Delta t$ constraint is shown to be genuinely necessary; the
paper's out-of-regime Sec 6.2 figures are honestly out-of-regime, not contradicted.
