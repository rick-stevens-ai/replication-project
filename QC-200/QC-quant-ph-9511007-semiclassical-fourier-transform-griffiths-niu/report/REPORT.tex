\begin{abstract}
We independently replicate the central quantitative claim of Griffiths
\& Niu (1996): the quantum Fourier transform (QFT) followed by
measurement can be re-implemented as a strictly \emph{semiclassical}
circuit --- Hadamards + single-bit measurements + single-bit phase gates
classically conditioned on prior measurement outcomes --- producing
\emph{identical} measurement statistics as the standard QFT circuit
that uses coherent controlled-phase two-qubit gates.  We build both
variants directly in Qiskit (v2.5.0) using the modern dynamic-circuits
\texttt{if\_test} primitive for the classical feed-forward, execute them
on the Aer state-vector simulator, and verify equivalence over
\emph{all} computational-basis inputs at $n\in\{3,4\}$ qubits and over a
family of Shor-style periodic superposition inputs at $n\in\{3,4\}$.
The maximum total-variation distance (TVD) between the standard-QFT and
the semiclassical-QFT measurement distributions was $0.032$ (on
$|x\rangle$ inputs, $8192$ shots per input) and $0.011$ (on periodic
superpositions, $16384$ shots), both well within shot noise. Gate
counts match the paper exactly: the semiclassical variant uses
$0$ two-qubit gates versus $n(n-1)/2$ for the standard variant.
\textbf{Verdict: REPLICATED.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper summary}

Shor's algorithms (factoring, discrete log) end with a QFT on an
$n=s{+}1$-qubit register followed by a computational-basis
measurement. The standard QFT circuit (\cite{griffiths_niu_1996}~Fig.~1)
uses $n$ Hadamards and $n(n-1)/2$ two-qubit controlled-phase gates:
\[
\ket{a}\;\xrightarrow{\text{QFT}}\;
F\ket{a}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}\sum_{c=0}^{q-1} e^{2\pi i a c/q}\ket{c},
\quad q=2^n.
\]
Griffiths \& Niu observe that because controlled-phase gates leave
their control bit unchanged, one may equally well \emph{measure} the
control immediately after its Hadamard and then have the classical
outcome deterministically decide whether or not to apply a single-qubit
phase to each less-significant qubit. Iterating this replacement
throughout the circuit yields their Fig.~2: a bit-by-bit sequential
protocol in which the $j$-th qubit is (a)~Hadamard-ed, (b)~measured
into a classical bit $c_j$, then (c)~each not-yet-measured qubit
$k<j$ has a phase gate $P(2\pi/2^{j-k+1})$ applied to it \emph{only if
the measurement result $c_j$ was $1$}. Zero coherent two-qubit gates
survive.

The paper's central quantitative claim (its "success in simplifying")
is that this semiclassical circuit produces \emph{identical}
measurement statistics as the standard QFT circuit followed by
measurement. It also makes a precise gate-count claim, and offers
consistent-histories intuition for why the substitution is valid.

\section{Method (exact commands and versions)}

\paragraph{Environment.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Host: \texttt{CherryRd} (macOS Darwin 25.3.0), CPU only.
\item Python: \texttt{3.13} inside a project-local venv.
\item Qiskit \texttt{2.5.0}, qiskit-aer \texttt{0.17.2}, NumPy latest, no GPU.
\item LLM inference: none required (this is a math/simulation replication);
      per QC-100 wave rule we only use free endpoints (Argo localhost:44497),
      and in fact used none here.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Files.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{report/evidence/replicate\_semiclassical\_qft.py}\ ---\ builds both
      circuit variants, runs Aer over all $|x\rangle$ inputs at $n{=}3,4$,
      computes TVDs against the analytic QFT distribution and against each other,
      writes \texttt{results.json} and \texttt{summary.txt}.
\item \texttt{report/evidence/replicate\_periodic\_input.py}\ ---\ replaces the
      $|x\rangle$ input with a Shor-style periodic superposition
      $\ket{\psi_p}=\frac{1}{\sqrt K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\ket{k\cdot p\bmod 2^n}$ for
      several $(n,p)$, so the target QFT distribution is highly non-uniform
      (concentrated on $c$ with $c\cdot p/2^n\in\mathbb{Z}$). Writes
      \texttt{results\_periodic.json}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Standard-QFT circuit (built from primitives, no library QFT).}
For each $j$ from $n-1$ down to $0$: \verb|H(q[j])|, then for $k$ from $j-1$
down to $0$: \verb|cp(2*pi/2^(j-k+1), q[k], q[j])|. After the loop, measure
\verb|q[j] -> c[n-1-j]| so that the classical register read little-endian equals
the integer $c$ used in the paper.

\paragraph{Semiclassical circuit (dynamic circuit).}
For each $j$ from $n-1$ down to $0$: \verb|H(q[j])|, then \verb|measure(q[j], c[n-1-j])|,
then for each $k<j$: \verb|with qc.if_test((c[n-1-j], 1)): qc.p(2*pi/2^(j-k+1), q[k])|.
The Qiskit \texttt{if\_test} block is exactly the classical-feed-forward
Griffiths \& Niu describe as ``a pulse of several volts traveling down a
coaxial cable''. Aer's \texttt{if\_test} handling is well documented and
was cross-verified against Qiskit's built-in dynamic-circuit primitives.

\paragraph{Reference distribution.}
For $\ket{x}$ inputs the analytic post-QFT distribution is uniform
$1/2^n$ (this is one motivation for the periodic-input test, since the
$\ket{x}$ probability can hide phase bugs). For periodic-superposition
inputs we compute the exact QFT-probability distribution
$\bigl|(M\ket{\psi_p})_c\bigr|^2$ where $M_{c,x}=e^{2\pi i x c/N}/\sqrt N$ in
NumPy.

\paragraph{Shots \& seeds.}
\texttt{shots=8192} per input for the $|x\rangle$ sweep;
\texttt{shots=16384} per input for the periodic-superposition sweep;
Aer seed derived from a fixed integer + input index for reproducibility.

\section{Results vs paper}

\subsection{Computational-basis inputs $\ket{x}$, $x=0..2^n-1$}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrrrr@{}}
\toprule
$n$ & inputs & shots & max TVD (full vs analytic) & max TVD (semi vs analytic) & max TVD (full vs semi) \\
\midrule
3 & 8  & 8192 & 0.0161 & 0.0131 & 0.0195 \\
4 & 16 & 8192 & 0.0201 & 0.0186 & 0.0316 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Both the standard QFT and the semiclassical QFT match the analytic
uniform distribution to within Monte-Carlo shot noise, and match each
other to within the same tolerance. Every one of the $8+16=24$
per-input pairwise TVDs is below the pre-registered $0.08$ threshold
(chosen at $\sim 4\sigma$ of the binomial noise for 16 bins/8192
shots), and the maximum observed is $0.0316$.

\subsection{Shor-style periodic superposition inputs}

Here the analytic post-QFT distribution is a comb concentrated on the
divisor-lattice values.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrlllrrr@{}}
\toprule
$n$ & period $p$ & expected peaks & observed peaks & shots & TVD(full|ana) & TVD(semi|ana) & TVD(full|semi) \\
\midrule
3 & 2 & \{0,4\}                 & \{0,4\}                 & 16384 & 0.0018 & 0.0011 & 0.0029 \\
3 & 4 & \{0,2,4,6\}             & \{0,2,4,6\}             & 16384 & 0.0035 & 0.0045 & 0.0063 \\
4 & 2 & \{0,8\}                 & \{0,8\}                 & 16384 & 0.0018 & 0.0012 & 0.0006 \\
4 & 4 & \{0,4,8,12\}            & \{0,4,8,12\}            & 16384 & 0.0035 & 0.0074 & 0.0067 \\
4 & 8 & \{0,2,4,6,8,10,12,14\}  & \{0,2,4,6,8,10,12,14\}  & 16384 & 0.0060 & 0.0089 & 0.0107 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The peak structure is reproduced exactly by both circuit variants in
all 5 cases, and the empirical TVDs between the standard and
semiclassical outputs are $\le 0.011$ --- again well within shot noise
for $16384$ shots on $\le 16$ bins. This is the strong test: the
periodic-superposition QFT distribution is far from uniform, so any
phase-management bug in the feed-forward would be visible immediately.

\subsection{Gate counts}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrr@{}}
\toprule
$n$ & Standard-QFT 2q CP gates & Semiclassical 2q CP gates & Semiclassical 1q classically-cond.\ phase gates \\
\midrule
3 & 3  & 0 & 3 \\
4 & 6  & 0 & 6 \\
5 & 10 & 0 & 10 \\
8 & 28 & 0 & 28 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Exactly the $n(n-1)/2$ scaling Griffiths \& Niu quote.  Note the
number of classically-conditioned $1$-qubit phase gates matches the
number of eliminated $2$-qubit gates one-for-one; this is a
consequence of unrolling the paper's compact ``per box'' recipe
(Eqs.~10--11) into the pairwise pattern used in Fig.~1.

\section{Verdict}

\textbf{REPLICATED.} All quantitative claims of Griffiths~\& Niu are
reproduced within Monte-Carlo error using real Qiskit dynamic
circuits: (i)~semiclassical output distribution $=$ standard-QFT
output distribution on both trivial basis and non-trivial periodic
inputs, (ii)~gate-count scaling as claimed, (iii)~zero coherent
two-qubit gates in the semiclassical variant. The consistent-histories
argument (C5) is an interpretive framing rather than a testable
empirical claim, so we do not attempt to verify it; the empirical
equivalence which the interpretation is meant to explain \emph{is}
verified.
