\section{Paper summary}
RB extracts average gate infidelity $r = \tfrac{d-1}{d}(1-f)$ from the decay
$p_m = A f^m + B$ of the survival probability under length-$m$ random Clifford
sequences. Helsen et al.\ derive tighter variance bounds (eqs.\ 9--11) whose
required per-length sample count is \emph{asymptotically independent of qubit
count}, and recommend IRLS over OLS due to the intrinsic heteroskedasticity of
RB data.

\section{Method (condensed)}
\textbf{Environment.} Python 3.13; qiskit 2.5.0; qiskit-aer 0.17.2; numpy 2.5.0.
\textbf{Simulation.} 2-qubit Clifford RB with lengths $m\in\{1,2,5,10,20,40,75,125,200\}$,
$N_{\max}=100$ sequences per $m$, 400 shots each; depolarizing noise
$p_\mathrm{cx}=10^{-2}$, $p_\mathrm{1q}=10^{-3}$; inverse-Clifford at end;
survival = $P(|00\rangle)$.
\textbf{Bootstrap.} 300 resamples for
$N\in\{5,10,15,20,30,50,75,100\}$; fit $A f^m + B$ via bounded
\texttt{curve\_fit}; $r = \tfrac{3}{4}(1-f)$.
\textbf{Analytical bound.} Eq.\,(10) SPAM-free unitarity-simplified variance
$+ $ Chebyshev at $\delta=\varepsilon=10^{-2}$; benchmarked against the paper's
$N=173$ single-qubit example.

\section{Results (headline)}
\textbf{Full-$N$ fit:} $A=0.7347$, $B=0.2486$, $f=0.97879$,
$r=\mathbf{0.015909}$; consistent at $\sim$5\,\% with the analytic prediction
$r_\mathrm{pred}\approx 1.47\!\cdot\!p_\mathrm{cx}\!\cdot\!\tfrac{3}{4} + n_{1q}\!\cdot\!p_{1q}\!\cdot\!\tfrac{3}{4} \approx 0.015$
from the empirically measured 1.47 CX/Clifford cost.

\section{Critique --- honest assessment}

\textbf{What we actually did (in scope).} A real Qiskit Aer 2-qubit RB
simulation with the noise-attached-to-native-gates convention, a proper
inverse-Clifford, a 300-resample bootstrap giving both mean and std of the
fitted $r$ as a function of $N$, and a closed-form implementation of the
paper's eq.\,(10) benchmarked to their example number. This is a genuine
reproduction of the practical scientific claim on independently generated data.

\textbf{What the critique flags below cover.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Sample-complexity was tested via bootstrap variance, but only
  once, at $d=4$.} The paper's variance framework (eqs.\,9--11) predicts a
  specific functional form $\sigma_r^2(N,m,r,d,q)$; we measured
  $\sigma_r(N)$ at fixed $(m\text{-set},r,d,q)$ and verified the qualitative
  ``few-sample'' behavior, but did \emph{not} compare our measured
  $\sigma_r(N)$ against the paper's predicted variance curve. That is a
  measurable claim we could have tested and did not.
\item \emph{No head-to-head comparison against the older Wallman--Flammia
  sample counts on our own data.} The paper's argument-of-value is that
  their $N$ is much smaller than ref [24]'s. We reproduced their bound's
  \emph{value} for the paper's example but did not simulate an actual
  ref-[24]-sized ensemble to demonstrate the empirical gap.
\item \emph{Small-$n$ bias not explicitly tested.} At $N=5$ we report a
  bias of only $\sim 1.7\times 10^{-5}$ against the $N=100$ reference, but
  we did not check for structured bias (skewness of the bootstrap
  distribution, coverage of nominal CIs, etc.). Curve-fit on very short
  ensembles can produce miscovered intervals even when the point estimate
  looks unbiased.
\item \emph{Eq.\,(9) was not implemented.} The 13\,\% gap between our
  $N=195$ and the paper's $N=173$ is definitionally the difference between
  eq.\,(10) (looser) and eq.\,(9) (tighter). We did not attempt eq.\,(9),
  which is the actual bound the paper uses to justify the $N=173$ number.
\item \emph{$q$-scaling (Fig.\,2b) untested.} The paper's most striking
  claim --- that $N$ is essentially $q$-independent up to prefactors --- is
  \emph{spot-checked from Fig.\,2} rather than reproduced. Reproducing this
  would require evaluating eq.\,(11) across $q\in\{1,\dots,6\}$.
\item \emph{OLS-vs-IRLS (C4) untested.} We used bounded \texttt{curve\_fit}
  (nonlinear OLS) throughout. The paper's fitter-choice claim is not
  re-litigated here.
\item \emph{Single noise model.} All numerics are under the standard
  isotropic depolarizing model. RB is known to behave differently under
  non-Markovian, coherent, or crosstalk noise; the paper's variance bound
  formally assumes Markovian gate-independent noise. Robustness of the
  ``few-sample'' claim under realistic non-Markovian noise is not tested.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{What we did \emph{not} claim but the queue verdict might imply.}
``REPLICATED'' here means C3 was empirically reproduced on independent
simulation data and C1 was reproduced analytically. It does \emph{not} mean
every claim in Helsen et al.\ was independently checked; C2, C4, C5 remain
UNTESTED and are flagged as such in the claims table.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED} for the central practical claim (C3) with an independent
Qiskit Aer simulation and bootstrap; \textbf{MATCH} for the analytical
bound C1 within 13\,\% (attributable to using eq.\,10 rather than eq.\,9);
UNTESTED for C2, C4, C5.
