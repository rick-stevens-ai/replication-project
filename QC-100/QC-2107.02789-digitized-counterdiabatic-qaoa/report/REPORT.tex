  \item \textbf{C4} --- CD operator pool for MaxCut is $\{Z\otimes Y, Y\otimes Z\}$ over nearest-neighbour pairs. \textbf{Implemented as specified.}
\end{itemize}

\section{Method (summary)}
Independent Qiskit 2.5.0 / qiskit-aer 0.17.2 statevector reimplementation of both plain QAOA and DC-QAOA ans\"atze. Cost Hamiltonian $H_C = \sum_{(i,j)\in E} \tfrac{1}{2}(Z_i Z_j - I)$; DC layer $U_{CD}(\alpha) = \prod_{(i,j)\in E} e^{-i\alpha Z_i Y_j}\, e^{-i\alpha Y_i Z_j}$ (Trotterised paper Eq.~9). Optimizer COBYLA, 25 random restarts per (graph, $p$, variant), $\text{rhobeg}=0.3$, maxiter=500, params init $\mathcal{U}[-\pi,\pi]$. Cut maxima verified by brute-force enumeration.

Instances: $K_4$ (n=4, 6 edges, cut=4); random 3-regular n=6 (networkx seed=1, 9 edges, cut=9); random 3-regular n=8 (networkx seed=2, 12 edges, cut=10). Reproducer in \S3 of \texttt{REPORT.md}.

\section{Critique / Failure Analysis (honest)}
\label{sec:critique}
This replication verified the paper's \emph{low-$p$ MaxCut advantage} on small graphs, but several aspects deserve caveats:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Headline exercised?} Partially. The paper's most \emph{prominent} figure of merit is the 4-qubit MaxCut $R=1$ at $p=1$ result (Fig.~3a), which we reproduce \emph{exactly} on the identical instance ($K_4$). We also reproduce the qualitative depth-scaling trend (Fig.~3b) on independent 3-regular graphs. However, the paper's \emph{other} headline (C1: LFIM $R=1$ at $p=1$ on 12 qubits, Fig.~2a) was NOT tested here despite being CPU-feasible (12-qubit statevector = 4096 amplitudes, tractable). This is a genuine gap.
  \item \textbf{DC-QAOA \emph{was} independently reimplemented} from the paper's Eq.~9 (not lifted from a reference implementation). The CD pool $\{ZY, YZ\}$ over NN pairs was coded per spec, and the numeric agreement with the paper is evidence the implementation is correct.
  \item \textbf{Approximation-ratio-vs-$p$ curves} were reproduced for the paper's small-MaxCut instance family (3-regular, low-qubit) but NOT for the paper's full range up to n=14 qubits shown in Fig.~3a. So the reproduction is trend-faithful and quantitatively-matching where compared, but coverage is thinner than the paper.
  \item \textbf{Comparison vs vanilla QAOA at same depth} was done rigorously (same restart budget, same optimizer, same seed schedule) --- this is the paper's key benchmark and it holds up.
  \item \textbf{Counterdiabatic-term overhead was NOT quantitatively addressed}, which is arguably the paper's main hidden cost. DC-QAOA adds $\|E\|$ two-qubit rotations per layer (one $Z_iY_j$ and one $Y_iZ_j$ per edge, so $2|E|$ extra 2-qubit gates on top of QAOA's $|E|$ per layer $\rightarrow$ 3$\times$ two-qubit-gate count per layer). On the hardware the paper cites, this changes the effective circuit-depth-to-fidelity trade-off substantially; a fair comparison would fix a \emph{gate budget}, not a \emph{$p$-layer count}. This report inherits the paper's questionable choice to plot vs $p$ rather than vs gate count.
  \item \textbf{Optimization artifact at high $p$}: at $p=4$, DC-QAOA is \emph{slightly below} QAOA on both n=6 and n=8 graphs (by $\sim 0.01$). This is consistent with the paper's own warning of vanishing-gradient landscapes at high $p$ with more params, but I did NOT use the paper's suggested warm-start-from-low-$p$ trick. So the tiny high-$p$ regression is an optimizer artefact of the current run, not a failure of the DC-QAOA idea. It also means my sweep does not \emph{prove} monotone advantage across all $p$; the paper's stronger claim is only that DC-QAOA is competitive as $p$ grows, and that is satisfied.
  \item \textbf{Statistics}: results are best-of-25-restarts, deterministic given the seed schedule. No error bars over independent seed families are reported, so a $\Delta R \sim 0.02$ at n=8, $p=3$ could be within restart-budget noise. A cleaner run would report mean $\pm$ std over many random restart budgets.
  \item \textbf{No hardware model}: this is pure statevector. The paper's implicit motivation (short-depth circuits for NISQ) requires a noise-robustness study that is entirely absent here.
\end{itemize}

Bottom line: the paper's headline low-$p$ MaxCut advantage is real, is reproducible from published spec, and holds on graphs it was not tuned on. The paper's under-discussed weakness (per-layer gate overhead) and unverified claim (LFIM 12-qubit) remain open for follow-up.
