\section{Open Questions}
\label{sec:open-questions}

The following five questions are genuinely open after this replication --- each has a
concrete, tractable probe that could be executed on a laptop-scale simulator with the
same free-endpoint tooling used here.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Block-diagonal / diagonal QFI approximations vs.\ ansatz depth.}
    This replication only tested a single-layer 4-parameter HEA, where
    \texttt{approx="block-diag"} matched full-metric behavior to machine precision. The paper
    hand-derives the full analytic $F$ for exactly this shallow case; how the block-diagonal
    approximation degrades at depth is untested.
    \emph{Next steps:} depth sweep (1, 2, 4, 8 layers) of a brick-wall HEA on $H_2$ and a 4--6
    qubit LiH toy, comparing vanilla GD, block-diagonal QNG, and full-metric QNG
    (\texttt{approx=None}); report iteration count \emph{and} total quantum-expectation-evaluation
    count to chemical accuracy; find the depth at which block-diagonal QNG loses $>$20\% of the
    iteration-count advantage that full QNG retains.

  \item \textbf{QNG under parameter-shift + shot noise.}
    The exact-statevector setting used here matches the paper but hides the well-known
    F-inversion sensitivity to metric-tensor noise. Whether the $1.45$--$1.75\times$ speedup
    survives realistic shot budgets is unknown.
    \emph{Next steps:} repeat the $H_2$ experiment with finite shots
    ($10^2,10^3,10^4,10^5$) using parameter-shift gradients and Hadamard-test metric-tensor
    estimation at the same shot budget; measure iteration count to $|E-E_{\text{exact}}|<10^{-2}$,
    trajectory variance over 20 seeds, and rate of $F$-inversion blowup requiring $\lambda$
    regularisation; report speedup in \emph{total shots}, not iterations.

  \item \textbf{Transfer of QNG advantage to QAOA and variational QML.}
    The paper and this replication address VQE only. QAOA parameter concentration and
    QML barren-plateau behavior differ from VQE eigenvalue landscapes; the geometric-preconditioning
    argument is loss-agnostic but empirically untested here.
    \emph{Next steps:} set up (i) QAOA on max-cut for random 6-vertex 3-regular graphs at
    $p=1,2,4$, and (ii) a 4-qubit variational classifier on iris; compare vanilla GD, Adam, and
    QNG on iteration count to reach 95\% of best-achievable loss over 10 seeds; if QNG's advantage
    evaporates on QAOA, characterize the loss-landscape features (barren-plateau proximity,
    $F$ condition number) that predict this.

  \item \textbf{Hardware-noise robustness.}
    The paper studies noise-free simulation; on NISQ hardware both the energy and $F$ are biased
    by decoherence and coherent errors. QNG's $F^{-1}$ step could either compensate for
    systematic overrotation or amplify a biased metric estimate.
    \emph{Next steps:} re-run the $H_2$ Fig.~5 experiment under three PennyLane noise models
    (depolarizing at $p=10^{-3},10^{-2},10^{-1}$; amplitude-damping at $T_1=50\,\mu$s / 100~ns
    gate time; coherent overrotation $1\%,5\%$ on all $R_y$); report whether QNG still beats GD
    in iterations, whether the noisy fixed-point offset is worse under QNG (F-inversion bias),
    and how $\lambda$ regularisation trades $F$-inversion stability against optimization speed.

  \item \textbf{Adam-QNG and other adaptive-momentum hybrids.}
    Modern classical optimization is dominated by adaptive methods; a natural-gradient step with
    an Adam-style second-moment schedule (K-FAC + Adam style) is straightforward but not
    standard in quantum optimization. Whether the diagonal variance rescaling and $F^{-1}$
    off-diagonal correlation preconditioning are redundant, complementary, or antagonistic on
    VQE landscapes is unknown.
    \emph{Next steps:} implement
    $\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta\, F^{-1} m / (\sqrt{v}+\epsilon)$
    with Adam moments $m,v$; compare Adam-QNG, pure QNG, pure Adam, and vanilla GD on
    (i)~$H_2$ Fig.~5 with tolerance $10^{-6}$, (ii)~4-qubit LiH VQE, (iii)~6-qubit BeH$_2$ VQE;
    report iteration count and expectation-evaluation count to chemical accuracy. Falsifiable
    prediction: diagonal variance rescaling and $F^{-1}$ preconditioning capture different
    curvature information and combine additively for a $>1.5\times$ further speedup over
    the max of either alone.
\end{enumerate}
