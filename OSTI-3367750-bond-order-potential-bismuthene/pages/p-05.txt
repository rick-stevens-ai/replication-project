418 Mechanical Fracture. The mechanical response of bismuthene
419 was assessed by performing uniaxial tensile tests in molecular
420 dynamics. After equilibration, a uniaxial engineering strain was
421 applied incrementally along a selected crystallographic direction at a
422 constant strain rate of 1 X 10~* ps~!. The stress was recorded at each
423 increment and the simulation was continued until structural failure
424 occurred. This procedure was applied to both the armchair and zigzag
428 directions to enable consistent comparison of directional fracture
426 behavior across the different RL-optimized potential parameter sets.
427 Perturbation and Modal Analysis. To probe out-of-plane
428 vibrational behavior, we performed a modal perturbation analysis on
429 the two-atom unit cell of low-buckled bismuthene. The two atoms
430 were displaced in opposite directions along the z-axis in incremental
431 steps, transforming the lattice continuously from its equilibrium low-
432. buckled configuration to a flat geometry and then back again. At each
433 displacement, the total potential energy was computed using each
434 Tersoff potential and compared with DFT reference energies.

435 Two simulation conditions were considered: (i) in-plane relaxation,
436 where the simulation box was allowed to relax in the x and y
437 directions, and (ii) fixed box, where lattice vectors were held constant.
438 This analysis provides insight into how each potential captures the
439 energetics associated with the low-buckled to flat transition, which is
440 strongly linked to the behavior of the flexural (ZA) phonon mode.
441 Mode Griineisen Parameters. Mode-resolved Griineisen
442 parameters were computed using Phonopy*” with force constants
443 obtained from phonolamps. A finite-difference approach was
444 employed, where phonon frequencies were calculated for uniformly
448 dilated lattices with + 0.5% strain. For each phonon mode, the
446 Griineisen parameter was extracted from the logarithmic derivative of
447 the mode frequency with respect to the lattice dilation. These
448 quantities provide an additional measure of anharmonicity and enable
449 direct comparison of thermal-transport-relevant behavior across the
450 three RL-optimized parameter sets.
