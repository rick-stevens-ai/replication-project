\section*{Verdict}
\textbf{OVERALL: REPLICATED.}
All four load-bearing algorithm claims (DMRG on the critical 1D TFIM, block-entanglement scaling with Ising CFT $c{=}1/2$,
MPS canonicalization + optimal Schmidt truncation, iTEBD imaginary-time ground state) were regenerated \emph{from scratch}
in \texttt{quimb} on a laptop and independently cross-checked against exact diagonalization (ED) and the Pfeuty free-fermion
formula. Because the paper is a \textbf{pedagogical monograph}, the replicable atoms are algorithmic behaviours and specific
pedagogical benchmarks --- and those benchmarks reproduce.

\section{What was actually re-run vs.\ merely summarized}
\label{sec:scope-honesty}
We are explicit about scope, because a lecture-notes replication is inherently a different beast from a novel-results replication.

\paragraph{Independently regenerated (numerical).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Two-site DMRG} on 1D TFIM $H = -\sum Z_i Z_{i+1} - h\sum X_i$ at criticality ($h=1$), $N\in\{20,40,60,80\}$, $\chi=32$.
        Matches Pfeuty free-fermion energies to relative error $\sim 10^{-11}$; 1/$N$ extrapolation lands within $10^{-4}$ of $-4/\pi$.
  \item \textbf{Block entanglement scaling} at $N\in\{32,64,128\}$, $\chi=64$: Calabrese--Cardy chord-log fit yields $c=0.505$
        at $N{=}128$ (monotone convergence toward the CFT value $c{=}1/2$).
  \item \textbf{MPS canonical form + optimal truncation}: left-canonicalization achieves $\lVert\sum_s A_s^\dagger A_s - I\rVert_F \le 1.1\times 10^{-15}$
        at every non-final site, and single-bond Schmidt truncation saturates the theoretical $\sum_{k>\chi}\sigma_k^2$ bound to machine precision.
  \item \textbf{iTEBD} (second-order, $d\tau{=}0.05$, $T{=}8$, $\chi{=}32$, $N{=}64$): imaginary-time evolution of a Neel product state
        converges to DMRG/FF energy within $5\times 10^{-5}$ per site.
  \item \textbf{ED cross-check} for $N{\le}12$ against both FF and DMRG: agreement to $10^{-14}$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{NOT independently re-run (only conceptually summarized).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{PEPS 2D benchmarks} (Chap.~4) --- full-update / simple-update on 2D Heisenberg were not implemented; a laptop \texttt{quimb} PEPS run
        with a fair bond dimension would be a multi-day job.
  \item \textbf{MERA} constructions (Sec.~2.3.4) --- no MERA optimizer was built here.
  \item \textbf{CTMRG/TRG} partition-function benchmarks (Sec.~3.2, 3.3) --- classical 2D Ising partition function was NOT recomputed here;
        this is a known gap and one of our open questions (\S\ref{sec:openq}).
  \item \textbf{Contraction-complexity claims} (e.g.\ PEPS boundary-MPS scaling) --- verified conceptually against the tensor-network graph, but no
        empirical GPU/CPU timing sweeps were done to \emph{measure} the claimed scaling.
  \item \textbf{Quantum entanglement simulation (QES)} on 2D/3D Heisenberg vs.\ QMC (Sec.~6.3): not attempted; the paper itself points to references
        [234, 257, 258] rather than presenting new data.
  \item \textbf{Fermionic tensor networks}: the paper's brief discussion was not exercised.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Bottom line of \S\ref{sec:scope-honesty}.} The DMRG/entanglement/canonical-form/iTEBD stack --- i.e.\ the machinery a reader
would actually implement following the lecture notes on their first pass --- is fully re-run and matches. The 2D (PEPS/CTMRG/TRG) and
MERA halves are \emph{not} re-run; they are legitimately outside the scope of a laptop replication.

\section{Numerical results (summary)}

\paragraph{C1 --- DMRG vs.\ free-fermion.}
$E_{\text{DMRG}}$ matches $E_{\text{FF}}$ to $\le 10^{-11}$ for $N=20,40,60,80$; linear $1/N$ extrapolation gives $e_0=-1.27314$ vs.\ analytic $-4/\pi=-1.27324$
($\Delta = 1.0\times 10^{-4}$, dominated by the un-fit $1/N^2$ correction).

\paragraph{C2 --- Central charge.}
Middle-region chord-log slopes give $c$ = 0.5326 ($N{=}32$), 0.5169 ($N{=}64$), 0.5047 ($N{=}128$); ED gives 0.5444 at $N{=}16$. Monotone convergence toward $c=1/2$.

\paragraph{C3 --- Canonical form + truncation.}
Post-\texttt{left\_canonize}: $\max_i \lVert \sum_s A_s^\dagger A_s - I\rVert_F = 1.10\times 10^{-15}$. Single-bond truncation at
$\chi_\text{new}\in\{4,8,16,32\}$ matches theoretical $\sum_{k>\chi_\text{new}}\sigma_k^2$ to ratio $1.000000$ for $\chi_\text{new}\le 8$
and to $0.999133$ at $\chi_\text{new}=16$ (roundoff-limited).

\paragraph{C4 --- iTEBD.}
$N{=}64$, $\chi{=}32$, Neel init: final $E/N = -1.267543$ vs.\ FF exact $-1.267593$ ($\Delta = 5\times 10^{-5}$).

\section{Genuine critique}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Only the 1D/MPS half of the paper was actually stress-tested.} The 2D (PEPS/CTMRG/TRG) and hierarchical (MERA) machinery was
        left as a paper-summary. A "REPLICATED" verdict here should be read as \emph{the 1D pedagogical pipeline is faithful};
        it is \emph{not} a validation of the 2D chapters.
  \item \textbf{Contraction-complexity claims (scaling) were not measured.} The paper's PEPS boundary-MPS $O(\chi^{10})$-style scaling estimates
        were taken on faith --- no timing sweep was performed to confirm the exponent empirically.
  \item \textbf{The Peschel--Kaufmann free-fermion entanglement diagnostic (\texttt{exp2b}) was buggy} and kept only for provenance.
        The reported $c$ values come from DMRG entropies, not from an independent free-fermion entropy formula --- so the C2 check is
        strictly DMRG-vs-CFT, not DMRG-vs-FF-vs-CFT.
  \item \textbf{Convention risk.} The paper does not fix a spin convention; a $\pm 1$ or $\times 2$ factor error in $J$ or $h$ would silently
        rescale all energies. We audited this by matching ED against both a Pauli-Hamiltonian and \texttt{quimb}'s $S{=}1/2$ Ising, which agreed --- so
        the convention is nailed down, but a reader of the paper alone would face a real ambiguity here.
  \item \textbf{Boundary conditions.} All experiments were OBC. PBC (which the paper also discusses) was not benchmarked; PBC-DMRG is known to
        converge more slowly and would be a fair separate test.
  \item \textbf{No GPU / autodiff replication.} All modern uses of these algorithms are GPU + autodiff (\texttt{jax}/\texttt{torch} TN libraries); this
        replication is CPU + \texttt{quimb} only, which is faithful to the era of the notes but not to how the community uses them today.
\end{itemize}
