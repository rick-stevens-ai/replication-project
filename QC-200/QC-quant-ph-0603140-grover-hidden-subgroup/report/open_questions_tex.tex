\begin{description}

\item[Q1.] \textbf{Concrete construction of a ``modified'' non-abelian QHS
that sees conjugacy structure.}  The paper leaves as its central open
question whether there is a modified non-abelian QHS algorithm on
$S_N$ that solves Grover's HSP.  Our Part-D verification pins the
failure mode down: it is entirely explained by (i)
Hallgren--Russell--Ta-Shma's normal-subgroup restriction and (ii) mutual
conjugacy of the stabilisers.  Any hypothetical modification must break
at least one of these, e.g.~by conditioning on a coset representative
rather than on a Fourier label alone.
\emph{Next steps.} Implement a coset-state-based sampler (not just
character-based) that measures in a basis mixing different irreducible
components; test on $S_4$ whether such a sampler achieves $\Omega(1)$
success on Grover's HSP at $N=4$, and compare its query count to the
$O(\sqrt N)=2$ Grover-optimum.  Zalka's bound puts a hard ceiling at
$O(\sqrt N)$.

\item[Q2.] \textbf{Noise-degradation of stabiliser invariance.}  Our
Part-B fidelity of $\mathrm{Stab}_{j_0}$-action on the Grover state is
exactly $1$ in the noise-free statevector.  Under realistic
depolarising or amplitude-damping channels the invariance should decay
at a rate that is a diagnostic for how much ``hidden symmetry'' the
physical device still respects.
\emph{Next steps.} Repeat Part B on Qiskit's density-matrix simulator
with per-gate depolarising noise $p\in\{10^{-4},10^{-3},10^{-2}\}$;
plot mean $\mathrm{Stab}$-fidelity vs $p$ and fit the decay.  Then run
on \texttt{ibm\_kyoto} or a small trapped-ion device for $N=4$.

\item[Q3.] \textbf{Smallest group evading Section-9's obstruction.}
What is the smallest finite group $G$ with subgroup $H$ such that
(a) $H$ is non-normal, (b) the largest normal subgroup of $G$ in $H$
is $\{e\}$, (c) the $\mathrm{Stab}$-analog subgroups are pairwise
conjugate, and yet some published non-abelian QHS algorithm still
identifies $H$?  Any such example carves out precisely the ``modified
QHS'' the paper asks about.
\emph{Next steps.} Survey Ivanyos--Magniez--Santha,
Moore--Rockmore--Russell--Sniady, and Kuperberg (dihedral) for known
efficient non-abelian QHS instances and check condition (b) for each.

\item[Q4.] \textbf{Multiple-marked-element extension.}  Does the paper's
``HSP = Grover'' reformulation extend cleanly to Grover with $M>1$
marked elements?  The stabiliser becomes
$\mathrm{Stab}_{\{j_1,\dots,j_M\}}=\bigcap\mathrm{Stab}_{j_i}$, which
is smaller.  Does Section~7 still go through, and does Section~9's
normal-content-trivial obstruction still hold?
\emph{Next steps.} Extend \texttt{code/grover\_hsp.py} with an
$M$-marked variant (oracle $=-1$ on any $j\in J$) for
$(N,M)\in\{(4,2),(8,2),(8,3),(16,4)\}$; re-verify
$P_{\rm succ}=\sin^{2}((2k{+}1)\arcsin\sqrt{M/N})$; re-check
Section~9-(i) for setwise stabilisers.

\item[Q5.] \textbf{Standard-block-only QHS?}  Part E shows
$\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{Stab}_0}^{S_N}(\mathbf 1) = \mathbf 1 \oplus
\mathrm{std}$.  If we prepared the coset state
$|\mathrm{Stab}_{j_0}\rangle$ and projected onto the standard-irrep
block, does the resulting reduced density matrix depend on $j_0$?  A
``yes'' would suggest a restricted QHS that identifies $j_0$ by
measuring only within the standard block.
\emph{Next steps.} For $N=4$ explicitly construct the
standard-representation projector $P_{\rm std}$ of $S_4$ (dim $3$) in
the regular-rep basis; compute
$\mathrm{Tr}[|\mathrm{Stab}_j\rangle\langle\mathrm{Stab}_j|\,P_{\rm std}]$
for $j=0,\dots,3$; check pairwise orthogonality.

\end{description}
