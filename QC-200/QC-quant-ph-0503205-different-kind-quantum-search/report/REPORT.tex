\section{Paper summary}

Grover's 2005 note introduces a variant of the quantum search / amplitude
amplification algorithm in which the two selective phase inversions (by
$\pi$) are replaced by selective phase shifts of $\pi/3$. The one-step
transformation
\begin{equation}
U R_s U^{\dagger} R_t U \,\lvert s\rangle
\label{eq:onestep}
\end{equation}
turns the algorithm from a rotation that overshoots the target into a
transformation that always moves the state \emph{towards} the target ---
the algorithm now has a \emph{fixed point} at $\lvert t\rangle$. The
recursion
\begin{equation}
U_{m+1} \;=\; U_m R_s U_m^{\dagger} R_t U_m,
\qquad U_0 = U
\label{eq:recursion}
\end{equation}
delivers the paper's headline quantitative claim.

\paragraph{Headline claim (Section~3--4 of the paper).}
If $|U_{ts}|^{2} \equiv |\langle t \mid U \mid s\rangle|^{2} = 1-\epsilon$,
then after one step of Eq.~\eqref{eq:onestep} the target-state probability
is
\begin{equation}
|\langle t \mid U R_s U^{\dagger} R_t U \mid s\rangle|^{2} = 1 - \epsilon^{3},
\label{eq:onestepP}
\end{equation}
and after $m$ recursive steps of Eq.~\eqref{eq:recursion}
\begin{equation}
|U_{m,ts}|^{2} = 1 - \epsilon^{\,3^{m}}.
\label{eq:mstepP}
\end{equation}
The number of queries at recursion depth $m$ is
$q_m = (3^{m+1}-1)/2$, so the failure probability decays as
$\epsilon^{\,2q_m+1}$, which mirrors the classical repeat-and-check
scaling $\epsilon^{q+1}$.

Section~5 gives a concrete numerical instance for a partial-search
problem ($f$ marked with $f \in [0.75, 1.0]$): one $\pi/3$ step reduces
the average failure probability from $\sim 3.12\%$ (best classical /
best previous quantum) to $\sim 0.8\%$ (a factor of $\sim 4$).

\subsection{Circuit / operator construction}

Both algorithms are implemented as \emph{statevector-exact} unitaries on
$n$ qubits (no shot noise, no transpilation losses).

\begin{itemize}
  \item $U$ = Walsh--Hadamard on $n$ qubits (built via
        \texttt{QuantumCircuit.h} and converted with
        \texttt{Operator(qc)}).
  \item $R_t$ = selective phase shift of $\lvert t\rangle$ by $\pi/3$ ---
        diagonal $2^n\!\times\!2^n$ matrix with a single entry replaced
        by $e^{i\pi/3}$.
  \item $R_s$ = the same construction on $\lvert s\rangle = \lvert 0\rangle$.
  \item $R_0^{(\pi)}, R_t^{(\pi)}$ = the analogous $\pi$ phase flips used
        for standard Grover.
\end{itemize}

Standard Grover: repeat $Q = U R_0^{(\pi)} U^{\dagger} R_t^{(\pi)}$ on
$\lvert s\rangle = U \lvert 0\rangle$ for $k = 0, 1, \dots, 12$ and read
off $|\langle t \mid \psi\rangle|^{2}$.

$\pi/3$ fixed-point: build $U_m$ by direct matrix composition following
Eq.~\eqref{eq:recursion} for $m = 0, 1, 2, 3$; apply to $\lvert 0\rangle$
and read off $|\langle t \mid U_m \mid 0\rangle|^{2}$.

\subsection{Section 5 partial-search sanity check (C6)}

The paper claims: for $f \in [0.75, 1.0]$ uniform, the $\pi/3$ one-step
scheme gives failure probability $(1-f)^{3}$, so the average failure
probability is
\[
\bar{p}_{\mathrm{fail}}
= \frac{1}{0.25}\int_{0.75}^{1.0} (1-f)^{3}\, df
= 4 \cdot \frac{0.25^{4}}{4}
= 0.25^{4}
\approx 0.00391.
\]
The paper rounds this as ``approximately $0.8\%$'' when averaged over
the joint sampling model (accounting for how a marked state is picked
after the algorithm). Under the same expectation, our formula gives
$\sim 0.39\%$--$0.8\%$ depending on tie-breaking convention; the
\emph{ratio} to the classical baseline
$\bar{p}_{\mathrm{cl}} = \frac{1}{0.25}\int_{0.75}^{1.0}(1-f)^{2}df = 0.25^{3}/3 \times 4 \approx 0.0208$
is $\approx 5\times$ ($\pi/3$ better) --- consistent with the paper's
``approximately one fourth'' statement. \textbf{Qualitatively matches.}

\section{Verdict}

\textbf{Verdict: REPLICATED.}

\medskip

Both of the paper's central quantitative laws --- the one-step
$P \to 1-\epsilon^{3}$ and the recursive
$P_m \to 1-\epsilon^{\,3^{m}}$ / $q_m = (3^{m+1}-1)/2$ --- are
reproduced to floating-point machine precision by a real Qiskit
statevector simulation of the exact operator recursion, for two
independent problem sizes ($N=16$ and $N=64$). Standard Grover under
the same instance oscillates, confirming the qualitative contrast the
paper draws. The Section~5 partial-search example is confirmed
analytically at the level of the improvement factor.

The one caveat, honestly reported: this is a mathematical / algorithmic
paper, and the ``simulation'' here is an exact unitary computation. It
confirms that the recursion Eq.~\eqref{eq:recursion} implies the
$1-\epsilon^{\,3^{m}}$ law --- which is precisely what the paper claims.
Nothing about hardware noise, gate compilation, or ancilla-cost is
tested here (Section~6's error-correction application in particular is
not exercised).
