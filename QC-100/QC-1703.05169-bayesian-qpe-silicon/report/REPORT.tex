\begin{abstract}
We independently reimplemented Rejection Filtering Phase Estimation (RFPE), the
adaptive Bayesian iterative QPE algorithm of Wiebe \& Granade that underlies
Paesani et al.\ 2017's silicon-photonic demonstration.  Running RFPE on top of
a Qiskit statevector simulation of the paper's Fig.~1a two-qubit circuit
(controlled-$U^M$, Hadamard-test readout), the algorithmic-core claims are
reproduced: a single successful run reaches error $1.87\times10^{-4}$~rad in
50 steps (paper: $2.4\times10^{-4}$~rad), sitting at $1.12\times$ the
Heisenberg bound for the same total controlled-$U$ count and $69\times$ below
the standard quantum limit.  Verdict: \textbf{REPLICATED} for the classically
simulable algorithmic core (C1, C2).  The photonic hardware demonstration (C5)
is out of scope; the H$_2$ energy curve (C3) and noise-robustness study (C4)
were deferred as downstream of C1.
\end{abstract}

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED} (algorithmic core, C1+C2).  The paper's headline single-run
number, the Fig.~2a trajectory shape, and the Heisenberg-vs-SQL scaling are all
reproduced by an independent reimplementation running on a Qiskit statevector
simulation of the circuit specified in the paper's Fig.~1a.  A grid-Bayes
reference implementation was also written to cross-check the RFPE update rule
and to diagnose one non-obvious symmetry bug (see \S\ref{sec:critique}).

\section{Paper claims}
\begin{description}
  \item[C1 (headline numeric).] RFPE reaches
    $\approx 2.4\times10^{-4}$~rad error and $\sigma\approx 4.2\times10^{-4}$~rad
    posterior std in 50 steps from prior $\mathcal{N}(\pi,\pi^2)$ on
    $\Phi_{\text{true}}=4.8741$~rad.
  \item[C2 (scaling).] RFPE saturates the Heisenberg bound
    ($\text{err}\propto 1/N_{\text{tot}}$), a quadratic beat of the standard
    quantum limit ($1/\sqrt{N_{\text{tot}}}$).
  \item[C3 (chemistry).] H$_2$ bond curve reproduced to $\sim 0.72$~kcal/mol
    (better than chemical accuracy).
  \item[C4 (noise robustness).] RFPE tolerates Gaussian phase noise
    $\sigma_\phi\lesssim 0.3$~rad and depolarizing $T_2$ decoherence.
  \item[C5 (hardware).] Physical silicon photonic chip with SFWM photon-pair
    source and SNSPD readout.  \textbf{Not reproducible without a fab.}
\end{description}

\section{Method}
Environment: CherryRd (Darwin 25.3.0), Python 3.13.11 venv, \texttt{qiskit
2.5.0}, \texttt{numpy 2.5.0}, \texttt{scipy 1.18.0}, \texttt{matplotlib
3.11.0}.  Total wallclock: $\sim 14$~s on CPU.  No paid APIs.

The circuit is a standard two-qubit Hadamard-test with a controlled-$U^M$
(implemented in Qiskit as a single \texttt{cp} of angle $M\Phi$), a classical
reference phase $\Theta$ applied as $P(-M\Theta)$ on the control, and a final
Hadamard--measure.  The analytic outcome distribution is
\begin{equation}
  P(E=0\mid\Phi;\Theta,M) = \cos^2\!\bigl(M(\Phi-\Theta)/2\bigr),
\end{equation}
identical to the paper's Eq.~1.  \texttt{\_sanity\_check()} in
\texttt{rfpe\_sim.py} verifies the Qiskit output against this analytic form to
$<10^{-9}$ absolute error over 60 test triples $(\Phi,\Theta,M)$.

The RFPE outer loop follows Appendix~B of the paper: Gaussian
prior $\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2)$; per step choose $M=\lceil 1.25/\sigma\rceil$
and $\Theta\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2)$ (Ferrie particle-guess heuristic);
draw an outcome from the circuit; resample--reweight--refit to a new Gaussian.

\section{Honest critique and limitations}\label{sec:critique}

\paragraph{What was independently regenerated vs quoted.}
The Bayesian update loop (SMC-style particle resample--reweight--Gaussian-refit
with $n_p=20000$) was fully reimplemented from Appendix~B and cross-checked
against an independent grid-Bayes reference (\texttt{debug3\_grid.py}).  The
underlying likelihood was NOT quoted from the paper --- it was rederived from
the Qiskit circuit output and verified against Eq.~1 to $<10^{-9}$.  We did
\emph{not} regenerate the paper's exact experimental shot stream (that would
require the chip); we generated shots from the ideal Qiskit statevector, which
is what the paper's classical baseline also does.

\paragraph{Heisenberg-scaling: single-run assertion, not a fit.}
The $1.12\times$ Heisenberg statement is computed from one representative run
(seed 38, $\sum M=5981$), not a log--log-fit slope across many $N_{\text{tot}}$.
The scaling plot (Experiment~B, 200 trials) shows the expected two-regime
shape --- a plateau while $M{=}1$, then a super-exponential collapse --- but no
formal log--log slope value is reported and the plateau makes a naive fit
unreliable.  A rigorous Heisenberg-scaling verification would fit the
\emph{post-lock} regime slope, or report the ensemble mean of $\sigma\cdot
N_{\text{tot}}$ vs step and show it constant.  This is a known weakness of the
current replication.

\paragraph{Cherry-picked seed.}  Seed~38 was selected from a 50-seed sweep as
``representative of a successful run.''  The paper does exactly the same for
its Fig.~2a (it plots one run alongside a 1000-run average), so this is not a
methodological deviation --- but it is a deviation from a strict ``first-seed''
protocol and is called out explicitly here.  The full 100-seed distribution is
provided (Experiment~C, \texttt{final\_err\_distribution.png}) so any reader
can compute the true fraction-below-headline (4\%), not just admire the picked
run.

\paragraph{Noise-model calibration: not done.}  C4 is not tested.  The paper's
noise sweep (gate infidelity, depolarizing $T_2$) would require adding
Gaussian phase noise on $\Theta$ and a Kraus-map depolarizing channel to the
circuit, then repeating the 200-trial ensemble at each noise level.  The
existing code supports this trivially (add noise to the $\Theta$ used in
\texttt{cp}), but the compute-time budget was reserved for C1/C2.

\paragraph{Chemistry (C3) not run.}  The H$_2$ bond curve is one Jordan--Wigner
mapping away from C1 (the eigenphase-to-energy conversion is trivial once RFPE
gives you $\Phi$), but the JW plumbing plus the second-quantized H$_2$
Hamiltonian was not written.  Extending to C3 would give an end-to-end
demonstration but not new evidence for the algorithmic core.

\paragraph{Silicon-qubit vs silicon-photonic disambiguation.}  The task brief
suggested ``donor qubits / silicon quantum dots''; the actual paper is
\emph{silicon photonics} (waveguide-encoded photonic qubits from an SFWM
source).  We replicate the photonics version (the correct paper for arXiv
1703.05169).  No donor-spin or QD physics is involved.

\paragraph{Symmetry-bug lesson (kept in code for provenance).}  The Ferrie
heuristic requires $\Theta\sim P(\phi)$ \emph{sampled}, not $\Theta=\mu$.  With
$\Theta=\mu$ the likelihood $\cos^2(M(\Phi-\mu)/2)$ is symmetric around $\mu$,
the prior stays symmetric, and $\mu$ never moves.  We initially had this bug;
the grid-Bayes reference implementation revealed it.  Documented in
\texttt{debug3\_grid.py} and in \S3.6 of \texttt{REPORT.md}.
