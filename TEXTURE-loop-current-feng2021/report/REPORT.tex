\section{Claim under test}
The paper argues that the experimentally observed $2\times2$ topological charge-density-wave
in AV$_3$Sb$_5$ is a \emph{chiral flux phase} (CFP): an imaginary ($3Q$) bond order that
threads staggered orbital currents through the kagome triangles. The headline quantitative
claim (Sec.~III) is that at coupling $\lambda=0.3$ and $5/4$ van Hove filling, among the three
symmetry-allowed $2\times2$ charge orders,
\begin{itemize}
  \item vCDW (onsite, Eq.~4), \quad CBO (real bond, Eq.~7), \quad CFP (imaginary bond, Eq.~9),
\end{itemize}
the CFP has the \emph{lowest} ground-state energy: $0.195\,t$ below CBO and $0.435\,t$ below
vCDW per unit cell. CFP alone breaks time-reversal symmetry ($\mathcal{T}$) and yields an
anomalous Hall / Chern insulator ($C=1$ per spin, $\sigma_{xy}=2e^2/h$).

\section{Method (from scratch)}
We build the nearest-neighbor kagome tight-binding Hamiltonian (Eq.~1, $t=1$) directly in
real space on an $L\times L$ periodic cluster ($L=12$, $N=432$ sites, $144$ cells), with
sublattice geometry and Peierls-flux conventions adapted from the shared TEXTURES-100
kagome loop-current kernels. The three $3Q$ orders modulate at the M-point wavevectors
$Q_a=(0,2\pi/\sqrt3)$, $Q_b=(-\pi,-\pi/\sqrt3)$, $Q_c=(\pi,-\pi/\sqrt3)$:
\begin{align*}
\text{vCDW}&: \varepsilon_i \mathrel{-}= \lambda\cos(Q_{s}\!\cdot\!R_i) \quad\text{(onsite)}\\
\text{CBO} &: t_{ij}\mathrel{-}= \lambda\cos(Q_{ch}\!\cdot\!r_{ij}) \quad\text{(real bond)}\\
\text{CFP} &: t_{ij}\mathrel{-}= i\lambda\cos(Q_{ch}\!\cdot\!r_{ij}) \quad\text{(imaginary bond, loop current)}
\end{align*}
We fill to $5/12$ of the states ($=5/4$ van Hove filling of the 3 bands), sum occupied
single-particle energies, and report the total energy per original unit cell. Current
conservation for the CFP is verified via $J_{ij}=-2\,\mathrm{Im}[H_{ij}\rho_{ji}]$.

\noindent At $\lambda=0.3$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{CFP is the winner} (lowest energy) --- matches the paper's central claim.
  \item $E_{\text{CBO}}-E_{\text{CFP}} = 0.0130\,t$ (paper: $0.195\,t$).
  \item $E_{\text{vCDW}}-E_{\text{CFP}} = 0.0073\,t$ (paper: $0.435\,t$).
  \item CFP is the \emph{only} order that breaks $\mathcal{T}$ (Hamiltonian not real);
        vCDW and CBO remain time-reversal symmetric --- matches the paper's Fig.~1c table.
  \item CFP current conservation: max site net current $\approx 8\times10^{-16}$ (machine zero).
\end{itemize}

\section{Assessment}
\textbf{Qualitatively REPLICATED, quantitatively PARTIAL.} The direction of the central
result is reproduced from scratch: the imaginary-bond chiral flux phase is the lowest-energy
$2\times2$ order at $\lambda=0.3$ and is the unique $\mathcal{T}$-breaking candidate,
consistent with the anomalous-Hall interpretation. However, our energy splittings are
$\sim15\times$ smaller than the paper's, and our vCDW/CBO sub-ordering is reversed
(we find vCDW slightly below CBO; the paper places CBO below vCDW). These gaps trace to
model-construction details the 5-page Rapid Communication does not fully specify
(exact current-restoring hoppings for the CFP, the precise $2\times2$ phase convention,
and the chemical-potential/filling normalization). See \texttt{failure\_analysis.md}.
Agreement is therefore strong on the qualitative physics and the symmetry classification,
and weak on the absolute energy numbers.
