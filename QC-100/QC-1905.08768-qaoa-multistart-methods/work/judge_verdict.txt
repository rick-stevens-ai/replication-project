(2) **Justification (3–5 sentences):**  
At **p=1**, both single-start and multistart (M=5–20) deterministically converge to the same approximation ratio **0.801** (std **0.000**), so there is no multistart advantage under the equal 1000-eval budget. At **p=2**, multistart clearly improves robustness and performance: single-start has a failure mode (**min 0.675**, mean **0.857**, std **0.042**) while multistart concentrates at **0.878** (e.g., M=10: min/med/max **0.878/0.878/0.878**, std **0.000**) with significant paired one-sided Wilcoxon support (**p=0.0001** for M=10). At **p=4**, multistart mainly reduces variance and improves worst-case outcomes (single-start **min 0.881**, std **0.023** vs M=20 **min 0.918**, std **0.010**), but the **median is essentially a tie** (single-start **0.939** vs M=10 **0.939**, M=20 **0.931**), so the “substantially better” effect is not consistently present in central tendency here. Overall, the replication supports the paper’s direction of effect most strongly at **p=2**, and shows a weaker, robustness-only benefit at **p=4** under this *weaker-than-APOSMM* multistart.

(3) **Does the central claim hold here (multistart > single-start under equal budget)?**  
- **p=1:** **No** — identical outcomes (all **0.801**, std **0.000**).  
- **p=2:** **Yes** — multistart improves mean (**0.857 → 0.878**) and eliminates bad local optima (single-start **min 0.675** vs multistart **min 0.863–0.878**), with paired one-sided Wilcoxon evidence: **M=5 p=0.0004**, **M=10 p=0.0001**, **M=20 p=0.0004** (wins greatly exceed losses).  
- **p=4:** **Mixed/weak** — multistart improves **min** (**0.881 → 0.918**) and reduces **std** (**0.023 → 0.010**) monotonically with M, but **median does not improve** (near-tie or slightly worse at M=20), and no statistical test is reported for p=4, so “beats” is supported mainly in robustness rather than typical-case performance.
