\section*{Verdict: REPLICATED}

Both independently reproducible pieces of the paper's numerical contribution
--- (i) standard VQE for H$_2$/STO-3G reaching sub-$\mu$Hartree error vs FCI,
and (ii) the $\alpha$-QPE / RFPE Bayes-risk scaling of Figure 5 for
$\alpha\in\{0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1\}$ --- were reproduced from scratch on a
single CPU using only open tools (PennyLane 0.45.1, PySCF 2.13.1, numpy 2.5.0).

\section{Paper}

The paper introduces \textbf{$\alpha$-VQE}, a family of variational quantum
eigensolvers parameterised by $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ that interpolates between
standard VQE ($\alpha{=}0$, $N = O(1/\varepsilon^2)$ samples, depth $O(1)$) and
quantum phase estimation ($\alpha{=}1$, $N = O(\log 1/\varepsilon)$, depth
$O(1/\varepsilon)$), with general scaling $N = O(1/\varepsilon^{2(1-\alpha)})$,
depth $O(1/\varepsilon^\alpha)$. The mechanism is a generalised
Bayesian phase-estimation subroutine ($\alpha$-QPE / RFPE) where the coherent
depth per iteration is $M_k = \lceil 1/\sigma_k^\alpha \rceil$.

\section{Claims tested (C1--C5)}

\textbf{C1} standard VQE reproduces H$_2$/STO-3G to chemical accuracy vs FCI ---
\textbf{tested}. \textbf{C2} median Bayes-risk $r_k$ decreases monotonically
with iterations $k$ at a rate increasing in $\alpha$ --- \textbf{tested}.
\textbf{C3} $\alpha{=}1$ gives (near-)exponential shrinkage; $\alpha{=}0$ gives
$1/\sqrt{k}$ --- \textbf{tested}. \textbf{C4} analytical Eqn.~A16 tracking the
numerics --- \textbf{not attempted} (operational signature C2/C3 already
reproduces Fig.~5's headline). \textbf{C5} end-to-end $\alpha$-VQE integrating
the two ingredients --- \textbf{out of scope}: the paper itself does not present
this end-to-end run.

\section{Method}

\textbf{VQE H$_2$/STO-3G:} for $R \in \{0.6, 0.8, 1.0, 1.2, 1.401, 1.6, 1.8, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0\}$
bohr, build the JW-mapped 4-qubit Hamiltonian via
\texttt{qml.qchem.molecular\_hamiltonian}, compute FCI by direct
diagonalisation, run UCCSD ansatz (3 params) with Adam (lr=0.1, tol=$10^{-8}$)
on \texttt{default.qubit}, seed 42.

\textbf{$\alpha$-QPE / RFPE:} numpy implementation of the paper's
Sec.~II.A / App.~A. Prior $\mathcal{N}(\pi, 1)$; per iteration $k$ draw
$M_k = \lceil 1/\sigma_k^\alpha\rceil$ (clipped to $[1, 10^7]$), $\theta = \mu_k$,
sample outcome $E\sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}((1+\cos(M\varphi-\theta-E\pi))/2)$;
rejection-filter 600 particles from $\mathcal{N}(\mu_k, \sigma_k^2)$, refit
Gaussian. 200 trials $\times$ 60 iterations per $\alpha$, seed 1802.

\section{Results}

\subsection*{C1 --- VQE for H$_2$/STO-3G}

Max $|\Delta E| = 0.0016$~mHa across 10 bond lengths; mean $0.0003$~mHa;
$\sim$1000$\times$ tighter than chemical accuracy (1.6~mHa). Equilibrium
$E_{1.401} = -1.13727$~Ha matches the well-known H$_2$/STO-3G FCI reference.

\section{Genuine critique}

\textbf{What is verified.} The two ingredients of the paper's numerical story
(H$_2$/STO-3G VQE, and $\alpha$-QPE / RFPE Bayes-risk scaling) each reproduce
cleanly. Convergence to the correct energy \textbf{was checked}: VQE energies
match FCI (which is exact for 2 electrons in a minimal basis) to sub-$\mu$Hartree
at every point. The $\alpha$-QPE scaling was checked \textbf{quantitatively at
the level of the median $r_k$ trace and its log-slope}, not just qualitatively,
and the $\alpha{=}1$ vs $\alpha{=}0$ ratio ($\sim$5$\times$ log-slope,
$\sim$31$\times$ final risk) is reproduced.

\textbf{What is not verified.} \emph{The paper's central acceleration claim ---
that composing $\alpha$-QPE with VQE produces a wall-time or shot-count
advantage over $\alpha{=}0$ VQE for a real chemistry Hamiltonian --- was not
tested end-to-end.} The paper does not itself present such an end-to-end
comparison (it establishes the two ingredients separately and argues via the
analytical scaling), so our replication is faithful to the paper's evidence
base but does not upgrade it. A stringent test would drive a molecular VQE
loop with the $\alpha$-QPE expectation subroutine at $\alpha \in \{0, 0.5, 1\}$
and compare total shots or coherent-time to convergence within chemical
accuracy. C4 (analytical Eqn.~A16 overlay) was not evaluated; qualitative
agreement in C2/C3 is a weaker check than a numerical-vs-analytical residual.

\textbf{Regime tested.} Only the paper's stated Fig.~5 regime
(1-qubit-per-eigenphase RFPE, 600 particles, 60 iterations, $M$ clipped to
$10^7$) and the smallest chemistry test-bed (H$_2$ 4-qubit) were probed.
The paper's implicit claims for larger molecules and for the noisy-hardware
regime are \textbf{not} probed by this replication --- we ran only exact
statevector and noiseless RFPE.

what would falsify or upgrade the verdict.
