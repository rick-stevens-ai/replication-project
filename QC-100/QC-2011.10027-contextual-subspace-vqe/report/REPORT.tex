\begin{abstract}
We independently re-implement the Contextual Subspace Variational Quantum
Eigensolver (CS-VQE) of Kirby, Tranter, and Love (Quantum 5, 456, 2021) on an
open-source Python stack (OpenFermion + PySCF + NumPy/SciPy) and reproduce
the two directly-testable numerical claims of the paper: (i)~the Section~2.4
mean fractional errors on a family of 10\,000 random 3-qubit
Hamiltonians, and (ii)~the reduced-qubit VQE energy for H$_2$/STO-3G under
the Jordan--Wigner (JW) mapping. Both claims reproduce to within
$\lesssim 0.5\%$ of the paper's headline values, and H$_2$ CS-VQE recovers
the full FCI energy on $q=1$ quantum qubit (vs.\ 4 for full VQE), directly
exercising the paper's qubit-reduction claim. Verdict: \textbf{REPLICATED}.
Larger-molecule sweeps (Fig.~2/3 in the paper: LiH, H$_2$O, N$_2$, \ldots) are
out of QC-100 laptop-CPU scope and were not re-run; the algorithmic mechanism
that produces those reductions is verified end-to-end on H$_2$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper summary}
CS-VQE partitions a molecular qubit Hamiltonian
$H = \sum_j h_j P_j$ into a \emph{noncontextual} piece $H_{nc}$ (Pauli terms
that admit a joint quasi-quantized value assignment; solvable classically via
the Kirby--Love (2019) closed form) and a \emph{contextual} piece $H_c$ (the
rest). The noncontextual ground state fixes single-qubit stabilizers in a
rotated basis; the full Hamiltonian is projected into their joint
$\pm1$-eigenspace, and VQE is run only on the residual $q = n - k$ qubits.
Choosing how many stabilizers to leave unfixed interpolates continuously
between the pure noncontextual classical answer and full VQE.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Environment}
Fresh Python venv on CherryRd (macOS, arm64, CPU-only):
NumPy 2.5.0, SciPy 1.18.0, Qiskit 2.5.0, qiskit-nature 0.8.0,
OpenFermion 1.7.1, PySCF 2.13.1, Python 3.13.

\subsection{Sec.~2.4 (10\,000 random 3-qubit $H$)}
14 real coefficients $h_j \sim \mathcal{U}(-1,1)$ per instance
(seed \texttt{20260703}); noncontextual set
$S_{nc}=\{ZII, IXI, IYI, IZX, IZY, IZZ, ZXI, ZYI, ZZX, ZZY, ZZZ\}$,
contextual set $S_c=\{IIX,IIY,IIZ\}$. Compute
$E_{\text{true}} = \min\text{eigval}(H)$ (8$\times$8);
$E_{nc}$ from the closed form
$E_{nc} = \min_{q_Z\in\{\pm1\}} [\,h_{ZII}\,q_Z - \|b(q_Z)\|_2\,]$;
and the CS-VQE correction as $\min\text{eigval}$ of $H_c'$ projected into
the $+1$-eigenspace of $A_0=\sum_j r_j^* A_j$. Wall time 3.6~s.

\subsection{H$_2$/STO-3G CS-VQE}
Bond length 0.7414~\AA, PySCF+OpenFermion, JW $\to$ 15-term 4-qubit
Hamiltonian. Reference $E_{HF}=-1.11668439$~Ha, $E_{FCI}=-1.13727017$~Ha,
correlation energy 20.6~mHa. Exhaustive scan over all $2^{15}=32\,768$
subsets, filtered by the graph-theoretic noncontextual test
(anticommutation-graph connected components must each be cliques and
commute across components); 6\,015 noncontextual partitions found. For
each, diagonalize $H_{nc}$ classically, project $H$ into its ground-state
subspace, diagonalize $\to E_{\text{CS-VQE}}$. Wall time $\sim 5$~s.

\section{Critique}
\paragraph{Was the CS decomposition independently implemented?} Yes ---
from the paper's Sections~2.1--3 and the Kirby--Love 2019 closed form,
without touching the authors' code. The graph-theoretic noncontextuality
test was re-derived and applied to all $2^{15}$ subsets of the H$_2$
Hamiltonian.

\paragraph{Was the reduced-qubit VQE energy reproduced?} Yes for H$_2$
(the paper's smallest headline example) to numerical precision at
$q=1$. For LiH/H$_2$O/N$_2$/\ldots\ (Fig.~2/Fig.~3) we did \emph{not}
re-run the sweep. The algorithm is a straight extension --- larger
Hamiltonian, same noncontextual search + projection --- but each row
would require a much larger classical eigensolve or a real ansatz VQE
run.

\paragraph{Was the non-contextual classical solution verified?} Yes.
$E_{nc}$ from the closed form matches direct diagonalization of $H_{nc}$
to $\sim 10^{-15}$ Ha across all tested partitions.

\paragraph{Was the comparison against full VQE made?} Yes for H$_2$: at
$q=n=4$ CS-VQE equals full-Hamiltonian $\min\text{eigval}$ to numerical
zero, confirming CS-VQE $\to$ full VQE in the trivial-partition limit.

\paragraph{Honest limitations.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
\item \textbf{No VQE optimization loop.} We diagonalize the projected
Hamiltonian directly (as the paper explicitly allows: ``quantum
corrections were simulated classically by directly evaluating the lowest
eigenvalues''). We did not run a parameterized ansatz + classical
optimizer + measurement estimator; sampling noise, ansatz-expressibility
limits, and barren-plateau behavior of the reduced-qubit VQE are
therefore not tested.
\item \textbf{Only one molecule.} H$_2$/STO-3G is trivially small.
Whether the qubit-reduction ratio actually grows with system size (the
paper's real claim of practical value) is not independently established
here.
\item \textbf{Only closed-shell.} We tested a spin singlet in a minimal
basis; open-shell / larger-basis-set behavior of the noncontextual
partition is untouched.
\item \textbf{No noise model.} All simulations are noise-free classical
linear algebra. Whether reduced-qubit CS-VQE is more or less robust to
device noise than full VQE (an obvious near-term-hardware question) is
not tested.
\item \textbf{Partition search is exhaustive, not heuristic.} The paper
puts significant weight on choosing $H_{nc}$ well (their Section~3);
our H$_2$ headline uses the best partition from exhaustive enumeration,
which is only tractable at $n\!=\!4$. Whether their proposed heuristic
finds this same partition on H$_2$ (and whether it scales) was not
tested here.
\end{itemize}

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED.} Both headline numerical claims (Sec.~2.4 mean
errors; H$_2$ CS-VQE $q\!<\!n$) reproduce on an independent stack;
the qubit-reduction mechanism is exercised end-to-end on the paper's
smallest molecular example.
