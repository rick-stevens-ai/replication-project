\section{Paper}
M.~Willsch, D.~Willsch, F.~Jin, H.~De~Raedt, K.~Michielsen. \emph{Benchmarking the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm.} arXiv:1907.02359v2 (published in Quantum Information Processing, 2020). The paper evaluates QAOA on weighted MaxCut (up to 16 variables) and 2-SAT (up to 18 variables), using three quality measures: (M1) success probability of measuring the true ground state, (M2) energy expectation $E_p(\gamma^\ast,\beta^\ast)$, and (M3) the normalised ratio $r=(E_p-E_{\max})/(E_{\min}-E_{\max})\in[0,1]$ that coincides with the approximation ratio $\alpha=\langle\text{cut}\rangle/C_{\max}$ for unweighted MaxCut when the trivial cut is~0.

\section{Claims exercised}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{C1}: $r/\alpha$ improves monotonically with QAOA depth $p$ on MaxCut. \emph{Central testable-on-simulator claim.}
  \item \textbf{C2}: At $p=1$ on random 3-regular MaxCut, mean $\alpha$ is above the Farhi--Goldstone--Gutmann lower bound $\alpha\ge 0.6924$.
  \item \textbf{C3}: The $p=1$ landscape has smooth stationary points (indirectly probed via COBYLA convergence).
  \item \textbf{C4}: Success probability $P_{\text{ground}}$ grows with $p$.
  \item \textbf{C5, C6}: D-Wave 2000Q and IBM Q hardware comparisons --- \emph{not tested} (no hardware access in this pass).
\end{itemize}

\section{Method (independent reimplementation)}
Independent Qiskit implementation (\texttt{code/qaoa\_maxcut.py}); no code copied from the paper.
Instances: 3 random 3-regular graphs and 3 Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi $G(n,0.5)$ graphs at $n\in\{6,8,10\}$ with fixed seeds; each solved by brute force for $C_{\max}$.
Circuit: $\lvert +\rangle^{\otimes n}$ initial state; per layer, cost unitary via edge-wise \texttt{rzz}$(\gamma_\ell)$ and mixer via \texttt{rx}$(2\beta_\ell)$.
Optimization: SciPy COBYLA with 3--4 restarts from $\text{Uniform}(0,\pi)^{2p}$; the paper uses Nelder--Mead. Statevector expectation values via \texttt{qiskit.quantum\_info.Statevector}, plus a 20{,}000-shot Aer QASM cross-check on 3reg\_n8 at $p=1$.
Environment: Qiskit 2.5.0, qiskit-aer 0.17.2, NetworkX 3.6.1, SciPy 1.18.0, NumPy 2.5.0 on Python 3.14; wall-clock 63.4~s for the whole sweep on a MacBook.

Monotone $p$-improvement holds in both families ($\Rightarrow$ C1 replicated).
At $p=1$ on the 3-regular instances $\alpha\in\{0.79, 0.82, 0.85\}$, all above the 0.6924 Farhi bound ($\Rightarrow$ C2 replicated; caveat that the small $n\in\{6,8,10\}$ 3-regular graphs may contain triangles, so this is stronger than the triangle-free bound really requires).
$P_{\text{ground}}$ on 3reg\_n10 rises $0.126\to 0.327\to 0.528$ across $p=1,2,3$ ($\Rightarrow$ C4 replicated).
Statevector vs 20{,}000-shot Aer for 3reg\_n8 at $p=1$: $\alpha$ agrees to $5.6\times 10^{-5}$, $P_{\text{ground}}$ to $2\times 10^{-3}$ (cross-backend sanity ✓).

\section{Critique --- what this replication does \emph{not} establish}
This section is deliberately blunt about the limits of what was exercised.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Scale is far below the paper's headline instances.} We ran $n\in\{6,8,10\}$; Willsch et al.\ report up to $n=16$ weighted MaxCut and $n=18$ 2-SAT. The qualitative claim (monotone $p$-improvement) is confirmed, but any \emph{quantitative} comparison to their $p=10$ success-probability curves is not made here --- our $P_{\text{ground}}$ at $n=10, p=3$ is not the same regime.
  \item \textbf{The 2-SAT problem family was not exercised at all.} The paper's headline success-probability numbers ($\sim 40\%$ at $p=10$ on 18-var 2-SAT) rest on 2-SAT instances; we only ran MaxCut. C4 is confirmed only in the MaxCut direction.
  \item \textbf{Optimizer substitution.} The paper uses Nelder--Mead; we used COBYLA. Both are derivative-free and reach comparable optima on these small instances, but a strict optimizer-controlled reproduction of the paper's landscape curves would use Nelder--Mead with the paper's restart schedule.
  \item \textbf{Landscape figures (Figs.~2--4) were not regenerated.} We infer smooth optimization from the fact that COBYLA converges reproducibly, not from a full $(\gamma,\beta)$ grid scan. A proper Fig.~2 replication would raster the 2D landscape and compare to the paper's contour plots.
  \item \textbf{No hardware.} C5 (D-Wave 2000Q dominates simulated QAOA) and C6 (IBM~Q hardware landscape noisier than simulator) cannot be tested without hardware access; QC-100 is a free-simulator wave. This is a scope gap, not a contradiction.
  \item \textbf{Classical baselines not run.} The paper's cross-platform comparisons implicitly rely on classical anchors (D-Wave). We did not run Goemans--Williamson, SDP, or simulated annealing on the same instances to give a modern classical yardstick. The result ``QAOA gets $\alpha\approx 0.95$ at $p=3$ on 3reg $n\le 10$'' is not, on its own, evidence of quantum advantage --- Goemans--Williamson also delivers $\alpha\ge 0.878$ on MaxCut.
  \item \textbf{Weighted MaxCut untested.} We used unweighted graphs. The paper's weighted-MaxCut curves (which are one of the two headline problem families) are not exercised here.
  \item \textbf{No instance-family variance analysis.} Three graphs per family is enough to see the trend but too few to estimate variance meaningfully across the 3-regular / ER distributions.
\end{itemize}
Net: the paper's \emph{simulator-side} qualitative and small-instance quantitative findings on MaxCut reproduce cleanly and cheaply. The paper's \emph{scale}, its 2-SAT storyline, its hardware storyline, and its comparisons against classical/annealer baselines are \emph{not} independently regenerated in this pass.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED} on the simulator-testable MaxCut claims (C1, C2, C4 fully; C3 partial via reproducible COBYLA convergence). C5, C6, and the 2-SAT / weighted-MaxCut / $n\ge 12$ scale of the paper are out-of-scope for this pass and remain \emph{not tested}.
