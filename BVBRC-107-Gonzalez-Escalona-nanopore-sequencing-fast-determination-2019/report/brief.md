# Brief — BVBRC-107

González-Escalona et al. (PLoS ONE, 2019, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0220494; PMID 31361781) sequenced three Shiga toxin-producing *E. coli* O26:H11 clinical/environmental strains (CFSAN027343, CFSAN027346, CFSAN027350) with three technologies (Illumina MiSeq–Nextera XT short-read, Oxford Nanopore MinION long-read, and Pacific Biosciences PacBio SMRT long-read) and used the assemblies for plasmid detection, in-silico virulence-gene typing (VirulenceFinder / CGE), AMR gene screen (ResFinder 2.1), Stx-phage location (PHASTER), and MLST/serotyping (Ridom SeqSphere+). The paper's central claim is that the two long-read platforms are congruent for closing STEC genomes and detecting plasmid-borne virulence/AMR genes that the short-read MiSeq–Nextera pipeline misses. This replication downloads the deposited GenBank chromosomes+plasmids (CP037941–CP037947) directly and re-runs (i) in-silico serotyping, (ii) MLST, (iii) plasmid replicon detection, (iv) virulence-gene screen, (v) AMR-gene screen, then compares gene-level results to the paper's Tables 4–8 and virulence-profile text using an LLM judge scored on a free Argo model.
