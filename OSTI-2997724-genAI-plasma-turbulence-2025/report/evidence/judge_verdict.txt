### Justification (4–6 sentences)
This is an end-to-end independent reimplementation that successfully reproduces the core “generative surrogate” functionality: high-fidelity reconstruction, spectral realism, stable rollouts (at least to 4000 frames), and large speedups versus numerical integration. Those results substantiate the main computational premise of GAIT—compress dynamics into a latent space and evolve them cheaply. However, the transport/particle-diffusivity claim (C4) fails decisively, with GAIT producing *much lower* diffusivity and much shorter correlation time than the replicator’s regenerated HW turbulence. Because the “ground-truth” simulator was materially altered (reduced resolution, modified HW coupling, hyperviscosity, friction, adaptive timestep, and an energy-ceiling limiter), C4’s mismatch cannot be cleanly attributed to GAIT vs. to changed physics/statistics in the training data. Overall, the replication supports most claims but not the transport claim, so the appropriate verdict is **PARTIAL**.

### Key caveats
- **Ground-truth mismatch is substantial:** 128² (vs 512²) plus modified HW physics and multiple numerical “safety” interventions (hyperviscosity, friction, CFL adaptation, amplitude limiter) can change intermittency, long-time correlations, and transport—directly impacting **C4**.
- **Energy-ceiling limiter may imprint long correlations** (as the replicator suspects), making τ_HW and thus D_HW atypical; this undermines direct comparison to the paper’s diffusivity numbers.
- **Rollout length not matched:** stability is shown for 4000 frames, but the paper’s headline is 100,000 frames; long-horizon drift/attractor issues might only appear later.
- **Training set size/regime differs:** 3000 snapshots and altered stationarity may shift what the latent RNN learns about transport-relevant rare events.
- **C4 requires protocol alignment:** particle advection details (time interpolation, velocity field derivation, integration scheme, ensemble size) are not specified here; small implementation differences can affect measured D even with identical fields.
