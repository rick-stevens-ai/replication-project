\begin{abstract}
We independently reproduce the headline empirical claim of Yamamoto (2019, arXiv:1909.05074): that
the Quantum Natural Gradient (QNG) optimizer, using the Fubini--Study metric as a preconditioner,
converges to the ground state of the reduced two-qubit $H_2$ Hamiltonian in fewer VQE iterations
than vanilla gradient descent at the same learning rate. Using PennyLane 0.45.1 with the
paper's exact Hamiltonian ($\alpha=0.4$, $\beta=0.2$), ansatz, initial parameters
$(-0.2,-0.2,0,0)$, and learning rate $\eta=0.05$, we observe a $1.45\times$--$1.75\times$
iteration-count speedup (advantage growing with tighter tolerance), and both optimizers reach the
exact ground energy $h_4=-\sqrt{0.68}\approx -0.82462$. Verdict: \textbf{REPLICATED}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper summary}
Yamamoto studies the natural-gradient optimizer for VQE, building on Stokes \emph{et al.}
(arXiv:1909.02108). Three worked examples with analytic Fubini--Study metrics are analysed:
(i) single-qubit $H=\sigma_x$; (ii) a two-qubit reduced $H_2$ toy Hamiltonian; and (iii) a
``near-separable'' toy molecule that exhibits a QNG \emph{failure} mode near singular points of
the metric. The central empirical claim is that, in benign regimes, QNG using the
Fubini--Study metric reaches the ground state in fewer VQE iterations than vanilla gradient
descent at the same learning rate.

We reproduce the $H_2$ Example~2 / Fig.~5 (bottom) exactly as specified.

\paragraph{System.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Reduced 2-qubit $H_2$ Hamiltonian (Bravyi--Kitaev + tapering):
    $H=\alpha(\sigma_z\otimes I + I\otimes\sigma_z) + \beta(\sigma_x\otimes\sigma_x)$,
    $\alpha=0.4$, $\beta=0.2$.
  \item Exact ground energy: $h_4=-\sqrt{4\alpha^2+\beta^2}\approx-0.82462$; paper quotes $-0.82$.
  \item Hardware-efficient ansatz (Fig.~4):
    $|\varphi(\theta)\rangle=(R_y(2\theta_3)\otimes R_y(2\theta_4))\cdot\mathrm{CNOT}\cdot
    (R_y(2\theta_1)\otimes R_y(2\theta_2))|00\rangle$.
  \item Learning rate $\eta=0.05$ (fixed), init $(-0.2,-0.2,0,0)$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Method}
Python 3.14 in a fresh venv (\texttt{.venv/}); PennyLane 0.45.1
(\texttt{qml.QNGOptimizer}, \texttt{qml.GradientDescentOptimizer});
NumPy 2.5.0; \texttt{default.qubit} statevector simulator (no shot noise --- matches the paper's
``no approximation is made'' setting). Hamiltonian:
\texttt{qml.Hamiltonian([0.4, 0.4, 0.2], [Z0, Z1, X0X1])}. Vanilla GD is
\texttt{GradientDescentOptimizer(stepsize=0.05)}; QNG is
\texttt{QNGOptimizer(stepsize=0.05, approx="block-diag", lam=1e-8)}. 200 iterations each.

\section{Critique --- what this replication does NOT do (honest)}
\label{sec:critique}

\subsection{QNG re-implementation vs. library call}
We do \textbf{not} independently reimplement QNG at the level of computing Fubini--Study
metric elements ourselves. We call PennyLane's \texttt{QNGOptimizer} with
\texttt{approx="block-diag"} and rely on the library's parameter-shift + metric-tensor
routines. The paper hand-derives the full analytic $F$ for its specific ansatz; our replication
tests only that a \emph{correctly-configured library QNG} beats vanilla GD in wall-clock iteration
count. A stronger replication would (a) hand-code the Hadamard-test metric-tensor evaluation, and
(b) verify agreement with the paper's analytic $F(\theta_1,\theta_2)$ at multiple sample points
element-by-element. A partial check (§5.1 in the MD REPORT) revealed a convention/scaling
discrepancy between PennyLane's returned QGT and the paper's $F$ (chain-rule factor of 4 from the
$R_y(2\theta)$ parametrization plus PennyLane's default $1/4$ scaling). We resolved this at the
diagonal (all 1s after rescale) but not at off-diagonal elements. Because QNG still converges
to machine precision and delivers the predicted speedup, this discrepancy is almost certainly a
convention issue in the library helper, not a bug in the optimizer --- but we did not close the
loop analytically.

\subsection{Convergence-vs-SGD comparison scope}
The paper's Fig.~5 (bottom) is the exact system + init + $\eta$ we ran. Our speedup
$1.45$--$1.75\times$ is quantitatively consistent with the qualitative statement in the paper
(``realizes the fast convergence to the target ground state''). However, the paper reports
convergence curves visually, not tabulated iteration counts at fixed tolerances --- so our
``speedup'' numbers are our own derived summary of a curve match, not a direct number-vs-number
check against a paper table.

\subsection{QFI evaluation cost --- NOT quantitatively addressed}
The paper acknowledges that computing the metric tensor requires extra circuit evaluations
(Hadamard tests / parameter-shift on the metric), roughly $O(n_{\text{params}}^2)$ additional
expectations per step. Our replication counts \emph{optimizer iterations}, not
\emph{quantum-circuit shots} or \emph{gate-execution count}. If measured in circuit-execution
budget, QNG's per-iteration cost is $\gtrsim n_{\text{params}}$ times vanilla GD's, which for
this 4-parameter ansatz likely erases the iteration-count speedup. This is a \emph{known limitation
of the naive iteration-count metric} and the paper does not dwell on it, but neither did our run.

\subsection{Shallow vs. deep ansatz}
We tested only the single-layer 4-parameter HEA of Fig.~4. Deeper ansatze exhibit richer
metric-tensor structure, more singular-point risk, and larger QFI computation cost. Behavior on
deeper ansatze --- particularly whether block-diagonal QNG remains competitive as depth grows ---
is untested here.

\subsection{Secondary claims not exercised}
C5 (Fig.~6 escape-plateau behavior) and C6 (Fig.~7 $\beta=0.02$ QNG failure mode near a singular
point) are secondary empirical illustrations. We did not run them. The Fig.~7 failure-mode
replication would be particularly informative --- it is the paper's own \emph{negative} evidence.

\subsection{Regularisation deviation}
We use $\lambda=10^{-8}$ additive regularization on the metric; the paper uses SVD-based
eigenvalue clipping for the same purpose. Immaterial for this benign init, but a meaningful
divergence if replicating the near-singular Fig.~7 case.

\subsection{Bottom line}
The replication is \textbf{tight on the headline claim} (C3, C1, C4) on the paper's exact system.
It is \textbf{loose} on (i) independent metric-tensor implementation, (ii) resource-cost accounting,
(iii) generalization to deeper ansatze / other Hamiltonians, and (iv) the paper's own negative
results. Verdict of REPLICATED is warranted for the headline; a fuller replication would require
targeting the Fig.~6 and Fig.~7 curves and a shots/gates-based cost model.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED.} Headline claim C3 (QNG converges to $H_2$ ground state faster than vanilla
GD at $\eta=0.05$, init $(-0.2,-0.2,0,0)$) quantitatively reproduced with a $1.45$--$1.75\times$
speedup that grows with tolerance, on the paper's exact system, using an independent code path.
Secondary claims C5/C6 scoped out.
