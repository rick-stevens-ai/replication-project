\begin{abstract}
This report documents a same-day, subagent-scale replication attempt of
Carrasco-Hern\'andez et al.\ (2023), which uses the TOPAS-nBio Monte Carlo
toolkit (Geant4-DNA backend) to compute DSB yields and downstream lethal
damage for the Auger/positron emitter \textsuperscript{64}Cu.  We
independently reproduce the paper's analytic lethal-damage chain (Table 2 from
Table 1 via Humm--Charlton Eq.\ 1) to within \SI{0.21}{\percent} for all five
tabulated radionuclides.  We reimplement and unit-test the paper's DSB
scoring rule (two opposite-strand SSBs $\leq 10$~bp apart).  We spot-check the
\textsuperscript{64}Cu decay spectrum against ICRP~107.  We explicitly do
\emph{not} rerun the underlying TOPAS-nBio Monte Carlo, which is the paper's
headline computation of the \SI{0.171(3)}{DSB/decay} primitive.  Verdict:
\textbf{PARTIAL}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper under replication}
Carrasco-Hern\'andez, J., et al.\ (2023), ``Cellular lethal damage of
\textsuperscript{64}Cu incorporated in mammalian genome evaluated with
Monte Carlo methods,'' \emph{Frontiers in Medicine} \textbf{10}: 1253746,
DOI \href{https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2023.1253746}{10.3389/fmed.2023.1253746}.

\section{Headline claims}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Using TOPAS-nBio with a full DNA-scale nuclear geometry (\SI{9.3}{\um}
    sphere, 6.08~Gbp, 46 chromosomes), the paper reports for
    \textsuperscript{64}Cu incorporated in DNA:
    \(\text{DSB/decay} = 0.171 \pm 0.003\) at \SI{0.25}{nm} from the axis and
    \(0.190 \pm 0.003\) at \SI{1.15}{nm}.
  \item Feeding those DSB yields into Humm--Charlton Eq.~1 with the
    \textsuperscript{125}I anchor $N_{\rm DSB}=194$ per 100 decays and
    $t=\SI{24}{h}$, the paper obtains 3107 atoms of \textsuperscript{64}Cu per
    cell (initial activity \SI{47.1e-3}{Bq/cell}) as the equi-lethal dose.
  \item DSBs are scored by the Nikjoo/Charlton proximity rule (two opposite-
    strand SSBs within 10~bp), \emph{not} by DBSCAN.  Our triage tag was
    incorrect on this point; the paper's Methods \S2.2 is explicit.
\end{enumerate}

\section{What was actually replicated}
See \texttt{REPORT.md} (top-level) for the full narrative.  In brief:
\begin{description}
  \item[R1 --- Table 2 recomputation.]
    Independent Python implementation of Humm--Charlton Eq.~1 with published
    half-lives (NNDC/ENSDF) and the paper's Table 1 DSB/decay values recovers
    all five entries of Table 2 within \SI{0.21}{\percent}
    (\textsuperscript{125}I worst case, consistent with paper's rounding of
    Table 1 to 3 s.f.).  Cross-check figure:
    \texttt{figures/fig01\_eq1\_crosscheck.png}.
  \item[R2 --- DSB scoring rule.]
    Standalone implementation with boundary unit tests (same-strand,
    opposite-strand, exactly 10~bp, greedy pairing).  Behaves as specified.
  \item[R3 --- Track-correlated DSB:SSB ratios.]
    Illustrative Monte Carlo of clustered SSBs shows the scoring rule yields
    DSB:SSB ratios in the published Nikjoo/Friedland range (0.02--0.05 for
    low-LET $e^-$, climbing to 0.16 for dense Auger cascades).
  \item[R4 --- \textsuperscript{64}Cu spectrum spot-check.]
    Sum of ICRP 107 Auger lines above \SI{1}{keV} is
    $\approx 0.228\ e^-/{\rm decay}$, consistent with the paper's
    ``$\sim 0.18\ e^-/{\rm decay}$'' claim if the very-lowest above-keV lines
    are excluded (per-shell binning depends on Geant4 version).
\end{description}

\section{Critique and honest limitations}
\label{sec:critique}
This is a genuine \textbf{PARTIAL} verdict, and the reader should not read
``PARTIAL'' as ``mostly done.''

\paragraph{The headline was \emph{not} exercised.}
The paper's \emph{original contribution} is the number
$0.171 \pm 0.003$~DSB/decay for \textsuperscript{64}Cu computed inside
TOPAS-nBio with a physical + chemical (radiolysis) stage on a realistic
nucleus geometry.  We did \emph{not} reinstall TOPAS-nBio, we did \emph{not}
build the paper's geometry, and we did \emph{not} rerun any of the 400{,}000-
history simulations.  Every downstream number in Table 2 anchors on that
primitive; if the primitive is off, the table is off, and we have contributed
zero independent evidence about the primitive.

\paragraph{Why we did not rerun.}
This is the same recurring pattern across the LUCID Monte-Carlo cohort:
(a) TOPAS is CC-BY-NC and gated behind an emailed license, so the tarball is
not fetchable by an unattended subagent;
(b) TOPAS-nBio adds a Geant4 low-energy EM build and a nBio extension, giving
a multi-hour install even on a well-equipped box;
(c) 400{,}000 histories per data point at DNA-scale physics + chemistry is a
parallel-cluster job (the paper used ``Tochtli-ICN-UNAM''), not a laptop job;
(d) no Zenodo/GitHub deposit of input decks or per-event SSB lists exists ---
the data-availability statement reads ``included in the article.''
Any one of these would be individually fixable; taken together they make a
same-day rerun infeasible in the subagent budget, and we do not claim to
have overcome them.

\paragraph{What our R2/R3 numbers do and do not mean.}
R2 shows the scoring rule is \emph{implementable} from the prose --- a
non-trivial guarantee, because published methods often omit boundary
conventions.  R3 shows the rule, applied to \emph{fabricated} clustered SSBs,
gives DSB:SSB ratios in a plausible range.  Neither R2 nor R3 constrains the
absolute yield the paper reports, and neither would catch a bug in the
physical or chemical stage.  Reader be warned.

\paragraph{Triage-tag error.}
Our upstream triage tagged this paper as ``DBSCAN clustering.''  It is not.
The paper explicitly uses the Nikjoo/Charlton proximity rule.  The DBSCAN tag
is from ref.~34 (the same group's 2020 paper).  We caught this on the
Methods re-read, but it points to how easily an LLM-driven triage can
mislabel a paper by neighbourhood.

\paragraph{Auger-spectrum sensitivity.}
Our R4 cross-check depends on which lines one counts.  The full ICRP-107
Auger sum for \textsuperscript{64}Cu is $\approx 1.8\ e^-/{\rm decay}$; the
$>1$~keV subset is $\approx 0.23$; the paper reports $\sim 0.18$.  All three
are ``correct'' answers to different questions.  This ambiguity is exactly
the kind of thing that makes MC-primitive replication brittle: a factor of
$\sim 10$ hides in the definition, not the physics.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{PARTIAL.}  Analytic chain replicated to $<0.21\%$; DSB scoring
algorithm reproducible from prose; \textsuperscript{64}Cu spectrum
consistent with public databases at the interpretation-dependent level;
TOPAS-nBio primitive \emph{not} re-derived.
