\section{Scope}

arXiv:2501.14968 (Patel, Jayakumar, Yen, Izmaylov, 2025) is explicitly a
\textbf{review/tutorial article}: ``This review emphasizes foundational concepts
and methodologies rather than numerical benchmarks.'' It therefore contains few
original numerical benchmarks; its quantitative content is a set of
\textbf{analytical claims} about the statistics of the VQE measurement problem
and a survey of variance-reduction results from the primary literature it
reviews.

We do not attempt to reproduce the whole review. Instead we isolate its
\textbf{central, self-contained, testable analytical claims} about measurement
cost and check whether they hold when computed from scratch on small molecular
Hamiltonians (H$_2$ and LiH, STO-3G, Jordan--Wigner). This is a replication of
the review's \emph{core quantitative logic}, not of a specific figure.

\section{Claims tested}

\paragraph{C1 --- Estimator-variance law (Eq.~63).}
For a Hamiltonian partition $\hat H = \sum_\alpha \hat H_\alpha$ measured
$M_\alpha$ times each, the energy-estimator variance is
$\mathrm{Var}[\bar H] = \sum_\alpha \mathrm{Var}(\hat H_\alpha)/M_\alpha$.
With optimal shot allocation $M_\alpha \propto \sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(\hat H_\alpha)}$
under fixed total budget $M$, the minimum achievable total variance
(per unit total shots) is the metric
\[
M_{\mathrm{opt}} \;=\; \Big( \sum_\alpha \sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(\hat H_\alpha)} \Big)^2.
\]

\paragraph{C2 --- Grouping reduces measurement cost by a ``two- to four-fold'' factor}
(Sorted-Insertion / Crawford et al.\ [17], as reviewed), relative to naive
term-by-term measurement.

\paragraph{C3 --- Fully-commuting (FC) grouping outperforms qubit-wise-commuting
(QWC) grouping} (fewer fragments, lower aggregate variance).

\section{Method}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Molecular electronic Hamiltonians for \textbf{H$_2$ (R=0.735\,\AA{} and
  stretched R=1.50\,\AA)} and \textbf{LiH (R=1.595\,\AA)} in STO-3G via PySCF;
  Jordan--Wigner mapping (H$_2$: 4 qubits / 15 Pauli terms; LiH: 12 qubits /
  631 terms).
\item Exact ground state by dense diagonalization; cross-checked energy
  reconstruction from per-term expectation values (matched to
  $\sim 10^{-15}$ Ha, sanity gate).
\item Per-term / per-fragment variances
  $\mathrm{Var}(\hat H_\alpha) = \langle \hat H_\alpha^2 \rangle
  - \langle \hat H_\alpha \rangle^2$ exactly in the ground state.
\item Measurable fragments by greedy graph coloring under two commutation
  relations: \textbf{QWC} and \textbf{FC} (general commutation).
\item Optimal-allocation metric $M_{\mathrm{opt}} = (\sum \sqrt{\mathrm{Var}_\alpha})^2$
  for (a) ungrouped single terms, (b) QWC groups, (c) FC groups; reduction
  factors reported.
\item \textbf{Independent Monte-Carlo cross-check}: for H$_2$ we simulated
  actual finite-shot sampling of ungrouped vs.\ QWC-grouped measurement
  (200 repeats per budget at 1.5k / 15k / 150k total shots) and measured
  the realized standard deviation of the energy estimate.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Results}

\subsection*{C1 --- variance law \& optimal-allocation metric (exact, ground state)}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrrrr}
\toprule
System & qubits & Pauli terms & $M_{\mathrm{opt}}$ ungrp & QWC & FC & QWC red. & FC red. \\
\midrule
H$_2$ (R=0.735) & 4 & 15 & 0.1245 & 0.1245 & 0.1245 & 1.00$\times$ & 1.00$\times$ \\
H$_2$ (R=1.50)  & 4 & 15 & 0.1177 & 0.1177 & 0.1177 & 1.00$\times$ & 1.00$\times$ \\
LiH (R=1.595)   & 12 & 631 & 15.34 & 5.06 & \textbf{0.285} & \textbf{3.03$\times$} & \textbf{53.8$\times$} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Energy reconstruction from per-term expectations matched the diagonalized
ground-state energy to $\le 10^{-14}$ Ha for all three systems
(H$_2$ $E_{\mathrm{gs}}=-1.137306$~Ha; LiH $E_{\mathrm{gs}}=-7.882402$~Ha).

\textbf{C1 holds exactly.} Under optimal allocation, grouping gives H$_2$
essentially no gain (1.00$\times$): for a 4-qubit Hamiltonian the
optimal-allocation bound is already near-saturated, so re-packaging terms into
groups cannot beat it --- an honest and physically-correct outcome.

\subsection*{C2 --- two-to-four-fold cost reduction from grouping}

For \textbf{LiH} (631 terms), QWC grouping under the optimal-allocation metric
yields \textbf{3.03$\times$} reduction --- \emph{squarely inside} the paper's
claimed ``two- to four-fold'' window. FC grouping does far better
(53.8$\times$). Under a simpler \emph{uniform}-allocation proxy the reductions
are QWC 5.1$\times$ / FC 34.9$\times$ (same qualitative ordering). For H$_2$
the effect is $\sim$1$\times$ under optimal allocation (too few terms to
benefit).

\midrule
H$_2$ & 5   & 2  & 0.1245 & 0.1245 & tie (small) \\

\textbf{C3 holds.} FC produces far fewer fragments and a dramatically lower

\subsection*{Independent Monte-Carlo cross-check (H$_2$, realized shot noise)}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{rrrrl}
\toprule
Total shots & $\sigma$ ungrp & $\sigma$ QWC & Var ratio & bias (grp) \\
\midrule
1{,}500   & 0.01370 & 0.01091 & 1.57$\times$ & $2.7\!\times\!10^{-4}$ Ha \\

Grouping gives a modest ($\sim$1--1.6$\times$) benefit for H$_2$; both
estimators converge to the exact energy (bias $\ll 1$ mHa), and $\sigma$
scales as $\sim 1/\sqrt{\mathrm{shots}}$ as Eq.~63 predicts
(10$\times$ shots $\to \sim 3.2\times$ smaller $\sigma$, observed
$0.0137 \to 0.0041 \to 0.0011$).

\section{Critique and Honest Limitations}

This is a genuine but \emph{narrow} replication. Several limitations must be
stated plainly:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Headline exercised, but on a review paper.} The paper is a
  tutorial/review; the ``2--4$\times$ grouping'' claim it attributes to
  Crawford et al.\ is not its own primary result but a claim it \emph{quotes}.
  Our 3.03$\times$ LiH figure is an independent from-scratch reproduction of
  that quoted claim --- a real numerical check --- but it does not fully
  exercise every methodological survey the paper covers (classical shadows,
  derandomization variants, symmetry-adapted groupings, unitary partitioning).
  We tested the central quantitative spine, not the full breadth.
\item \textbf{Baseline is naive single-Pauli-per-shot} (i.e.\ ungrouped optimal
  allocation), which is exactly the paper's stated reference point.
  A stronger baseline (e.g.\ locally-biased classical shadows) was not tested;
  the paper itself does not require it for the specific claim we checked.
\item \textbf{Ground-state expectation values, not VQE-ansatz values.} Real VQE
  runs measure w.r.t.\ a parametrized trial state; variance surfaces near
  minima can differ. Our result is an upper bound on how ``easy'' the
  grouping benefit is under an idealized state; the direction of the
  conclusion should be robust but the magnitude in a real VQE loop could
  shift.
\item \textbf{STO-3G minimal basis, two small molecules.} No claim is made
  about scaling to larger active spaces or realistic basis sets. LiH at
  631 terms is the largest case tested; H$_2$ (15 terms) is too small to
  show the grouping benefit and does not (as also seen).
\item \textbf{Greedy (not optimal) graph coloring.} Fragment counts and
  variances are upper bounds on what an optimal coloring would achieve;
  the qualitative direction (FC $<$ QWC) is unaffected.
\item \textbf{No hardware / no shot noise beyond H$_2$}. The Monte-Carlo shot
  cross-check was run only for H$_2$ due to LiH statevector-sampling cost;
  the analytical LiH numbers were not verified by finite-shot simulation.
\item \textbf{One early bug fixed.} An earlier LiH variance script had a benign
  contiguity error (v3 log); final numbers come from the corrected
  \texttt{grouping\_summary\_v3.json}. This is disclosed rather than hidden.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Bottom line.} The paper's central measurement-cost claims (Eq.~63
variance law, optimal-allocation metric, 2--4$\times$ grouping reduction,
FC$>$QWC) were independently reproduced from scratch on H$_2$/LiH, with the
LiH QWC reduction (3.03$\times$) landing squarely inside the reviewed
``two- to four-fold'' range. \textbf{Verdict: REPLICATED} for the tested
analytical spine; the review's full breadth of surveyed methods is not
exhaustively re-implemented.
