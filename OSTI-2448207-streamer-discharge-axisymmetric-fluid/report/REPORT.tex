\section{Paper Under Test}
Fierro et al.\ describe a massively parallel, first-order fluid (drift--diffusion)
plasma solver targeting streamer discharges in 2-D $r$-$z$ axisymmetric geometry on
a fixed (non-AMR) mesh, engineered to scale to $10^8$ elements on $\sim\!10^3$
processors. Core equations:
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial n_s}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\Gamma}_s &= Q_{\text{src}}, \\
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_s &= \pm \mu_s n_s \mathbf{E} - D_s \nabla n_s, \\
\nabla^{2}\varphi &= -\rho/\varepsilon_{0}, \qquad \mathbf{E} = -\nabla\varphi, \\
Q_{\text{src}} &= \bar{\alpha}\,\mu_e |\mathbf{E}|\,n_e.
\end{align}
Discretization: finite-volume flux integration with 1st-order upwind advective
faces, 2nd-order central diffusive faces, and forward-Euler updates; Poisson via
Trilinos/Belos GMRES ($\text{tol}=10^{-6}$) preconditioned with MueLu multigrid.
Semi-implicit $n \leftrightarrow \varphi \leftrightarrow \mathbf{E}$ coupling
(2--5 sub-iterations). Validated against the community streamer benchmark case~1
(CWI model) plus spatial-convergence and strong/weak scaling studies.

\section{GENUINE CRITIQUE}
This section is written to \emph{stress-test} the replication rather than defend it.

\paragraph{What the MMS confirmation does and does not prove.}
The MMS result (observed order $0.995$) confirms that a correct reimplementation
of the paper's face-flux stencils on a smooth, periodic 1-D problem does indeed
produce first-order convergence, matching the paper's Fig.~7 slope. However, this
is a \emph{necessary but not sufficient} check of the paper's discretization
claim. The paper's Fig.~7 was obtained on a fully coupled 2-D $r$-$z$ nonlinear
streamer with a moving ionization front, whereas the MMS surrogate is
1-D, periodic, and linear (fixed $v$, $D$; no self-consistent field). It certifies
the \emph{stencil}, not the coupled behavior. Any subtle order degradation from
the semi-implicit $n\leftrightarrow \varphi \leftrightarrow \mathbf{E}$ iteration,
axisymmetric geometric source terms, or non-smooth ionization fronts would be
invisible here.

\paragraph{C1--C4 are the easy claims.}
These are arithmetic/CFL self-consistency; passing them shows only that the paper
is internally consistent, not that its physics is right. They are checked because
falsifying them would be devastating, not because passing them is convincing.

\paragraph{The unresolved central physics claims.}
C6 (peak-$E$ convergence $<\!1\%$ at $\Delta h\approx 4\,\mu$m), C7
(within 5\% of CWI), C8 (upwind velocity overestimate), and C9 (HPC scaling) are
precisely the load-bearing scientific claims of the paper---and none of them were
tested. The 1-D nonlinear surrogate went unstable at fine $\Delta h$ due to
explicit-source blow-up; this is a legitimate failure mode of a forward-Euler
scheme applied to a local-field ionization source, but it also means the
critique has \emph{no independent evidence} about whether the paper's coupled
convergence claim would survive an honest reimplementation. A verdict of
REPLICATED would be unwarranted on the current evidence.

\paragraph{Judge dependency.}
The LLM-judge (Argo gpt-5.2) was fed the evidence artifacts and returned
``moderate / PARTIAL,'' which agrees with the human synthesis. This is not
independent corroboration; the judge sees only what we hand it and shares
failure modes with any single-model evaluator. Treat the judge score as a
consistency indicator, not a second opinion.

\paragraph{Environmental risks.}
CherryRd is blocked on \texttt{osti.gov}; PDF acquisition required an SSH proxy
through \texttt{uicgpu}. The MD5 of the fetched artifact
(\texttt{41204e9adef92fa85c980f66c0d8d39f}) should be recorded so that any
future rerun works from an identifiable source. \texttt{pdftotext -layout}
occasionally reflows two-column CPC text; nothing quoted here depends on
delicate table parsing, but claim IDs pulled from the extracted text should be
re-checked against the PDF for any \emph{new} claim added later.

\paragraph{Bottom line.}
The replication verifies that (a) the paper's numbers add up and (b) its stated
discretization is first-order as advertised. Everything about whether the code
\emph{as run at scale} matches CWI streamer physics remains untested by this
effort. The honest verdict is \textbf{PARTIAL}.
