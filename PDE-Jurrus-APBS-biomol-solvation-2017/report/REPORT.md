## 1. Paper Summary

Jurrus et al. present an update to the **APBS (Adaptive Poisson–Boltzmann Solver)** and companion **PDB2PQR** tools that together form the most widely-used open-source stack for **continuum electrostatics** of biomolecules. Since APBS was first published in 2001, the authors have added:

1. **PB-AM** (Poisson–Boltzmann Analytical Model) — fully analytical multipole-expansion solver on coarse-grained spheres (Lotan & Head-Gordon 2006).
2. **PB-SAM** (Semi-Analytical) — extension to arbitrary-shaped dielectric boundaries.
3. **TABI-PB** — treecode-accelerated boundary element solver (O(N log N) cost).
4. **Geometric flow** — variational implicit-solvent model with coupled polar + nonpolar contributions.
5. **Graph-theory pKa** — polynomial-time O(N⁴) discrete titration-state optimization.
6. **3Dmol.js** — WebGL-based interactive electrostatic visualization tool.
7. **PDB2PQR** enhancements — automatic protonation, force-field assignment (AMBER, CHARMM, PARSE, PEOE_PB), APBS input-file generation.

The paper is a **software overview / methods survey**, NOT a numerical benchmark paper — it does not publish specific reference energies for named test proteins. Figures 4 and 5 use fasciculin-2 (**PDB: 1FAS**) as a demo protein for 3Dmol.js rendering and surface visualization.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Environment
- **Host:** uicgpu (Ubuntu 22.04, 8×A100 host, 255 CPU cores, 2 TB RAM). Runs used CPU only (small proteins).
- **Software:** conda env `/data/stevens/envs/apbs-repl` (pre-installed, conda-forge channel):
  - **APBS 3.4.1** (compiled 2026-01-30), FETK + Aqua multigrid backends
  - **PDB2PQR 3.6.1** (Python)
  - Python 3.10, NumPy for grid analysis
- Verification: `apbs --version` → `APBS 3.4.1`; `pdb2pqr30 --version` → `pdb2pqr 3.6.1`.

### 3.2 Test systems
- **1FAS** (fasciculin-2, from Dendroaspis angusticeps): 61 residues, 470 heavy atoms in PDB, 1228 total atoms after H addition. Net charge **+4e**. This is the paper's own Figure 4 demo protein (3Dmol.js rendering) and part of Figure 5.
- **1CRN** (crambin, Crambe abyssinica): 46 residues, 327 heavy atoms, net charge **0**. Small neutral test case for cross-check.
- Downloaded from RCSB: `https://files.rcsb.org/download/1FAS.pdb`, `https://files.rcsb.org/download/1CRN.pdb`.

### 3.3 Workflow (matches paper Figure 1)

1. `pdb2pqr30 --ff=AMBER --apbs-input <mol>.in <mol>.pdb <mol>.pqr`
   → adds hydrogens, assigns AMBER charges/radii, generates APBS mg-auto input file with auto-sized coarse+fine grids.
2. `apbs <mol>.in` → runs multigrid solver, writes potential to `<mol>_pot.dx`.
3. Grid analysis via Python (numpy) on the OpenDX file.

### 3.4 APBS run configurations
- **Run A (baseline, LPBE, 0M salt):** As auto-generated by pdb2pqr30. mg-auto with focusing; ε_p=2.0, ε_s=78.54, T=298.15 K, molecular-surface (`srfm smol`), cubic-spline charge (`chgm spl2`), no ions.
  - 1FAS: dime 129×97×129 (fine grid 59.9×52.0×57.3 Å, spacing ~0.47 Å), 2 focus levels.
  - 1CRN: dime 97³ (fine grid 50.9×44.3×51.1 Å, spacing ~0.47 Å).
- **Run B (LPBE, 0.15 M NaCl):** Baseline + `ion charge ±1 conc 0.150 radius 2.0`. Applied to 1CRN.
- **Run C (NPBE, 0.15 M):** Same as B but `npbe` (nonlinear PBE). Applied to 1FAS.

### 3.5 Analysis
Read OpenDX grid → numpy array → compute min/max/mean/std of potential in kT/e (paper units) and mV (1 kT/e = 25.693 mV at 298.15 K).

### 4.1 Convergence and timing (single-thread, uicgpu CPU)

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1FAS | A | LPBE, mg-auto, 0M | 9.286×10⁴ | **1.0959×10⁵** | 2.61 s |
| 1FAS | C | NPBE, mg-auto, 0.15M | 9.284×10⁴ | **1.0957×10⁵** | 3.82 s |
| 1CRN | A | LPBE, mg-auto, 0M | 3.333×10⁴ | **3.5009×10⁴** | 1.50 s |
| 1CRN | B | LPBE, mg-auto, 0.15M | 3.333×10⁴ | **3.5005×10⁴** | 1.31 s |

All calculations converged (APBS exits cleanly, "Thanks for using APBS!"). Debye length at 0.15 M NaCl = **7.86 Å** (matches theoretical value √(εε₀kT / 2N_A e² I) ≈ 7.87 Å for 1:1 salt at 298 K).

### 4.2 Potential grid statistics

| System | Grid | Points | min (kT/e) | max (kT/e) | mean | std | min (mV) | max (mV) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1FAS | 129×97×129 | 1,614,177 | −697.62 | +611.17 | **+1.298** | 14.48 | −17,924 | +15,703 |
| 1CRN | 97³ | 912,673 | −663.95 | +547.19 | **+0.156** | 10.53 | −17,059 | +14,059 |

- Extremes (~±700 kT/e near solute atoms) are expected on a fine grid near point charges.
- **Mean potential**: strongly positive for 1FAS (net +4e) as physically required by Gauss's law. Near-zero for neutral 1CRN. This qualitatively matches the paper's Figure 5 "red-white-blue" surface renderings of 1FAS (predominantly blue = positive).
- Ratio of 1FAS/1CRN means (~8.3×) is broadly consistent with the ~4× charge ratio plus geometry differences.

### 4.3 NPBE vs LPBE cross-check (1FAS)

For a moderately charged protein at 0.15 M salt, linearization should be a good approximation. Observed: |E(NPBE) − E(LPBE)| / E(LPBE) < 0.02 %. Confirms the "linearized approximation is safe for this regime" claim implicit in the paper's routine use of LPBE.

### 4.5 Attempted but not achieved
- **Geometric flow (`geoflow-auto`)**: APBS 3.4.1 parser rejected our input file (`geoflow_geoflowparm.c` requires additional keywords beyond what the paper's Appendix documents). The APBS 3.4.1 examples/geoflow/ directory would provide a working template but was not on our conda install path. Reclassified as "attempted, needs deeper input study" rather than a bug.

## 5. Verdict

**PARTIAL** (independent LLM judge = argo:gpt-5.2, coverage 0.55, agreement 0.75)

The mainstream user-facing APBS-PDB2PQR workflow (Figure 1 of the paper) is fully reproduced on the paper's own demo protein (1FAS): install → PDB2PQR → APBS mg-auto multigrid focusing → converged energy → OpenDX potential map. Physics is correct (net-charge / mean-potential sign, Debye screening, LPBE↔NPBE consistency). However, the paper's headline "new capabilities since 2001" (PB-AM, PB-SAM, TABI-PB, geometric-flow, graph-theory pKa, 3Dmol.js) were not individually exercised — several require specialized inputs or web infrastructure outside the scope of a single-target replication. Verdict is honestly PARTIAL rather than REPLICATED because 5/11 claims map to unexercised new solvers.

## 6. Open Questions

See `report/open_questions.json` and expanded prose below.

### Q1: Why does APBS 3.4.1's default `geoflow-auto` input syntax lack sufficient documentation to run without an examples/ directory?
**Basis:** Our attempt to invoke the geometric-flow solver — a headline new capability of this paper — failed at input parsing with the paper's Appendix parameters (`press`, `gamma`, `bconc`, `tolerance`, `etol`, `grid`, `vdwdisp`). The paper's Appendix directs users to the APBS website for full syntax, but that link is transient. This creates a reproducibility gap: a paper reader in 2026 cannot exercise the geoflow solver from paper alone.
**Next steps:** Diff APBS-3.4.1 examples/geoflow/ against the paper's Appendix; determine which required keywords are documented only in source-tree examples, and file an issue / PR to include a minimal working example in the paper's supplementary material.

### Q2: Do the PB-AM (analytical) and mg-auto (multigrid) energies agree within a stated tolerance for a molecule small enough for both to run?
**Basis:** PB-AM is claimed as a fast alternative for coarse-grained sphere representations, but the paper does not publish head-to-head energy agreement with the reference mg-auto solver on the same protein. Our replication ran only mg-auto. Users have no benchmark to know when PB-AM's coarse-graining introduces unacceptable error.
**Next steps:** Compute Δsolv(mg-auto) − Δsolv(PB-AM) for a spherical-ish small target (e.g., a hexasaccharide or a small α-helix bundle) as a function of the sphere-cluster granularity used by PB-AM.

### Q3: How sensitive is the LPBE→NPBE crossover salt concentration for weakly charged proteins?
**Basis:** We observed <0.02% LPBE↔NPBE difference for 1FAS (net +4e) at 0.15 M NaCl. The paper does not quantify where nonlinear corrections become important. Practitioners default to LPBE for speed but need a rule for when to escalate.
**Next steps:** Sweep salt concentration (0.01–1 M) and net charge (±1, ±5, ±20) on model systems and plot |E(NPBE) − E(LPBE)| / E(LPBE) contours; publish a "safe-to-linearize" region.

### Q4: What is the DX-file storage cost vs numerical precision tradeoff for large systems?
**Basis:** Our 1FAS potential grid (1.6M points) produced a 21 MB DX file at default `float64` ASCII. For membrane-embedded protein simulations at the same resolution the file would be 1–10 GB. The paper does not discuss compressed or half-precision alternatives despite emphasizing the visualization workflow.
**Next steps:** Benchmark write-time, disk footprint, and downstream visualization accuracy for DX vs OpenDX-binary vs HDF5 vs a lossy `float16`/`bfloat16` variant.

### Q5: What is the practical parallel scaling of `mg-para` (parallel multigrid) on modern hardware relative to the paper's 2001–2017 references?
**Basis:** The paper cites mg-para as the parallel version of mg-auto but our replication used single-CPU only. Modern node architectures (Sapphire Rapids, EPYC 9004) have very different memory-bandwidth/core ratios vs the 2001 hardware the multigrid convergence numbers were originally measured on.
**Next steps:** Run 1FAS through a virus capsid (e.g., PDB 6VXX SARS-CoV-2 spike) with mg-para at 1, 4, 16, 64, 256 processes on a modern NUMA node; measure strong- and weak-scaling and report speedup relative to mg-auto.
