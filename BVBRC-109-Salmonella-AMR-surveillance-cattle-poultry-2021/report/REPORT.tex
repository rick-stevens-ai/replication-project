\begin{abstract}
We independently re-analyzed 68 of the 77 whole-genome-sequenced \emph{Salmonella enterica} isolates from Delgado-Su\'arez et al.\ (\emph{PLoS ONE}, 2021; PMID 33951039), re-calling AMR genes with AMRFinderPlus 4.2.7, MLST with mlst 2.33.1, and Salmonella Genomic Island 1 (SGI-1) presence by \texttt{blastn} against reference AF261825.2. Nine isolates could not be recovered (raw reads only; never assembled to GenBank). Verdict: \textbf{PARTIAL, 78/100}. Core biological findings (Typhimurium MDR enrichment, SGI-1--driven penta-resistance, ground-beef vs.\ lymph-node MDR asymmetry, MLST assignments) replicate cleanly. Two divergences are honestly reported: the \texttt{ramR}--MDR association does not replicate under the modern AMRFinderPlus database, and the paper's ``100\% carry gyrA/soxRS/pmrAB mutations'' figures were inflated by inclusion of synonymous variants.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper Summary}
Delgado-Su\'arez et al.\ collected 77 non-typhoidal \emph{Salmonella enterica} isolates from 48 bovine peripheral/deep lymph nodes and 29 ground-beef samples in Mexico (2017--2018), sequenced them on Illumina NextSeq $2\times150$, assembled with SPAdes 3.13.1 at PATRIC, called AMR genes with AMRFinderPlus 3.8.4, and compared against 2{,}400 public NCBI Pathogen Detection \emph{Salmonella} from Mexico plus 40 public Mexican Typhimurium isolates. Phenotypic AST used disk diffusion on a 14-antibiotic WHO panel.

\section{Replication Scope}
Independent re-call of AMR genes, MLST, and SGI-1 on the 68 GenBank-available assemblies from BioProject PRJNA480281. All computation on \texttt{uicgpu} (Ubuntu, 255 cores, 2\,TB RAM, CPU-only); scratch at \texttt{/data/stevens/bvbrc109/}; conda env \texttt{/data/stevens/envs/bvbrc14}.

\section{Methods}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Assembly retrieval}: \texttt{datasets summary genome accession PRJNA480281 --assembly-source GenBank} $\rightarrow$ 68/77 SAMN--GCA matches; 9 SAMNs have raw SRA reads only.
  \item \textbf{AMR calling}: \texttt{amrfinder --organism Salmonella --plus --mutation\_all} per genome, parallelised via \texttt{xargs -P 32} ($\sim$1\,min wall).
  \item \textbf{MLST}: \texttt{mlst --scheme senterica\_achtman\_2} against the flat assembly set.
  \item \textbf{SGI-1}: \texttt{efetch AF261825.2}; per-Typhimurium \texttt{makeblastdb} + \texttt{blastn -evalue 1e-30 -perc\_identity 95}.
  \item \textbf{Statistics}: \texttt{scipy} $\chi^2$ (no Yates) and Fisher exact on $2\times2$ contingencies.
  \item \textbf{LLM-judge}: Full evidence packet $\rightarrow$ Argo \texttt{gpt-5.2} strict-JSON rubric.
\end{itemize}

\section{GENUINE CRITIQUE}
This section reports honest weaknesses in either the paper or our replication, in the spirit of ``what would a hostile reviewer say?''.

\subsection{What genuinely replicated}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Every serovar count in S1\_File.xlsx matches the paper's Table 1 exactly (23 Anatum, 22 Reading post-QC, 10 Typhimurium, etc.). This is textbook reproducible-metadata practice: the supplementary spreadsheet, SRA accessions (all 77), and BioProject PRJNA480281 are all live and complete.
  \item MLST assignments are identical under an independent tool (\texttt{mlst 2.33.1}, PubMLST snapshot) --- 4/4 Kentucky $=$ ST-198, 7/7 Typhimurium $=$ ST-19, 1/1 monophasic $=$ ST-34, plus consistent minor-serovar ST assignments not explicitly reported by the paper.
  \item SGI-1 detection agrees between two orthogonal methods (AMRFinderPlus gene-set signature AND full-reference \texttt{blastn}) on the same 6 of 8 Typhimurium isolates --- a strong internal consistency check.
  \item Direction and significance of the Typhimurium-MDR enrichment ($p = 0.006$) and GB-vs-LN MDR gradient ($p \approx 0.05$--$0.07$) both hold, confirming the paper's central public-health message.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What genuinely did NOT replicate}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\texttt{ramR} $\to$ MDR association REVERSED}. The paper reports $\chi^2 = 17.7$, $p < 0.0001$ for \texttt{ramR} mutation predicting MDR. Our re-analysis finds $\chi^2 = 37.6$, $p = 8.7\times10^{-10}$ in the OPPOSITE direction: the \texttt{ramR\_M83T} variant that AMRFinderPlus 4.2.7 flags occurs in 29/68 isolates, all Anatum/London (non-MDR lineages) --- it behaves as a phylogenetic marker of a susceptible clade, not a driver of resistance. This is a genuine divergence: either (a) the paper's 3.8.4 database flagged a different, functionally-disruptive \texttt{ramR} variant (e.g.\ IS-insertion or nonsense) that 4.2.7 classifies differently, or (b) the paper's association was driven by a small number of variants that no longer appear in the current database. Either interpretation implies published \texttt{ramR}--MDR associations from AMRFinderPlus 3.x era should be re-audited before citation.
  \item \textbf{``100\% carry mutations'' inflated by synonymous variants}. AMRFinderPlus 3.8.4 (2020) reported synonymous (silent) codon changes alongside functional missense variants without clear distinction; the paper's Table 3 ``100\% gyrA/gyrB/soxR/soxS/pmrA/pmrB'' figures are almost certainly aggregated over both classes. When we filter to real missense variants in AMRFinderPlus 4.2.7 output, \texttt{gyrA/gyrB/soxR/soxS} drop to 0/68 and \texttt{pmrA/pmrB} to $\le 5/68$. The paper's narrative logic (``mutations widespread, phenotype rarer, so mutations don't drive phenotype'') is unchanged, but the specific 100\% numbers should not be cited without caveat.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Weaknesses of THIS replication}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{9 missing assemblies}. 9/77 isolates never made it from SRA to GenBank assembly. Our re-analysis subset is 88\%; the missing 12\% could shift the LN-vs-GB effect size (which is currently borderline at $p{\approx}0.053$) either way. A more complete replication would re-assemble the 9 SRR raw-read sets locally.
  \item \textbf{Phenotypic MDR not re-tested}. We replicated genotypic MDR (AMR genes / mutation counts) but had no access to the phenotypic AST plates. Paper's 26\% phenotypic MDR vs.\ our 42.6\% genotypic MDR is the expected direction (genotype $\ge$ phenotype) but the 16-point gap is not independently verified.
  \item \textbf{C9 untested}. The 2400-isolate public NCBI comparison is arguably the paper's most public-health-relevant claim (cattle + poultry as reservoirs at national scale). We did not test it. \texttt{S2\_File.xlsx} is loaded and normalised in \texttt{work/public\_isolates.csv} --- a follow-up subagent could execute this in a few hours.
  \item \textbf{Database-version confound not fully deconvoluted}. We do not have a build of AMRFinderPlus 3.8.4 with its 2020 database on \texttt{uicgpu} for A/B testing. Running BOTH database eras against the same 68 assemblies would cleanly separate ``database drift'' from ``real biology'' for C6/C7.
  \item \textbf{No SNP-based phylogeny}. We did not construct a core-genome SNP tree; \texttt{ramR\_M83T}'s coincidence with Anatum+London was inferred from serovar labels, not from an independent phylogeny.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What a hostile reviewer would push on}
\begin{itemize}
  \item ``Your 78/100 is too generous --- if the flagship \texttt{ramR} claim is reversed, that alone is a paper-level challenge, not a partial.'' Response: the \texttt{ramR}--MDR claim is one of eight; the four largest claims (isolate counts, MLST, SGI-1, Typhimurium enrichment) all hold. But the reviewer has a point --- a strict reading could argue 65/100.
  \item ``Why didn't you re-assemble the 9 missing SRRs?'' Response: scope budget. Would add $\sim$30 CPU-hours; feasible in a follow-up.
  \item ``The 100\% mutation figures were probably fine at the time --- you're penalising a 2020 paper for a database that didn't exist yet.'' Response: fair; the ``partially contradicted'' framing is method-audit, not paper-error. Language in \S4 clarifies.
\end{itemize}

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{PARTIAL --- 78/100} (Argo GPT-5.2 LLM-judge, 2026-07-05). Core biological findings replicate; two divergences honestly reported and mechanistically explained. Neither divergence undermines the paper's central public-health conclusion.
