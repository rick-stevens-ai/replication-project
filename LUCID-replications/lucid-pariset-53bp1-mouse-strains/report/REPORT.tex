\section{Method}

\subsection{Model implementation}
All five equations coded in \texttt{code/replicate\_pariset.py}:
\begin{align}
\text{Eq. (1)} \quad & \text{RIF}/\mu\text{m}(t) = (a/C_l)\cdot \text{LET}\cdot [q\,e^{-t/\tau} + (1-q)] \\
\text{Eq. (2)} \quad & \text{RIF}/\text{cell}(t) = (b/C_l)\cdot \text{dose}\cdot [q\,e^{-t/\tau} + (1-q)] \\
\text{Eq. (3)} \quad & \text{RIF}/\text{cell}(t) = 1.28 \cdot e^{-t/\tau} \quad \text{(X-ray, 0.1 Gy)} \\
\text{Eq. (4)} \quad & \text{RIF}/\text{cell}(t) = (12.8/C_l)\cdot \text{dose}\cdot e^{-t/\tau} \\
\text{Eq. (5/6)} \quad & \text{RIF}(t) = a\,e^{-t/\tau} + 0.7\cdot \text{RIF}(48\,\text{h})
\end{align}

\section{Critique --- what did NOT work and where the paper is weak}

This section is deliberately non-whitewashed. See also \texttt{failure\_analysis.md}
for a longer-form treatment.

\paragraph{Paper-side weaknesses (surfaced by re-pass).}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{No data deposit.} Confirmed by full-text parse: no supplementary
    information file, no figshare/Zenodo/GitHub URL. Raw 53BP1 foci counts (76 mice
    $\times$ 4 time points $\times$ 3 LET $\times$ multiple doses) are not accessible.
    This is a serious reproducibility gap for a paper whose central claims rest on a
    curated ex-vivo dataset.
    \item \textbf{Fig. 4 is the sole source of per-strain $(\tau, q)$.} These are
    presented as bar heights only, not as a numeric table. Every downstream analysis
    (Tables 1B, 2; Fig. 7B, 7C) is therefore only recoverable through digitization.
    \item \textbf{Fig. 7C is inferentially over-reached.} At $n=4$ strains, the
    critical Pearson $|r|$ for $\alpha=0.05$ is 0.950. Only 2/19 digitized organs
    reach this; \emph{zero} survive Bonferroni correction across the organ panel.
    The paper does not display significance stars on Fig. 7C (the honest choice),
    but the surrounding prose treats these correlations as ``significant,'' which
    over-sells them.
    \item \textbf{Fig. 7B headline $r = 0.61$ ($n=10$) is statistically borderline.}
    Derived $p_{\text{two-sided}} \approx 0.061$. The paper treats this as the
    foundational ``ex-vivo predicts in-vivo'' result, but without the raw B-cell
    survival counts we cannot fully validate; even accepting the reported $r$, the
    result is $p \geq 0.05$ by standard convention.
    \item \textbf{Table 1A is un-falsifiable.} Per-particle, per-strain
    $(\tau, q, \text{RIF}_{\max})$ inputs simply do not appear in the paper. Fig. 4A
    shows only the \emph{combined} $^{40}$Ar + $^{56}$Fe fit. This means the entire
    HZE Pearson matrix is un-checkable from public material.
    \item \textbf{Fig. 6 (MegaMUGA SNP) is completely opaque.} No genotype file, no
    plink deposition, no SNP list; only figure-level conclusions.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Replication-side weaknesses (own our limits).}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Digitization noise, not measured error.} Our $\pm 0.5$ h /
    $\pm 0.01$ error bars are eyeballed. The 4 strains that miss Table 2 cell-match
    might well match under a proper WebPlotDigitizer + tick-anchored uncertainty
    propagation --- we chose not to over-engineer this.
    \item \textbf{Fig. 7C $p$-values assume Pearson normality.} At $n = 4$ this is a
    poor approximation. A permutation ceiling would be more honest but is dominated
    by the fact that $n = 4$ cannot support 19 organ comparisons regardless.
    \item \textbf{We did not attempt to re-parse the PDF with Marker or Nougat.}
    UICGPU parsing was already scheduled for this DOI and had not landed by
    2026-06-23; \texttt{pdftotext -layout} was adequate for the numeric claims that
    matter, but a proper Marker MMD would help the qualitative prose grep.
    \item \textbf{Strain-level $(\tau, q)$ from digitization only captures the point
    estimates.} The paper's uncertainty on those fits (from bootstrap or from the
    original per-cell counts) is inaccessible to us.
    \item \textbf{No independent digitization by a second annotator.} Table 2 quadrant
    mismatches (4/15) could be genuine paper--replicator disagreement or could be
    digitization noise near a median boundary; a second-annotator study would
    disambiguate. Not attempted.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Verdict}

\textbf{PARTIAL REPLICATION.} The mathematical core, identifiability, per-strain
kinetics, headline Table 1B correlation, Table 2 quadrant structure, LET scaling,
Eq.~(3) prefactor, sublinear dose--response, and Fig. 7C qualitative direction all
reproduce. The two data-blocked claim families (Table 1A per-particle, Fig. 6
SNPs, and half of Fig. 7B) are named with exact missing artifacts under the 6/22
rule. The single \emph{disagreement} between replication and paper (Fig. 7C
inferential over-reach at $n=4$) is a paper-side issue, not a mismatch.
