# Brief — OSTI 3023663

**Paper.** Lu, Fan, Zhang, Cao, Zhang, Li (2026). *Towards Generalizable and Efficient Circuit Topology Design: A Graph-Transformer-based Surrogate Model with Curriculum Learning*. ACM TODAES 31(4) Art. 65. https://doi.org/10.1145/3768167 · OSTI 3023663.

**What/why.** The authors design **GTN**, a Graph-Transformer surrogate model for predicting power-converter circuit performance (efficiency, output voltage) from topology + duty-cycle, replacing NGSPICE with a ~196× faster learned evaluator. Key idea: a topological graph that has **both** device and connection-node vertices, with two attention streams — **neighbor aggregation** (Kirchhoff current law) and **minimal-loop aggregation** (Kirchhoff voltage law) — merged per layer. Curriculum learning pre-trains on 5-component circuits and fine-tunes on 7/10-component circuits to bridge the cold-start gap for scarce large-scale data. Claim: RSE improvements of 16.1% over Circuit-GNN and 87.7% over state-space averaging on 5-comp; consistent ≥14% improvement over best GNN baseline on 7- and 10-comp; 33–51% additional improvement with curriculum learning.

**Replication task.** No public dataset or code was released (dataset is 959k NGSPICE-simulated 5-comp converters generated by the authors). This replication (a) verifies the paper's method and claims by careful reading, and (b) executes an **independent minimal reproduction**: on a synthetic-but-faithful proxy dataset of two-phase switched-capacitor/inductor converters, we implement GTN's two-attention architecture from scratch in PyTorch Geometric, compare it head-to-head to GCN and GAT baselines with identical training budgets, and test the paper's central qualitative claim that the loop-aware Graph-Transformer beats plain GNNs on circuit-topology regression, including a generalization test (train on smaller circuits, test on larger unseen topologies).
