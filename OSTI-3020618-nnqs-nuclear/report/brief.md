# Brief — OSTI-3020618

**Paper:** Lovato, Carleo, Fore, Hjorth-Jensen, Kim, Rios, Rocco (2026), *Neural-network quantum states for the nuclear many-body problem*, FERMILAB-PUB-26-0101-PPD, arXiv:2602.13826.

**What / why.** This is a comprehensive review of how artificial-neural-network representations of the nuclear many-body wave function (NNQS) — combined with continuum quantum Monte Carlo — have extended ab initio calculations of finite nuclei, infinite matter and lepton–nucleus scattering. Because the paper is a review, its "reproducible core" is the set of benchmark calculations it aggregates from the underlying primary papers (Refs. [47–52]). The single cleanest, hardware-independent claim to reproduce is the *deuteron NNQS proof-of-principle* (Sec. 4.1, Fig. 4.2–4.4), which asserts that a minimal one-hidden-layer MLP with N_hid ~ 10 nodes, trained by RMSprop on a Gauss–Legendre momentum grid, converges the deuteron binding energy to within ~keV of exact diagonalization and reaches wave-function fidelity F > 0.999. This is exactly what we independently reproduced on uicgpu (1×A100) with a coupled-channel Yamaguchi separable NN potential tuned to the experimental deuteron binding energy: best seed at N_hid=10 achieves ΔE = 0.52 keV and F = 0.99997 versus exact — a like-for-like confirmation of the method claim (Claim C1/C3), while Table-4.1 pionless-EFT SJ numbers (C2) remain out of reach without a full 4-body VMC engine.
