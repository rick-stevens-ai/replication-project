\section{Paper in one paragraph}
The authors whole-genome-sequenced three \emph{E.\ faecalis} strains isolated from Bangladeshi Nile tilapia (BFFF11, BFF1B1) and Thai sarpunti (BFPS6) with streptococcosis-like disease using Illumina MiSeq, assembled with SPAdes, annotated with Prokka + PATRIC/RASTtk, and screened for virulence factors (VirulenceFinder + VFDB + PATRIC/Victors) and antimicrobial-resistance genes (ResFinder + ARG-ANNOT + CARD + PATRIC). They report 69 virulence genes and 39 antibiotic-resistance genes across the three strains, with several strain-specific patterns (11 capsule genes \emph{cpsA--K} exclusively in BFFF11; four tetracycline-resistance genes \emph{tet(M)/tet(L)/tet(S)/tet(45)} exclusively in BFPS6; cytolysin \emph{cylI/cylR2} in BFPS6/BFF1B1 but not BFFF11; and others).

\textbf{Finding:} Paper Table 1 column labels for BFFF11 and BFF1B1 are \textbf{swapped} relative to what NCBI stores under those strain names. The genome-size numbers match to the base pair --- but 2{,}761{,}629 bp belongs to strain BFFF11 (NCBI CP045918), not BFF1B1 as printed. Paper Table 1 N50/L50 for BFPS6 (270{,}331 / 2) matches our recomputed values exactly; the other two rows appear to have been populated from pre-closure draft assemblies, then the closed chromosomes were deposited. \subsection{Antimicrobial-resistance screen --- AMRFinderPlus 3.12.8, DB 2024-07-22.1 (\S3b)}
\begin{lstlisting}
amrfinder -n assemblies/<strain>.fna -O Enterococcus_faecalis \
          --plus --threads 8 -o amr/<strain>_amr_v2.tsv
\end{lstlisting}
Outputs: BFFF11 = \emph{lsa(A)}; BFF1B1 = \emph{lsa(A)}; BFPS6 = \emph{lsa(A), tet(L), tet(M)}. All calls 99--100\% pident, 100\% coverage (WP\_002398829.1 for \emph{lsa(A)}, WP\_001574277.1 for \emph{tet(L)}, WP\_001574275.1 for \emph{tet(M)}). \emph{lsa(A)} shared across all three matches paper Table 2 exactly; \emph{tet(L)} and \emph{tet(M)} exclusively in BFPS6 matches paper Table 2 exactly. The paper's 39-gene AMR count comes from combining ARG-ANNOT + ResFinder + CARD + PATRIC, several of which score housekeeping targets (\emph{gyrA/gyrB, rpoB/rpoC, murA}, \ldots) as ``resistance'' via mutation-in-target logic; AMRFinderPlus with \texttt{--plus} intentionally excludes those.

\subsection{Virulence screen --- VFDB set-A tblastn (\S3c)}
Downloaded \texttt{VFDB\_setA\_pro.fas.gz} (4{,}732 curated virulence proteins) from \texttt{mgc.ac.cn/VFs/Down/}. Filtered to 50 sequences (43 unique symbols) covering all paper-mentioned targets. BLAST DB built per assembly with \texttt{makeblastdb}; queried with \texttt{tblastn -evalue 1e-10 -num\_threads 8}; kept best hit per query at pident $\geq$ 40\%, qcov $\geq$ 40\%. Per-strain hits reproduce paper Table 2 exclusivity patterns (see \S3c of \texttt{REPORT.md} for the full per-gene pident/qcov matrix).

\section{Genuine critique}
\label{sec:critique}

\subsection{What actually replicated}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Assembly-level statistics (C1, C4):} bp-exact match on BFFF11 and BFF1B1; 0.05\% drift on BFPS6 (SPAdes vs.\ RefSeq post-processing). This is as good as it gets.
  \item \textbf{Core, always-present virulence set (C2a):} all 12 genes called in all 3 strains at $\geq$98\% pident, $\geq$95\% qcov. High confidence.
  \item \textbf{Strain-exclusive patterns (C2c, C2d, C2e, C2f):} \emph{prgB}/\emph{asa1}/\emph{ctrA}/\emph{cylI}/\emph{cylR2} presence/absence matches paper exactly, with independent VFDB curation --- these are the most credible claims in the paper because they are qualitative (yes/no) and the independent tool agreed on every one.
  \item \textbf{Capsule cluster distribution (C2b, partial):} 9 of 11 \emph{cps} genes exclusively in BFFF11. The pattern is real.
  \item \textbf{Key AMR calls (C3a partial, C3b partial):} \emph{lsa(A)} in all 3; \emph{tet(L)}/\emph{tet(M)} exclusively in BFPS6. These are the clinically meaningful acquired-resistance calls in the paper and they hold up.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What did NOT replicate (or could not be independently confirmed)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Total gene counts (69 VF + 39 AMR).} We did not reproduce the aggregate counts because we did not stand up all four VF databases (VirulenceFinder + VFDB + PATRIC/Victors) or all four AMR databases (ARG-ANNOT + ResFinder + CARD + PATRIC) in parallel. Approximately 16 VF genes called by the paper via Victors/PATRIC were not recoverable from VFDB set-A. The total counts are therefore \emph{database-dependent} and not independently verified here.
  \item \textbf{\emph{tet(S)} and \emph{tet(45)}:} not called by NCBI AMRFinderPlus at defaults. Paper reports these at 77--100\% ARG-ANNOT identity. Tool-scope difference, not a contradiction --- but also not an independent confirmation.
  \item \textbf{\emph{mph(D)} and \emph{dfr(E)}:} claimed present across all 3 strains but not called by AMRFinderPlus. ARG-ANNOT/PATRIC-only entries with weaker NCBI curation. Not confirmed.
  \item \textbf{``Only \emph{cpsA} in BFF1B1'' (part of C2b):} VFDB conflates \emph{cpsA} with the housekeeping gene \emph{uppS} and \emph{cpsB} with \emph{cdsA}. Because these housekeeping paralogs are always present, the paper's ``only cpsA'' claim in BFF1B1 cannot be cleanly separated from housekeeping signal in an independent tblastn rerun.
  \item \textbf{Phenotypic resistance:} the paper is \emph{in silico}-only. No MIC data, no disk-diffusion validation. Predicted resistance $\ne$ phenotypic resistance --- this is not a replication gap but a paper-scope limit worth naming.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Novel side-finding: paper Table 1 header swap}
Table 1 as printed labels the 2{,}761{,}629 bp column as BFF1B1 and the 3{,}067{,}042 bp column as BFFF11. NCBI stores those genomes the other way around (CP045918.1 = BFFF11 = 2{,}761{,}629 bp; CP046022.1 = BFF1B1 = 3{,}067{,}042 bp). Additionally, the N50/L50 values in Table 1 for BFFF11 and BFF1B1 (\textasciitilde384k / \textasciitilde343k) do not match the fully closed single-chromosome NCBI records (N50 = full chromosome length) --- Table 1 appears to have been populated from pre-closure draft assemblies. This is a paper-side clerical inconsistency worth flagging in any downstream citation.

\subsection{Methodological weaknesses of the original paper}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Database-aggregation opacity:} summing calls across 4 VF databases and 4 AMR databases without a per-database attribution table makes the totals irreproducible unless every one of the 8 databases is available at the exact version used. Version drift alone will move the 69/39 counts.
  \item \textbf{No de-duplication of housekeeping-vs-virulence hits.} Because Victors/PATRIC score many mutation-in-target loci as ``resistance,'' the reported AMR count is inflated relative to a curated acquired-resistance tool (AMRFinderPlus \texttt{--plus}).
  \item \textbf{No mobile-genetic-element / plasmid analysis.} \emph{tet(M)}, \emph{tet(L)}, \emph{lsa(A)} are frequently plasmid- or ICE-borne; the paper does not report chromosomal-vs-mobile linkage, which matters for horizontal-transfer risk.
  \item \textbf{No phenotype validation.} \emph{In silico} genotype-to-phenotype claims should be corroborated with MICs; the paper does not.
  \item \textbf{Assembly-provenance mismatch:} Table 1 stats appear to describe draft assemblies while deposited records are closed chromosomes.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What would strengthen a re-do}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Publish per-database, per-strain hit tables (with database version pin) rather than aggregate 69/39 counts.
  \item Add MobileElementFinder + PlasmidFinder + oriTfinder to distinguish chromosomal from mobile AMR loci.
  \item Include at least disk-diffusion phenotype for the four tetracycline-resistance calls in BFPS6.
  \item Correct Table 1 column headers and repopulate stats from the deposited closed chromosomes.
  \item Report AMRFinderPlus results alongside ARG-ANNOT/CARD/ResFinder so acquired-resistance calls are separable from mutation-in-target calls.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Bottom line}
The paper's \emph{qualitative} core --- strain-exclusive virulence and tetracycline-resistance patterns --- replicates cleanly with independent tooling. The \emph{quantitative} core (69 VF + 39 AMR aggregate counts) is a database-and-version-dependent number that we neither confirm nor refute. \textbf{PARTIAL REPLICATION (strong)} is the honest verdict: strong on what we could touch, silent on what requires reinstantiating the paper's full 8-database stack.
