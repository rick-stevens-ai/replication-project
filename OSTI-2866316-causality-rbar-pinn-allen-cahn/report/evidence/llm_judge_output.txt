### (a) Coverage of testable claims
- **Covered (directly testable without COMSOL):** the *causality-training mechanism itself* (Eqs. 9–11 weighting behavior), and the *“erroneous convergence” pathology* (low training loss yet wrong PDE solution) on an Allen–Cahn PINN with an independent numerical reference.

- **Not covered / not testable here:** the paper’s **phase-field Allen–Cahn setup with COMSOL FEM comparison**, and the **RBAR adaptive refinement** component (central to the claimed improvement in the 2026 paper).

### (b) Claims reproduced vs not
- **Reproduced:**
  - Causality weighting implemented correctly and behaves as intended (later slabs down-weighted; weights can collapse toward 0).
  - The motivating failure mode: **vanilla PINN reaches low loss (~1e-2) but large solution error (~69% relative L2)** (“erroneous convergence”).
  - Qualitative structure: **good early-time accuracy and severe late-time degradation** for both methods.
- **Not reproduced:**
  - The **improvement claim** that causality training (and especially causality+RBAR) fixes erroneous convergence / yields better accuracy: causal PINN was **worse** than vanilla under the reduced-compute configuration (0.861 vs 0.692 relative L2), and RBAR was not implemented.

### (c) Verdict
**PARTIAL**

### (d) 2-sentence justification
The replication credibly validates the *core causality-weighting mechanism* and reproduces the paper’s central motivating pathology (low loss yet wrong Allen–Cahn solution), including the early-to-late time error growth pattern. However, it does **not** reproduce the paper’s key improvement outcome (causality helping, and causality+RBAR fixing convergence), and RBAR/COMSOL-based phase-field benchmarks were not replicated, so the main performance claim remains unconfirmed.
