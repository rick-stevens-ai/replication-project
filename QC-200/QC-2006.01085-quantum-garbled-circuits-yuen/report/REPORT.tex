\begin{abstract}
Brakerski \& Yuen (2020) construct the first \emph{decomposable randomized encoding scheme
for quantum computation}: a garbling scheme that turns any quantum circuit $C$ and quantum
input $x$ into an encoding $\hat C(x)$ from which the honest evaluator can recover $C(x)$
and \emph{nothing else}. This is a \emph{theoretical} cryptography paper --- the primary
results are constructions and proofs, not benchmark numbers. Consequently we replicate the
core building blocks it invokes and generalizes, and verify the two properties that admit
concrete numerical checks: \textbf{(i) perfect correctness} of the Clifford slice of the
scheme and \textbf{(ii) perfect statistical hiding} of the garbled state via the Pauli
one-time-pad property. \textbf{Verdict: SPOT-CHECK.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper summary}
The paper presents a Quantum Garbled Circuit (QGC) scheme with perfect correctness and
perfect information-theoretic security (at the cost of a double-exponential blow-up in
depth); with quantum-secure PRGs the blow-up becomes polynomial. Two applications are
highlighted: (1) a decomposable randomized encoding for quantum computation (the first),
and (2) a $\Sigma$-protocol for QMA with a single-bit challenge and delayed-input
property. Encoding runs in constant-depth $\mathsf{QNC}^0_f$.

The construction has two conceptual layers:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Quantum layer:} computation-by-teleportation for the Clifford part of the
        circuit --- prepare EPR pairs per wire, apply the circuit's gates to the
        second-halves, then teleport connected wires. Non-Clifford ($T$) gates are handled
        via a gadget that consumes a magic-state resource; the accumulated Pauli
        ``teleportation mask'' $X^{a'} Z^{b'}$ is deterministically computed from the
        intermediate teleportation keys and the circuit.
  \item \textbf{Classical layer:} the correction function $f_{\text{corr}}$ that turns
        intermediate teleportation keys into the final mask is itself compiled into a
        classical randomized encoding (Yao-style garbled circuit), which the decoder
        evaluates to recover $(a',b')$ and strip the mask off the received quantum state.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Method}
\subsection{Environment}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Python 3.14 in venv \texttt{work/venv/}.
  \item Qiskit 2.5.0, NumPy 2.5.1 (independent cross-check).
  \item \texttt{cryptography} (AES-GCM) for the classical Yao encryption primitive.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Reproduction protocol}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \texttt{report/evidence/yao\_and\_gate.py}: implements Yao's original construction
        for a 2-input AND gate using AES-256-GCM per wire label; garbled table is
        permuted; the evaluator tries all 4 rows and only the correct label decrypts.
  \item \texttt{report/evidence/qgc\_clifford\_teleport.py}: implements the Clifford
        slice of the QGC construction directly on density matrices. The Pauli-frame
        update rules encode $G X^a Z^b = X^{a'} Z^{b'} G$ for $G \in \{H, S,
        \text{CNOT}\}$. We verify:
        \begin{itemize}
          \item Correctness of $H|0\rangle$, $HSH|0\rangle$, and CNOT on
                $|0\rangle|+\rangle$ (decoded state = ideal state, fidelity 1).
          \item Statistical hiding: averaging $X^a Z^b \rho (X^a Z^b)^\dagger$ over
                uniform $(a,b)$ yields $\mathbb{I}/2$ (1q) and $\mathbb{I}/4$ (2q) --- the
                maximally mixed state, which is trivially indistinguishable across inputs.
        \end{itemize}
  \item \texttt{report/evidence/qiskit\_crosscheck.py}: independent Qiskit implementation
        of the same tests using \texttt{qiskit.quantum\_info.Statevector},
        \texttt{DensityMatrix}, \texttt{Operator} to shield against numpy-only bugs.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Per-claim: what worked, what didn't}

\paragraph{C1 (Yao baseline).} Fully worked. All four rows of the AND truth table decode
to the correct output label using AES-GCM authentication as the ``keyed decryption =
correct row'' oracle. This is exactly the classical primitive Brakerski-Yuen invoke as
$f_{\text{corr}}$.

\paragraph{C2 (Clifford correctness).} Fully worked at the toy scale. The Pauli-frame
update tables for $H$ and $S$ match the paper's rule $GX^aZ^b = X^{a'}Z^{b'}G$ (mod global
phase), and the two-qubit CNOT rule
$\text{CNOT}(X^a Z^b \otimes X^c Z^d)\text{CNOT} = X^a Z^{b\oplus d} \otimes X^{a\oplus c} Z^d$
was verified in-code. The decoded state matches the ideal $C(x)$ to machine precision.

\paragraph{C3 (Statistical hiding).} Fully worked at the toy scale. Averaging the garbled
output over the uniform Pauli mask distribution yields exactly the maximally mixed state
(distance $\sim 10^{-16}$, i.e.\ numerical zero). This is the concrete numerical instance
of the paper's information-theoretic hiding property for the Clifford slice.

\paragraph{C4 (Constant-depth $\mathsf{QNC}^0_f$).} Not tested numerically. This is a
structural claim about circuit depth. A future extension would use Qiskit's
\texttt{DAGCircuit} depth counter on the explicit garbling circuit to spot-check that
depth is bounded by a constant when fan-out is available.

\paragraph{C5 ($T$-gate gadget).} Deferred. Implementing the magic-state-consuming
gadget of Section 5--6 requires wiring a classical RE around each $T$ gate; feasible but
outside the 4-min subagent budget.

\paragraph{C6 (QMA $\Sigma$-protocol).} Not tested. This is a cryptographic protocol
proof, not a numerical experiment.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{SPOT-CHECK.} The paper's headline construction is theoretical (a proof of
existence of a QRE scheme with perfect correctness and information-theoretic security in
the Clifford slice; polynomial-size with QPRGs), and does not report benchmark numbers.
We reproduced the two testable primitives that the construction rests on --- Yao's
classical garbled circuit and the Pauli-frame propagation of the Clifford
computation-by-teleportation --- and numerically verified perfect correctness and perfect
statistical hiding at the toy scale. Qiskit and numpy implementations agree to machine
precision. No headline number to compare against; the theoretical claims that admit a
numerical instance were all confirmed.
