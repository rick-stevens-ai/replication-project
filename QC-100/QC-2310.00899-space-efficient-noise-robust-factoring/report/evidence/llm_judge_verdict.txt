VERDICT: PARTIAL

Your work reproduces and validates an end-to-end Shor-style **order-finding → continued fractions → gcd** pipeline on an actual Aer simulator, which is genuinely relevant because Ragavan–Vaikuntanathan (and Regev) ultimately rely on the same conceptual reduction from factoring to extracting order/period information plus classical postprocessing. The observed per-shot success rates for small \(N\) (order-4 ≈ 0.75, order-2 ≈ 0.5, order-6 ≈ 0.28–0.30) are consistent with standard phase-estimation/continued-fractions behavior, so this is a meaningful sanity check that the “base layer” works as expected. However, the paper’s headline contributions are **asymptotic resource improvements** (the \(O(n\log n)\) qubit construction with a concrete constant) and two substantial algorithmic components (Fibonacci/Kaliski modular exponentiation and the robust lattice-based filtering) that you did not implement or test. Your noise sweep is useful as a qualitative demonstration of circuit fragility/robustness under depolarizing noise, but it does not directly test the paper’s **constant-fraction corrupted-samples** guarantee, which is a statement about the *classical* postprocessing tolerating adversarial/abstractly corrupted outputs rather than gate-level depolarization. Because you verified a necessary subpipeline but not the novel constructions or their claimed asymptotic advantages/robustness guarantees, “REPLICATED” would overstate what was achieved. At the same time, it’s more than a spot-check because you ran full end-to-end factoring for multiple \(N\) and bases with measured success probabilities and a noise sweep.

Caveats to flag:
- Do not frame the results as reproducing the paper’s main claims (C1/C5/C6); they currently validate only the **common Shor/Regev substrate** (C2/C3) plus a **non-matching** noise experiment (partial C4 at best).
- Small-\(n\) qubit counts are not informative for the paper’s asymptotic constant (10.32\(n\))—low-order terms dominate and your implementation is not the same circuit family anyway.
- The depolarizing-noise sweep should be explicitly labeled as “circuit-level robustness of textbook order-finding,” not evidence for the paper’s “constant-fraction corrupted samples” postprocessing theorem.
- If you want a tighter connection to C4/C6 without implementing the full quantum construction, consider injecting *synthetic corrupted samples* into the classical postprocessing stage (e.g., replace a constant fraction of measured outcomes with adversarial/random values) and test whether a Regev-style filtering implementation still recovers the order/factors.
