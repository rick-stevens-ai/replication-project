\section*{TL;DR}

\textbf{VERDICT: REPLICATED.} \quad COVERAGE=9/10 \quad AGREEMENT=10/10

McMahon, Schuemann, Paganetti, Prise (\emph{Scientific Reports} 6:33290, 2016)
present the original analytic Medras model: a closed-form mechanistic
double-strand-break (DSB) induction, two-compartment (fast NHEJ / slow HR
or complex-NHEJ) repair with MMEJ fallback, and misrepair-geometry kernel
that jointly predicts foci kinetics, PFGE misrepair, chromosome
aberrations, HPRT mutations, and clonogenic survival from
\textbf{11 fitted parameters}. The authors released, under CC BY 4.0, a
35-page Supplementary Information PDF with the full derivations, and a
Supplementary Code ZIP containing 6 Python modules plus the curated
180-row DNA-endpoint and 187-row survival datasets used for global
fitting.

Re-running the authors' code (after a $\approx$4-line Python 2 $\rightarrow$ Python 3
port, no algorithmic changes) on this host reproduces \textbf{all 11
fitted parameters within the paper-quoted $\pm 1\sigma$ uncertainty} of
Table 1 and regenerates \textbf{all 6 model-curve TSVs} underlying Figs.
1--5, with survival spot-checks at 2 Gy and 6 Gy that match Fig. 5 and
the two qualitative findings the paper highlights (NHEJ-defective cells
more resistant in G2 than G1; delayed plating more resistant than
immediate). The only omitted figure is \textbf{Fig. 7} (observed-vs-predicted
MID scatter, $R^2 = 0.91$ / $0.96$): the underlying per-cell-line MIDs
exist in the regenerated survival TSV; only the scatter PNG is
un-assembled. See \S~Critique for honest limitations.

\section{Paper summary}

The paper argues that a compact ($\sim$11 free parameters) mechanistic
model, grounded in DSB induction kinetics, two-compartment repair,
MMEJ-fallback, and an analytic misrepair-geometry kernel, can
simultaneously explain endpoints as diverse as \SI{53}{BP1}/$\gamma$H2AX
foci decay, PFGE misrepair vs.\ dose, dicentric and inter-arm-exchange
aberration yields, HPRT deletion/point-mutation ratios, and clonogenic
survival across multiple cell lines and cell-cycle phases. The
mechanistic story is: DSBs are induced linearly in dose $\times$ genome
content (5.738 DSB/Gy/Gbp, fixed from literature); a fraction $p_c$ are
``complex'' and repair on the slow branch; NHEJ has near-perfect fidelity
$\mu_{\text{NHEJ}} = 0.985$ while MMEJ has poor fidelity
$\mu_{\text{MMEJ}} = 0.465$; misrepair pairs breaks with a Gaussian-in-
distance kernel of range $\sigma \approx 0.043 R_{\text{nucleus}}$;
survival in G1 follows $\exp(-N_{\text{dic}} - N_{\text{del}>3\text{Mbp}})$
and in G2 follows $\exp(-N_{\text{dic}} - N_{\text{inter-arm}})$, with
optional apoptosis and mitotic-catastrophe multipliers $\psi$ and $\phi$
for cycling cells.

The primary quantitative outputs are Table 1 (the 11 fitted parameters
with $\pm 1\sigma$), Figs. 1--6 (model curves vs.\ data), and Fig. 7
(a validation MID scatter with $R^2 = 0.91$ in G1 and $0.96$ in G2).

\section{Method}

\textbf{Paper's method} (Methods + Supplementary Methods \S S1--\S S5):
closed-form analytic Medras model, weighted nonlinear least-squares
(scipy \texttt{leastsq}), first over 180 curated DNA points
(9 free parameters), then over 187 curated survival points (2 free
parameters, $\psi$ and $\phi$, with DNA parameters fixed).

\textbf{This replication's method:} identical. We re-ran the authors' own
source (from the SI ZIP) after three textual Python~2 $\rightarrow$
Python~3 changes across the six modules:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \texttt{xrange $\rightarrow$ range}
  \item bare \texttt{print X} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{print(X)}
  \item \texttt{DNAModelFit.py:58}: \texttt{row = map(float, row)}
        $\rightarrow$ \texttt{row = list(map(float, row))}
\end{enumerate}

No equations, branching logic, parameter bounds, initial guesses,
weighting, or fit routines were modified. Environment: CherryRd (Darwin
25.3.0, x86\_64), Python 3.14.4, numpy 2.4.3, scipy 1.18.0. No paid
endpoints, no GPU, no HPC.

\textbf{Substitutions:} none.

\section{Critique (honest limitations)}
\label{sec:critique}

This is one of LUCID-100's cleanest wins, but ``clean'' is not
``complete''. Genuine limitations:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Fig. 7 ($R^2 = 0.91$ / $0.96$) is not numerically tested.}
        The underlying per-cell-line MIDs are recoverable from
        \texttt{Model Data - Survival.tsv}, but the observed-vs-predicted
        scatter and its regression $R^2$ values are not re-assembled in
        this audit. The MC-derived R$^2$ headline is therefore
        \emph{structurally bracketed}, not verified. A future pass should
        parse per-line observed MIDs from
        \texttt{Full Survival Data Sets.csv}, compute predicted MIDs from
        the reproduced survival curves, fit a linear regression, and
        publish the resulting R$^2$ values. Until then, the numeric
        R$^2$ claim in the paper's abstract/Fig. 7 caption is
        \emph{unaudited}.

  \item \textbf{Geometry constants $A = 0.757$, $B = 5.39$ (SI Fig. S2)
        are not re-derived from scratch.} The paper pins these once from
        an independent Monte Carlo run whose code is \emph{not} in the
        supplementary archive. The analytic re-fit uses the published
        pinned values directly, so any error, dataset bias, or version
        drift in the 2016 MC that produced $A,B$ propagates silently
        into this audit's parameter values. The sibling Medras-MC 2021
        repo (\texttt{sjmcmahon/Medras-MC}, replicated in
        \texttt{lucid-medras-mc/}) is a plausible substitute for a
        re-derivation, but was not exercised here.

  \item \textbf{Fit-uncertainty intervals are known-underestimates by
        the authors' own admission.} SI \S S5 states that the
        covariance-matrix $1\sigma$ intervals may underestimate the true
        uncertainty ``by a factor of a few'' due to an ad-hoc 5\%
        data-extraction inflation. Our within-$\pm 1\sigma$ verdicts
        (11/11) therefore live inside a genuinely-optimistic uncertainty
        envelope --- a proper profile-likelihood or MCMC re-analysis
        might show wider intervals and put more parameters at the edge.

  \item \textbf{The high survival $\chi^2 / N = 16.3$ is not
        interrogated.} We accept the authors' framing that clonogenic
        survival has large inter-experimental variance, but we did not
        stratify residuals by cell line, phase, or dose range to check
        whether the misfit is uniform or concentrated in a subset that
        would identify a specific mechanistic gap.

  \item \textbf{Reproducibility friction (documented but not fixed
        upstream).} The Python 2.7 source is a live foot-gun in 2026;
        the SI ZIP has no internal manifest or SHA256SUMS; the
        cell-line-index $\rightarrow$ primary-citation map (SI Table S1
        vs.\ numeric CSV column) is implicit rather than shipped as a
        joined TSV; \texttt{requirements.txt} is absent. We ported and
        verified locally, but the upstream artifacts remain harder to
        pick up cold than they need to be.

  \item \textbf{Cell-line panel coverage is aggregate, not
        stratified.} All 11 cell lines in SI Table S1 enter the global
        fit and appear in the regenerated TSV, but we did not compute
        per-cell-line goodness-of-fit or verify that no single line
        dominates the fit --- a concentration of residuals in one line
        would materially change the story.

  \item \textbf{No sensitivity or identifiability analysis of the
        11-parameter fit.} With 11 parameters against $\sim 180$ + $187$
        curated points spread over heterogeneous endpoints, parameter
        identifiability and cross-parameter correlations (e.g.\
        $p_c \leftrightarrow p_{\text{fail}}$, $\lambda_S \leftrightarrow
        \lambda_M$) are non-trivial. This audit did not compute the
        parameter covariance matrix's off-diagonal structure nor run a
        profile-likelihood scan.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Bottom line:} 11/11 parameters within $\pm 1\sigma$ and 6/7
figure curves regenerated on the authors' own code and data is a
\emph{strong} REPLICATED result --- but the strength is largely inherited
from the authors' generosity in shipping the code, not from a
particularly deep independent audit. The residual questions above
(especially \#1--\#4) are the reason this is 9/10 coverage, not 10/10.
