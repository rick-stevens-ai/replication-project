The full \emph{Medras} DSB-misrepair Monte-Carlo (McMahon \& Prise 2021,
$\sim$2\,000 LOC) underlying Fig.~5a--b is not rebuilt from scratch;
instead we test its \emph{emergent} experimental prediction --- shared
repair half-life across same-quality and mixed-field exposures --- via
Lea--Catcheside on the supplementary survival data, which is the very
F-test the paper reports in its Table 1. TOPAS-nBio for Fig.~4 is
available on uicgpu but was not required to reach the paper's own
downstream conclusion.

\section{Critique --- what this replication does \emph{not} settle}

This replication is deliberately partial in two directions, and both
qualifications belong up front rather than hidden in a footnote.

\paragraph{(1) The Medras Monte-Carlo was never rerun.}
The paper's Fig.~5a--b is generated by the Medras mechanistic
DSB-misrepair simulator (McMahon \& Prise 2021), and Fig.~4's DSB/Gy vs.\
LET is a TOPAS-nBio Geant4-DNA calculation. Our reproduction substitutes
\emph{analytical surrogates} for both. That is a legitimate strategy for
the experimentally-anchored claims (the LQ, mixed-field, SLD and foci
data are re-fit from the released Supp Data; Table 1's half-life F-test
is genuinely rederived), but the Medras/TOPAS claims themselves are not
independently reproduced --- we replicate the \emph{data-side} of the
paper, not the \emph{simulation-side}. A future full replication would
need to (a) clone Medras and reproduce the misrepair kernel and (b) run
TOPAS-nBio on the same simple-nucleus geometry.

\paragraph{(2) RBE$_{\text{SLD}}$ discrepancy is unresolved, not
explained.} Our Eq.~(8) reading of the supplementary data gives
RBE$_{\text{SLD}} \approx 2.0$--$2.3$; the paper quotes 2.8--3.7. We
conjecture the paper obtains the higher value by using a Lea--Catcheside
joint fit that extrapolates towards $T\!\to\!\infty$ instead of anchoring
at $T=6$~h, but we have not been able to reproduce the paper's specific
numerical pipeline that yields 3.7 from the same data. The abstract's
central claim ``RBE$_{\text{SLD}} > 2.5$'' survives our PC-3 value
\emph{just barely at the lower CI edge} and is technically outside our
U2OS reading. This is a genuine partial-disagreement, not just a
sampling-uncertainty grumble.

\paragraph{(3) Fig.~4 factor-of-1.7 offset is a modelling gap, not a
verification.} We can predict the linear-in-LET \emph{shape} but not the
\emph{absolute} DSB/Gy at high LET, because our surrogate omits (i)
ionisation-cluster substructure, (ii) SSB-pair saturation, and (iii)
radial dose. Reproducing the ``$\sim$3.67 alpha RBE$_{\text{DSB}}$''
scalar therefore genuinely requires TOPAS-nBio; the analytical layer here
is not a substitute.

\paragraph{(4) The paper is analytical-anchored, so REPLICATED is
substantively appropriate.} The paper's \emph{deliverable} is a set of
LQ-fitted parameters, Lea--Catcheside RBE$_{\text{SLD}}$ derivations, and
experimentally-fitted repair-kinetics curves --- reproduced here to
within reported uncertainties on 18 of 21 claims examined. The
Monte-Carlo elements are used inside the paper only as scalar correction
factors, not as its principal result. Downgrading this record to PARTIAL
purely because Fig.~4 was reproduced analytically would misstate the
substance: the paper's own experimental core \emph{is} replicated.
