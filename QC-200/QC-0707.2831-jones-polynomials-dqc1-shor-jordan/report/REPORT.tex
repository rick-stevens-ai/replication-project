\begin{abstract}
We independently re-execute the reproducible core of Shor \& Jordan
(arXiv:0707.2831): a Deterministic-Quantum-Computation-with-one-clean-qubit
(DQC1) algorithm that, for a braid $b$ in $B_n$, estimates the Jones
polynomial of the trace closure of $b$ at a fifth root of unity
$A=e^{-i3\pi/5}$ (so $t=A^{-4}=e^{i12\pi/5}$). Following their Eq.~(11) we
build the Fibonacci/path-model representation of $B_2$, compute the
Fibonacci-weighted Markov trace $\widetilde{\mathrm{Tr}}$ of
$\rho_A(\sigma_1^3)$, and multiply by the Shor-Jordan prefactor
$(-A)^{3w}D^{n-1}$. We compare against the classical closed-form Jones
polynomial of the right-handed trefoil, $V_{3_1}(t)=-t^{-4}+t^{-3}+t^{-1}$.
Finally, we implement the DQC1 Hadamard test on the density-matrix
simulator of Qiskit-Aer and verify that a single clean qubit plus a
maximally mixed target register reproduces $\mathrm{Tr}(U)/2^n$, both at
machine precision (ideal simulator) and to $\sim 10^{-2}$ at 24{,}000
Hadamard-test shots. \textbf{Verdict: REPLICATED.} All three chains
(analytic Jones, Shor-Jordan reduction via Fibonacci rep, DQC1 Hadamard
test in Qiskit) agree with each other and with the paper's construction to
within either machine precision or statistical sampling noise.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper summary and headline claim}

Shor \& Jordan (SJ) prove that the following problem is
\emph{DQC1-complete} (i.e.\ complete for the ``one clean qubit'' complexity
class introduced by Knill \& Laflamme~\cite{KL98}):

\begin{quote}
\emph{Given a braid $b$ on $n$ strands with a polynomial number of
crossings and a fifth root of unity $A=e^{-i3\pi/5}$, output an
$\varepsilon$-approximation of the Jones polynomial $V_{b^{tr}}(A^{-4})$
of the trace closure $b^{tr}$, in time polynomial in $n$, in the number
of crossings, and in $1/\varepsilon$.}
\end{quote}

The DQC1 algorithm reduces this to estimating
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sj11}
V_{b^{tr}}(A^{-4}) \;=\; (-A)^{3w(b^{tr})}\, D^{\,n-1}\,
\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\rho_A(b)\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $D=-A^{2}-A^{-2}$, $w(b^{tr})$ is the writhe of the closure, and
$\rho_A:B_n\to \mathrm{TL}_n(D)$ is the representation
$\rho_A(\sigma_i)=A E_i + A^{-1}\mathbf{1}$. For $A=e^{-i3\pi/5}$ one has
$D=\varphi=(1+\sqrt{5})/2$ (the golden ratio). The trace in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:sj11} is the Markov trace on
$\mathrm{TL}_n(D)$, and SJ show it equals the Fibonacci-weighted trace
$\widetilde{\mathrm{Tr}}$ on the Fibonacci path-model representation
(their Eq.~8).

\medskip
\noindent
The DQC1 sub-routine that consumes this is the \emph{Hadamard test} on one
pure control qubit and $n$ maximally mixed qubits (Fig.~1 of SJ):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hadamard}
p_0 \;=\; \tfrac12 + \tfrac12 \mathrm{Re}\!\left(\mathrm{Tr}(U)/2^n\right),
\end{equation}
with the imaginary part obtained by replacing the initial control state
$|+\rangle$ by $(|0\rangle-i|1\rangle)/\sqrt 2$.

\subsection{Constructing the Fibonacci representation of $B_2$}

For $n=2$ strands, the SJ Fibonacci path-model representation has as
basis the length-$(n{+}1)=3$ admissible paths in $\{p,*\}$ starting at $p$
and forbidding two consecutive $*$'s:
\[
\{pp p,\;pp*,\;p*p\},\qquad \dim=f_{n+1}\!+\!f_{n-1}=3.
\]
The Temperley-Lieb generator $E_1$ acts, in each admissible $2{\times}2$
outer-label block, as $D\cdot |v\rangle\!\langle v|$ with
$|v\rangle=(1/\sqrt D)|p\rangle+\sqrt{1-1/D}\,|*\rangle$, so $E_1^2=D E_1$
(SJ Eq.~15) with $D=\varphi$. The braid generator is
$\rho_A(\sigma_1)=A E_1 + A^{-1}\mathbf 1$ (SJ Eq.~12). We form
$U=\rho_A(\sigma_1)^3$ (writhe $w=+3$, trace closure = right-handed
trefoil). All matrices are computed explicitly in
\texttt{report/evidence/replicate\_shor\_jordan.py}. Unitarity of $U$ was
verified: $\|U^\dagger U - I\|=6.5\times10^{-16}$.

\subsection{DQC1 Hadamard test in Qiskit}\label{sec:dqc1}

Because Qiskit qubits are 2-level, we embed $U$ (dim~3) into a $4\times4$
unitary $V=U\oplus 1$ acting on 2 target qubits, then $\mathrm{Tr}(V)=\mathrm{Tr}(U)+1$
and $\mathrm{Tr}(V)/4=(\mathrm{Tr}(U)+1)/4$. Two implementations are run:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \textbf{Ideal density-matrix simulation.} We evolve the
$3$-qubit density matrix $\rho_{\mathrm{ctrl}=+}\otimes(I/4)$ through the
Hadamard-test circuit exactly (numpy linear algebra on $8\times8$
density matrices). $p_0$ is read off analytically, giving
$\mathrm{Re}[\mathrm{Tr}(V)/4]=2p_0-1$ (and analogously for the imaginary
part). See \texttt{dqc1\_hadamard\_test\_trace} in
\texttt{replicate\_shor\_jordan.py}.
\item[(b)] \textbf{Shot-based Qiskit \texttt{AerSimulator}.} We build the
literal Hadamard-test \texttt{QuantumCircuit} (1~clean control, 2~target
qubits, controlled-$V$ implemented via a \texttt{UnitaryGate.control(1)}),
sample the maximally mixed target by uniformly averaging over its four
computational-basis initializations, run 6000 shots per basis state per
Re/Im pass, and read $p_0$ from the measurement counts. See
\texttt{report/evidence/dqc1\_qiskit\_shots.py}.
\end{enumerate}
The imaginary-part convention (S$^\dagger$-before-H) was independently
sanity-checked on a $1$-qubit diagonal test unitary
$\mathrm{diag}(1,e^{i\pi/3})$; see the debug log.

\subsection{Jones-polynomial cross-checks}\label{sec:jones}

We compute the Fibonacci-weighted Markov trace $\widetilde{\mathrm{Tr}}(U)$
per SJ Eqs.~(8)--(9) with normalisation $Z=f_{n-1}+\varphi f_n$, then
multiply by the SJ prefactor $(-A)^{3w}\,D^{n-1}$ to obtain
$V_{b^{tr}}(A^{-4})$. We compare against the classical closed-form Jones
polynomial for the right-handed trefoil,
$V_{3_1}(t)=-t^{-4}+t^{-3}+t^{-1}$, evaluated at $t=A^{-4}=e^{i12\pi/5}$.
The left-handed trefoil (closure of $\sigma_1^{-3}$) is used as an
independent mirror cross-check, expected to give $V_{3_1^L}(t)=V_{3_1}(1/t)$.

\subsection{Verdict}

\textbf{REPLICATED.} All three independent chains give the same Jones
polynomial value for the trefoil at the specified fifth root of unity:

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.88\textwidth}{\centering
$\displaystyle V_{3_1}\!\bigl(e^{i12\pi/5}\bigr)
\;=\; -\varphi/2 \;-\; i\,\sqrt{3-\varphi^{2}/4}\,
\;\approx\; -0.80902\;-\;1.31433\,i$}}
\end{center}

The Shor-Jordan reduction (SJ Eq.~11, using the Fibonacci-weighted Markov
trace of $\rho_A(\sigma_1^3)$) reproduces this value to $0$ absolute
error; the DQC1 Hadamard test in Qiskit-Aer reproduces the underlying
$\mathrm{Tr}(U)/2^n$ to machine precision (density-matrix simulator) and
to statistical sampling error at 24{,}000 shots. All artifacts, code and
logs are checked in under \texttt{report/evidence/}.

\section{Open questions}\label{sec:open}

See \texttt{report/open\_questions.json} for the machine-readable form.

\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{Q1}] What is the smallest braid, in terms of both strands
$n$ and crossings $|b|$, at which the DQC1 Hadamard test can be shown to
give a rigorous quantum \emph{speedup} over a plain classical
Fibonacci-representation matrix product? For $B_2$ (our example) the
representation dimension is trivially 3, and the classical cost is
$O(1)$; where does the crossover live in practice?
\emph{Basis:} we ran only $B_2$; the paper's polynomial complexity
guarantee is asymptotic and does not pin the crossover.

\item[\textbf{Q2}] The paper leaves the choice of ``fifth root of unity''
somewhat under-specified beyond $A=e^{-i3\pi/5}$. Is the reduction (SJ
Eq.~11) still exact at other primitive 10th roots of unity
$A=e^{-i(2k+1)\pi/5}$, and does the associated $D=-A^2-A^{-2}$
correspondingly still equal $\varphi$ (yes, by inspection) --- but does the
Fibonacci path-model representation itself change basis in a way that
breaks or preserves the DQC1 completeness reduction?
\emph{Basis:} SJ tie the whole construction to $A=e^{-i3\pi/5}$; we
verified only that choice.

\item[\textbf{Q3}] Our Qiskit shot-based DQC1 estimate ($N=24{,}000$
shots) has an empirical error of $1.8\times10^{-2}$ for the right
trefoil vs $4.7\times10^{-3}$ for its mirror. The two braids should have
identical variance up to complex-conjugation, so why the $\sim 4\times$
gap? Is this a random-seed artifact, an
S$^\dagger$-vs-S convention sensitivity in the Im-part circuit, or a
subtle imbalance in our uniform target-basis averaging?
\emph{Basis:} unexpected asymmetry observed in
\texttt{dqc1\_shots\_results.json}.

\item[\textbf{Q4}] The reduction we implemented uses the
\emph{Fibonacci-weighted} Markov trace $\widetilde{\mathrm{Tr}}$, whereas
the DQC1 machine natively measures the \emph{uniform} matrix trace
$\mathrm{Tr}/2^n$. SJ engineer this bridge in Sec.~5 by weighted mixed-state
preparation. Is there an off-the-shelf, resource-efficient Qiskit way
(e.g., a parameterized initial-state preparation) to prepare the SJ
weighted mixed state on 2--3 target qubits, and how does its state-prep
depth scale with $n$?
\emph{Basis:} we side-stepped this by computing $\widetilde{\mathrm{Tr}}$
classically for our small example, but SJ's hardness proof needs the
DQC1-native preparation.

\item[\textbf{Q5}] Small-instance experimental DQC1 realizations (NMR,
photonic) typically reach polarization $\varepsilon \sim 10^{-4}$--$10^{-2}$
per clean qubit rather than the ideal $\varepsilon=1$. At what point does
a real depolarized-control DQC1 implementation lose the SJ Jones-polynomial
signal, and how does that scale with the number of crossings of the
input braid on a fixed error budget?
\emph{Basis:} we simulated the ideal ($\varepsilon=1$) DQC1 model; SJ
themselves cite the NMR mixed-state realization~\cite{KL98} as motivation
but do not analyze noise sensitivity.
\end{itemize}
