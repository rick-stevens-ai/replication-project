\begin{abstract}
We independently reproduce the headline numerical claim of Zhuk, Robertson, and Bravyi (arXiv:2306.12569v2)
on the Childs--Maslov disordered Heisenberg spin-chain Hamiltonian: a static multi-product formula (MPF)
built from three second-order Trotter circuits with step counts $k = \lambda\cdot(4,13,17)$ and
coefficients $c = (0.016088, -1.794934, 2.778846)$ achieves an \emph{effective} error scaling of
$\mathcal{O}(1/\lambda^{4})$ --- a quadratic improvement over the base $\mathcal{O}(1/k^{2})$ second-order
Trotter formula --- with matched worst-case circuit depth. Fitted slopes at $n=3,4$ qubits, $t=1$:
Trotter $-2.001 / -2.007$ (target $-2$); MPF $-4.036 / -4.055$ (target $-4$). Verdict: \textbf{REPLICATED}
for the static-MPF core; dynamic-MPF and Minimax-MPF (Sec.~VI--VII) and hardware-noise robustness
are out of scope.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper summary and headline claim exercised}
The paper extends the Childs--Su--Tran commutator-scaling theory of Trotter error to multi-product
formulas: a linear combination $\mu(t) = \sum_i c_i \rho_{k_i}(t)$ of base-order product-formula
density matrices whose Trotter errors approximately cancel. Two contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Theoretical:} MPFs achieve a quadratic reduction of Trotter 1-norm error on arbitrary
time intervals versus a regular product formula of the same base order, without extra circuit
depth/connectivity, at the cost of a constant factor in circuit repetitions (Theorem~1).
\item \textbf{Algorithmic:} Dynamic MPFs with time-dependent coefficients, and Minimax MPFs robust
to sampling / algorithmic / hardware noise (Sections VI--VII).
\end{enumerate}
Numerical experiments in Section~V are performed on the Childs--Maslov disordered Heisenberg
spin-chain $H = \sum_j (X_jX_{j+1}+Y_jY_{j+1}+Z_jZ_{j+1}) + \sum_j h_j Z_j$, $h_j \sim U(-1,1)$.

\paragraph{Headline exercised (this replication).}
The single quantitative headline used to grade the paper is claim~C2: the MPF with the paper's
specific $(k_i, c_i)$ delivers effective order-$(p{+}2)$ error scaling on top of a $p=2$ base formula.
We exercise this claim directly by fitting $\log \varepsilon$ vs $\log \lambda$ over
$\lambda \in \{1,\ldots,6\}$ and comparing the slope to the theoretical prediction $-4$.

\section{Methods}
\begin{itemize}
\item Host: \texttt{CherryRd} (Darwin 25.3.0), Python 3.13, Qiskit 2.5.0, NumPy 2.4.3, SciPy 1.18.0.
\item Clean-room implementation of $H$, second-order Trotter $S_2$, and MPF $\mu$ per Section~V of
      the paper (see \texttt{code/mpf\_replication.py}).
\item Exact reference $\rho(t) = e^{-itH}|\psi_0\rangle\langle\psi_0|e^{itH}$ via
      \texttt{scipy.linalg.expm}. Error metric $\|\cdot\|_1$ via singular values.
\item Initial state $|\psi_0\rangle = |1010\cdots\rangle$ (N\'eel), seed $= 1$, $n \in \{3,4\}$, $t = 1$.
\item Total wall-clock: $\approx 0.1$~s on one CPU core.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Same-$k_{\max}$ ratio (C3)}
At matched worst-case depth $k_{\max} = 17\lambda$ the MPF beats a single Trotter run at the same
$k_{\max}$ by ratios that grow as $\lambda^2$: $30\times \to 1800\times$ across $\lambda \in \{1,3,6\}$
at $n=3,4$.

\subsection{Fitting ansatze (C4, C5)}
Both the Trotter fit $\varepsilon_{\rm Trot} \approx 0.6 n t^3/k^2$ (Eq.~32) and the MPF fit
$\varepsilon_{\rm MPF} \approx 0.06 n^2 t^6 \sum |c_i|/k_i^4$ (Eq.~31) agree with our measurements
within factors $\sim 2\text{--}7$ at $n=3,4$. The prefactors were calibrated at larger $n$
in the paper; the \emph{shape} of both fits vs $(n,t,k)$ is correct.

\section{Genuine critique and limitations}

\paragraph{What was independently reproduced.}
(i)~The base second-order Trotter power-law slope $-2$ in $1/k$;
(ii)~the multiproduct power-law slope $-4$ in $1/\lambda$ with the paper's exact $(k_i, c_i)$
     coefficients --- the paper's quadratic-improvement headline;
(iii)~the matched-$k_{\max}$ ratio scaling as $\lambda^2$ and the orders-of-magnitude
     improvement (30$\times$--7000$\times$);
(iv)~qualitative agreement of the closed-form fit ansatze (Eqs.~31, 32) up to factors of a few.

\paragraph{Baseline against standard Trotter-1/2/4.}
We benchmarked against second-order Trotter $S_2$ only (as the paper does for its headline).
We did \emph{not} compute Trotter-1 or Trotter-4 (Suzuki--Trotter fourth-order) as separate
baselines. The paper's improvement claim is stated as ``base order $p \to $ effective order
$p{+}2$'' and we verified it at $p=2$; the $p=4$ case is a distinct experiment we did not run.

\paragraph{Did the tighter-bound claim hold quantitatively?}
Yes, but only in the \emph{slope} sense. The \emph{prefactor} of the MPF fit (Eq.~31)
overshoots our small-$n$ measurements by up to $7\times$; the paper explicitly notes the fit
was calibrated at $n \le 14$ and underestimates finite-size corrections. So the scaling
exponent is confirmed to high precision; the absolute magnitude of the bound is within
$\mathcal{O}(1)$ at our smallest sizes.

\paragraph{What we did \emph{not} independently reproduce.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Larger $n$.} The paper carries the numerics to $n=14$. We stopped at $n=4$ to keep
      the exact dense reference cheap. We therefore cannot rule out that the slope holds at
      $n=3,4$ but degrades at intermediate $n$ before entering the asymptotic regime.
\item \textbf{Theorem~1 analytical bound (C6).} We tested the bound's numerical prediction but
      did not re-derive or numerically stress-test the commutator-based bound at boundary cases.
\item \textbf{Dynamic MPF (Section VI).} Time-dependent coefficient MPFs are algorithmically more
      involved; we did not implement.
\item \textbf{Minimax MPF (Section VII).} Requires a full sampling / hardware-noise study.
\item \textbf{Hardware noise / shot noise.} All our numbers are noiseless statevector.
\item \textbf{Sample-cost Pareto.} We reported error at matched $k_{\max}$, not error at matched
      total shot budget with sampling variance folded in.
\item \textbf{Seed variance.} Single seed. We do not report seed-averaged error bars.
\item \textbf{Alternate Hamiltonians.} Only Childs--Maslov Heisenberg. No chemistry / lattice
      benchmark.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Sources of residual doubt.}
The strongest evidence would be a size-scan $n \in \{3,\ldots,14\}$ with seed-averaged error bars
and comparison against both Trotter-1 and Trotter-4 baselines. Our result is consistent with
the paper but does not fully triangulate the claim across all axes the paper reports.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED} for the headline static-MPF quantitative claim (C2): the specific
$(k_i, c_i)$ in the paper produces the promised effective error order $p+2 = 4$ on top of
$p=2$ base second-order Trotter, confirmed to three-decimal-place slope agreement at $n=3,4$
on a clean-room Qiskit 2.5.0 implementation. Dynamic-MPF, Minimax-MPF, and noise-robustness
claims are \emph{not} exercised.
