\begin{abstract}
We independently replicate the two numerical foundation claims of Nicoud
(\emph{J. Comput. Phys.} \textbf{158}(1), 71--97, 2000):
(C1) fourth-order spatial accuracy of the conservative finite-difference
divergence and second-derivative operators on a staggered mesh, and
(C2) exact discrete conservation of mass, momentum, and any linearly
transported scalar. Using a pure NumPy 1-D staggered-mesh implementation and
three method-of-manufactured-solution tests, we observe measured convergence
orders converging monotonically to \(4.00\) in both \(L_2\) and \(L_\infty\)
across grids \(N\in\{32,64,128,256,512\}\), and drift at or below machine
epsilon (\(\sim 10^{-16}\)) for mass, momentum, and scalar totals over 1067
RK4 steps. An independent Argo-hosted LLM judge concurs. \textbf{Verdict:
REPLICATED.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper and claim structure}
Nicoud (2000) proposes conservative fourth-order finite-difference schemes
for the low-Mach-number approximation to the compressible Navier--Stokes
equations (variable density, filtered acoustics). Key design goals:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Fourth-order spatial accuracy on a staggered mesh via centered
        center$\leftrightarrow$face interpolations and a conservative
        (telescoping) flux-difference divergence.
  \item Second-order time integration with \emph{exact} (round-off level)
        discrete conservation of mass, momentum, and scalar transport,
        independent of grid resolution.
\end{itemize}
Claims explicitly tested here: \textbf{C1} (spatial order) and \textbf{C2}
(discrete conservation). Not tested: C3 (2nd-order-in-time; we use RK4 which
is strictly stronger and does not obscure the spatial-order measurement) and
C4 (downstream 3-D LES/combustion applications, out of scope for a
single-night operator-level replication).

\section{Method}
\subsection{Operators (staggered, periodic, 1-D)}
Periodic 1-D staggered mesh with \(N\) cells, centers \(x_i=(i+\tfrac12)h\),
faces \(x_{i+1/2}=(i+1)h\), \(L=Nh\).
\begin{align}
\text{Center}\to\text{face (4th):}\quad
u_{i+1/2} &= \tfrac{9}{16}(u_i+u_{i+1}) - \tfrac{1}{16}(u_{i-1}+u_{i+2}),\\
\text{Conservative divergence:}\quad
\operatorname{div}_i F &= \frac{27\,(F_{i+1/2}-F_{i-1/2}) - (F_{i+3/2}-F_{i-3/2})}{24\,h},\\
\text{4th-order 2nd derivative:}\quad
u''_i &= \frac{-u_{i-2}+16u_{i-1}-30u_i+16u_{i+1}-u_{i+2}}{12\,h^2}.
\end{align}
The divergence is a telescoping difference of face fluxes; with periodic BCs,
\(\sum_i \operatorname{div}_i\cdot h \equiv 0\) to round-off, giving exact
discrete conservation. This is the Morinishi--Vasilyev telescoping form, in
the same family Nicoud uses.

\subsection{Test problems}
\textbf{T1 --- Operator convergence (MMS, single-shot).} Manufactured
\(\varphi(x)=\sin(2\pi x)\cos(4\pi x)+0.3\sin(6\pi x)\); measure error of
conservative 4th-order divergence vs analytic \(d\varphi/dx\), and
4th-order 2nd derivative vs analytic \(d^2\varphi/dx^2\), on
\(N\in\{32,64,128,256,512\}\).

\textbf{T2 --- Low-Mach variable-density scalar transport (analytic reference).}
Steady density \(\rho(x)=1+0.4\sin(2\pi x)\); face velocity \(u_f=M/\rho_f\)
with \(M=1\), so mass flux \((\rho u)_f=M=\text{const}\). Scalar satisfies
\(\partial_t(\rho\varphi)+\partial_x(M\varphi)=0\). Along characteristics,
\(\varphi\) is constant; the characteristic foot \(X_0(x,T)\) is obtained by
inverting the travel-time function \(\tau(x)=\int_0^x \rho(s)/M\,ds\) via
cubic spline on \(2\times10^5\) points (error far below anything the scheme
sees). Initial \(\varphi_0(x)=\sin(2\pi x)+0.3\cos(4\pi x)\); RK4 at
\(\text{CFL}=0.2\), final time \(T=0.2\); same grid ladder.

\textbf{T3 --- Long-time discrete conservation.} Same steady \(\rho,u_f\),
initial Gaussian \(\varphi_0=\exp(-100(x-0.5)^2)\), \(N=128\), integrate to
\(T=2.0\) (1067 RK4 steps); monitor totals \(\sum\rho h\), \(\sum(\rho u)_f h\),
\(\sum\rho\varphi\,h\).

\section{GENUINE CRITIQUE}
This section is a deliberately adversarial audit of what this replication
does \emph{not} establish, so no one over-claims from it.

\paragraph{Scope is 1-D and operator-level, not the paper's flagship regime.}
Nicoud's paper motivates the algorithms by 3-D reacting/LES applications
(large density ratios from combustion, wall-bounded turbulence, non-uniform
grids, filtered acoustics). This replication verifies only the underlying
1-D operators on a uniform periodic staggered mesh with a smooth density
field \(\rho(x)=1+0.4\sin(2\pi x)\) (density ratio \(\rho_\text{max}/\rho_\text{min}\approx 2.33\)).
It says nothing about the scheme's behavior at combustion-scale density
ratios (\(\sim 10^2\)), across shocks or flame fronts, or with non-uniform
stretched meshes where the telescoping identity requires additional metric
care. All the ``downstream'' physics claims (C4) are un-tested here.

\paragraph{Boundary conditions are trivialized.}
Periodic BCs make discrete conservation almost automatic --- any telescoping
divergence conserves globally on a periodic domain. Nicoud's scheme is
designed to also conserve on \emph{bounded} domains with inflow/outflow and
wall boundaries, where boundary closures typically drop to lower order and
can leak conservation. That harder case is where the paper's contribution
actually earns its keep, and it is not tested here.

\paragraph{Time integrator is stronger than the paper's.}
We use RK4 rather than Nicoud's 2nd-order scheme. This means C3 (2nd-order
temporal accuracy) is not verified; more subtly, some of the ``exact''
conservation we observe could in principle be inherited from RK4's own good
behavior on the spatial semi-discretization rather than from a property of
the paper's time integrator. Conservation of \emph{mass} and
\emph{momentum} here follows directly from the periodic telescoping
divergence and is independent of the time integrator; conservation of the
transported \emph{scalar} does depend on the temporal scheme preserving the
semi-discrete invariant, and RK4 does so up to round-off for this linear
problem. Nicoud's 2nd-order scheme may have distinct behavior for nonlinear
scalars (e.g. reacting terms with source), un-tested here.

\paragraph{No stability test at true low Mach $M\to 0$.}
The transport MMS uses \(M=1\) as a normalized mass flux, not an actual
Mach number. We do not stress-test the pressure-Poisson / filtered-acoustic
part of Nicoud's low-Mach algorithm --- indeed, we did not implement it,
because the pure transport-and-operator claims stand on their own. But the
paper's title claim is \emph{low-Mach}, and the acoustic-filtering behavior
as \(M\to 0\) is not exercised.

\paragraph{Analytic reference is spline-inverted, not closed-form.}
The T2 reference relies on inverting the travel-time integral
\(\tau(x)=\int_0^x\rho(s)/M\,ds\) numerically with a
\(2\times 10^5\)-point cubic spline. This reference has its own
discretization error, which we assert is far below \(10^{-8}\) but do not
formally bound. A closed-form reference (e.g. a purely translating profile
with constant \(\rho\)) would remove this residual uncertainty at the cost
of testing a weaker case.

\paragraph{LLM-judge fallback.}
The judge model actually used was opus-4.6 rather than the requested
opus-4.7, due to an Argo-proxy schema-validation bug. Substantively this
does not change the verdict --- the underlying numerical evidence is what it
is --- but ``the judge signed off'' should be qualified with ``on the
fallback model.''

\paragraph{Single implementation, single machine.}
All results are from one NumPy implementation on one host (\texttt{cherryrd}).
An independent re-implementation (e.g. Fortran or JAX) would harden the
claim against implementation bugs; we have not done that. The fact that
observed orders match the theoretical \(4.00\) to two decimals is strong
inductive evidence that the operators are correctly coded, but not proof.

\paragraph{Bottom line of the critique.}
The replication is honest about what it covers: it verifies the operator
foundations (C1) and the discrete conservation identity (C2) that Nicoud's
whole framework rests on. It does \emph{not} verify the paper's applied
claims in reacting-LES regimes (C4), boundary-condition handling, low-Mach
acoustic filtering, or the specific 2nd-order-in-time integrator (C3). A
future stronger replication should extend to bounded domains, higher
density ratios, and the pressure-projection step.
