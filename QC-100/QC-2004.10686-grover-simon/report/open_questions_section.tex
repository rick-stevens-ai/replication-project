\section{Open Questions}

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{Do the paper's extrapolated resource counts for full-scale
SIMON32/64 through SIMON128/256 (Tables~4--5) match a byte-accurate reversible
synthesis?} \\
\emph{Basis:} This replication only exercised the 20-qubit reduced instance
($n{=}3$, $T{=}4$). Independent audits of Grover-oracle resource counts for
other lightweight ciphers (e.g.\ Grassl et al.\ AES) have historically differed
by 10--30\% from initial paper estimates once uncomputation and comparator
chaining are done rigorously. \\
\emph{Next steps:} Rebuild SIMON32/64 reversible oracle end-to-end at full
width; run Qiskit \texttt{count\_ops()} and a T-depth pass; compare gate-by-gate
against Table~4. Free: CPU-only.

\item \textbf{Does Grover-on-SIMON extend cleanly to other lightweight ciphers
(SPECK, PRESENT, GIFT, LED), and what is the T-depth ratio Grover-SIMON :
Grover-SPECK for equal key length?} \\
\emph{Basis:} SIMON's round function is unusually Toffoli-cheap (AND/XOR/rot
only). SPECK adds modular addition (ripple-carry adder); PRESENT is
S-box-heavy. The paper does not compare across ciphers. \\
\emph{Next steps:} Port the reduced-instance oracle template to SPECK32/64 and
PRESENT-80 using published reversible circuits (Jaques et al.\ 2020, Almazrooie
et al.). Reuse this replication's Grover harness. Free: standard Qiskit.

\item \textbf{How robust is the Grover-on-SIMON success probability under
realistic noise, and at what per-gate error rate does the peak-on-$K$ signal
drop below the second-best key by less than 2$\sigma$?} \\
\emph{Basis:} The paper's simulation is noise-free statevector. Our 4-iteration
reduced-SIMON oracle has $\sim$200 Toffolis --- squarely in the regime where
Cai/Xu 2022 saw $>50\%$ Grover amplification loss at $p_{\mathrm{err}} \sim 10^{-3}$
per two-qubit gate. \\
\emph{Next steps:} Rerun \texttt{grover\_simon.py} with Qiskit-Aer noise
models (depolarizing $p \in \{10^{-4}, 10^{-3}, 5{\times}10^{-3}, 10^{-2}\}$
and T1/T2 calibrated to IBM Eagle/IonQ Aria). Identify break-even threshold.
Free.

\item \textbf{What is the concrete resource cost of Grover-on-SIMON128/256
under specific surface-code distances ($d{=}15, 21, 27$), and how does it
compare to NIST PQC security level 5?} \\
\emph{Basis:} The paper leaves surface-code magic-state distillation and
routing overhead as future work. NIST PQC level 5 is anchored to
AES-256-under-Grover ($\sim$$10^{13}$ T-gates, $\sim$7000 logical qubits per
Gidney 2020). \\
\emph{Next steps:} Feed Table~5 T-count/T-depth into Litinski's magic-state
factory calculator or Azure Quantum Resource Estimator; report logical
qubits, physical qubits at $p{=}10^{-3}$, and runtime. Cross-reference
against AES-256. Free web tools.

\item \textbf{Do quantum side-channel / Simon-algorithm-chained attacks on
SIMON offer better-than-quadratic speedup, and can this replication's
reversible oracle be repurposed to test them?} \\
\emph{Basis:} SIMON's linear layer is bit-rotation-only and commutes with
algebraic symmetries that Simon's-algorithm (unrelated to the cipher name)
can exploit for period-finding. Kaplan et al.\ 2016 showed some lightweight
ciphers admit Q2-model attacks with exponential speedup. \\
\emph{Next steps:} Analytically check whether SIMON under a fixed key admits
a nontrivial plaintext-period; if yes, prototype Simon-on-SIMON reusing our
reversible \texttt{encrypt\_inplace}. Compare query complexity to Grover-only.
Free.

\end{enumerate}
