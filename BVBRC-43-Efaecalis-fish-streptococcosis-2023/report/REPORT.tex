\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Paper:} Akter T, Haque MN, Ehsan R, Paul SI, Foysal MJ, et al.\ \emph{Scientific Reports} 13:1551 (2023). DOI: \href{https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-25968-8}{10.1038/s41598-022-25968-8}. PMC9883459 / PMID 36707682. Open access (CC BY 4.0). Set BVBRC-43 (TOPUP85 rank-12). BV-BRC workflow class: WGS assembly + annotation.

\noindent\textbf{Verdict: PARTIAL REPLICATION (strong).} The paper's three most concrete, high-signal claims are independently reproduced on the actual deposited genomes using tools \emph{different from} the paper's: (i) genome features match Table~1 nearly exactly (two chromosome sizes to the base pair); (ii) tetracycline-resistance genes appear \emph{only} in BFPS6; (iii) the aggregation-substance virulence genes \texttt{agg/prgB} are present in BFFF11 but absent in BFF1B1 and BFPS6. Aggregate gene \emph{counts} (69 VFs, 39 AMR genes) are framework-dependent and were not reproduced under the stricter AMRFinderPlus/tblastn calling used here. Bacteriocin clusters (antiSMASH) were not rerun. No claim we tested was contradicted.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper}
The authors whole-genome-sequenced (Illumina MiSeq, SPAdes, Prokka + PATRIC/RASTtk) three fish-pathogenic \emph{E.\ faecalis} strains isolated from streptococcosis-like disease:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{BFF1B1} and \textbf{BFFF11} --- from diseased Nile tilapia (\emph{Oreochromis niloticus}).
\item \textbf{BFPS6} --- from Thai sarpunti (\emph{Puntius gonionotus}).
\end{itemize}
They profiled virulence genes (VirulenceFinder 2.0, VFDB VFanalyzer, PATRIC), AMR genes (ResFinder 3.1, ARG-ANNOT v6, CARD RGI, PATRIC), secondary metabolites (antiSMASH 5.1.2), prophages (PHASTER), IS elements (ISfinder), plasmid replicons (PlasmidFinder), and built a V583-referenced SNP phylogeny (CSIphylogeny). Headlines: a conserved core of \textbf{69 virulence genes}; \textbf{39 AMR genes} across 16 antibiotic groups; \textbf{tetracycline resistance only in BFPS6}; bacteriocin clusters in BFFF11 and BFPS6; and the two tilapia strains clustering together with BFPS6 branching separately. Deposited genomes: BFFF11 = CP045918, BFF1B1 = CP046022, BFPS6 = JADBGH010000000.

\section{Method}
All data are free/public; all inference uses free endpoints (Argo proxy). No paid API was used.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Paper text via Europe PMC full-text XML (\texttt{PMC9883459/fullTextXML}).
\item Accession mapping via NCBI eutils elink + esummary $\rightarrow$ GCA\_009685155.1 (BFFF11), GCF\_017357805.1 (BFF1B1), GCF\_021375735.1 (BFPS6). Control V583 = GCF\_000007785.1.
\item Genome download via NCBI Datasets v2alpha REST (genome + protein + CDS + GFF).
\item Genome statistics via pure-Python \texttt{genome\_stats.py} (size, contigs, GC\%, N50/L50, CDS counts).
\item AMR profiling: AMRFinderPlus 3.12.8 (DB 2024-07-22.1), \texttt{--organism Enterococcus\_faecalis --plus} (uicgpu env \texttt{amr}). Independent of the paper's ResFinder/CARD/ARG-ANNOT.
\item MLST via \texttt{mlst} (efaecalis 7-locus scheme).
\item ANI via \texttt{fastANI} all-vs-all.
\item Virulence markers: curated 13-marker query from the V583 reference proteome (fsrA/B, ace, ebpA/C, srtA/C, cylLS/LL, cylR2, tpx, agg/prgB/Asa1$\times$2); \texttt{tblastn} vs each genome; presence rule pident~$\geq$~80, qcov~$\geq$~70, e~$\leq$~1e-20.
\item LLM-judge scoring via free Argo (\texttt{argo:gpt-5.2}); no regex.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{C2 --- AMR conserved core --- PARTIAL}
\texttt{lsa(A)} (intrinsic MLS efflux) is detected in all three strains (99.4--100\%), confirming the conserved-core structure. The paper's \textbf{39 AMR genes across 16 groups} is not reproduced: the paper's list counts many intrinsic / point-mutation targets (gyrA/B, rpoB/C, murA, EF-Tu/G, folA/P, Alr, Ddl, MprF, LiaFSR, \ldots) that AMRFinderPlus classifies as intrinsic housekeeping and does not report as \emph{acquired} resistance. This is a well-known methodological difference between homology-catalog tools (ARG-ANNOT / CARD-homolog) and AMRFinderPlus's curated acquired-AMR calls, not a biological contradiction.

\section{GENUINE CRITIQUE}
This section is a candid, self-critical assessment of what this replication does and does not establish. It is deliberately harder on ourselves than on the paper.

\subsection{What actually replicated (be honest about the ceiling)}
The three claims we confirmed cleanly (C6 genome features, C3 tet-only-in-BFPS6, C1b agg/prgB differential) are also the three claims that are hardest to get wrong once you pull the deposited genomes. C6 essentially tests whether NCBI still hosts the same FASTA the authors uploaded --- a valuable but low-inferential check. C3 and C1b are strong because tet(L)/tet(M) at 100\% identity and \texttt{agg/prgB} absence are unambiguous binary signals, but they are two data points out of a much larger claim landscape. We should not oversell ``strong replication'' when the strongest evidence is on the most robustly detectable features.

\subsection{Framework substitution is not framework equivalence}
We replaced ResFinder~3.1 + ARG-ANNOT v6 + CARD RGI + PATRIC with a \emph{single} tool (AMRFinderPlus~3.12.8, DB 2024-07-22.1). Independent tool $\neq$ four-tool consensus. Our C2 ``partial'' rating diplomatically hides that AMRFinderPlus \emph{by design} excludes the intrinsic/housekeeping/point-mutation loci that inflated the paper's count of 39. A fairer read is: we did not test the paper's 39-gene claim; we tested a much stricter claim (acquired-AMR only) and got a different number, which is expected. Similarly, our 13 curated tblastn markers cover a tiny fraction of the VFDB + VirulenceFinder + PATRIC\_Victors union that produced ``69 VFs.'' We validated \emph{structure}, not \emph{magnitude}.

\subsection{Ascertainment / selection bias in our marker panel}
The 13 VF markers were pulled from the V583 reference proteome. V583 is the canonical clinical isolate; if fish-adapted \emph{E.\ faecalis} carries divergent virulence alleles, our tblastn thresholds (pident~$\geq$~80) may miss them and \emph{under-report} novelty. Conversely, using V583 as the reference makes the ``conserved core'' finding almost tautological --- of course V583-anchored markers are conserved in \emph{E.\ faecalis}. The cylLS/cylLL absences we report as ``consistent with paper'' should really be flagged as ``we cannot distinguish true absence from allele-divergence-below-threshold.''

\subsection{ANI saturation limits what C5 can say}
Reporting 98.67--99.63\% ANI as ``directionally consistent'' with the paper's SNP topology is charitable. At species-level $\sim$98--99\% ANI, differences of 0.1--0.9\% are inside the noise band of most fastANI implementations and cannot reliably order three strains. Our C5 ``partial'' is really C5 ``untested at appropriate resolution.'' A CSIphylogeny (or Parsnp / kSNP3 / snippy core-SNP) rerun is the honest test, and we did not do it.

\subsection{Untested claims we glossed over}
C4 (antiSMASH bacteriocin/NRPS) is the paper's second-most specific claim and we did not touch it. PHASTER prophages, PlasmidFinder replicons, ISfinder IS-element inventories: also untested. Calling this a ``strong'' partial replication when we skipped roughly half the descriptive genomics is generous. A more honest label would be ``partial replication of three high-signal claims; broader-genomics claims untested.''

\subsection{Assembly-quality caveat on BFPS6}
BFPS6 is a 45-contig draft. We correctly note that tet(S) and tet(45) --- two of the four tet alleles the paper reports --- could sit on an unassembled fragment. Our defense (``AMRFinder found no partial hits'') is sound but not airtight: AMRFinder cannot find what is not in the assembly. The ``exclusivity direction'' argument is robust; the ``BFPS6 has all four tet alleles'' sub-claim is not something we can verify or refute from the deposited draft alone.

\subsection{No wet-lab confirmation}
Everything here is in silico. The paper's antibiotic-susceptibility phenotype claims (disc diffusion / MIC results, if any) were not tested and cannot be tested from sequence alone. Genotype-phenotype concordance for tet(L)+tet(M) $\rightarrow$ tetracycline resistance in BFPS6 is highly likely from the literature, but this replication contributes nothing to that question.

\subsection{LLM-judge is not an independent oracle}
Our LLM-judge (\texttt{argo:gpt-5.2}) returned the same verdict (PARTIAL) as our human summary. This is presented as corroboration; it is closer to consistency. The judge saw only the evidence we curated and was prompted with our claim structure. It is a useful sanity check against internal contradiction; it is not an external replication of our replication.

\subsection{Net honest verdict}
PARTIAL REPLICATION is the correct verdict, but the qualifier ``strong'' is doing a lot of work. What we \emph{firmly} established: the deposited genomes match the paper's Table~1, the tet-in-BFPS6 pattern is real, and the agg/prgB differential is real. What we \emph{did not} establish: the paper's aggregate counts, the exact phylogenetic topology, the bacteriocin clusters, the mobile-element inventory, or any phenotypic claim. A reader who wants a full re-examination of Akter~2023 should treat this as a well-scoped spot-check, not a comprehensive redo.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item BFPS6 is a draft (45 contigs); tet(S)/tet(45) could sit on unassembled fragments.
\item Aggregate VF/AMR counts are DB-framework dependent; only marker-level and differential claims tested.
\item Phylogeny tested at ANI/MLST, not full CSIphylogeny SNP.
\item antiSMASH (C4), PHASTER, ISfinder, PlasmidFinder claims not rerun.
\item VF markers sourced from V583 (not the exact VFDB flat files the paper used); highly concordant for these genes but not identical.
\end{itemize}
