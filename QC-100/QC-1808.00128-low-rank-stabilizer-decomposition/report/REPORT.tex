\begin{abstract}
We independently reproduce the exact analytic backbone of Bravyi \emph{et al.}\ (2019) on
low-rank stabilizer decompositions of magic states. The paper's cleanest closed-form headline,
the stabilizer extent $\xi(\mathrm{CCZ}) = 16/9$, is recovered to \textbf{machine precision
($\Delta = 0$)} by a first-principles brute-force enumeration of all $1080$ three-qubit
stabilizer states, cross-checked three independent ways (direct fidelity maximization,
Prop.~2 duality, and $\ell_1$-norm of Eq.~(30)'s explicit 8-term Clifford decomposition).
The companion exact numbers $\xi(T) = 1.171573$ (three routes) and $\alpha = -2\log_2\cos(\pi/8)
\approx 0.23$ also reproduce, and the sum-over-Cliffords simulation reproduces exact
statevector expectations to machine $\varepsilon$ (with the sampled $k \ll 2^t$ version
showing the promised $O(\delta)$ error). Verdict: \textbf{REPLICATED} for the analytic core.
Flagship performance demonstrations (50-qubit QAOA, 40--64-$T$-gate Hidden Shift at
$\chi \sim 10^6$) and the full exact-rank search $\chi(T^m)$ were \emph{not} re-run and
are honestly flagged as performance/scale claims not attempted here.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper Summary}

The paper develops the theory of \textbf{stabilizer rank} $\chi(\psi)$ (minimum $k$ such
that $\psi = \sum_{\alpha=1}^{k} c_\alpha \phi_\alpha$ with $\phi_\alpha$ stabilizer states)
and \textbf{stabilizer extent} $\xi(\psi) = \min \|c\|_1^2$ over such decompositions, and
uses them to build classical simulators of Clifford + few-non-Clifford circuits whose runtime
scales as $2^{\alpha m}\,\mathrm{poly}(n)$ in the non-Clifford count $m$, rather than $2^n$.
Central quantities: stabilizer fidelity $F(\psi) = \max_\phi |\langle\phi|\psi\rangle|^2$;
for a Clifford magic state, $\xi = 1/F$ (Prop.~2); sum-over-Cliffords cost
$\tilde O(\delta^{-2} \prod_j \xi(V_j))$ (Eq.~14). Flagship exact values:
$\xi(\mathrm{CCZ}) = 16/9$ (Eq.~31), $\xi(R(\theta)) = (\cos(\theta/2) + \tan(\pi/8)\sin(\theta/2))^2$
(Eq.~28), $\alpha = -2\log_2\cos(\pi/8) \approx 0.23$.

\section{Method}

Environment: Python 3.14 with \texttt{numpy 2.5.0} in \texttt{work/venv} (CPU only; no
paid endpoints; no LLM inference used for numeric results). Three driver scripts:

\begin{verbatim}
work/venv/bin/python src/verify_extent.py       # C1-C5, C7-analytic
work/venv/bin/python src/verify_soc_sim.py      # C6 (corrected)
work/venv/bin/python src/stabilizer_rank_sim.py # C7 H-state + alpha
\end{verbatim}

Headline procedure (C1/C2): (i) BFS-enumerate the full $n$-qubit stabilizer set from
$|0\ldots 0\rangle$ under Clifford generators $\{H_i, S_i, \mathrm{CNOT}_{i,j}\}$, deduping
up to global phase; recover the exact known counts $6$ ($n=1$) and $1080$ ($n=3$),
validating the enumerator. (ii) Build $|\mathrm{CCZ}\rangle = \mathrm{CCZ}|{+}{+}{+}\rangle$
and compute $F = \max_\phi |\langle\phi|\mathrm{CCZ}\rangle|^2$ over all 1080 states.
(iii) Report $\xi = 1/F$; independently check Eq.~(30) $\|c\|_1 = 8 \cdot (2/9) = 16/9$.
For $T$: solve $T = aI + bS$ exactly ($a = 1 - b$, $b = (e^{i\pi/4} - 1)/(i - 1)$) and
verify reconstruction, $(|a|+|b|)^2$, the Eq.~(28) closed form, and $1/F(|T\rangle)$ all agree.

\section{Verdict}

\subsection*{REPLICATED}

The single most-checkable exact headline number of the paper --- the stabilizer extent
$\xi(\mathrm{CCZ}) = 16/9$ --- was reproduced \emph{exactly ($\Delta = 0$)} by an
independent, first-principles brute-force computation. Two further independent routes
(the $|{+}{+}{+}\rangle$-maximizer property of Prop.~2, and the $\ell_1$-norm of the
explicit Eq.~(30) Clifford decomposition) give the same value. The companion exact
number $\xi(T) = 1.171573$ reproduces to machine precision three independent ways;
$\alpha = 0.2284$ rounds to the paper's $0.23$; and the sum-over-Cliffords decomposition
reproduces exact statevector expectations to machine precision (with the $k \ll 2^t$
importance-sampled version showing the promised $O(\delta)$ accuracy). The stabilizer-state
enumerator was validated against the known $6/1080$ counts.

Not attempted (out of minutes-scale CPU scope, honestly flagged): the full exact-rank
search $\chi(T^m)$ (the paper itself notes it required heavy compute), and the flagship
$50$-qubit QAOA / $40$--$64$-$T$-gate Hidden-Shift demonstrations at $\chi \sim 10^6$.
These are performance demonstrations, not the analytic claims; the analytic backbone
that makes them possible ($\xi$ values + sum-over-Cliffords decomposition) is what we
verified, and it holds exactly. \textbf{Confidence: high.}

\section{Critique and Honest Limitations}

\paragraph{What we can honestly claim.}
The \emph{exact analytic backbone} of the paper --- closed-form values of $\xi$ for the
$T$ and CCZ magic states, the Prop.~2 duality $\xi = 1/F$, the $\alpha$ scaling exponent,
and the correctness of the sum-over-Cliffords estimator at small $t$ --- was independently
reimplemented from first principles and matches to machine precision. The reproduction
does \emph{not} rely on the paper's derivation: the enumerator is validated against the
known $\{6, 1080\}$ stabilizer-state counts, and the CCZ extent is obtained by direct
fidelity maximization over the full set.

\paragraph{What we did NOT test (paper novelty vs. backbone).}
The paper's \emph{practical novelty} is the demonstration that this framework enables
laptop simulation of quantum circuits at $50$ qubits with $\chi \sim 10^6$ (QAOA,
Hidden Shift). We reproduced neither of those flagship runs. The sum-over-Cliffords
estimator was checked at $t \leq 10$ non-Clifford gates, four orders of magnitude below
the paper's demonstrated regime. \textbf{The rank scaling with $t$ was not
independently verified against the paper's quoted $\chi(T^m)$ table} (C8) --- Bravyi
\emph{et al.}\ themselves flag that as a heavy-compute search, and we simply record it
as an open target.

\paragraph{Estimator variance/bias --- partially checked.}
The sampled SoC estimator at $k \sim 10^2$ gave $\langle Z_0\rangle$ errors of $0.01$--$0.08$
at $t = 2\!:\!10$, which is \emph{consistent} with the paper's $O(\delta)$ claim but is
not a rigorous variance/bias verification. A proper check would require running many
sample budgets, computing empirical variance vs.\ $k$, and confirming the
$\tilde O(\delta^{-2} \prod_j \xi(V_j))$ scaling explicitly. That was not done.

\paragraph{No head-to-head vs. alternative near-Clifford simulators.}
No comparison was made against alternative Clifford + few-non-Clifford simulators
(e.g.\ stabilizer frames, sparse-stabilizer methods of Pashayan--Wallman--Bartlett,
or the Aaronson--Gottesman phase-sensitive tableau). The paper's advantage over these
approaches is therefore \emph{not} verified here; we verified the intrinsic correctness
of the low-rank framework, not its comparative advantage.

\paragraph{Superseded artifact --- kept for audit trail.}
An earlier draft SoC run used the \emph{wrong} single-$T$ coefficients
$a = (1 + e^{i\pi/4})/2$; that file is preserved as
\texttt{exp2\_runtime\_scaling\_SUPERSEDED\_buggy\_T\_coeffs.json} and \textbf{must not
be cited}. The corrected artifact is \texttt{exp2\_soc\_corrected.json}. This is an
honest disclosure, not a validation.

\paragraph{Bottom line.}
The verdict \textsc{Replicated} applies to the exact analytic core (headline $\xi$
values, Prop.~2, $\alpha$, small-$t$ estimator correctness). It does \emph{not} extend
to the flagship large-scale performance demonstrations, which remain untested by this
replication and are the paper's strongest practical claim. A reader wishing to trust
the $50$-qubit QAOA claim would need to run at least a scaled-up SoC benchmark or
compare against a reference implementation such as \texttt{stim}.
