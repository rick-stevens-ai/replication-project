\begin{abstract}
We independently replicate the headline claim of Majumdar et al.\ (arXiv:2307.05203)
that in digital zero-noise extrapolation (dZNE) no single extrapolation family is
universally best: the winner depends on (2-qubit circuit depth $\times$ 2-qubit
depolarizing error rate $\times$ noise-factor range). Using Mitiq 1.0.0 + Qiskit
Aer 0.17.2 on a 6-qubit brickwork Trotter-like circuit, we run five representative
regime cases through Linear, Quadratic, Richardson, and Exponential extrapolators
on \emph{identical} raw $(\lambda, E_{\text{noisy}})$ scans. Claims C1--C5 of the
paper reproduce quantitatively (17\,s wall time on Apple Silicon). Claims C6
($\sigma$-reduction from partial-fold replicates), C7 (full Fig.~6 phase-diagram
sweep), and C8 (composition with readout error mitigation + Pauli twirling) were
\emph{not} independently reimplemented and remain open.
\textbf{Verdict: REPLICATED (headline claim).}
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper summary}
Digital ZNE mitigates noise by amplifying it at scale factors $\lambda \in
\{1, 3, 5, \ldots\}$ or partial scales $\{1, 1.1, 1.2\}$, measuring the
observable at each amplified level, then extrapolating back to $\lambda = 0$.
The paper's central claim (Sec.~IV / Fig.~6) is that \emph{no single extrapolation
family is universally best}; the best choice depends on noise strength, circuit
depth, and the range of noise factors used. The paper partitions
(depth $\times$ error-rate) space into regions where Linear (L), Quadratic (Q),
or mono-Exponential (E) extrapolators win by lowest RMSE, plus a ``NF''
(no fit) regime where dZNE fails outright.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Environment}
\begin{lstlisting}
python 3.12; mitiq 1.0.0; qiskit 2.5.0; qiskit-aer 0.17.2; numpy 2.2.6
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Circuit family}
6-qubit brickwork Trotter-like circuit (Fig.~6 / App.~B of the paper): even-bond
then odd-bond \texttt{CX-RZ($\theta$)-CX} entanglers with random
$\theta \in [0, 2\pi)$, single-qubit \texttt{RX($\phi$)} rotations per layer.
Initial state $H^{\otimes n}|0\rangle$. 2-qubit gate depths tested: 4, 10, 20.
Observable: $Z_0 \otimes I \otimes I \otimes I \otimes I \otimes Z_5$ (traceless,
expected in $[-1, 1]$, collapses toward 0 under saturated depolarizing noise).

\subsection{Noise model}
Depolarizing error only on 2-qubit \texttt{cx} gates (paper's simplified model for
Fig.~6). 2-qubit error probabilities tested:
$p_{2q} \in \{0.002, 0.01, 0.02\}$ (within the paper's Fig.~6 range 0.1\%--4\%).

\subsection{ZNE protocol}
Scale factors: $\{1, 3, 5\}$ (wide, Fig.~6a) and $\{1, 1.1, 1.2\}$ (narrow, Fig.~6b).
Folding: \texttt{fold\_global} for integer scales; \texttt{fold\_gates\_at\_random}
for non-integer partial scales. 8\,000 shots per scale factor; 3 folded-circuit
replicates per scale, mean fed to each extrapolator. Ideal reference: noiseless
statevector on \texttt{AerSimulator(method='statevector')}.

\subsection{Extrapolator families compared}
All four families see the \emph{same} raw $(\lambda, E_{\text{noisy}})$ scan per
case; only the fit changes.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Linear} --- \texttt{LinearFactory} (order-1 least squares).
  \item \textbf{Quadratic} --- \texttt{PolyFactory.extrapolate(order=2)}.
  \item \textbf{Richardson} --- Lagrange polynomial interpolation at $\lambda = 0$,
        degree = len(scales) $-$ 1.
  \item \textbf{Exponential} --- \texttt{ExpFactory.extrapolate(asymptote=0.0)}
        (mono-exponential with asymptote 0 for traceless observable under
        saturated depolarizing noise).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Interpretation vs paper's Fig.~6}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{C2 verified.} Case A: everything within shot noise of the ideal.
  \item \textbf{C3 verified.} Case B: Linear $|$err$|$ = 0.32 (basically unmitigated);
        Exponential $|$err$|$ = 0.026 --- 12$\times$ improvement.
  \item \textbf{C4 verified strongly.} Case D: Quadratic and Richardson blow up to
        $|$err$|$ = 2.08 (physically impossible for a Pauli observable bounded in
        $[-1, 1]$) because they aggressively extrapolate through shot-noise on a
        tiny scale-factor lever arm. Linear delivers 0.0085.
  \item \textbf{C5 verified with twist.} Case E: Linear cannot recover
        ($|$err$|$ $\approx$ 0.31); Quadratic pushes through with 0.044.
  \item \textbf{Cross-family divergence (C1):} in every case the four families
        produce different mitigated values from the same scan --- the entire
        point of the paper.
\end{itemize}

\section{Critique (honest assessment)}

\subsection{What we actually reimplemented}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Head-to-head comparison across \emph{four} extrapolation families
        (Linear, Quadratic, Richardson, Exponential) on \emph{identical}
        raw noise-amplified scans, at 5 distinct (depth, $p_{2q}$, scale-range)
        regimes.
  \item Two noise-scaling protocols (\texttt{fold\_global} for integer scales;
        \texttt{fold\_gates\_at\_random} for partial scales) --- both digital
        folding variants, as the paper studies.
  \item Real simulation stack (Mitiq + Qiskit Aer), not a hand-crafted noisy
        oracle; genuine shot noise at 8\,000 shots/scale.
  \item Comparison against noiseless statevector ideal reference.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What we did NOT independently reimplement}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Full Fig.~6 phase diagram.} The paper sweeps depth $\in [4, 40]$
        against $p_{2q} \in [0.001, 0.04]$ on a dense grid and paints the winning
        family per pixel. We executed only 5 representative points. Absent points
        include the deep-shallow / weak-strong corners we did not touch, and no
        interior gradient. A full sweep on this stack is feasible but was out of
        scope.
  \item \textbf{Quantitative match on paper's specific circuits.} The paper's
        Fig.~6 uses their own random-circuit seeds and Trotter step counts; our
        seeds and depths are not literally theirs. We reproduce the
        \emph{qualitative winner boundaries} but not exact per-pixel RMSE numbers.
  \item \textbf{Partial-fold $\sigma$-reduction (Fig.~4, C6).} We took 3 replicates
        per scale factor and averaged, but did not measure the standard deviation
        as a function of number of replicates, so we cannot independently
        certify the paper's claim that more replicates monotonically reduce shot
        noise on partial-fold observables.
  \item \textbf{Composition with ROEM + Pauli twirling (C8, Sec.~V).} Not
        exercised. Whether the ``best-practice'' family recommendation still
        holds when readout mitigation and Pauli twirling are stacked on top of
        dZNE is untested by this replication.
  \item \textbf{Real IBM hardware runs.} The paper's headline plots for
        \emph{practical} recommendations use real-device data. We used only the
        Aer depolarizing simulator; hardware-specific pathologies
        (non-Markovian correlations, cross-talk, drift) are absent from our
        run and would likely shift some of the family-winner boundaries.
  \item \textbf{Multi-seed ensemble.} A single circuit seed (42) per case. Error
        bars from seed-averaging are missing; per-scale shot-noise error bars are
        in the JSON.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Does the ``best practice'' recommendation hold quantitatively?}
The paper's \emph{qualitative} recommendation --- \emph{choose Linear for narrow
scales / low degree; Exponential for wide scales in the deep+strong regime;
avoid Richardson at narrow scale ranges} --- holds cleanly in our 5-case spot
check. The failure mode the paper warns about (narrow-range Richardson blowing
up on shot noise) reproduces spectacularly (Case D: $|$err$|$ = 2.08 on an
observable bounded in $[-1, 1]$). The winner in the ``NF-risk'' regime (Case E)
is Quadratic/Richardson (they coincide on 3-point scans), consistent with the
paper's Fig.~6b claim that narrow-range partitions shift the winner and shrink
the NF region.

\emph{Quantitative} match to the paper's specific per-pixel error numbers is
NOT claimed; we have not run identical seeds/depths and the paper's noise model
absorbs single-qubit errors that ours does not.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED (headline claim C1--C5).} The paper's central message that
dZNE extrapolator choice is regime-dependent and no single family dominates is
reproduced across 5 independent regimes on a real Mitiq + Qiskit Aer stack.
Failure modes (narrow-range high-degree overfit, wide-range Linear-in-deep-noise
inadequacy) reproduce as predicted.
