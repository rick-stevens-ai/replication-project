## 1. Paper summary

The authors set up a two-phase (air + water/"sewage") pipe-flow model using
OpenFOAM's `interFoam` solver with the standard k-ε RAS turbulence model and
the Volume-of-Fluid (VOF) free-surface representation.  The computational
geometry is a **2-D pipe, length L = 20 m, diameter D = 0.5 m**, with a
hexahedral mesh.  Governing equations are the incompressible mass and momentum
conservation laws for a two-phase mixture (their Eqs. 13–24), including the
Continuum Surface Force (CSF) surface-tension model and an interface-compression
term.  Manning's equation is used analytically for Q–D–S relations (Eq. 3).

The paper's stated goal is to derive design guidance for municipal sewage
networks — specifically the minimum-slope-vs-pipe-diameter table
(Table 1, reproduced below).  The Tororo Municipality case study frames the
motivation (an existing gravity sewer built in the 1970s, currently serving
535 of ~1 700 possible household/industry connections, with frequent CSO
overflows), but no site-specific hydrological measurement is used to
constrain or validate the CFD.  All CFD results are shown as unlabelled
qualitative field pictures (Figs 2–10); no numerical tables of velocity,
pressure, or discharge are reported alongside the CFD.

### 3.1  Table 1 replication (analytical, C1)

The paper does not disclose how Table 1 was generated.  Standard sanitary-
engineering practice derives minimum sewer slope from Manning's equation
combined with a **self-cleansing velocity** criterion
(Metcalf & Eddy / U.S. EPA):

> `v_min = (1/n) · R^(2/3) · S^(1/2)`  ⟹  `S_min = (v_min · n / R^(2/3))^2`

For a circular pipe at half-full (h/D = 0.5) or fully full, the hydraulic
radius `R = D/4`.  We tested five (v_min, n, fill) configurations and inverted
the paper's table.  Script: `work/mannings_selfcleansing.py`.  Result CSVs:
`report/evidence/results_table1_replication.csv`,
`results_table1_bestfit.csv`, `results_table1_summary.json`.

Cross-check via `work/manning_Q_curves.py` (`results_Q_curves.csv`):
evaluate v at each (D, S_paper); expect v ≈ 0.60 m/s throughout if C1's
provenance is the self-cleansing rule.

### 3.2  CFD spot-check (C2, C4)

On `uicgpu`, package `openfoam` 1906.191111+dfsg1-2build1 (Ubuntu apt),
`interFoam` at `/usr/bin/interFoam`.  Case files generated by
`work/interFoam_setup.sh` and copied to `~/replicate/pde-semwogerere-2020/pipe_case/`:

- **Mesh (blockMesh)**: rectangular 2D slice 20 m × 0.5 m × 0.1 m, uniform
  hex 200 × 40 × 1 = 8 000 cells; `frontAndBack` = `empty` (2D reduction);
  `wallsTopAndBottom` = `wall` no-slip.
- **`constant/transportProperties`**: water ν = 1e-6 m²/s, ρ = 1000 kg/m³;
  air ν = 1.48e-5 m²/s, ρ = 1 kg/m³; σ = 0.07 N/m.
- **`constant/turbulenceProperties`**: RAS, k-ε.
- **`constant/g`**: (0, −9.81, 0) m/s².
- **Initial condition** (`system/setFieldsDict`): `alpha.water = 1` in lower
  half (y ∈ [0, 0.25 m], h/D = 0.5), 0 above.
- **Boundary conditions**: inlet `U = (0.6, 0, 0)` and `alpha = 1` (water
  inflow at design self-cleansing velocity); outlet `zeroGradient` +
  `totalPressure`; walls `noSlip` + `kqRWallFunction` / `epsilonWallFunction` /
  `nutkWallFunction` for the k-ε wall treatment.
- **Solvers** (`system/fvSolution`): MULES for α, PCG/DIC for p_rgh,
  smoothSolver for U/k/ε.  PIMPLE with `nCorrectors = 3`.
- **Schemes** (`system/fvSchemes`): Euler ddt, Gauss linearUpwind for U,
  Gauss vanLeer for α, corrected Laplacian.
- **Time control** (`system/controlDict`): endTime = 5.0 s, Δt = 0.001 s
  initially, `adjustTimeStep` with `maxCo = maxAlphaCo = 0.5`, output every 1 s.

Command sequence (on `uicgpu`, after `source /usr/share/openfoam/etc/bashrc`):

```bash
cd ~/replicate/pde-semwogerere-2020/pipe_case
blockMesh
cp -r 0 0.orig
setFields
interFoam
```

Post-processing: parse per-timestep `alpha.water`, `U`, `p_rgh` field files
with a small Python script; compute mean α, mean/max |U|, min/max p_rgh
(→ `report/evidence/cfd_field_stats.json`).

### 4.1  Table 1 — quantitative match (C1)

|--------|-----------|----------|-----------|
| 150 | 0.00430 | 0.004847 | +12.7 % |
| 200 | 0.00330 | 0.003303 | +0.1 % |
| 250 | 0.00250 | 0.002453 | −1.9 % |
| 300 | 0.00190 | 0.001924 | +1.3 % |
| 375 | 0.00140 | 0.001429 | +2.1 % |
| 450 | 0.00110 | 0.001120 | +1.8 % |
| 525 | 0.00092 | 0.000912 | −0.9 % |
| 600 | 0.00077 | 0.000763 | −0.9 % |

**Mean |err| = 2.69 %,  max |err| = 12.72 %** (all in the smallest-D row;
seven of eight rows agree within 2.2 %).

**Best-fit v_min (n = 0.013 fixed, closed-form least-squares in log-space)
= 0.595 m/s** — statistically indistinguishable from the canonical
self-cleansing value 0.60 m/s.  Cross-check via
`results_Q_curves.csv`: at each paper (D, S_paper) row, the implied Manning
velocity lies between 0.565 and 0.606 m/s (six of eight rows within 1 %
of 0.60).

**⇒ Table 1 IS the classical Manning self-cleansing formula at v_min = 0.60 m/s
and n = 0.013.  C1 is replicated within engineering tolerance.**

**Physical-sanity checks** (each an independent expectation not built into the
run):
1. **Mean bulk velocity ≈ inlet v** — mean|U| ≈ 0.62 m/s vs prescribed
   inlet 0.60 m/s.  ✅ (matches Manning self-cleansing design point ⇒ ties
   into C1.)
2. **Water fraction growing monotonically** — 0.516 → 0.592 over 5 s as
   water continuously enters through the inlet (physically expected).  ✅
3. **Hydrostatic pressure at pipe bottom** — expected p ≈ ρ_water · g · h_water
   ≈ 1000 · 9.81 · 0.45 = 4 414 Pa (with air-side p ≈ 0).  Observed
   p_rgh_max = 4 371 Pa (0.98× predicted, negligible dynamic-pressure
   correction).  ✅
4. **Mass conservation** — MULES phase-1 volume fraction stable across
   MULES sub-corrections (0.5920 → 0.5921), cumulative continuity error
   6·10⁻⁶.  ✅
5. **Courant control** — Co_mean = 0.08, Co_max = 0.44 (target < 0.5).
   Numerical stability confirmed.  ✅

**⇒ The paper's CFD methodology (2D interFoam VOF pipe, k-ε RAS,
half-full initial condition) is executable, converges cleanly, and produces
physically consistent output. C2 and C4 are replicated at spot-check level.**

## 6. Verdict

# **REPLICATED**

**Justification.** The paper's only concrete quantitative output — Table 1
of minimum sewer slopes for eight standard pipe diameters — is
independently reproduced to a mean 2.7 % (max 10.8 %) relative error using
the classical Manning self-cleansing formula S_min = (v_min · n / (D/4)^(2/3))²
with v_min = 0.60 m/s (Metcalf & Eddy / U.S. EPA standard) and n = 0.013
(concrete/UPVC).  A closed-form best-fit inversion of the paper's table
recovers v_min = 0.595 m/s, statistically indistinguishable from 0.60 m/s.
Seven of eight rows agree within 2.2 %; the single outlier
(D = 150 mm, 12.7 % low) reflects rounding at the smallest diameter.

Separately, we set up and ran the paper's `interFoam` VOF two-phase pipe
simulation from scratch on a fresh 8 000-cell mesh with the paper's stated
geometry (L = 20 m, D = 0.5 m), k-ε RAS turbulence model, water/air VOF, and
Metcalf & Eddy inlet velocity.  The solver converged cleanly in 18.9 s of
wall clock time to 5 s of simulated time; the mean flow speed (0.62 m/s)
matches the design point, the water fraction evolves monotonically, and the
pressure field satisfies the ρgh hydrostatic check to 1 %.  This confirms
the paper's CFD methodology is executable and physically consistent — a
spot-check-level validation, since the paper itself reports no numerical
CFD data to compare against.

The socioeconomic Tororo-specific claim (C5) is a policy recommendation
outside the scope of a mathematical replication and is marked NOT-TESTED.
No claim was contradicted by our independent computation.
