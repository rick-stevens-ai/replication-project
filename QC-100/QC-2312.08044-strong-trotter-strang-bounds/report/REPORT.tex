\begin{abstract}
We independently reproduce the standard-regime headline scaling predictions of
Burgarth \emph{et al.}, arXiv:2312.08044v2, using two independent quantum-chemistry-relevant
Hamiltonians: a 4-site transverse-field Ising model (TFIM) and a Hubbard dimer at half-filling
(canonical minimal-basis H$_2$ toy model, constructed via Jordan--Wigner). For fixed evolution
time $t=1.0$ we sweep the Trotter-step count $r \in \{2,4,\ldots,256\}$ and measure both
operator-norm and physical-state errors of the 1st-order Trotter and 2nd-order Strang
splittings against the exact matrix exponential.
All eight fitted log-log slopes lie within $\sim 2\%$ of the theoretical
predictions $-1$ (Trotter) and $-2$ (Strang), reproducing the paper's central
bounded-Hamiltonian scaling machinery (Claims C1, C2). We did \emph{not} attempt the
paper's novel unbounded-Hamiltonian / fat-tailed-state result (hydrogen ground state
$N^{-1/4}$ scaling, Claim C4), which requires a real-space Coulomb simulation beyond
the scope of a small-instance CPU reproduction. Verdict: \textbf{REPLICATED} on the
headline-exercised claims.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper summary}
The paper develops \emph{state-dependent} (strong) error bounds for the $p$-th-order Trotter
product formula applied to Hamiltonians $H = A + B$, replacing standard operator-norm
bounds with tighter estimates that depend on how the input state $|\psi\rangle$ couples to
higher moments of $A$ and $B$. Two headline consequences:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Standard (non-pathological) regime.} The bounds recover the classical scaling:
  \begin{align*}
    \| e^{-iHt} - (e^{-iAt/r}e^{-iBt/r})^r \| &= \mathcal{O}(t^2/r), \\
    \| e^{-iHt} - (e^{-iAt/2r}e^{-iBt/r}e^{-iAt/2r})^r \| &= \mathcal{O}(t^3/r^2).
  \end{align*}
  \item \textbf{Novel pathological regime.} For fat-tailed states of unbounded
  Hamiltonians (illustrated on the hydrogen atom), the higher-order hierarchy breaks:
  the ground-state Trotter error scales as $N^{-1/4}$ and 2nd-order Strang provides
  no scaling advantage.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Method}
\subsection{Environment} macOS, Python 3.14.6, NumPy 2.4.3, SciPy 1.18.0. LLM-judge run against
local Argo proxy \texttt{http://localhost:44497/v1} (free endpoints only). Runtime $\sim 1$ s CPU.

\subsection{Model Hamiltonians}
\textbf{Test A -- 4-site TFIM:} $H = -J\sum_i Z_iZ_{i+1} - h\sum_i X_i$, $J=1.0$, $h=0.7$. \\
\textbf{Test B -- Hubbard dimer:} $H = -t\sum_\sigma (c^\dagger_{0\sigma}c_{1\sigma} + \mathrm{h.c.}) + U\sum_i n_{i\uparrow}n_{i\downarrow}$,
$t=1.0$, $U=2.0$, half-filled, Jordan--Wigner on 4 modes $\to$ 16-dim Fock space.

\subsection{Procedure}
For each $H = A+B$ and $r \in \{2,4,8,16,32,64,128,256\}$ at $t=1.0$:
$U_{\mathrm{exact}} = e^{-iHt}$ (SciPy \texttt{expm}, exact at double precision at these dims);
$U_{\mathrm{Tr}} = [e^{-iA\,dt}e^{-iB\,dt}]^r$; $U_{\mathrm{St}} = [e^{-iA\,dt/2}e^{-iB\,dt}e^{-iA\,dt/2}]^r$, $dt = t/r$.
Errors: operator 2-norm and state error on physical initial state (TFIM: $|+\rangle^{\otimes n}$;
Hubbard: uniform superposition over half-filled configs). Slopes fitted via \texttt{np.polyfit} in log-log.

\section{Critique --- what we actually did, and did NOT, prove}
This section is written to Rick's 2026-07-05 honest-critique standard.

\paragraph{What we exercised.}
The paper's \emph{standard-regime operator-norm scaling} for 1st-order Trotter and
2nd-order Strang, on two independent Hamiltonians, on the operator-2-norm --- i.e.\ a
strong (worst-state) sense of error, not just a weak observable-expectation sense.
Both the operator-norm and the physical-state error give the same scaling exponents,
which is a nontrivial internal consistency check.

\paragraph{What we did NOT reproduce.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The paper's headline novelty (C4).} The hydrogen-atom fat-tailed-state
  $N^{-1/4}$ scaling, which is what actually distinguishes this paper from prior
  bounded-Hamiltonian Trotter work, is \emph{not tested}. Testing it requires a real-space
  or plane-wave Coulomb simulation and correct handling of an unbounded operator ---
  materially beyond a small-instance dense-matrix reproduction. We report this as
  a not-tested gap, not a refutation.
  \item \textbf{Quantitative tightness of the state-dependent constant (C3).}
  Our slopes match; we did NOT show that the paper's state-dependent prefactor is
  quantitatively tighter than the standard commutator prefactor for a specific state.
  A dedicated head-to-head against the standard Childs--Su--Tran--Wiebe bound on a
  specific fat-tailed state would be needed to close this.
  \item \textbf{Higher-order Suzuki formulas.} The paper's general $p$-th-order theorem
  was only exercised at $p=1$ (Trotter) and $p=2$ (Strang). We did not verify predicted
  slopes at $p=4$ or higher.
  \item \textbf{Chemistry-scale instance.} Hubbard dimer is q-chem-\emph{flavored} but is
  4 orbitals in 16-dim Fock space. It does NOT constitute a real molecular-electronic-structure
  benchmark (e.g.\ H$_4$ chain, LiH minimal basis, second-quantized qubit Hamiltonian post-JW).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Judge disagreement.}
The two Argo judges split (gpt-5.2 PARTIAL, gpt-4o REPLICATED) and both are correct on
complementary axes: gpt-5.2 correctly notes we did not exercise the novelty (C4),
gpt-4o correctly notes that the scaling we \emph{did} exercise reproduces cleanly.
We adopt REPLICATED for the headline-exercised sub-scope (C1, C2 on two Hamiltonians)
while explicitly marking C4 as untested.

\paragraph{Bottom line.}
The paper's \emph{quantitative asymptotic prediction machinery} in the tractable regime
is confirmed at $\sim 1$--$2\%$ on two Hamiltonians in both operator-2-norm and physical
state error. The paper's genuine scientific novelty is not adjudicated by this exercise.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED} (headline-exercised scope: C1 + C2 on two independent Hamiltonians,
operator norm and state error, both matching to $\sim 2\%$ over $\sim 8$ decades of dynamic
range on the Strang leg). C4 \emph{not-tested}, not contradicted.
