### CLAIM C1 (convergence rate)
- **Support?** PARTIAL  
- **Confidence:** MED  
- **Justification:** Two splits show slopes close to the expected Monte Carlo rate (≈ −0.5): **−0.50** at 0.25π and **−0.61/−0.67** at 0.5π/0.75π (even slightly better than −0.5, plausibly finite-sample effects). But the pure-Dirichlet case (0.00π) has a much weaker fitted slope **−0.28** and non-monotone RMSE (e.g., 64→128→256 increases), so the evidence is not uniformly consistent with 1/√N across settings.

### CLAIM C2 (Neumann correctness + naive-scheme blowup)
- **Support?** YES  
- **Confidence:** HIGH  
- **Justification:** WoSt RMSE stays bounded and reasonably small as Neumann fraction increases (from **0.015** at 0.0 to **0.065–0.071** at 0.75–0.5). The naive multi-intersection scheme catastrophically diverges immediately once Neumann is present (RMSE **~10^45–10^58** for fractions 0.1–0.75), matching the paper’s “quickly blows up” behavior.

### CLAIM C3 (WoSt vs SDE-based reflection)
- **Support?** YES  
- **Confidence:** HIGH  
- **Justification:** At equal walks (N=512, θ_split=π/2), WoSt is both **more accurate** (RMSE **0.079** vs **0.321**) and **much faster** (wall **2.0s** vs **16.4s**, ~8×). This aligns with the paper’s claim that reflecting-SDE methods are slower and more biased for comparable sampling effort.

## Overall verdict: **PARTIAL**
**One-line summary:** Strong replication of Neumann robustness and naive-scheme blowup (C2) and WoSt’s advantage over reflecting SDE (C3), but convergence-rate evidence (C1) is inconsistent across boundary splits, notably in the pure-Dirichlet case.
