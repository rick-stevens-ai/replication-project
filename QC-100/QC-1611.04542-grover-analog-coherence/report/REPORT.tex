\section{Paper summary}

Anand \& Pati (arXiv:1611.04542v1, 14 Nov 2016) study the discrete
analogue of the Farhi--Gutmann \emph{analog} Grover search algorithm,
mapped onto $n$ qubits with $N = 2^n$ basis states. Starting from the
equal superposition $|s\rangle = (1/\sqrt{N}) \sum_i |i\rangle$ and
evolving under the discrete Grover oracle$+$diffuser, they track two
coherence monotones --- the $\ell_1$-norm of coherence
$C_{\ell_1}(\rho) = \sum_{i\ne j} |\rho_{ij}|$ and the relative-entropy
of coherence $C_r(\rho) = S(\rho_{\rm diag}) - S(\rho)$. The central
quantitative signature (Sec.\ III, Eqs.\ 15--16, Fig.\ 1) is that both
coherence monotones are non-zero throughout the search and hit zero
\emph{exactly at the instant the success probability $P$ peaks to $1$}.
They further connect two-qubit concurrence to $\dot P$ and prove an
$n$-party monogamy inequality is preserved for all $t$.

\section{Method}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Fetched paper (\texttt{arxiv.org/pdf/1611.04542}); extracted text.
\item Environment: macOS 25.3.0, Python 3, Qiskit 2.5.0, NumPy, matplotlib.
\item Wrote \texttt{code/grover\_coherence.py}: standard Grover oracle
      marking $|w\rangle{=}|0\ldots0\rangle$ (X-mask $\to$ H $\to$ MCX $\to$
      H $\to$ X-mask), standard diffuser
      (H$^{\otimes n}\,$X$^{\otimes n}\,$(H$\,$MCX$\,$H)$\,$X$^{\otimes n}\,$H$^{\otimes n}$),
      initialize $|s\rangle$ with $n$ Hadamards, evolve by
      \texttt{Statevector.from\_instruction}, record $P$, $C_{\ell_1}$,
      $C_r$ per iteration $k$.
\item Ran for $n = 3, 4, 5$ ($<5$~s wall-clock).
\item Produced three-panel plot (\texttt{plot\_tradeoff.py}).
\end{enumerate}

No LLM calls. No fabricated data. All numbers are direct outputs of the
Qiskit statevector simulator.

\section{Verdict: REPLICATED}

The paper's central quantitative signature --- coherence monotones
$C_{\ell_1}$ and $C_r$ collapse toward zero \emph{iff} success
probability $P$ peaks toward one --- is reproduced on real Qiskit
statevector simulation for $n = 3, 4, 5$. The Grover $k_{\rm opt}$
formula and $\sin^2$ closed form are confirmed to numerical precision,
and the collapse ratio $C_{\ell_1}(k_{\rm peak})/C_{\ell_1}(0)$ scales
$0.22 \to 0.14 \to 0.011$ as expected.

\section{Critique --- what this replication does \emph{not} establish}

The verdict rests only on claims C1--C3 (Grover optimum $k$ and
coherence-collapse-at-peak). Honest limitations:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{This is the discrete algorithm, not the analog Hamiltonian
      one.} The paper's title emphasizes the \emph{analog} Grover
      search of Farhi--Gutmann,
      $H = E(|w\rangle\langle w| + |s\rangle\langle s|)$, whose true
      speedup and coherence behavior live on continuous time
      $t_m = \pi / (2E)$. We reproduced only its discrete analogue
      (oracle$+$diffuser). The paper's own claim is that the two share
      the same coherence-collapse signature in the appropriate limit;
      we \emph{observed} that limit at $n{=}5$ but did not run the
      time-continuous Farhi--Gutmann Hamiltonian evolution and did not
      independently verify the "same signature in both algorithms"
      correspondence.
\item \textbf{$\sqrt{N}$ scaling was checked only at $n\in\{3,4,5\}$.}
      Three points on a $\sqrt{N}$ curve is confirmatory, not
      conclusive. A large-$n$ or a fit-based scaling check would
      strengthen the C1 evidence.
\item \textbf{Decoherence dependence was NOT probed.} The paper is
      framed in a coherence-resource picture; a real-hardware test
      would insert dephasing/relaxation channels
      (\texttt{qiskit\_aer.noise}) and study how the collapse behavior
      degrades with $T_1, T_2$. We ran pure statevector only, so
      cannot make any quantitative claim about noise-robustness.
\item \textbf{No digital-Grover-baseline vs.\ analog-Grover comparison.}
      A meaningful "analog speedup vs.\ digital speedup" statement
      requires both protocols on the same success-probability target.
      Only the discrete side is here.
\item \textbf{C4 (two-qubit concurrence vs.\ $\dot P$) and C5
      ($n$-party monogamy inequality)} were not tested. These are the
      paper's structural entanglement results and require reduced
      density matrices $\rho_{ij}$ plus Wootters' concurrence formula
      (C4) and the CKW-generalized $n$-tangle inequality (C5). Their
      omission does not affect the coherence verdict but leaves the
      entanglement half of the paper unverified.
\item \textbf{Verdict scope.} "REPLICATED" here means "the paper's
      headline coherence-collapse-at-peak signature is reproducible on
      an independent Qiskit statevector implementation for the
      discrete Grover algorithm." It is \emph{not} a claim that the
      analog algorithm was independently simulated on continuous
      time, that noise-thresholds were mapped, or that the monogamy
      inequality was numerically verified.
\end{enumerate}
