\section*{Critique (Rick, 2026-07-05 hard requirement)}
Three axes of critique with real teeth:

\paragraph{1. sRNA target-prediction verification: computational only, no CLIP-seq.}
The paper's PprS$\leftrightarrow$\emph{pprM} interaction is supported by MAPS (MS2-pulldown) pull-down enrichment, EMSA (in vitro binding), and Northern (RNA co-migration). MAPS is a \emph{genome-scale binding proxy} but is not base-pair-resolved and can capture indirect scaffolding via shared RBP or ribosomal contamination. Neither the paper nor this reproduction validates the predicted sRNA:target base-pairing footprint by CLIP-seq (e.g.\ CLASH, iCLIP, PARE-seq) that would give direct evidence of the seed pairing. \emph{pprM} passes MAPS and shows sign-consistent expression response, but the \emph{mechanism} (direct antisense pairing at the pprM 5$'$UTR) rests entirely on EMSA fragments and an in silico target prediction. My redo cannot address this gap because it depends on assay type not deposited.

\paragraph{2. \emph{D.\ radiodurans}-specific vs general-bacterial signature separation: not attempted.}
The paper implicitly treats the PprS/\emph{pprM} axis as a \emph{Deinococcus}-specific radiation-tolerance mechanism, but the IR-effect gene lists (S6/S7) are dominated by DNA-repair categories (recA, recFOR, uvr, ssb) that respond to genotoxic stress in essentially every bacterium. Neither the paper nor my reproduction separates the \emph{Deinococcus}-unique signature (e.g.\ Mn-antioxidant loading, DdrABC, PprA) from the pan-bacterial SOS-like response. Cross-species comparative context is absent from both the paper and the reproduction; my S6/S7 perfect numerical match confirms the paper's calls but does not distinguish \emph{Deinococcus} novelty from generic radiation response.

\paragraph{3. Coverage scope: 5/10 paper analyzable units, headline S4 fails.}
In-silico-testable coverage is 5/7 (71\%), below the 80\% protocol bar for a clean REPLICATED call. Wet-lab (EMSA, Northern, qRT-PCR, survival) is unreproducible in silico by construction. Proteomics (S1) is scoped-out by compute envelope. Critically, the failed contrast S4 contains the paper's flagship gene \emph{pprM} down-regulation in PprSKD-sham (padj $1.3\times10^{-9}$ in paper $\to$ $0.52$ in my redo). This is not a boundary-gene drift; it is a categorical disagreement on the headline claim, which the paper's own dataset cannot support without the specific R-DESeq2 dispersion pipeline. n=3 with CV=0.51 in WT-0 library sizes is an intrinsic power problem that no reanalysis can fix.
