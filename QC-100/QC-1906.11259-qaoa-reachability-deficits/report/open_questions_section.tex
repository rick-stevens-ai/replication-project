\section{Open Questions}

The five items below are genuinely open after this replication ---
none are answered by our sweep or by the original paper, and each has
a concrete, free-endpoint-only next-step probe.

\subsection*{Q1. Ansatz-expressibility limit vs.\ optimizer failure}
\textbf{Question.} Is the deficit at low \(p\) a fundamental
manifold-coverage property of the X-mixer QAOA state family, or is
part of it an artifact of our COBYLA + 4-restart optimizer getting
stuck?
\textbf{Basis.} The paper argues manifold coverage but never reports
optimizer robustness. McClean 2018 / Larocca 2022 / Wang 2021 have
since made this the central question.
\textbf{Next step.} Re-run \(\alpha\in\{2,3,5,7,10\}\times p\in\{1,2,4\}\)
with COBYLA, L-BFGS-B (analytic-gradient statevector), and BOBYQA,
20 restarts each. Spread of best-of-restarts \(f\) across optimizers
tells us how much is expressibility and how much is optimization.
Cost \(\sim 1.5\)\,hr CPU. Free.

\subsection*{Q2. Modern QAOA variants (warm-start, ADAPT) on the same instances}
\textbf{Question.} Does warm-start QAOA (Egger et al.\ 2020) or
ADAPT-QAOA (Zhu et al.\ 2022) close the deficit at the same low \(p\),
or does the \(\alpha\)-dependence survive the more expressive ansatz?
\textbf{Basis.} 1906.11259 predates both variants; the head-to-head
test on the same 3-SAT ensemble has not been published.
\textbf{Next step.} Extend our sweep code with (i) warm-start from a
classical relaxation and (ii) ADAPT greedy pool
\(\{X_i,X_iX_j\}\) up to 4 operators. Same 15 instances/\(\alpha\).
CPU-only, \(\sim 4\)--6\,hr on m1. Free.

\subsection*{Q3. Noise interaction with reachability}
\textbf{Question.} Does hardware noise make the deficit worse or does
noise-induced concentration flatten the landscape enough to
accidentally reduce \(f\)?
\textbf{Basis.} Paper is pure noiseless statevector. Post-2019 work
(Franç\'a \& Garc\'ia-Patr\'on 2021, Marshall 2020) suggests noise can
dominate the effective reachability. Nobody has computed the noisy
analog of Fig.~1 on the paper's instance family.
\textbf{Next step.} Add a density-matrix backend
(64\(\times\)64 fits trivially at \(n=6\)) with 1e-4/1e-3 single/two-qubit
depolarizing and readout error. Re-run \(p\in\{4,8\}\) across \(\alpha\).
Free.

\subsection*{Q4. Structural per-instance predictors of the deficit}
\textbf{Question.} Can the large within-\(\alpha\) spread of
per-instance \(f\) be explained by structural features (backbone
size, factor-graph community, clause overlap, unit-propagation depth)
rather than by \(\alpha\) alone?
\textbf{Basis.} Paper reports means; per-instance predictors have not
been analyzed. Our
\texttt{data/qaoa\_3sat\_sweep.json} contains all 105 per-instance
values, so this can be done as \emph{pure post-processing on existing
data} without any new sims.
\textbf{Next step.} Compute (a) backbone (exhaustive SAT, \(n=6\)
cheap), (b) factor-graph metrics, (c) unit-propagation depth per
instance. Regress \(\log f_{\rm instance}(p{=}4)\) against features
and report \(R^2\). \(<1\)\,hr, zero new simulations.

\subsection*{Q5. Alternative mixers (XY, Grover-reflection) and universality of the $\alpha$-scaling}
\textbf{Question.} Is the \(\alpha\)-scaling of the deficit
mixer-invariant (a property of the problem class), or does it change
with the mixer (a property of the ansatz)? Paper's Fig.~2 comments on
one alternative mixer qualitatively but never presents a joint
scaling plot.
\textbf{Basis.} Modern mixer-design work (Hadfield QAOA+, Bärtschi \&
Eidenbenz 2020 Grover mixers) makes this a live question.
\textbf{Next step.} Add XY-mixer and Grover-reflection mixer to
\texttt{qaoa\_reachability.py}. Fix \(p=4\), sweep \(\alpha\in\{1,3,5,7,10\}\),
15 instances each. Fit log--log slope in the \(\alpha\in[3,10]\)
regime; report whether the slope is mixer-invariant. \(\sim 2\)\,hr
CPU. Free.
