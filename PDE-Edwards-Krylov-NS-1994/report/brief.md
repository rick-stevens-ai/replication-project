# Brief — Edwards, Tuckerman, Friesner, Sorensen (1994)

**Title.** Krylov methods for the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations.
**Journal.** J. Comput. Phys. 110, 82–102 (1994). DOI 10.1006/jcph.1994.1007.

**What & why.** The paper proposes a unified Krylov-subspace-based framework for the three core problems of computational fluid dynamics for the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations at low-to-moderate Reynolds number: (i) time evolution by **exponential propagation** (nonlinear generalisation of Krylov exp(tA)v), (ii) **steady-state solving** by inexact Newton with GMRES/ORTHORES on the Jacobian, and (iii) **linear stability analysis** by the (then-new) **implicitly restarted Arnoldi process with polynomial filtering** (Sorensen 1992, which later became ARPACK). All three methods only need matrix-free actions of F(U) and DF(U); the viscous operator is never inverted. The demonstration application is the primary-to-secondary Couette–Taylor bifurcation (Taylor vortices → wavy vortices) computed with a Chebyshev(r)–Fourier(z) pseudospectral discretisation at η=0.8703, α=2.0076, Re=131.025, grids N_r×N_z = 24×27 up to 32×72. Reproducing the full Couette–Taylor code base is out of scope for a bounded independent replication; instead this replication reproduces the paper's **methodological claims** on Krylov behaviour (exp(tA)v accuracy, K≈20 sweet spot, linear Newton–GMRES convergence, IRAM leading-eigenvalue convergence) on a faithful but smaller 2-D incompressible NS demonstration (2-D lid-driven cavity + Poiseuille eigenvalues), so that the *machinery* the paper introduced is independently exercised on real Navier–Stokes systems.
