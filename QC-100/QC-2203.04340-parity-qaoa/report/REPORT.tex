\section{Paper and Verdict}
Ender, Messinger, Fellner, Dlaska, Lechner (Innsbruck / Parity Quantum
Computing GmbH), arXiv:2203.04340. The paper studies the parity-encoded
(LHZ) QAOA architecture and introduces a \emph{hybrid} variant that
splits constraints between explicit penalty enforcement ($H_C$) and
implicit constraint-preserving driver lines. A continuous knob
$n_r=n_C/n_C^{\mathrm{tot}}\in[0,1]$ interpolates between the fully
explicit ($n_r=1$) and fully implicit ($n_r=0$) limits.

\textbf{Verdict: REPLICATED.} The paper's headline claim
(Fig.~7 noiseless panel: monotone decrease of median residual energy
$E_{\mathrm{res}}$ as $n_r$ decreases, and inverse monotone increase
of ground-state fidelity) is reproduced from a first-principles
statevector simulator (numpy only, no Qiskit dependency) on the
$N=6$ complete-graph Ising problem at $p=3$ across 24 random instances.

\section{Claims Tested}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{C1} (monotone $E_{\mathrm{res}}(n_r)$): tested, \textbf{reproduced}.
\item \textbf{C2} (inverse monotone fidelity): tested, \textbf{reproduced}.
\item \textbf{C3} (implicit parity QAOA vs standard unencoded QAOA): tested, \textbf{reproduced} ($\sim 8\times$ better $E_{\mathrm{res}}$ at $p=3$).
\item \textbf{C4} (driver-line depth $O(N)$ implicit vs depth $O(1)$ explicit): verified structurally at $N=6$.
\item \textbf{C5} (CNOT noise scan): \textbf{not attempted} --- noise-free simulation only.
\end{itemize}

\section{Independent Reimplementation}
The simulator (\texttt{code/parity\_qaoa.py}, $\sim 600$ LoC) was written
from scratch: instance generation ($J_{ij}\sim U[-1,1]$, $\binom{6}{2}=15$
couplings), enumeration of the 32 constraint-fulfilling (CF) parity code
states via the GF(2) map $b_i \mapsto (b_i\oplus b_j)_{i<j}$,
diagonal-phase evolution for $H_Z$ and $H_C$, and the multi-qubit
$\exp(-i\beta\prod_k \sigma_x^{(k)})$ implemented as
$\cos\beta\,I - i\sin\beta\,P$ acting via a qubit-mask permutation.
Optimiser is the paper's own stochastic accept-if-improves protocol
(8 random starts $\times$ 150 moves per start per instance).

Sanity checks (all passed): $|H_{\mathrm{CF}}|=32=2^{N-1}$; $H_C=0$ on
every CF state; $H_Z(\mathrm{CF})$ agrees with the logical Ising energy
of the associated spin configuration on all 32 states (mod global flip);
all 5 implicit driver lines are verified to preserve the CF subspace.

\section{What Was Not Reproduced}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Fig.~6 depth scan across $N$.} Only $N{=}6$ tested. The
$O(K/N)$ vs $O(1)$ scaling was confirmed structurally (5 driver lines
$\times$ 5 qubits at $N{=}6$) but not extended to $N\in\{8,10,12\}$;
that requires the $2^{2N-1}$ Hilbert space and was out of QC-100 budget.
\item \textbf{Fig.~7 CNOT-error sweep.} Only the $y$-intercept
(noiseless) was reproduced. A per-gate depolarising noise model
(Qiskit-Aer) was skipped in favour of keeping the simulator
dependency-free.
\item \textbf{Fig.~5 modularisation for large $N$.} Structural claim, no
numerical test at $N{=}6$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Honest Critique}
The replication is strong on the noiseless ordering (the plot's
$y$-intercept) but leaves several substantive gaps:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Restricted problem class.} We used
$J_{ij}\sim U[-1,1]$ on $K_6$ exactly as in the paper. The parity-QAOA
advantage over standard QAOA has \emph{not} been re-verified in this
replication for weighted-MaxCut, non-binary, or higher-order Ising
problems where the encoding overhead could look very different.
\item \textbf{No noise model.} C5 is the claim that most matters for
NISQ deployment (does the ordering survive $\sim 10^{-2}$ CNOT error?).
Not tested here. An Aer noise-model rerun on the same $N{=}6$ instances
would be a targeted, low-cost follow-up.
\item \textbf{Standard-QAOA baseline is a single-depth data point.}
Our comparison at $p{=}3$ favours implicit parity QAOA by $\sim 8\times$
in $E_{\mathrm{res}}$, but we did not sweep $p$ for the standard-QAOA
baseline. It is plausible standard QAOA closes some of the gap at
larger $p$, which the paper does not address either.
\item \textbf{Fidelity numbers are small in absolute terms} (best
median fidelity $0.20$ for $n_r{=}0$). This is faithful to the paper but
means the "ordering" is on the tail of a broad distribution --- for
practical certification one would want $F\gtrsim 0.5$, requiring larger
$p$ or a better optimiser.
\item \textbf{Optimiser is deliberately weak.} The paper uses stochastic
accept-if-improves; so do we. A modern gradient / interpolation heuristic
(e.g.\ INTERP, TQA, or ADAM on parameter-shift gradients) would very
likely change the absolute numbers even if the ordering is robust.
\item \textbf{Connectivity-overhead trade-off is asserted, not
quantified.} We confirmed the structural counts (5 lines $\times$ 5
qubits for implicit; single-qubit-parallel for explicit), but did not
compile to a specific hardware coupling map and count physical CNOT
depth, which is what a device-level claim actually requires.
\item \textbf{Instance count 24 vs paper's 96.} Medians are already tight
at 24, but the tails (upper quartile of $n_r{=}1$) would firm up with
$96$. Cheap to redo.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Reproducibility}
Environment: Python 3.14.6, numpy 2.5.0, scipy 1.18.0, networkx 3.6.1;
no Qiskit / PennyLane. Rerun:
\begin{lstlisting}
cd code && source ../venv/bin/activate
python parity_qaoa.py --N 6 --p 3 --instances 24 \
       --n_starts 8 --n_moves 150 --seed 2026
\end{lstlisting}
Wall time $\approx 21$ min on a single CherryRd CPU core.
Evidence: \texttt{report/evidence/results.json} (per-instance
$E_{\mathrm{res}}$, fidelity, summary stats).
