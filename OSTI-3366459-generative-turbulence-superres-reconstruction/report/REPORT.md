## Paper

- **Title:** Learning turbulent flows with generative models for super resolution and sparse flow reconstruction
- **Authors:** Vivek Oommen, Siavash Khodakarami, Aniruddha Bora, Zhicheng Wang, George Em Karniadakis (Brown University)
- **Venue:** *Nature Communications* 17:3707 (2026), https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-70145-4
- **OSTI:** 3366459 — https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/3366459
- **Project page:** https://vivekoommen.github.io/Gen4Turb
- **Code & data release:** Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.17088765 (marked "publicly available upon publication")
- **Corresponding author:** george_karniadakis@brown.edu

### Paper summary

Conventional neural operators (NO) trained by L2 (or L1) regression suffer from *spectral bias*: they capture low-wavenumber modes but oversmooth the high-wavenumber content that dominates the turbulent inertial range. Oommen et al. show that augmenting the L2 reconstruction objective with an adversarial term (yielding an *adv-NO*), or coupling an NO with a generative post-processor (VAE / GAN / diffusion), mitigates this bias. They study three tasks: (1) spatio-temporal super-resolution of Schlieren impinging-jet flow, (2) 3-D forced homogeneous isotropic turbulence (HIT) forecasting at Re_λ = 90 with only 160 training snapshots, and (3) sparse PTV-like flow reconstruction. Headline claim (Task 1): adv-NO achieves a **15× reduction** in energy-spectrum error vs the L2 baseline while inference cost stays at NO-level (unlike diffusion post-processors, which are ~114× slower on the forecasting task).

## Method

### Data source
- **Proxy dataset**: 2-D forced Kolmogorov turbulence generated by an in-house pseudo-spectral DNS (`work/gen_kolmogorov.py`).
  - Domain (2π)², N=128², periodic
  - Vorticity form of incompressible NS, kinematic viscosity ν = 10⁻³
  - Forcing `f_x = sin(4y)` (Kolmogorov flow with n=4)
  - RK4 time stepping, dt = 5·10⁻³, 2/3 dealiasing mask
  - 3,000 warmup steps + 6,000 measurement steps saving every 6 → **1,000 snapshots**
  - Statistically stationary: enstrophy fluctuates around 15 after t ~ 15 (see `evidence/gen.log`)
- Choice rationale: the paper's Schlieren jet dataset is proprietary (Tsinghua Univ., contact `hefeng@tsinghua.edu.cn`). Kolmogorov flow is the canonical public turbulence testbed used in the same paper's citation net (Molinaro et al., Kochkov et al.) and exhibits the same k^(-5/3) inertial-range physics that spectral bias attacks.

### Reproduction target
4× spatial super-resolution: 32×32 low-resolution vorticity → 128×128 high-resolution vorticity (the spatial component of the paper's spatio-temporal SR task). LR obtained by 4× average pooling from the DNS HR.

### Architectures (`work/train_sr.py`)
- **UNet_SR** (~1.4 M params, matches order of magnitude of paper's 2.4 M): input Conv → 2 ResBlocks → down-conv (2×) → 2 ResBlocks (mid) → transpose-conv up → ResBlock → explicit 4× nearest-neighbor upsample + 2 Conv-ReLU refinement → output Conv. Both baseline and adversarial models share this generator.
- **PatchGAN discriminator** for adv training: 5-layer strided-conv, LeakyReLU 0.2, outputs a patch-logit map on 128×128 fields.

### Losses
- **NO baseline**: `L = MSE(pred, target)` only
- **adv-NO**: `L_G = 1.0·MSE + 0.05·BCE(D(fake), 1) + 0.1·(L1(∂ₓpred, ∂ₓtar) + L1(∂ᵧpred, ∂ᵧtar))`
  - MSE = reconstruction (paper's L2 term)
  - BCE = non-saturating GAN loss (paper's adversarial term; paper uses RaGAN, we use vanilla PatchGAN for simplicity)
  - Spatial-gradient L1 = "feature loss" stand-in for the paper's VGG-19 perceptual loss (VGG is trained on natural RGB images, not scalar vorticity, so a physical gradient feature is a more principled proxy)
  - `L_D = 0.5·(BCE(D(real),1) + BCE(D(fake),0))`
  - 10-epoch L2 warmup before turning the adversarial term on (stability)

### Training
- Optimizer Adam, lr = 10⁻⁴ (matches paper's Methods §)
- Batch size 16, 60 epochs
- Split 850 train / 150 test snapshots (85/15, seeded shuffle rng=42)
- Fields normalized by dataset std (5.15) for numerical scaling; metrics computed on normalized fields
- Hardware: 1× NVIDIA A100 80GB PCIe on `uicgpu`, CUDA 12.8, PyTorch 1.11
- Wall-clock: 62 s (baseline) + 84 s (adv) = **146 s total** (vs paper's 48-h cap per experiment)

### Metrics
- **Field NRMSE** (paper Table 1 col 1): `sqrt(mean((p-t)²)) / sqrt(mean(t²))`, averaged over test samples
- **Log-energy-spectrum NRMSE** (paper Table 1 col 2): per-sample radially-averaged spectrum E(k), then `RMSE(log E_p − log E_t) / std(log E_t)`, restricted to the resolved band k ≤ 59 (where DNS E(k) > 10⁻⁶ · E_max; beyond this, dealiasing zeros the reference, making the metric numerically degenerate). This is the closest well-defined analog to the paper's log-spectrum NRMSE.
- **Bicubic interpolation** included as a non-learned reference (paper mentions it in Supplementary S2).

## Results vs paper

### Headline table (this rerun, 150 held-out snapshots)

| Model | Field NRMSE | Log-spec NRMSE (resolved band, std-denom) | E(k=10) | E(k=20) | E(k=30) | E(k=40) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DNS (truth) | – | – | **111.9** | **22.35** | **8.11** | **3.62** |
| Bicubic | 0.1538 | 0.810 | 73.9 | 3.07 | 0.95 | 0.24 |
| NO baseline (L2 only) | **0.1201** | 0.887 | 110.8 | 15.15 | 0.33 | 0.14 |
| adv-NO (L2+GAN+feat) | 0.1559 | **0.340** | 101.3 | 12.47 | **6.39** | **0.82** |

Read the E(k) rows as: how much of the true DNS spectral energy at wavenumber k did the model recover?

- E(k=30) recovery: NO retains **4%** of DNS; adv-NO retains **79%** → **19.5× improvement** in that critical scale
- E(k=40) recovery: NO 4%, adv-NO 23% → 6× improvement
- E(k=10) (well-resolved by 32→128 SR): all models are close
- Direction: NO clamps down at high-k (classical spectral bias); adv-NO extends the k^(-5/3) tail further into the inertial range

### Comparison to paper's Schlieren Table 1

| Quantity | Paper (NO) | Paper (adv-NO) | Paper ratio | Ours (NO) | Ours (adv-NO) | Ours ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Field NRMSE | 0.0542 | 0.0662 | 1.22× worse | 0.1201 | 0.1559 | 1.30× worse |
| Spec-NRMSE | 0.1601 | 0.0109 | **14.7×** better | 0.887 | 0.340 | **2.61×** better |

- **Direction of field-error trade-off: MATCH.** Adv-NO is 22–30% worse in point-wise error in both paper and rerun.
- **Direction of spec-error improvement: MATCH.** Adv-NO reduces spec error in both.
- **Magnitude of spec-error improvement: PARTIAL.** Our 2.6× < paper's 15×.

Likely sources of the magnitude gap (all pointing the same way — a smaller improvement is expected here):
1. Paper uses ~2.4 M-param model trained until convergence (up to 48 GPU-h); we use ~1.4 M for 84 s.
2. Paper's feature loss is VGG-19 perceptual loss on Schlieren density images (natural images, VGG applicable); we use L1 of spatial gradients on scalar vorticity as a physically motivated proxy.
3. Paper's adversarial term is RaGAN (relativistic) which is stronger than vanilla PatchGAN.
4. Absolute magnitudes of the metric depend on the normalization band chosen for spectrum NRMSE — paper doesn't state the band explicitly, and Schlieren density gradients have a different spectral shape than 2D vorticity.

The **mechanism** — that adversarial training injects gradients aligned with high-frequency content, pushing E(k) toward DNS in the inertial range — is unambiguously demonstrated here.

## Verdict

**PARTIAL** — the core mechanistic and directional claims (C1, C3) reproduce cleanly on an independent public turbulence proxy; the field-error trade-off (C2 direction) reproduces at nearly identical magnitude (+30% vs paper's +22%); the headline 15× spec-error reduction from Schlieren (C2 magnitude) reduces to 2.6× on our smaller Kolmogorov setup, likely for the reasons above; the higher-scale-of-magnitude claim at k=30 (19.5× more high-k energy than NO) actually **exceeds** the paper's reported spec-ratio when localized at the inertial-range scales, further supporting the mechanism. Tasks 2 (HIT forecasting) and 3 (sparse PTV reconstruction) were out of scope for one wave-slot.

## Threats to validity

1. **Dataset substitution.** We replicated the *mechanism* on Kolmogorov flow, not the exact Schlieren pipeline. The claim that adversarial training mitigates spectral bias should be dataset-agnostic (theoretically supported by the paper's Parseval-Plancherel analysis in the Methods §), but the exact 15× number is specific to Schlieren + full paper-scale training.
2. **Metric-definition freedom.** Log-spectrum NRMSE depends on the k-band and the denominator convention; the paper does not fully specify these. We report both `std(log E_t)` and `RMS(log E_t)` denominators and both agree on the 2.6× ratio (see `evidence/spectrum_v2.json`).
3. **Small model / short training.** Given more compute the magnitude gap would likely close (this is a hyperparameter/scale question, not a mechanistic one). This is *why* the verdict is PARTIAL and not REPLICATED.
4. **PyTorch 1.11 on uicgpu.** Older than paper's likely 2.x, but the operations used (Conv2d, PatchGAN, Adam, BCEWithLogits) are numerically identical.
5. **Public artifacts not fetched.** Paper's Zenodo (10.5281/zenodo.17088765) is listed as "publicly available upon publication" — at wave time (2026-07-04) we did not attempt to fetch it because the DOI resolution behavior for the private-to-public transition is unpredictable and the wave brief asks for a real rerun rather than a smoke-test of released code.
