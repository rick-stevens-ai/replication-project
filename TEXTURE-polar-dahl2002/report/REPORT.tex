\section{The paper and the testable claim}
Dahl's paper is a critical review of S.\,T.\ Lagerwall's book on ferroelectric
and antiferroelectric liquid crystals. It contains no simulation, but it states
a sharp, operationally testable diagnostic for distinguishing \emph{true}
bistability from a mimicking nonlinear-lossy response:

\begin{quote}
``If we have two bistable states in the material, with a potential barrier
between, the width of the hysteresis loop should be independent of the
frequency, while a nonlinear, lossy material should have a hysteresis loop
width that is approx.\ proportional to the frequency.''
\end{quote}

\section{Model (from scratch)}
We build a $0$D Landau--Ginzburg--Devonshire (LGD) polarization model for a
uniform SSFLC monodomain with scalar order parameter $P$:
\begin{equation}
F(P) = \tfrac12 a P^2 + \tfrac14 b P^4 + \tfrac16 c P^6 - E(t)\,P ,
\end{equation}
evolved by overdamped TDGL / Landau--Khalatnikov dynamics
\begin{equation}
\gamma\,\frac{dP}{dt} = -\frac{\partial F}{\partial P}
   = -\bigl(a P + b P^3 + c P^5\bigr) + E(t), \qquad E(t)=E_0\cos(\omega t).
\end{equation}
Two regimes are compared under identical drive:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Double-well} ($a=-1<0$): two degenerate minima at
$\pm P_0=\pm0.924$ separated by a barrier $\Delta F=0.224$ at $E=0$ ---
genuine ferroelectric bistability.
\item \textbf{Single-well nonlinear lossy} ($a=+1>0$): no barrier at $E=0$,
same nonlinear $b,c$; any loop is dissipative.
\end{itemize}
Parameters: $b=1,\;c=0.2,\;\gamma=1,\;E_0=0.45,\;dt=2\times10^{-3},\;kT=10^{-4}$.
We sweep $\omega\in\{0.02,0.05,0.1,0.2,0.5,1.0\}$ ($\sim$1.5 decades) and measure
the P--E loop width (coercive field $E_c$) and loop area.

\textit{Provenance:} the LGD free-energy form and Landau--Khalatnikov update are
adapted from \texttt{ollie\_tdgl\_phasefield\_polar\_skyrmion\_kernel.py}
(author: Ollie), reduced to the $0$D scalar polarization needed for this
diagnostic.

In the low-frequency switching window ($\omega\le0.2$, where both potentials
form a $P{=}0$-crossing loop) the log--log slope of loop area vs.\ $\omega$ is:
\begin{itemize}
\item Double-well: slope $=-0.02$ $\Rightarrow$ frequency-\emph{independent}
loop width (Dahl's bistable prediction).
\item Single-well lossy: slope $=+1.06$ $\Rightarrow$ width $\propto$ frequency
(Dahl's lossy prediction).
\end{itemize}
The double-well retains a finite coercive field ($E_c\to0.42$) as $\omega\to0$,
while the lossy single-well loses its width ($E_c\to0.009$, $\sim40\times$
smaller). See Fig.~\ref{fig:diag}.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED.} The from-scratch LGD/TDGL model reproduces Dahl's
diagnostic quantitatively: genuine double-well bistability gives a
frequency-independent hysteresis-loop width, whereas a nonlinear lossy
(monostable) potential gives a width proportional to frequency. Confidence:
high (clean, mechanism-level agreement; slopes $\approx0$ and $\approx+1$ as
predicted). Applicability: the $0$D uniform-monodomain reduction captures the
switching physics; the full spatial SSFLC director field is out of scope.
