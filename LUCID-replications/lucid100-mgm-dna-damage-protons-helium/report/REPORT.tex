\section{Paper Under Test}
Onecha V.V., Schuemann J., Paganetti H., Bertolet A.
\emph{Extending the Microdosimetry Gamma Model (MGM) to estimate induced
DNA damage and its complexity at macroscopic scale by protons and helium
ions.} Phys.\ Med.\ Biol.\ \textbf{70}(20), 2025.
DOI: \href{https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6560/ae117e}{10.1088/1361-6560/ae117e}.
HHS Public Access manuscript PMC12905799 (PubMed 41067246).

The paper extends the analytical MGM framework of Bertolet 2023
(\emph{Front.\ Oncol.}) from photon microdosimetry to a proton- and
helium-capable macroscopic damage-yield model, wrapped in a new TOPAS
C\texttt{++} extension (TOPAS-MGM) that scores per-nucleus MDS
(Multiply-Damaged Site) counts and complexity distributions during
condensed-history transport. Validation runs: (i) mono-energetic cell
layers 0.5--150 MeV p and 0.25--150 MeV/u He; (ii) 170 MeV p and
135 MeV/u He water-phantom Bragg curves; (iii) $^{211}$At and $^{225}$Ac
radiopharmaceutical-therapy (RPT) cell monolayers; (iv) timing benchmark
vs full TOPAS-nBio Geant4-DNA option-2 reference.

\section{Critique -- Honest Assessment}\label{sec:critique}

\textbf{This section is deliberately blunt. The queue label REPLICATED is
not supported by the on-disk evidence; the correct verdict is PARTIAL.}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The Monte-Carlo code was NOT re-run.} Not a single TOPAS,
    TOPAS-nBio, or Geant4-DNA transport was executed as part of this
    replication. Every quantitative check in \S 4 either
    (a) evaluates the published MGM analytical polynomials in Python
    on scalar $y_F$ values, or (b) pushes an externally-tabulated
    Geant4-DNA LET value through those polynomials. This is a
    \emph{model-arithmetic replication}, not a \emph{Monte-Carlo
    replication}. The paper's central contribution -- the TOPAS-MGM
    C\texttt{++} extension that scores MDS per nucleus during
    condensed-history transport -- is entirely outside what was
    reproduced.
  \item \textbf{Published aggregate numbers were compared against
    MGM predictions, not against re-derived MC output.} All C1--C9
    anchors are the paper's summary/headline numbers (mean complexity,
    MDS/Gy/Gbp, Bragg-peak ratios) as read from the paper's figures and
    text. There is no independent MC dataset to cross-check them
    against; the closest thing is the sibling Geant4-DNA LET tables
    used in P1--P3, which are LET(E) values, not $y_F$ spectra.
  \item \textbf{The proton-vs-helium LET-matched comparison
    (paper Fig 3) is NOT reproduced.} On the contrary,
    P3 explicitly demonstrates that MGM \emph{cannot} reproduce it
    (He/p ratio $\approx 1$ at matched LET, whereas paper shows
    He > p). The paper's He > p separation is genuine new information
    from the TOPAS-MGM extension per-track scoring; the analytical
    MGM core has no track-structure knob and by construction cannot
    do this. This is an important intellectual finding of the
    replication (identifying an MGM model LIMIT), but it is
    \emph{not} a positive reproduction of Fig 3.
  \item \textbf{Damage complexity spectrum: only aggregate mean
    complexity is checked, not the full distribution.} The paper
    reports per-MDS histograms (Fig 4c) and cMDS fractions ($C \ge 3$,
    $C \ge 5$). The replication reports mean $\bar C$ and coarse
    fractions from evaluating the paper's own $a(y_F), b(y_F)$
    polynomials -- it does not sample the full paper distributions
    from an MC run.
  \item \textbf{Bragg-peak claims are only spot-checked at 2 depths.}
    C8 (proton BP ratio 1.07 vs 1.12) and C9 (helium BP ratio
    1.6--1.9 vs 4.0) test 1 depth each; the full Fig 6 depth scan
    (typically $\sim 50$ depths $\times$ 2 particles) is not
    reproduced. C9's factor-of-2 FAIL is left as a known gap.
  \item \textbf{Cell DNA content is assumed, not verified.} 6.4 Gbp
    human diploid is used throughout, but the paper does not state
    which value it used. P4 shows that closing the C6 puzzle (9.4 vs
    30) may require a different Gbp; this is unresolved and any
    per-Gbp-normalised number in \S 4 is only meaningful up to that
    common assumption.
  \item \textbf{Queue verdict vs on-disk verdict mismatch.} Queue
    label = REPLICATED; on-disk verdict = PARTIAL (coverage 4/10,
    agreement 7/10; below the 5/10 coverage bar most reasonable
    thresholds would call REPLICATED). The on-disk verdict is
    preserved as authoritative and the mismatch is flagged in
    \texttt{failure\_analysis.md}. See LUCID sampling note in the
    task brief: $\sim 33\%$ of queue-REPLICATED LUCID dirs sampled
    have similar on-disk PARTIAL / NO-GO downgrades.
  \item \textbf{Supplementary material was not retrieved.} PMC SI
    for PMC12905799 is reCAPTCHA-gated. It likely contains the
    exact $a(y_F), b(y_F)$ fit parameters, AAPM TG-268 reporting,
    and per-MDS histograms that would tighten C1--C5.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Verdict}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Verdict: PARTIAL} (on-disk, authoritative). Coverage 4/10;
    agreement 7/10.
  \item \textbf{Reproducibility blockers:}
    (1) TOPAS-MGM C\texttt{++} extension is unpublished; author org
    \texttt{MGHPhysicsResearch} has 8 repos, none is TOPAS-MGM;
    no code-availability statement in the paper.
    (2) PMC SI reCAPTCHA-gated; needs a human-driven browser session.
    (3) TOPAS-nBio reference runs require an HPC allocation plus TOPAS
    academic license; CherryRd is disallowed for heavy MC per project policy.
  \item \textbf{Positive contributions of this audit:}
    P3 documents a previously-undocumented MGM model LIMIT
    (LET-only, cannot separate He from p at matched LET);
    P4 rules out $y_F$-spectrum averaging as the explanation for the
    paper's Fig 4a per-Gy scale;
    the LET-sweep of 29 (E, particle) points via MGM against an
    independent Geant4-DNA dataset adds genuine U3 coverage.
\end{itemize}
