\begin{abstract}
We independently replicate the central proof-of-principle numerical experiment of
Mitarai, Nakagawa \& Mizukami (\emph{Phys.\ Rev.\ Research} \textbf{2}, 013129, 2020;
arXiv:1905.04054v2), which introduces analytical expressions and low-depth quantum circuits
for VQE energy derivatives (forces, Hessians, response tensors). Using an open-source stack
(PennyLane 0.45 + NumPy) on a laptop CPU, we reproduce the paper's own H$_2$/STO-3G/$r=0.735$~\AA{}
test system with a 2-layer hardware-efficient ansatz. Analytical force $=+2.005\times10^{-4}$~Ha/\AA{}
agrees with the exact full-diagonalization force $+2.295\times10^{-4}$~Ha/\AA{} to
$|\Delta|=2.9\times10^{-5}$~Ha/\AA{}, while the naive VQE reoptimize-at-$r\pm\delta$ finite-difference
force is $-9.26\times10^{-3}$~Ha/\AA{} --- $\sim 330\times$ worse and even wrong-signed at this precision.
\textbf{Verdict: REPLICATED.} The paper's headline claim --- analytical derivatives beat numerical
differences on the paper's own benchmark --- is directly exercised on the paper's own test system.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper Summary}
The paper derives analytical expressions and quantum circuits for energy derivatives within VQE, for
both ground and excited states. The key first-order result reduces at the variational optimum
$\theta^{*}(R)$ to the Hellmann--Feynman form
\begin{equation}
\left.\frac{dE}{dR}\right|_{\theta=\theta^{*}} = \left\langle \psi(\theta^{*}) \right| \frac{\partial H(R)}{\partial R} \left| \psi(\theta^{*}) \right\rangle ,
\end{equation}
sidestepping the need to re-optimize the ansatz at $R\pm\delta R$. Higher-order derivatives require
response equations for $\partial\theta^{*}/\partial R$ (their Eq.~10). The proof-of-principle
experiment in Sec.~7 is H$_2$/STO-3G at $r=0.735$~\AA{} built via PySCF+OpenFermion, simulated on
Qulacs with a 2-layer hardware-efficient ansatz.

\section{Method}
Environment: macOS 25.3.0, Python 3.12.13, PennyLane 0.45.1, NumPy 2.5.0, SciPy 1.18.0. All simulation
is state-vector (\texttt{default.qubit}), no shot / gate noise. Molecule: H$_2$, STO-3G, $r=0.735$~\AA{},
symbols/coords in Bohr, Hamiltonian via \texttt{qml.qchem.molecular\_hamiltonian(\ldots,\allowbreak method="dhf")}
$\rightarrow$ 4-qubit, 15-Pauli-term operator (matches the standard PySCF/OpenFermion result).

\paragraph{Reference (FCI).} 16$\times$16 matrix $=$ \texttt{qml.matrix(H)}; smallest eigenvalue via
\texttt{numpy.linalg.eigvalsh} is the exact ground state in the STO-3G basis.

\paragraph{VQE.} HF reference $|1100\rangle$ via \texttt{BasisState}, then $L{=}2$ layers of
$R_x(\theta)R_y(\theta)$ on each of 4 wires followed by a CNOT chain (paper's Fig.~3 with the small
practical variant of splitting $R_x, R_y$). Optimizer: Adam ($\eta=0.3$, seed 42, $\sigma_{\text{init}}=0.1$),
diff method = parameter-shift, $\leq 250$ iterations.

\paragraph{Analytical force (paper's central object).} At converged $\theta^{*}$:
\[
\frac{dE}{dR}\bigg|_{\theta^{*}} \approx \frac{\langle\psi(\theta^{*})|H(R{+}\delta)|\psi(\theta^{*})\rangle - \langle\psi(\theta^{*})|H(R{-}\delta)|\psi(\theta^{*})\rangle}{2\delta}, \quad \delta = 10^{-3}\ \text{\AA}.
\]
This is a two-point FD \emph{of the Hamiltonian coefficients} with the wavefunction held fixed --- no
VQE reoptimization noise leaks in.

\paragraph{Numerical-difference baseline.} Fully re-run VQE at $r\pm\delta$ with $\delta=5\times10^{-3}$~\AA{}
(larger, so the true $\Delta E$ isn't drowned below VQE convergence noise), then centered FD.

\paragraph{Exact reference force.} Full-diag energy at $r\pm10^{-4}$~\AA{}, centered FD.

\paragraph{PES scan.} $r\in\{0.4,0.5,\ldots,1.5\}$~\AA{} at 0.1~\AA{} spacing, FCI at each point.

The analytical force is $\sim 330\times$ closer to the exact reference than the numerical-difference
force and does so from a single optimized VQE run with two extra expectation-value evaluations. This
is exactly the failure mode of finite differences the paper motivates its formalism to avoid.
PES scan minimum sits between $r=0.70$ and $0.80$~\AA{} at $E\approx-1.1373$~Ha, consistent with
Fig.~4.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED.} The headline claim --- analytical VQE derivatives reproduce the exact derivative
to well below the numerical-difference noise floor --- is directly and quantitatively reproduced on
the paper's own H$_2$/STO-3G/$r=0.735$~\AA{} benchmark using an open-source stack on a laptop CPU
($\sim 5.5$~min wall time).

\section{Critique and Failure Analysis}
\label{sec:critique}
This replication is honest about the following limits, and readers should not overstate what was
demonstrated here:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Only first-order was independently re-derived.} We reproduced $dE/dR$ at the variational
optimum. The paper's second- and third-order derivative circuits (their Eq.~10 response equations for
$\partial\theta^{*}/\partial R$, and the cubic PES fit in Fig.~4) were \emph{not} independently
reimplemented here. Their consistency with the framework is inherited, not re-verified.
\item \textbf{No independent implementation of the parameter-shift derivative circuits themselves.}
We used PennyLane's built-in \texttt{diff\_method="parameter-shift"} on the ansatz parameters. This
is functionally the same rule the paper derives (their Sec.~2), but we did not reimplement the
$R\pm\pi/2$-shifted expectation-value circuits from scratch and cross-check them against a direct
symbolic gradient of $\langle H\rangle_{\theta}$. That would be the true "independent re-derivation."
\item \textbf{No geometry optimization or vibrational-analysis application demonstrated.} The paper
motivates analytical derivatives partly by enabling geometry optimization (BFGS with analytical
gradients) and vibrational analysis (harmonic frequencies from the Hessian). We did not close the
loop by running an actual VQE geometry optimizer or extracting an H$_2$ harmonic frequency from a
second derivative. This is a genuine gap: our replication proves the first-derivative arithmetic is
right, but does not prove the paper's downstream chemistry application works end-to-end from our
implementation.
\item \textbf{Measurement-overhead analysis is qualitative, not quantitative.} The paper is careful
about circuit counts and shot budgets for the analytical vs numerical routes. Our replication is on
a noiseless state-vector simulator and does \emph{not} quantify measurement-overhead per gradient
call (e.g., number of Pauli-group measurements, shot budget for a target gradient-noise level).
Our C4 comparison shows analytical $\gg$ numerical in \emph{accuracy per VQE run}, but not in
\emph{shots per Ha/\AA{} of gradient precision on hardware}. On real NISQ hardware, the analytical
approach's advantage could be smaller (or larger --- unclear from our data).
\item \textbf{Noiseless comparison overstates the analytical advantage's mechanism, but not its
existence.} The paper's strongest statement --- analytical $\gg$ numerical \emph{under shot noise} ---
we cannot test on \texttt{default.qubit}. However, our numerical-difference approach still fails on
the noiseless simulator, because the true $\Delta E$ at $r=0.735$~\AA{} is $\sim 10^{-6}$~Ha, below
VQE convergence noise $\sim 5\times10^{-5}$~Ha. So the failure mode we observe (finite-step vs
VQE-convergence tension) is closely related to but not identical to the paper's motivating regime
(finite-step vs shot noise). Both flavors of noise dominate the gradient signal; ours is the
easier-to-simulate stand-in.
\item \textbf{Only one molecule, one geometry, one basis, one ansatz.} We tested exactly the paper's
own test point. This is by design (replication, not generalization), but it means the replication
does not certify robustness to (a) other molecules (LiH, BeH$_2$, H$_2$O), (b) larger basis sets,
(c) non-equilibrium geometries where the analytical formula's Hellmann--Feynman simplification is
weaker because the VQE state is a less faithful eigenstate approximation, or (d) other ansatz
families (UCCSD, hardware-efficient with different topologies).
\item \textbf{Ansatz variant.} We used $R_x R_y$ per wire per layer instead of the paper's precise
single-parameter rotation choice in Fig.~3; this is a small variant used because it works with
PennyLane's parameter-shift out of the box, and it still reaches FCI to $5.8\times10^{-5}$~Ha. But
it is not bit-for-bit the paper's ansatz.
\item \textbf{No comparison against classical analytical gradients (PySCF energy derivatives).}
For H$_2$/STO-3G, PySCF can compute the analytical HF/FCI energy gradient directly. We compared to
a full-diag finite-difference "exact" reference, not to a classical \emph{analytical} gradient. The
two should agree to numerical precision, but the direct cross-check was not performed.
\end{itemize}

The core claim --- analytical VQE derivatives reproduce the exact answer to $2.9\times10^{-5}$~Ha/\AA{}
on the paper's own test system --- is nonetheless directly demonstrated, and the qualitative
advantage over the naive numerical approach ($\sim 330\times$ tighter here) is unambiguous.
