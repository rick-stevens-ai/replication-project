\section{Paper}
Andrew M.\ Childs, Dmitri Maslov, Yunseong Nam, Neil J.\ Ross, Yuan Su,
\textbf{``Toward the first quantum simulation with quantum speedup,''}
\emph{Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences} \textbf{115}(38),
9456--9461 (2018). DOI \texttt{10.1073/pnas.1801723115}; preprint
arXiv:1711.10980.

\section{Paper Claims (Targets of Replication)}
The paper synthesizes fault-tolerant circuits for three leading Hamiltonian
simulation algorithms---product formulas (PF), Taylor-series / LCU, and
quantum signal processing (QSP)---applied to spin systems, and reports
resource (T-count, qubit) estimates for 50--100 spin instances. Two
components of the paper are directly reproducible in a laptop classical
simulation:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Claim A --- Empirical error scaling of product formulas.} For
  an order-$p$ Suzuki--Trotter product formula with $r$ steps over time $t$,
  the empirical error scales as $O((t/r)^{p+1}\cdot r)=O(r^{-p})$. Hence on
  a log--log plot of error vs $r$, the slope should be $-1$ (PF1), $-2$
  (PF2), $-4$ (PF4).
  \item \textbf{Claim B --- Empirical error $\ll$ analytical worst-case
  bound.} A-priori (commutator) Trotter bounds substantially over-estimate
  actual error; this motivates the paper's use of empirical estimates for
  resource counting.
\end{itemize}

The benchmark Hamiltonian is a nearest-neighbor Heisenberg chain with
random on-site $z$-field:
\[
H=\sum_j\left(X_jX_{j+1}+Y_jY_{j+1}+Z_jZ_{j+1}\right)+\sum_j h_j Z_j,
\qquad h_j\sim U[-h,h].
\]

\section{What Was Actually Executed}
Pure \texttt{numpy}/\texttt{scipy} operator-level simulation on the full
$2^n\times 2^n$ Hilbert space.

\begin{itemize}
  \item System: $n=6$ spins (dim $64$), total time $t=1.0$, seed 20260702,
  $\|H\|_2=10.77$, 11 Hamiltonian terms.
  \item Exact evolution: $U_{\text{exact}}=e^{-iHt}$ via \texttt{scipy.linalg.expm}.
  \item Implemented: PF1 (Lie--Trotter), PF2 (symmetric Strang), PF4
  (Suzuki 4th-order fractal with $u=1/(4-4^{1/3})$,
  $S_4=S_2(u)^2\,S_2(1-4u)\,S_2(u)^2$).
  \item Metric: spectral-norm operator error $\|U_{\text{exact}}-U_{\text{PF}}\|_2$
  vs $r\in\{1,\dots,128\}$.
  \item Additionally: PF1 empirical error vs first-order commutator bound
  $(t^2/2r)\sum_{i<j}\|[H_i,H_j]\|_2$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Honest Critique (Failure Analysis)}

\subsection*{What was genuinely reproduced}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Fitted log--log slopes $-0.97/-1.98/-4.20$ vs theoretical
  $-1/-2/-4$ --- essentially exact.
  \item Per-doubling error reductions $2\times/4\times/16\times$ --- essentially exact.
  \item Bound-vs-empirical ${\sim}4\times$ asymptotic gap --- qualitatively
  supports Claim B, though for a tight first-order commutator bound (not the
  looser published bounds the paper explicitly critiques).
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{What was NOT reproduced (honest gaps)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The paper's headline is not the scaling exponents} --- those
  are textbook. The headline is fault-tolerant T-count / qubit resource
  estimates for 50--100 spin Heisenberg instances, and a claimed
  \emph{quantum speedup} vs best classical simulation of the same
  Hamiltonian. \textbf{Neither the headline resource tables nor the speedup
  claim was independently reproduced.}
  \item No comparison against standard Trotter-1/2/4 baseline on the
  \emph{specific} sizes and error targets the paper uses ($n=50,100$);
  the replication uses $n=6$.
  \item The paper's ``nearly-linear-in-$t$'' resource scaling and the
  quantitative speedup vs classical simulation were not verified at
  accessible sizes because the toolchain gap (fault-tolerant Clifford+T
  synthesis + tightened commutator-bound derivations) was not bridged.
  \item Only PF1 was compared against the analytic commutator bound; PF2
  and PF4 empirical-vs-bound gaps (which the paper highlights as much
  larger) were not measured.
  \item LCU / Taylor-series and QSP algorithms were not implemented at all.
  Cross-algorithm comparison --- one of the paper's central contributions
  --- is entirely absent.
  \item The $n=6$ system size is far below the classically-hard regime
  ($n\gtrsim 40$--$50$) where the speedup argument is quantitatively meaningful.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Reproducibility blockers}
\begin{itemize}
  \item No released reference implementation of the paper's compiler /
  bound derivations.
  \item Fault-tolerant synthesis requires a Clifford+T optimizer
  (Meet-in-the-middle, gridsynth, etc.); no such toolchain was set up here.
  \item QSP phase-angle computation for the paper's specific signals is
  numerically delicate and requires the paper's own scripts (not released).
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Honest verdict framing}
The replication cleanly reproduces the \emph{scaling laws} that the paper
uses as an \emph{input assumption} for its resource estimates. It does
\emph{not} reproduce the \emph{output} of the paper (the resource tables
and the speedup claim). The queue verdict ``REPLICATED'' is defensible
only under the reading that the empirically-scoped simulation claims
(A and B) are the target, not the marquee resource-estimation
contribution. A stricter reading would grade this PARTIAL. This report
flags that distinction explicitly.
