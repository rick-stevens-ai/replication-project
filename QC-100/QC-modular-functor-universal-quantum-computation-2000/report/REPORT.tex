\section{Paper summary}

Freedman--Kitaev--Larsen--Wang (\FKW) define a computational model, \emph{CS5}, based on the SU(2) Chern--Simons modular functor at the fifth root of unity $\qexp$. The label set is $\{0,1,2,3\}$. Qubits are embedded in the Hilbert space $V^0(D^2, 3k)$ of a disk with $3k$ marked points labeled 1 and boundary label 0; the mapping-class-group action of the braid group $B_{3k}$ approximates arbitrary poly-local unitaries acting on the qubits. The paper's main results are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Theorem 2.1.} Quantitative approximation: for every $g \in U(4)$ there is a braid $b_l$ of length $\le C/\varepsilon^2$ such that $\|\omega_0 \rho_0(b_l) - g \oplus \mathrm{id}_1\| + \|\omega_2 \rho_2(b_l) - g \oplus \mathrm{id}_4\| \le \varepsilon$.
  \item \textbf{Theorems 2.2, 2.3.} CS5 (with label + $\sigma_z$ measurements and fresh ancillae) efficiently and fault-tolerantly simulates BQP; a strictly unitary variant ECS5 does the same.
  \item \textbf{Theorem 3.1 (iv).} For $\lambda = [4,2]$, $n = 6$: every $\rho_{\lambda,\beta,6}(\sigma_i)$ has eigenvalues $\{-1, q\}$ with multiplicities $3$ and $5$.
  \item \textbf{Theorem 3.2.} Modular-functor braid representations $\rho_r$ coincide with irreducible sectors of the Jones representation on the corresponding $(2,r)$-Young diagrams.
  \item \textbf{Theorem 4.1 (density).} The closure of the image of $\rho_{[3,3]} \oplus \rho_{[4,2]}: B_6 \to U(5) \times U(8)$ contains $\mathrm{SU}(5) \times \mathrm{SU}(8)$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Numerical construction}

The core file \texttt{work/fkw\_replication.py} (415 LOC) implements, from scratch:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Admissibility rules (Eq.~3 of the paper): $a + b + c$ even, triangle inequalities, $a + b + c \le 2(r - 2)$.
  \item Recursive fusion to compute $\dim V_n^\ell = \sum_{\ell'} \dim V_{n-1}^{\ell'} \cdot [\text{admissible}(\ell', 1, \ell)]$ with base $\dim V_0^0 = 1$.
  \item Enumeration of standard tableaux of $(2,r)$-Young diagrams with the paper's inductive-admissibility condition (delete $n, n-1, \dots$ and check that every intermediate shape is a $(2,r)$-diagram).
  \item Temperley--Lieb generators $e_i$ from Eq.~(13)--(14) using
    $\alpha_{t,i} = \frac{[d_{t,i} + 1]}{[2] [d_{t,i}]}, \quad
     [k] = \frac{\sin(\pi k / r)}{\sin(\pi / r)}$
    with $d_{t,i} = c_1 - c_2 - (r_1 - r_2)$ (columns/rows of $i, i+1$ in $t$).
  \item Braid generators $\rho(\sigma_i) = q - (1 + q) e_i$ (Eq.~15).
  \item Verification of $C_1$--$C_7$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Per-claim what-worked / what-didn't}

\subsection*{C1 --- Modular-functor dimensions}
Worked cleanly. The paper's Eq.~(4) values were reproduced exactly by the recursive fusion, using only the admissibility rules (3). This validates our understanding of the admissibility rules and gluing axiom implementation.

\subsection*{C2 --- Unitarity of Jones-representation braid generators}
Worked at machine precision. The maximum deviation $\|U^\dagger U - I\|$ across all $\sigma_i$ generators for $\lambda \in \{[2,1], [3,3], [4,2]\}$ was $\le 1.05 \times 10^{-15}$.

\subsection*{C3 --- Braid + Temperley--Lieb relations}
Worked at machine precision. Every $\sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i = \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1}$, every $[\sigma_i, \sigma_j] = 0$ for $|i - j| \ge 2$, and every $e_i^2 = e_i$, $e_i e_{i+1} e_i = \beta^{-1} e_i$ was verified with residual $\le 10^{-15}$.

\subsection*{C4, C5 --- Spectrum and multiplicities}
Worked. Every $\rho(\sigma_i)$ has spectrum $\{-1, q\}$ with no stray eigenvalues in any of the three sectors. For $\lambda = [4,2]$, all five generators $\sigma_1, \ldots, \sigma_5$ have multiplicities exactly $(3, 5)$, matching Theorem 3.1(iv).

\subsection*{C6 --- Density}
Partially verified numerically, as the theorem itself is not fully numerical.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Diagnostic (a).} 2\,000 uniformly-random braid words of length 30 through $\rho_{[2,1]}$ yielded 261 distinct SU(2)-traces. A finite-subgroup image would give far fewer (typically $< 30$).
  \item \textbf{Diagnostic (b).} BFS approximation of the Hadamard gate through $B_3$ shows Frobenius distance decreasing from 2.00 (length 0) to 0.10 (length 11) --- a factor-of-20 improvement in 11 braid moves --- although the search was capped at 400\,000 states which caused oscillation at depths $\ge 12$.
  \item \textbf{Diagnostic (c).} 50\,000 random-braid samples produced a distribution of $\mathrm{tr}(U)/2$ within RMS $\approx 0.08$ of the SU(2)-Haar density $(2/\pi)\sqrt{1 - x^2}$. This is not full equidistribution but is consistent with the uniform-word measure being biased away from Haar for finite lengths.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{C7 --- Paper's printed $\rho_{[2,1]}(\sigma_2)$}
\textbf{Failed as printed.} Both natural readings of the printed 2\,$\times$\,2 matrix,
\[
A = \begin{pmatrix} q^2/(q+1) & -q\sqrt{[3]}/(q+1) \\ -q\sqrt{[3]}/(q+1) & 1/(q+1) \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
B = \begin{pmatrix} q^2/(q+1) & \phantom{-}q\sqrt{[3]}/(q+1) \\ -q\sqrt{[3]}/(q+1) & -1/(q+1) \end{pmatrix},
\]
are \emph{not} unitary. However, the paper's general formula (13)--(15), independently constructed, yields a $\rho_{[2,1]}(\sigma_2)$ that \emph{is} unitary with spectrum $\{-1, q\}$. This looks like an OCR/typesetting artifact in the printed matrix rather than a substantive error --- but a copy-paste of the printed matrix would give the wrong answer. See Open Question Q2.

\section{Verdict + justification}

\textbf{Verdict:} \textbf{REPLICATED}. Independent LLM judge (Argo \texttt{argo:gpt-5.1}, $T = 0$): coverage 90\,\%, agreement 98\,\%, one-line summary ``Core modular-functor dimensions, braid representations, and empirical density all independently reproduced.''

Every numerical/algebraic claim the paper makes explicitly ($C_1$--$C_5$) is reproduced from first principles at machine precision. The density theorem (Thm~4.1) is a mathematical theorem, but its two numerical ingredients (image not stuck in a finite subgroup; gate approximation improves with word length) are satisfied. The one place the replication disagrees with the paper --- the explicit printed matrix in Section 3 --- appears to be a typesetting artifact rather than a mathematical error.

We do \emph{not} claim to have reproduced Theorems 2.2 or 2.3 (BQP simulation); those are polynomial-reduction theorems whose ingredients (Solovay--Kitaev, Aharonov--Ben-Or) are cited rather than proven in the paper. Attempting them would require weeks of engineering and is far outside the wave-replication budget.

\section{Open Questions}

\paragraph{Q1.} Our brute-force BFS for approximating the Hadamard gate through $B_3$ in the $\rho_{[2,1]}$ sector plateaus around Frobenius distance $\sim 0.10$ by length $\sim 11$ and does not improve monotonically after that. Is this an artifact of the BFS-with-pruning heuristic, or does the $\rho_{[2,1]}$ image have a systematic ``spectral gap'' that limits what short braids can approximate --- as opposed to the \emph{closure} being dense?

\paragraph{Q2.} The paper's printed $\rho_{[2,1]}(\sigma_2)$ matrix (Section~3) is not unitary under either natural literal reading of the typography. Which specific fix (sign flip, $\sqrt{[3]}$ misprint, or a hidden basis-conjugation convention) recovers unitarity, and is the same typo present in the arXiv v1, arXiv v2, and the later published Bulletin AMS version?

\paragraph{Q3.} The random-braid trace distribution in $\rho_{[2,1]}$ shows RMS deviation $\sim 0.08$ from the SU(2)/center Haar density even at 50\,000 samples with braid length uniform in $[20, 60]$. Is this a fixed-length equidistribution artifact, or evidence that the natural uniform-word probability measure on $B_3$ does not converge to Haar measure in this representation?

\paragraph{Q4.} The paper embeds $k$ qubits into $V^0_{3k}$, whose dimension grows as a Fibonacci-related recurrence rather than $2^k$. What is the actual overhead ratio $\dim V^0_{3k}/2^k$ for small $k$ ($k = 1, 2, \ldots, 10$), and how does the resulting ``qubit-smearing error probability'' per gate compare to the $O(\varepsilon^2)$ bound the paper cites for those $k$?

\paragraph{Q5.} The Aharonov--Ben-Or fault-tolerance threshold cited in the paper ($\varepsilon < 10^{-6}$) predates modern surface-code and concatenated-code threshold analyses by $\sim 15$ years. Is CS5-with-braid-approximation-error still fault-tolerant under contemporary threshold analyses (Aliferis--Gottesman 2005; Aharonov--Kitaev--Preskill 2006), or does the ``systematic-not-random'' nature of Solovay--Kitaev residual errors push the effective threshold materially lower for this specific topological model?
