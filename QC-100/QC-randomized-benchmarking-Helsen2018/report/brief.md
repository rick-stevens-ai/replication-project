# Brief

**Paper:** Helsen, Xue, Vandersypen, Wehner — *A new class of efficient randomized benchmarking protocols* (arXiv:1806.02048, npj Quantum Information 5, 71 (2019)).

**What/why:** The paper gives a representation-theory framework for randomized benchmarking (RB) that (a) recovers the textbook single-exponential Clifford-RB decay and (b) shows that for gatesets that are *not* the full Clifford group the survival probability is a *sum* of exponentials, one decay rate per irreducible subrepresentation of the gateset's Pauli-transfer-matrix (PTM) representation (their Eq. 2, generalizing the standard Eq. 1). I independently re-implemented the RB core in the pure-numpy PTM/Liouville picture (single-qubit Clifford group and Pauli group generated from scratch, exact + Monte-Carlo simulation, known-noise channels) and reproduced: (C1) standard Clifford RB recovers the injected average gate fidelity to ~1e-12; (C3) the Clifford PTM rep is multiplicity-free with exactly 2 irreps ⇒ one decay rate; (C2) the Pauli group (4 irreps) under anisotropic noise produces a genuine multi-exponential decay where a single-exponential fit is badly biased (SS 2e-3) but a double-exponential fit is exact (SS 2.5e-26) and recovers the injected per-axis rates {0.97, 0.85} exactly — the paper's central Eq.-2 claim.
