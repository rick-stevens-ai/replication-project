\section{Target}
Lund, Salimath \& Hals, arXiv:2106.15187v2 (2021). \textbf{Headline claim:}
\emph{The three spin-wave bands of a kagome antiferromagnet pump ac spin
currents with mutually orthogonal spin polarizations.} Method class:
analytic effective-action / linear spin-wave theory (LSWT). Compute target:
CPU. We built two from-scratch calculations.

\section{Physics reproduced}
\subsection{(A) LSWT of the 120$^\circ$ kagome Heisenberg AFM}
$H = J\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\mathbf S_i\cdot\mathbf S_j$, $J>0$, 3
sublattices with 120$^\circ$ in-plane order. Holstein--Primakoff bosons in
local frames, quadratic Bogoliubov--de-Gennes Hamiltonian, Colpa
diagonalization (eigenvalues of $gM$) on a coarse $12\times12$ Brillouin-zone
grid. Result: three magnon bands, the lowest of which is a
\textbf{zero-energy flat band} (mean $1.5\times10^{-8}$, std
$1.5\times10^{-8}$ in units of $JS$), the well-known kagome-AFM weathervane
degeneracy, plus two dispersive bands topping at $\sim2.12\,JS$.

\subsection{(B) The paper's k=0 uniform-precession modes}
Using the App.~A constants ($a=S=\hbar=1$, $J=1$, $K=0.10$, $K_z=0.05$):
\[
a_1=\tfrac{24\hbar S}{\sqrt3 a^2},\;
a_2=\tfrac{36S^2J}{\sqrt3 a^2},\;
K_1=\tfrac{8\sqrt3(K_z+K)S^2}{a^3},\;
K_2=\tfrac{16\sqrt3 K S^2}{a^3},
\]
the linearized equation $a_1^2\ddot{\mathbf r}+4a_2\mathbf K\cdot\mathbf r=0$
with $\mathbf K=\mathrm{diag}(K_1,K_1,K_2)$ gives $\omega^2=4a_2\mathbf
K/a_1^2$. The three normal modes are polarized \textbf{exactly along
$x,y,z$} (eigenvectors of a diagonal matrix), with orthogonality error
$0.0$.

\section{(C) Full $k$-resolved bands and polarization purity}
\label{sec:kresolved}
The paper's orthogonal-polarization statement is made at $k=0$ (uniform
precession, Fig.~2b). To test its \emph{finite-$k$} robustness --- the
regime a real driven film actually explores --- we diagonalized the
Bogoliubov--de-Gennes problem on a full $24\times24$ Brillouin-zone grid
(Colpa/para-diagonalization, eigenvectors normalized under the metric
$g=\mathrm{diag}(\mathbb 1_3,-\mathbb 1_3)$) and projected each magnon
eigenvector onto the lab frame. For a Bogoliubov mode
$a_i=u_i b+v_i^* b^\dagger$ the transverse fluctuation on sublattice $i$ has
an in-plane amplitude $(u_i+v_i)\,\hat e^{x}_i$ and an out-of-plane amplitude
$-i(u_i-v_i)\,\hat e^{y}_i$ (with $\hat e^{y}_i=-\hat z$ for all three
sublattices, since the $120^\circ$ order lies in the $xy$ plane; verified
numerically: $\max_i|\hat e^x_i\!\cdot\!\hat z|=0$,
$\min_i|\hat e^y_i\!\cdot\!\hat z|=1$). Summing over sublattices gives the
lab-frame polarization $\mathbf P=\sum_i[(u_i+v_i)\hat e^x_i-i(u_i-v_i)\hat
e^y_i]$; the \emph{purity} is $\max_{\alpha}|P_\alpha|^2/|\mathbf P|^2$.

A small easy-axis anisotropy ($K_{\rm ani}=0.05\,JS$) is added here to lift
the accidental zero-energy flat band and give the three bands well-defined
energies. Results:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Three bands with distinct character survive across the BZ.}
The lowest band becomes a \emph{finite-energy flat band} at
$0.557\,JS$ (energy range $[0.557,0.557]$, i.e.\ still dispersionless), and
two dispersive bands span $[0.557,2.227]$ and $[0.781,2.227]\,JS$.
\item \textbf{Polarization is pure at $\Gamma$ and degrades smoothly.} At
$k=0$ the three modes are polarized along $\hat y,\hat x,\hat z$ with purity
$\{0.994,1.000,1.000\}$ --- exactly the paper's $x/y/z$ picture. Averaged
over the whole BZ the mean single-axis purity is $0.79$ (min $0.35$ at
zone-boundary band-touching points), and the out-of-plane ($z$) band retains
$\gtrsim0.98$ $z$-weight near $\Gamma$ and at the zone corners.
\item \textbf{Purity vs $|k|$} (6 radial bins) stays in the range
$0.69$--$0.87$; it dips only where the two in-plane bands cross, then
recovers. This \emph{quantifies} the finite-$k$ envelope contamination of
the pumped-current polarization that the $k=0$ theory cannot address.
\end{itemize}

\section{(D) Berry curvature and magnon thermal Hall}
We evaluated the magnon Berry curvature by the Fukui--Hatsugai--Suzuki
plaquette method (link variables built with the BdG $g$-metric inner
product) on the same grid, and the thermal Hall conductivity
$\kappa_{xy}\propto-\sum_{k,n}c_2(\rho_n)\Omega_n$ with the standard
$c_2$ weight. \textbf{Result: the intrinsic magnon thermal Hall vanishes.}
This is required by symmetry: lund2021's Hamiltonian has only Heisenberg
exchange plus collinear-in-plane $120^\circ$ order and easy-axis anisotropy
--- \emph{no} Dzyaloshinskii--Moriya interaction and no scalar spin
chirality --- so an effective time-reversal forces $\Omega_n\equiv0$.
Numerically the two in-plane bands are degenerate/cross along symmetry lines
(min direct gap $4.4\times10^{-16}$), which makes the single-band FHS flux
gauge-ill-defined (it saturates at $|F|=\pi$ on $\sim1\%$ of plaquettes ---
a branch-cut artifact, reported transparently as \texttt{raw\_*} fields, not
physical curvature). A well-defined nonzero magnon thermal Hall would
require adding an out-of-plane DMI term $D_z$; this bounds the topological
consequence of the band structure for the paper's model as \emph{zero}.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED.} The central, checkable physics of the kagome
application (three modes, orthogonal $x/y/z$ polarizations, resonance-frequency
formulas) is reproduced from scratch and matches the paper analytically and
numerically. This is now backed by the \emph{full $k$-resolved} band picture
(Sec.~C): the three bands retain distinct, dominantly single-axis character
across a $24\times24$ BZ (mean purity $0.79$, $0.99$--$1.00$ at $\Gamma$),
directly generalizing the paper's $k=0$ statement and quantifying its
finite-$k$ robustness. The band topology is bounded (Sec.~D): the DMI-free
model carries zero magnon Berry curvature / thermal Hall by symmetry. The
full spin-pumping/ISHE transport chain (Eqs.~17--18) remains analytic and is
verified structurally, not integrated numerically end-to-end.
