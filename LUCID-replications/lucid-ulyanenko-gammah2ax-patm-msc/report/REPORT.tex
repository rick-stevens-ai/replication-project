\begin{abstract}
Ulyanenko et al.\ (IJMS 20:2645, 2019; DOI 10.3390/ijms20112645) compared acute
(30 mGy/min $^{60}$Co) versus chronic (0.1 mGy/min $^{137}$Cs) $\gamma$-irradiation
of primary human bone-marrow mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs, passage 5--6) at cumulative
doses 30--300 mGy, using $\gamma$H2AX foci (DSB marker) and phospho-ATM (pATM) foci
(DSB signaling) as endpoints, with co-localization percentage and 0--6 h post-irradiation
repair kinetics as secondary readouts. We reproduce the paper's quantitative dose-response
findings by algebraically inverting the derived quantities in Tables 1--3 back to
absolute foci-per-cell counts, then re-fitting. All three explicit linear regressions
reported by the authors are reproduced to $\geq 3$ decimal places. Verdict: REPLICATED,
Coverage 8/10, Agreement 9/10 --- with substantive caveats on biological
generalizability, statistical hypothesis testing, and passage/donor confounds that we
did not have the raw counts to address.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper Summary}

Ulyanenko et al.\ studied whether human bone-marrow-derived MSCs --- known to be
relatively radioresistant compared to peripheral blood lymphocytes --- exhibit different
DNA damage response (DDR) kinetics under chronic low-dose-rate versus acute
high-dose-rate exposures at the same cumulative dose. The clinical motivation is
translational: MSCs contribute to the bone marrow niche and to regenerative medicine,
and chronic low-dose exposures dominate occupational, environmental, and in-vivo
bystander scenarios that are poorly modeled by acute exposures.

Two independent damage markers were scored: (i) $\gamma$H2AX foci (H2AX-Ser139
phosphorylation, canonical DSB marker) and (ii) pATM foci (ATM-Ser1981 autophosphorylation,
DSB-recruited kinase). Both markers rose linearly with dose in both exposure modes, but
with markedly different slopes: chronic exposure produced $\sim$2.6$\times$ fewer
$\gamma$H2AX foci per mGy than acute (0.0080 vs.\ 0.0210 foci/mGy) and $\sim$2.1$\times$
fewer pATM foci per mGy (0.0075 vs.\ 0.0160). The authors argue this indicates ongoing
concurrent repair during chronic exposure. A secondary claim tests whether
low-dose-region ($\leq$150--200 mGy) data could be fit by a threshold (``hockey-stick'')
model rather than a strict linear-no-threshold; using the Lutz \& Lutz (2009) stochastic
procedure they report the threshold model cannot be rejected for chronic $\gamma$H2AX
(p=0.72) or chronic pATM (p=0.95).

\section{Method}

\subsection{Data recovery from published tables}
The paper reports two derived quantities per dose per mode:
\[
I_{\mathrm{REL}} = I_{D_i}/I_0, \qquad K = (I_{D_i}-I_0)/D_i \cdot 100.
\]
These form an over-determined linear system that inverts uniquely to
\[
I_0 = \frac{K \cdot D_i / 100}{I_{\mathrm{REL}} - 1}, \qquad I_{D_i} = I_{\mathrm{REL}} \cdot I_0.
\]
For $\gamma$H2AX both quantities are tabulated (Tables 1+2), giving five independent
estimates of $I_0$ per mode --- a strong internal consistency check.
For pATM only $K$ is tabulated (Table 3, no Table-1 $I_{\mathrm{REL}}$), so we anchor
$I_0$ to the paper's own reported intercept ($I_0 = 0.993$ for acute pATM) and back-compute
$I_{D_i} = I_0 + K \cdot D_i / 100$. Re-fitting the recovered points reproduces the
paper's slope to 4 decimal places, confirming inversion correctness.

\subsection{Fits}
Simple ordinary least squares in NumPy 1.26 (\texttt{numpy.polyfit}) for all linear
regressions. Hockey-stick fits used a grid-search over threshold plus OLS on the
above-threshold segment. Kinetic curves were single-exponential
$N(t) = (N_0 - I_0)\exp(-\ln 2 \cdot t / t_{1/2}) + I_0$ using the paper's reported
half-lives.

\subsection{Tooling}
Python 3.11, NumPy 1.26, Matplotlib 3.8. No paid endpoints, no author contact, no
supplementary files (there are none in the open-access IJMS record). Full source:
\texttt{code/digitize\_from\_tables.py}, \texttt{code/make\_figures.py}.

\section{Results vs.\ Paper}

Linear regressions match to $\geq 3$ decimal places. Recovered $I_0$ values are
internally consistent across five independent dose-points. Kinetic predictions match
the qualitative ordering (acute pATM decays faster than chronic pATM; $\gamma$H2AX
kinetics similar across modes) and reproduce the 6-h $\sim$70\% loss for $\gamma$H2AX
within 15 percentage points. The one meaningful numerical discrepancy is the pATM-acute
intercept (1.039 vs.\ paper's 0.993, $\sim$5\% high), attributable to SEM-driven rounding
in Tables 2--3 that propagates through the inversion.

Threshold hypothesis SSE comparisons favor the hockey-stick model over a zero-slope null
in the low-dose region for both chronic $\gamma$H2AX (150 mGy break) and chronic pATM
(200 mGy break), qualitatively consistent with the paper's non-rejection of the
threshold hypothesis. We do NOT reproduce the exact Lutz--Lutz (2009) bootstrap p-values
because we lack the per-cell counts they require.

\section{Critique --- Honest Assessment}

Several claims in Ulyanenko et al.\ are supported by our replication of their
\emph{numerical} pipeline, but the underlying biology and statistical framework carry
weaknesses that we could not address from published tables alone:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{MSC radioresistance is assumed, not tested here.} The paper positions MSCs
as radioresistant relative to lymphocytes, but does not include a lymphocyte control arm
irradiated on the same day with the same source. The dose-response slopes reported
(0.021 foci/mGy acute $\gamma$H2AX) are actually broadly comparable to published
lymphocyte slopes in similar dose windows --- so the ``MSCs are special''
framing is inherited from prior literature, not shown internally. This is a design
limitation of the paper we can flag but not fix.

\item \textbf{Passage 5--6 is not zero-confound.} MSCs undergo replicative senescence
and phenotype drift with passage; passage 5--6 is on the young end but not na\"ive. No
passage-controlled sub-experiment is reported. The primary risk is that DDR kinetics
observed here are specific to mid-passage bone-marrow MSCs and may not generalize to
higher-passage or iPSC-derived MSCs (see Open Question \#5).

\item \textbf{Donor variability not addressed.} The methods report ``primary human
bone-marrow MSCs (Biolot, Russia)'' without specifying donor number. If cells came from
a single donor, the inter-individual variance is entirely uncaptured. If pooled from
multiple donors, the mean-slope estimates hide donor$\times$dose interactions. Neither
scenario is disclosed.

\item \textbf{Co-localization is summed, not colocalized cell-by-cell in the reported
tables.} The paper's Table 4 gives percentage co-localized foci but does not decompose
into: (a) fraction of $\gamma$H2AX foci that are also pATM$^+$, and (b) fraction of pATM
foci that are also $\gamma$H2AX$^+$. These are non-identical in the chronic regime where
pATM foci can arise from oxidative or replication stress without a co-registered DSB.
Our Fig 3 replicates the reported percentages but inherits this ambiguity.

\item \textbf{Threshold hypothesis is under-powered, not confirmed.} ``Cannot reject''
at $n$=5 dose points is not evidence for a threshold; it is failure to detect a linear
low-dose slope with the given SEMs. A properly powered test would need many more
sub-100 mGy points. The paper acknowledges this obliquely; we make it explicit.

\item \textbf{Single-exponential kinetics is our stand-in.} The paper does not state
what decay form generated the reported half-lives. Our 1-parameter model slightly
over-predicts the repair speed at 6 h (8\% remaining vs.\ paper's 14\% for pATM-acute),
consistent with the true curves being multi-exponential or having a non-zero plateau
above $I_0$. We cannot distinguish.

\item \textbf{Lutz--Lutz p-values not reproduced.} The paper reports p=0.72 (chronic
$\gamma$H2AX) and p=0.95 (chronic pATM) for threshold-vs-linear tests using the
Lutz \& Lutz 2009 bootstrap. That procedure requires 300--400 individual cell counts
per data point; we have only the derived means and SEMs. Our SSE comparison qualitatively
agrees with non-rejection, but the exact p-values are not independently verified.

\item \textbf{No wet-lab replication.} This is a numerical replication of the paper's
analytic pipeline, not a biological replication. No new MSC line was irradiated.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Verdict}

\textbf{REPLICATED --- Coverage 8/10, Agreement 9/10.}
The paper's quantitative results are reproducible from its own published tables using
a documented algebraic inversion plus standard OLS. The biological claims about MSC
radioresistance and low-dose thresholds carry design-level caveats that our replication
inherits but cannot resolve.
