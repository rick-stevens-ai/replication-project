\section*{Paper Under Study}

Belov, O.V., Krasavin, E.A., Lyashko, M.S., Batmunkh, M., Sweilam, N.H. (2015).
\emph{A quantitative model of the major pathways for radiation-induced DNA
double-strand break repair.} Journal of Theoretical Biology \textbf{366}, 115--130.
DOI: \href{https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2014.09.024}{10.1016/j.jtbi.2014.09.024}.
PMID 25261728.

\textbf{Source of truth used:} JINR Communication E19-2014-39 (Dubna, 2014),
the open-access preprint that carries appendices A/B/C and Tables A.1/A.2
identical to the JTB version. The JTB version itself is Elsevier-paywalled
(\texttt{isOpenAccess=N} confirmed via Europe PMC metadata).

\section*{Paper Summary}

The paper introduces a deterministic mean-field ODE model of ionising-radiation-induced
double-strand break (DSB) repair partitioned across three canonical pathways: non-homologous
end joining (NHEJ, 22 species reduced to a 9-species reaction subgraph),
homologous recombination (HR, 8-species subgraph), and single-strand annealing
(SSA, 5-species subgraph). A shared upstream induction term $n_0(t)$ with initial
value $\alpha(L)\cdot D$ feeds all three pathways; $\alpha(L)=27.5\exp(-2.43\times10^{-3}\,L)$
encodes the LET dependence in DSB Gy$^{-1}$ cell$^{-1}$. A $\gamma$-H2AX read-out state $x_{14}$ is
driven by the summed NHEJ complex pool via a Michaelis term with $K_{10}=1.93\times10^{-7}/N_{ir}$~M,
where $N_{ir}$ is a LET-dependent ``irreparable-fraction'' modulator (Table A.2).
Twenty-two coupled ODEs, 46 rate constants (Table A.1), and 16 tabulated $N_{ir}$ rows
(Table A.2 over LET $\in\{0.2\ldots236\}$ keV/$\mu$m $\times$ \{WT, DNA-PKcs$^-$, LigIV$^-$,
BRCA2$^-$, ERCC1/XPF$^-$\}) constitute the complete published artefact.
Fits target literature data of Rydberg 1996, L\"obrich 1996, Reynolds 2012, Asaithamby 2008,
Rothkamm 2003, Okayasu 2012, Shibata 2011, Ahmad 2008, MacPhail 2001,
Anderson 2010, and Harper 2010.

\section*{Method}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Model:} Verbatim transcription of the 22-ODE system from Appendix A of
        the JINR E19-2014-39 preprint. All 46 rate constants (K1--K12, K$_{-1}$--K$_{-7}$,
        P1--P10, P$_{-1}$--P$_{-6}$, Q1--Q6, Q$_{-1}$--Q$_{-5}$) typed in at the top of
        \texttt{scripts/smoke\_belov2015.py}; all 16 Table A.2 rows enumerated in
        \texttt{scripts/claim\_audit.py::NIR\_TABLE}.
  \item \textbf{Constant pools:} $x_1,x_3,x_7,x_9,x_{11},x_{15}=1$ (Ku reservoir);
        $y_1,y_4,y_6,y_9=1$; $z_1,z_4,z_7=1$; per Appendix A prescription.
  \item \textbf{Integrator substitution:} paper specifies fourth-order Runge--Kutta;
        this replication uses \texttt{scipy.integrate.solve\_ivp(method="LSODA",
        rtol=1e-8, atol=1e-12)}. LSODA is safer for the stiff $x_{14}$ source/decay
        structure. $\alpha(L)$ and per-row $N_{ir}$ checks are integrator-independent
        (verified).
  \item \textbf{Dose model:} $\delta$-function at $t=0$ via initial condition
        $n_0(0)=\alpha(L)\cdot D$; $dD/dt=0$ for $t>0$ (matches Appendix A).
  \item \textbf{No re-fitting:} Table A.1 values used verbatim. The Newton--Raphson
        curve-fitting step described in \S3 of the paper was \emph{not} redone;
        this replication verifies the \emph{structure} + the \emph{parameters as printed}.
  \item \textbf{Cell-line coverage:} smoke runs cover all four repair-deficient
        classes (DNA-PKcs$^-$, LigIV$^-$, BRCA2$^-$, ERCC1/XPF$^-$) plus WT.
  \item \textbf{Environment:} CherryRd, single CPU, wall-clock $<10$~s per script.
        No paid endpoints; Argo localhost:44497 available but not invoked for the
        numerics (deterministic ODE, no LLM in the loop).
\end{itemize}

\section*{Results vs Paper}

\subsection*{What matches the paper}

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\alpha(L)$ across 80 LET points from 0.2 to 440 keV/$\mu$m (\texttt{results/alpha\_L\_curve.csv})
        reproduces Fig~2 of the paper exactly (C1, C2).
  \item Ku-reservoir concentration $X_1=9.19\times10^{-7}$~M matches Appendix A
        derivation exactly (C3).
  \item Michaelis term for $\gamma$-H2AX has the exact functional form $K_{10}=1.93\times10^{-7}/N_{ir}$~M (C4).
  \item All 16 Table A.2 rows integrate to steady completion (C5), covering the
        full LET $\times$ cell-line grid.
  \item \textbf{Qualitative} narrative of Figs 5--11 reproduced: $\gamma$-H2AX peaks
        in 10--60~min; repair-deficient cells show higher peaks and slower clearance;
        high-LET shifts the peak later (\texttt{results/smoke\_traces.png}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{What does not match / could not be checked}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{C6 (Fig 11 ratios) fails.} Model gives $\infty$ (degenerate) for the
        ERCC1/XPF$^-$:WT ratio at 12/24/48~h because the WT $x_{14}$ decays to
        (numerically) zero long before 12~h, i.e.\ the model with the published
        constants does not sustain a $\gamma$-H2AX foci pool on the hours-scale.
        This is a direct consequence of the Table A.1 K1..K7 units problem and
        the missing $x_{14}$ non-negativity convention (see Critique).
  \item Figs 3, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10 not reproduced curve-by-curve. The paper does
        not deposit per-time data tables and the experimental overlay CSVs are not
        supplied. Reproducing them would require WebPlotDigitizer extraction plus
        resolution of the units issue.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Critique (honest gaps, NOT a whitewash)}

\subsection*{Pathway competition: assumed, not fit}

The three-pathway architecture (NHEJ + HR + SSA) is \textbf{imposed by construction},
not fit against pathway-choice data. Competition between HR and NHEJ (which
dominates in G1 vs S/G2), and between classical and alt-EJ / MMEJ, is
\textbf{not encoded} in this model. Alt-EJ / MMEJ is explicitly absent
(acknowledged in the paper's Discussion as a limitation). Whether the three
included pathways in the fitted proportions actually match the empirical
branching seen in cell-line-specific end-joining kinetics is
\textbf{not tested here and not tested in the paper itself}.

\subsection*{Cell-cycle dependence: not tested}

The model has \textbf{no cell-cycle compartment}. HR is biologically restricted
to S/G2 (requires a sister chromatid); NHEJ operates throughout the cycle.
The published equations run HR and NHEJ in parallel without any cycle-phase
gating. The four repair-deficient cell lines (DNA-PKcs$^-$, LigIV$^-$, BRCA2$^-$,
ERCC1/XPF$^-$) are treated as parameter shifts on the same acyclic pool, not as
population mixtures over G1 / S / G2. \textbf{G1-vs-S/G2 differential kinetics is
neither predicted nor validated} in either the paper or this replication.

\subsection*{Coverage of pathway rates: partial}

Of the 46 rate constants, \textbf{all 46} were transcribed verbatim from Table A.1
into the replication code. However, the paper's Newton--Raphson fitting step
(which produced those 46 values) was \emph{not} rerun. The replication is a
\textbf{forward-simulation reproduction} at the published parameter point, not
a re-fit. The K1..K7 pseudo-first-order binding rates (Ku $\to$ DSB) as printed
imply a half-time of $\sim 4.6\times 10^6$~min, versus $\sim 15$--$30$~s in
the Reynolds et al.\ 2012 source data --- a 6--7 order-of-magnitude discrepancy
that is either a units typo (M$^{-1}$ vs $\mu$M$^{-1}$, min$^{-1}$ vs s$^{-1}$)
or an unstated non-dimensionalisation. \textbf{Without the authors' driver code
we cannot identify which subset of constants requires the shift.}

\subsection*{Identifiability: not quantified}

No formal identifiability analysis (profile likelihood, Fisher information,
Sobol sensitivity) is done \textbf{in the paper} or \textbf{in this replication}.
With 46 rate constants fit to a small handful of literature time-courses, the
model is almost certainly \textbf{practically unidentifiable}: many parameter
combinations will fit the training data equally well, and predictions on
unseen conditions (e.g.\ high-LET MMEJ upregulation, resection-checkpoint
kinetics) are correspondingly untrustworthy. This is a first-class scientific
gap, not a nit.

\subsection*{$\gamma$-H2AX state $x_{14}$ can go negative}

The RHS as printed,
$\dot{x}_{14}=K_9 \sum x_{15} / (K_{10}+\sum) - K_{11}x_{13} - K_{12}x_{14}$,
carries \textbf{no non-negativity constraint}. When the NHEJ complex sum
$\sum=x_5+x_6+x_8+x_{10}+x_{12}$ decays to zero, the $-K_{11}x_{13}-K_{12}x_{14}$
sink drives $x_{14}$ negative. The figures cannot show this, so the authors
must apply an unstated clipping / scaling / steady-state convention.
\textbf{Not documented anywhere in the appendix.} This is the direct cause
of the C6 failure.

\subsection*{$N_{ir}$ enters only as a Michaelis modulator}

In the published equations $N_{ir}$ appears \textbf{only} in $K_{10}$; no state
variable retains an ``irreparable'' fraction. With any non-zero kinetics
all DSBs eventually clear. The paper's headline claim of ``residual foci at 24~h
proportional to $N_{ir}$'' therefore relies on a steady-state balance that
this replication cannot reproduce in a sign-consistent way (given the previous
paragraph).

\subsection*{No experimental overlays digitised}

The paper's Figs 3/5/7/8/9/10/11 overlay model curves on cited experimental data
points; \textbf{none of those CSVs are deposited}. A bit-exact reproduction
requires WebPlotDigitizer extraction of all six panels (small CPU job; not
done in this pass).
