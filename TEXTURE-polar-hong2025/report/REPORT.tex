\begin{abstract}
We independently reproduce the phase-field \emph{headline claim} of Gupta
\emph{et al.} (2025): that time-dependent Ginzburg--Landau (TDGL) phase-field
relaxation of the polarization field in a thin STO/PTO/STO trilayer stabilizes
a \emph{pure vortex phase} --- a periodic array of alternating clockwise /
counter-clockwise polar vortices --- as the equilibrium texture arising from
competition among Landau, elastic, electric, and gradient energies. Using a
minimal 2D TDGL model built from scratch (crediting two shared kernels for
method structure), the relaxed polarization spontaneously organizes into a
balanced array of $+21/-21$ topological vortices with a well-defined lateral
periodicity, whereas a control run with the electric (bound-charge) energy
switched off collapses to a nearly uniform domain. All five pre-registered
claim checks pass. \textbf{Verdict: REPLICATED (mechanism-level).}
\end{abstract}

\section{The claim}
The paper reports (Fig.~1g and Methods) that phase-field simulations
``confirm the presence of a \emph{pure vortex phase} in the trilayer'' and a
mixed vortex\,$+$\,a1/a2 twin-domain phase in the [PTO16/STO12]$_{10}$
superlattice, with observed vortex periodicities of $\sim$14~nm and
$\sim$10~nm respectively. The simulation solves
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \vec P}{\partial t} = -L\,\frac{\delta F[\vec P]}{\delta \vec P},
\qquad
F = \int_V \left(f_{\text{Landau}} + f_{\text{elastic}} + f_{\text{electric}}
+ f_{\text{gradient}}\right)\,dV,
\end{equation}
with periodic in-plane boundary conditions and a superposition method out of
plane. The paper cites prior literature for the coefficient sets and does not
tabulate them; the driving mechanism, not the exact coefficients, is what is
reproducible from the excerpt.

\section{From-scratch model}
We reduce the 3D problem to a 2D $(x,z)$ cross-section of the ferroelectric
slab with a two-component polarization $\vec P=(P_x,P_z)$ on a
$160\times40$ grid; the central 50\% of $z$ is the PTO film (dead/dielectric
layers above and below). The free-energy variation is
\begin{align}
\frac{\delta F}{\delta \vec P} =
\underbrace{a\vec P + b\,|\vec P|^2\vec P}_{\text{Landau (double well)}}
\;\underbrace{-\,K_z P_z\,\hat z}_{\text{uniaxial (PTO $c$-axis)}}
\;\underbrace{-\,g\,\nabla^2\vec P}_{\text{gradient}}
\;\underbrace{-\,\varepsilon\,\nabla(\nabla\!\cdot\!\vec P)}_{\text{electric / bound charge}} .
\end{align}
The electric term derives from $f_{\text{electric}}=\tfrac12\varepsilon
(\nabla\!\cdot\!\vec P)^2$, penalizing bound charge $\rho_b=-\nabla\!\cdot\!\vec P$.
Minimizing it drives $\vec P$ toward a \emph{divergence-free} field, i.e.\
closed flux loops --- which \emph{is} the polar vortex. The gradient term sets
the wall width and hence the vortex period; the film confinement (elastic/thin
-film effect) fixes the vertical rotation scale. Parameters:
$a=-1,\,b=1,\,g=0.6,\,\varepsilon=4.0,\,K_z=0.8$; explicit-Euler TDGL,
$dt=0.01$, $12000$ steps; periodic in $x$, edge in $z$.

\paragraph{Topology.} Vortex cores are counted as integer winding numbers
$w=\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint d\theta$ of the polar director
$\theta=\operatorname{atan2}(P_z,P_x)$ around each lattice plaquette
(a 2D adaptation of the Berg--L\"uscher plaquette angle-sum). $w=+1/-1$
mark counter-rotating vortex cores. The lateral period is read from the FFT
of the mid-film $P_x(x)$ row.

\section{Agreement with the paper}
The paper's central phase-field statement --- a \emph{pure vortex phase}
emerging from Landau$+$elastic$+$electric$+$gradient competition --- is
reproduced qualitatively and topologically: a balanced, periodic, flux-closure
vortex array that exists \emph{only} when the electric (bound-charge) energy is
active. Our absolute period ($\sim$5~nm at our thin-film calibration) is the
right \emph{order} but smaller than the reported $\sim$14~nm, as expected for a
minimal 2D model with un-tuned coefficients and a coarser gradient stiffness.
We therefore claim a mechanism-level replication of the vortex-phase existence
and topology, not a quantitative match of the periodicity.
