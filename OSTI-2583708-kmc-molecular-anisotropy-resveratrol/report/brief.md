# Brief

**Paper.** Janicki et al. (Sandia National Laboratories), *Modelling and Simulation in Materials Science and Engineering* **33** 055010 (2025), DOI 10.1088/1361-651X/ade176 — "On-lattice kinetic Monte Carlo approaches for modeling molecular anisotropy in resveratrol crystallization."

**Why replicate.** The paper claims two SPPARKS enhancements — (i) non-orthogonal (hex/HCP) simulation boxes with 3D random deposition, (ii) a bound-sphere `diffusion/disphere` app style with a `resv` lattice — and applies them with a 74-point DFT-derived binding-energy library to reproduce experimentally observed resveratrol crystal aspect ratios. The paper points to two public GitHub branches (`nonorth`, `resveratrol` at github.com/tdjanic-snl/spparks) and to supplementary DFT tables + input scripts. If these all resolve cleanly, the paper is fully in scope for independent replication of the on-lattice KMC infrastructure claims.

**What we did.** Cloned the two branches (both live at github.com/tdjanic-snl/spparks). Built the `resveratrol` branch of SPPARKS on uicgpu with mpicxx + std=c++17 (211 source files → `spk_uic`, 895 KB text). Diffed both branches vs upstream master. Verified the HCP + hex-region + 3D-random-deposition additions compile and run correctly on a 48×16×24 HCP box (36,864 sites, 200×250×210 nm), reproducing the paper's Section 3.2 box geometry and running 10 seeds through `deposition event 0.1 0.0 0.0 0.0 5.0 1 9` at T=0.0270 eV/k_B. Cross-checked that the `diffusion/disphere` app style and `resv` lattice style claimed in Section 2.2 of the paper are **not** actually present in either public branch.

**Verdict: PARTIAL.** The infrastructure claim (HCP + hex-region + 3D-random deposition) reproduces cleanly. The core resveratrol claim (bound-sphere disphere app + DFT-parametrized aspect-ratio match) cannot be reproduced from the public artifacts because the `diffusion/disphere` app style, the `resv` lattice, and the DFT energy tables promised in "Supplemental Materials" are missing from the released GitHub branches and are behind a captcha-blocked IOP supplementary link. A 10-seed control sweep with an isotropic monotonic Arrhenius ladder gives W:L = 0.53 ± 0.03 and H:L = 0.87 ± 0.04, distinctly more isotropic than the experimental crystal peaks reported (~0.3-0.4 and ~0.5-0.6) — consistent with the paper's central claim that anisotropy requires the bound-sphere logic that has not been publicly released.
