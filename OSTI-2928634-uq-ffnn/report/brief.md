# Brief

**Paper.** Diamzon & Venturi (2026), "Uncertainty propagation in feed-forward neural network models," *Neural Networks* 194:108178, DOI [10.1016/j.neunet.2025.108178](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neunet.2025.108178). OSTI 2928634.

**What.** The paper derives closed-form expressions for the mean, variance, covariance, and one-point PDF of a leaky-ReLU MLP output when the input vector is a mean plus a bounded uniform perturbation `z ~ U[-β,β]^Nx`. The key trick is linearizing each leaky-ReLU at the deterministic pre-activation `h_n = W_n r_{n-1} + b_n`, which yields `Var(g_j) ≈ β²/3 ‖q_j‖²` and `Cov(g_i,g_j) ≈ β²/3 q_i·q_j` with `q_j = A_j J_L` (Eqs. 37, 41, 42). Table 2 reports the analytic and Monte-Carlo correlation coefficients matching to `|Δ|≤0.05` for `β=1.5` on a 5-layer network approximating a nonlinear integro-differential operator on a Gauss–Legendre–Lobatto grid.

**Why replicate.** The claims are mathematically self-contained and, if correct, give a large speedup over Monte Carlo for forward UQ on deterministic MLPs. The critical claim to test independently is not "does the formula match on the authors' one network" (which is a code-verification exercise) but "does it match on an independently trained network of the same class." Free compute at UICGPU (A100) is more than adequate.

**Our finding.** We independently implemented the operator, the GLL discretization (Nx=Ny=31), the MLP (N=64, leaky-ReLU α=0.01), the analytic moments (Eqs. 37/41/42), the analytic PDF via the sinc-product Fourier form (Eq. A.4), and a 100k-sample Monte-Carlo benchmark. On *our* independently trained networks the analytic moments reproduce MC very well at small `β` (`β=0.1`: variance median-relative-error 1.8–2.1%, off-diagonal correlation RMSE ≈ 0.03–0.06) — consistent with the paper's advertised regime — but degrade substantially at large `β` (`β=1.5`: variance median-rel-err 36–48%, correlation RMSE 0.32–0.46). The paper's own Table 2 shows nearly perfect agreement at `β=1.5` (max deviation 0.05), which our runs do not reproduce. In addition, the paper's Fig. 10 claim that linearization error *decreases* with depth is not confirmed by our L=20 network. **Verdict: PARTIAL** — the method is real and works well in the small-noise regime, but the large-β Table 2 agreement and the depth-improvement claim do not hold on our independently trained instances.
