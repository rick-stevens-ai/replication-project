\section{Paper Summary}
Urazhdin (2023) analyzes, from symmetry alone, how atomic orbital angular momentum (OAM)
is transported in crystalline solids. The central physics: unlike spin, orbital moment is
directly coupled to the crystal potential, so its continuity equation acquires a
\emph{crystal-field torque} term $\hat{\Gamma} = (i/\hbar)[U,\hat{L}] = \mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}$.
Consequently OAM is conserved only under continuous rotational symmetry (Noether), which no
crystal possesses. The paper works out the consequences in a minimal $t_{2g}$ cubic-oxide
tight-binding model and on a triangular lattice via Slater--Koster two-center integrals,
concluding that inter-site hopping strongly favors orbital-moment \emph{reversal} over
conservation, limiting orbital-Hall accumulation to a few atomic spacings from interfaces.

\section{Method}
Pure \texttt{numpy}/\texttt{sympy}, CPU, minutes (see \texttt{work/reproduce.py}).
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{C1:} symbolic evaluation of $(\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F})_z$ with $\mathbf{F}=-\nabla U$
and of $-\partial U/\partial\phi$; confirmed identity, hence Noether conservation iff $U$ is
rotation-invariant.
\item \textbf{C2:} constructed the orbital-selective hopping Hamiltonian
$\hat H=-V\sum(1-\delta_{l,m})c^\dagger_{n+l,m}c_{n,m}$, diagonalized, verified dispersion,
band width $8V$, and $k_i=k_j$ degeneracy (max error $0$).
\item \textbf{C3:} time-evolved $\psi=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(c^\dagger_{xz}+i\sigma c^\dagger_{yz})|0\rangle$;
recovered $\langle L_z\rangle$ oscillation, $\langle L_x\rangle=\langle L_y\rangle=0$, and
frequency $(\varepsilon_2-\varepsilon_1)/h=1.934\times10^{14}$ Hz; analytic vs numeric deviation
$1.5\times10^{-14}$.
\item \textbf{C4:} Slater--Koster reduction with $V_{dd\pi}=-\tfrac23V_{dd\sigma}$,
$V_{dd\delta}=\tfrac16V_{dd\sigma}$ (sympy).
\end{enumerate}

\section{What Worked / What Didn't}
Every claim reproduced. The only friction was a \textbf{normalization-convention ambiguity} in
$V_{20}$: the normalized complex-basis element gives $-0.2551$, while the paper's $-0.36$ matches
the \emph{unnormalized real} element $E(x^2-y^2,3z^2-r^2)=-5\sqrt3/24$. The paper does not state
its normalization; we adopt the matching convention and flag the issue (Open Question 1) because
it changes any predicted OHE amplitude by $\sim40\%$.

\section{Critique of the Replication}
This is an analytic paper, so replication is a strong test of internal consistency but says nothing
about experimental correctness (there is no data to compare). The reproduction is exact where the
paper is quantitative; the weakest link is not our re-derivation but the paper's undocumented $V_{20}$
normalization and its minimal-model assumptions (nearest-neighbor only, single $t_{2g}$ manifold,
coherent evolution with no dephasing).

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED.} All five claims reproduced analytically to the paper's stated precision, with
one documentation-level convention ambiguity identified and resolved.

\section*{Open Questions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Which $V_{20}$ normalization convention is physically correct for OHE-magnitude prediction
(paper matches unnormalized real $-5\sqrt3/24$, not normalized $-0.255$)?
\item Does the $\sim1a$ normal-moment relaxation survive beyond-nearest-neighbor hopping and
realistic $t_{2g}$--$e_g$ mixing from DFT?
\item How robust is the $1.9\times10^{14}$ Hz coherent $\langle L_z\rangle$ oscillation to
temperature, e--ph coupling, and disorder?
\item Can the crystal-field-torque continuity relation and reversal-dominance be extended to $e_g$
orbitals and non-cubic crystal fields?
\item What quantitative OHE accumulation length does the model predict for a specific real interface,
and is it testable against orbital-torque experiments?
\end{enumerate}
