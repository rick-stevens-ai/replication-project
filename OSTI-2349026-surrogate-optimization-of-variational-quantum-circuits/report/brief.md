# Brief

Independent replication of Gustafson et al., "Surrogate optimization of variational quantum circuits", *PNAS* 122(36) e2408530122 (2025), DOI 10.1073/pnas.2408530122. The paper introduces a surrogate-Hessian parallel line-search (adapted from the QMCPACK/STALK atomic-relaxation algorithm) to optimize VQE-style quantum circuits in the presence of sampling noise, claims 2–4× fewer function calls than Powell on H₂O, N₂, H₄ chemistry Hamiltonians, and demonstrates a 40-qubit transverse-field Ising run on IBM's `ibm_brisbane`. We pulled the actual public STALK v0.1 code, reimplemented the paper's transverse-field Ising Hamiltonian (Eq. 2, same J₁=1.0, J₂=0.9, hₜ=0.4) with the paper's 4-parameter nearest-neighbor entangling ansatz (Eq. 5–6), and independently benchmarked surrogate line search against Powell, COBYLA, BFGS, CG, SLSQP under Gaussian sampling noise on an exact statevector at Ns=4 qubits (scaled down from 40 for local tractability). Result: surrogate line search reaches gap<0.1 in ~2.5× fewer noisy calls than Powell (median across 5 seeds), and gradient-based methods (BFGS, CG) fail to converge under noise — both qualitative claims of the paper reproduce. Full quantitative replication of the paper's 40-qubit IBM QPU run and the N₂/cc-pVDZ chemistry benchmarks is out of reach without the paper's private sparse-wave-function simulator (SWS) and IBM Quantum access.
