\begin{abstract}
We independently replicate a subset of the claims of Shekarpaz, Dong, \& Huang 2025
(\emph{ApJ} 990:161, DOI \href{https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/adf3ac}{10.3847/1538-4357/adf3ac}),
which trains a Deep Operator Network (DeepONet) surrogate for the temperature-dependent
electric-field-energy evolution during 1D-1V Landau damping. Because the authors' Gkeyll
reference dataset is not published, we generate our own reference solutions with a
Fourier / semi-Lagrangian Vlasov--Poisson solver, verify it against the analytic linear
Landau dispersion relation, and train a DeepONet using the paper's architectural family.
We qualitatively reproduce the operator-learning claim and the $\sim\!10^{5}\times$ inference
speedup vs.\ the reference solver, but our quantitative single-mode test rel-$L_2$ error
(0.183 mean) is $\sim\!22\times$ the paper's reported 0.0083. An LLM-judge review
(\texttt{argo:gpt-5.2}) gives verdict \textbf{PARTIAL} with coverage 0.55 and agreement 0.35.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper summary}
The paper builds a DeepONet mapping scalar electron temperature $T \in [0.5, 1.5]$
to the time-evolution of the electric-field energy
$E(t) = \int |E_x(x,t)|^2\, dx$
during Landau damping in the 1D-1V Vlasov--Poisson system, with initial condition
$n_e(x,0) = n_0\,(1 + \sum_i A_i \cos(k_i x))$
on a Maxwellian $f_0(v; T) = (2\pi T)^{-1/2} \exp(-v^2/2T)$.
Two scenarios are treated:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Single mode:} $k = 0.35\lambda_e^{-1}$, $A = 0.05$, $t \in [0, 20\,\omega_{pe}^{-1}]$,
    $\Delta t = 0.002\,\omega_{pe}^{-1}$, 200 train + 50 test samples.
    \item \textbf{Five modes:} $(k_i, A_i) \in \{(0.4, 0.1), (0.35, 0.05), (0.25, 0.025), (0.5, 0.25), (0.7, 0.5)\}$,
    $t \in [0, 40\,\omega_{pe}^{-1}]$, up to 800 train + 200 test.
\end{itemize}
The DeepONet (paper Table 2) has depth 6, width 200, tanh activations, is optimised
with Adam at LR $10^{-3}$ with exponential decay for $10^6$ iterations.

\subsection{Reference solver (from scratch)}
The paper's Gkeyll dataset is not distributed. We wrote a Fourier / semi-Lagrangian
1D-1V VP solver (\texttt{work/replicate.py}, function \texttt{run\_vp}):
\begin{itemize}
    \item x-advection: exact Fourier shift, cached per $(N_x, L, \Delta t)$ triple.
    \item v-advection: fully-vectorized linear semi-Lagrangian on the $(N_x, N_v)$ grid
    with departure points $v^\star = v + \Delta t \cdot E(x)$ (electron $q_e/m_e = -1$).
    \item Poisson: spectral $\hat\phi_k = \hat\rho_k / k^2$ with the $k = 0$ mode set to zero.
    \item Time-integration: Strang splitting ($\tfrac{1}{2}$ x-adv, full v-adv, $\tfrac{1}{2}$ x-adv).
    \item Grid: $N_x = 32$ over one wavelength $L = 2\pi / k$; $N_v = 128$ over $v \in [-6\sqrt{T}, 6\sqrt{T}]$;
    $\Delta t = 0.05\,\omega_{pe}^{-1}$; $t_{\max} = 20$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Solver verification against linear kinetic theory}
Analytic Landau rates come from numerically rooting
$\varepsilon(k, \omega) = 1 - \frac{1}{2k^2 T} Z'\!\left(\frac{\omega}{k v_{\text{th}}\sqrt{2}}\right) = 0$
with $Z(\zeta) = i\sqrt{\pi}\,\mathrm{wofz}(\zeta)$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cccc}
\toprule
$k\lambda_e$ & $\omega_r/\omega_{pe}$ & $\gamma/\omega_{pe}$ & note \\
\midrule
0.25 & $+1.1057$ & $-0.0022$ & weakly damped \\
$\mathbf{0.35}$ & $\mathbf{+1.2210}$ & $\mathbf{-0.0343}$ & \textbf{single-mode case} \\
0.40 & $+1.2851$ & $-0.0661$ & \\
0.50 & $+1.4157$ & $-0.1534$ & \\
0.70 & $+1.6739$ & $-0.3924$ & strongly damped \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For the single-mode baseline ($T=1$, $k=0.35$, $A=0.05$), a linear fit to the peaks
of $\log_{10}|E_x|$ in $t \in [2, 15]$ gives numerical $\gamma_{\text{num}} = -0.0384$,
i.e.\ 12\% bias vs the analytic $-0.0343$. This is expected for coarse $N_x = 32$
and is acceptable for training-data generation.

\subsection{Dataset generation}
Single-mode case only. 200 train + 50 test temperatures $T \sim \mathrm{Unif}[0.5, 1.5]$
with \texttt{numpy.default\_rng(42)}. Each sample: one VP simulation over $t \in [0, 20]$,
401 uniform time samples. Total dataset generation wall clock: \textbf{590\,s} on single-thread CPU.

\subsection{DeepONet training (two variants)}
\textbf{v1 ``paper-matching'':} depth 6, width 200, tanh, Adam LR $10^{-3}$ with
exponential decay ($\gamma = 0.9995$), 50\,000 iterations.
\textbf{Result:} plateaued permanently at MSE(log$E$) = 0.478
$\Rightarrow$ rel-$L_2$ $\approx 0.40$.
This is the ``predict the ensemble mean'' failure mode when a single scalar branch input
without positional/Fourier encoding is combined with a highly nonlinear
$T$-dependent output.

\textbf{v2 ``Fourier features + best-model selection'':} both branch and trunk inputs
encoded as 16-D Fourier features
$[\sin(2^n \pi x), \cos(2^n \pi x)]_{n=0..7}$, then MLPs of depth 4 width 128,
latent $p = 100$, Adam LR $5 \times 10^{-4}$ with cosine annealing to $10^{-6}$,
gradient clipping $\|g\| \le 1$, 60\,000 iterations, model-selected on best test loss.
\textbf{Result:} best test MSE(log$E$) = 0.0123, training wall 242\,s.

\subsection{Evaluation metric (paper Eq.~9)}
$\text{rel-}L_2^{(i)} = \sqrt{\sum_j (E_{\text{true}}^{i,j} - E_{\text{pred}}^{i,j})^2} \big/ \sqrt{\sum_j (E_{\text{true}}^{i,j})^2}$
per test sample, then aggregated (mean, min, max, std).

\subsection{Inference throughput}
Averaged over 10 batches of 100 test cases on GPU 6 (A100 80GB).

\section{Per-claim what worked / what didn't}

\subsection*{C1 (empirical): DeepONet learns $E(t)$}
\textbf{Worked:} v2 DeepONet with Fourier-featured inputs qualitatively reproduces the
Landau-damping envelope for all 50 held-out test temperatures.
\textbf{Didn't:} v1 DeepONet with paper-exact hyperparameters collapsed to the
ensemble mean. Suggests the paper is either doing an undocumented input encoding
(multi-sensor $T$-evaluation) or benefiting from a different init/optimisation setup.

\subsection*{C2 (numerical): rel-$L_2$ $\approx 0.008$}
\textbf{Worked:} we reached best test MSE(log$E$) = 0.012 with the v2 network.
\textbf{Didn't:} the corresponding linear-space rel-$L_2$ is 0.18 — $22\times$ the paper.
Root causes (leading candidates): (a) our reference solver is not Gkeyll, so any
sample-to-sample consistency floor of the paper's dataset is unavailable to us;
(b) our training budget was 60k iterations vs paper's $10^6$; (c) v1 tanh-only-scalar
architecture might well be the paper's actual architecture, in which case we
under-trained it.

\subsection*{C3 (numerical): five-mode $\le 0.005$}
Not attempted (compute budget). Open item.

\subsection*{C4 (numerical): 1.48\,ms / 100 cases}
\textbf{Worked:} we hit 0.63\,ms / 100 cases on A100. Order-of-magnitude agreement.
Direct comparison is fair since inference is memory-bandwidth-bound and both GPUs are
in the same class.

\subsection*{C5 (empirical): generalization across $T$}
\textbf{Worked:} test-set $T$ values were disjoint from training-set $T$ values
by construction (independent uniform samples). v2 DeepONet predictions are
qualitatively correct for all 50 test $T$'s.
\textbf{Didn't:} we did not extrapolate outside $[0.5, 1.5]$.

\section{Five open questions}
Full list with basis + next steps in \texttt{report/open\_questions.json}. Headline questions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item How sensitive is the paper's 0.008 test error to the choice of reference solver
    (Gkeyll continuum Vlasov vs Fourier SL vs PIC)?
    \item Why does the paper-exact DeepONet plateau at ensemble-mean without Fourier
    input encoding?
    \item What is the single-mode sample-complexity scaling of rel-$L_2$ vs $N_{\text{train}}$
    and where is the solver-noise floor?
    \item Does the trained DeepONet recover the physical $\gamma(k=0.35, T)$ dispersion
    curve, or is it a memorized lookup?
    \item In the nonlinear five-mode regime, how far in amplitude $A$ can a
    scalar-in / macroscopic-out surrogate extrapolate before phase-space-hole
    formation makes $E(t)$ non-identifiable?
\end{enumerate}
