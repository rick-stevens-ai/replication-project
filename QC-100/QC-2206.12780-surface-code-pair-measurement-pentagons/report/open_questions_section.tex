\section{Open Questions}

The five questions below are genuinely open in the sense that this
replication window did \emph{not} answer them, and each has a concrete
free-compute probe attached. They target the gap between what the paper
established (idealized-noise threshold ordering on Cairo pentagons under
correlated MWPM) and what a hardware-relevant deployment would need.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Bias-noise robustness.} Does the pentagon-over-chao ordering
survive under biased (Z-dominated) noise, or does the pair-measurement
hook-error structure collapse the advantage in the regime physical hardware
actually operates in?
\emph{Probe:} rewrite the paper's Stim noise model to biased-depolarizing at
$\eta\in\{3,10,30,100\}$, re-run the same 12 $(d,p)$ points, check ordering
survives and how the pentagon/chao ratio moves with $\eta$.

\item \textbf{Decomposed-primitive cost.} How much of the pentagon advantage
is retained when the two-qubit pair measurement is replaced by a realistic
$H+\text{CNOT}+MZ+\text{reset}$ decomposition under the same top-level $p$?
\emph{Probe:} decompose each pair-measurement gate in the Stim circuit,
reweight per-gate noise, re-run head-to-head at $d\in\{5,7\}$ across the
same $p$ grid, compare pre- and post-decomposition pentagon/chao ratios.

\item \textbf{GNN decoder vs correlated MWPM.} Can a graph-neural-net decoder
trained on pentagon-tiling syndrome graphs beat correlated MWPM by enough
to close the pentagon-vs-honeycomb gap at moderate $p$?
\emph{Probe:} sample 10M syndrome/error pairs per $(c,d,p)$ triple with
Stim, train a small message-passing GNN, compare LER against pymatching and
against the paper's correlated-MWPM numbers on identical shots. Free
compute: uicgpu A100.

\item \textbf{Dynamical-decoupling composition.} Does the pentagon code
compose cleanly with active DD on idle data qubits, and does DD recover
part of the biased-noise regime deficit from question~1?
\emph{Probe:} add a tunable coherent-Z fraction to the Stim idle noise
channel, model DD as a Pauli twirl at rate $p_{\text{dd}}$, sweep
$p_{\text{dd}}$, check the pentagon/chao ratio.

\item \textbf{Topology-embedding overhead.} For a fixed hardware topology
(heavy-hex or neutral-atom grid), does the Cairo pentagon layout embed with
acceptable SWAP overhead, and does the effective post-embedding threshold
still beat chao on the same topology?
\emph{Probe:} enumerate a minimum-SWAP embedding of the Cairo tiling onto
heavy-hex via subgraph isomorphism, add embedding SWAPs to the Stim circuit
at rate $p$, re-run head-to-head vs chao under the same embedding rule,
report threshold shift and pentagon/chao ratio degradation.
\end{enumerate}
