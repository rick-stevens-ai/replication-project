\begin{abstract}
We independently rebuild, from scratch (no author code), the real-space
Bogoliubov--de Gennes (BdG) calculation behind the central pairing-correlation
result of Dar, Scheurer \& Schrade, ``Altermagnetic spin textures coupled to
superconductors'' (arXiv:2607.15249v1). A conventional $s$-wave superconductor is
proximitized by a circular altermagnetic N\'eel domain wall; the projected
low-energy Hamiltonian carries an emergent radial spin--orbit field
$\alpha(\mathbf r)$ and an altermagnet-only emergent Zeeman field
$V_z(\mathbf r,\chi)$. We diagonalize the $4N^2=7744$-dimensional BdG matrix on a
$44\times44$ grid ($\sim$69~nm box) and extract the on-site singlet amplitude and
the equal-spin $p$-wave triplet bond intensity $I_t(\mathbf r)$.

\medskip
\noindent\fbox{\parbox{0.96\textwidth}{\centering
\textbf{VERDICT: PARTIAL} \quad (Coverage $\approx$ 5/10, Agreement $\approx$ 6/10)\\[4pt]
The mechanism reproduces: triplets are generated \emph{only} at the wall
(localization fraction $0.99$) and the spin-resolved intensities have the correct
angular character ($I_t^{\uparrow\uparrow}$ peaks near $\chi=\pm\pi/2$,
$I_t^{\downarrow\downarrow}$ near $\chi=0,\pi$). The \emph{discriminating} headline
claim --- a fourfold angular hotspot modulation \emph{unique to the altermagnet}
that \emph{vanishes} in the antiferromagnetic (AFM) limit $\varrho_z=0$ --- is
\emph{not} reproduced: the on-wall angular modulation is $0.119$ (AM) vs $0.122$
(AFM), essentially identical, and the fourfold ($\ell=4$) Fourier power is
$2.88\times10^{-11}$ (AM) vs $3.00\times10^{-11}$ (AFM). The triplet signal is also
only $0.38\%$ of the singlet, so this is a difference-of-small-numbers regime.}}
\end{abstract}

\section{The paper's claims (the comparison anchors)}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Triplet generation is localized at the wall.} Equal-spin triplets
arise only where the emergent SOC $\alpha_p(\mathbf R)=\hbar\varrho_3\phi'(R)p_R$
is nonzero, i.e.\ where $\phi'(R)\neq0$ (the wall). [Sec.~VI, Eq.~15; Fig.~4a,b]
\item \textbf{Fourfold hotspot modulation, unique to the altermagnet.} In the AM
case ($\varrho_z\neq0$) the on-wall triplet intensity $I_t(\chi)$ forms a fourfold
``hotspot'' pattern; \emph{this angular modulation disappears in the AFM limit}
$\varrho_z=0$ (Fig.~4a vs 4b). This is the sharpest, most falsifiable claim.
\item \textbf{Spin-resolved anisotropy.} $I_t^{\uparrow\uparrow}$ peaks near
$\chi=\pm\pi/2$; $I_t^{\downarrow\downarrow}$ peaks near $\chi=0,\pi$; the total is
enhanced where they overlap (Fig.~4c,d).
\item \textbf{$d$-wave form factor.} The AM node condition is a spin-dependent
ellipse set by the $d$-wave $V_z\propto\cos2\chi$; the AFM limit gives a circle.
\item \textbf{(Scoped out)} Supercurrent-induced quadrupolar torque (Sec.~VII);
nodal$\leftrightarrow$fully-gapped transitions; the winding of the admixed
triplet's momentum dependence.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Governing equations reimplemented}
We implement the projected low-energy effective Hamiltonian for a planar radial
N\'eel domain wall (paper Eq.~3/8), with $\hbar=1$, energies in eV, lengths in nm:
\begin{align}
\hat h_{\rm proj}(\mathbf r,\mathbf p) &=
 \xi(\mathbf r,\mathbf p)\,\sigma_0 + b_z(\mathbf r,\mathbf p)\,\sigma_z
 + \tfrac12\{p,\alpha(\mathbf r)\}\,\sigma_x,\\
\xi &= \varrho_0 p^2 + \varrho_0 V_0(\mathbf r)-\mu, & V_0 &=\tfrac14\phi'(r)^2,\\
b_z &= \varrho_z(p_x^2-p_y^2)+\varrho_z V_z, & V_z &=\tfrac14\phi'(r)^2\cos2\chi,\\
\boldsymbol\alpha(\mathbf r)&=\varrho_3\,\phi'(r)\,\hat{\mathbf r}, &
\phi(r)&=\tfrac{\pi}{2}\tanh\!\big((r-R_0)/w\big).
\end{align}
A fixed uniform proximity singlet $\Delta_0$ (semiclassical, non-self-consistent)
closes the BdG block (paper Eq.~7):
\begin{equation}
H_{\rm BdG}=\begin{pmatrix}\hat h_{\rm proj}&\Delta_0(-i\sigma_y)\\
-\Delta_0(-i\sigma_y)&-\hat h_{\rm proj}^{*}\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Kinetic terms use second-order central finite differences on the grid; the
emergent SOC operator $\tfrac12\{p,\alpha\}$ is symmetrized to enforce
hermiticity. From the anomalous block $F_{ab}=\langle c_a c_b\rangle=\sum_{E>0}u_{a}v_b^{*}$
we extract the on-site singlet $\psi_s(\mathbf r)=|\tfrac12(\langle c_\uparrow c_\downarrow\rangle-\langle c_\downarrow c_\uparrow\rangle)|$
and the equal-spin $p$-wave bond intensity
$I_t(\mathbf r)=\sum_\sigma\sum_{\delta\in\{+\hat x,+\hat y\}}|\langle c_{\mathbf r\sigma}c_{\mathbf r+\delta,\sigma}\rangle|^2$
(a discrete realization of paper Eq.~16).

\subsection{What reproduced}
Claims 1 and 3 reproduce cleanly and qualitatively. Triplet intensity is
overwhelmingly concentrated in the annulus around $R_0$ (localization fraction
$0.99$), exactly as Eq.~(15) demands ($\alpha_p\propto\phi'(R)$ vanishes away from
the wall). The spin-resolved intensities show the paper's Fig.~4(c) anisotropy:
$I_t^{\uparrow\uparrow}$ is largest near $\chi=\pm\pi/2$ and $I_t^{\downarrow\downarrow}$
near $\chi=0,\pi$ (see \texttt{angular\_It\_up\_AM} / \texttt{angular\_It\_dn\_AM} in
the evidence JSON), consistent with the emergent-SOC triplet-generation mechanism.

\subsection{What did NOT reproduce (the discriminating claim)}
The paper's sharpest, most falsifiable statement --- that the fourfold angular
modulation is \emph{unique to the altermagnet and disappears when $\varrho_z=0$}
--- is not seen. Our AM and AFM on-wall $I_t(\chi)$ profiles are nearly identical:
modulation depth $0.119$ vs $0.122$, and fourfold Fourier power
$2.88\times10^{-11}$ vs $3.00\times10^{-11}$. In our discretization the angular
structure is dominated by the emergent SOC $\alpha\propto\hat{\mathbf r}$ (present
in \emph{both} AM and AFM), while the altermagnet-only $V_z\propto\cos2\chi$
contributes a change of $\lesssim3\%$ --- below the level at which we can claim a
clean AM-only fourfold pattern. Compounding this, $\sqrt{I_t}/\psi_s\approx0.4\%$:
the triplet is a tiny admixture on top of the singlet, so the AM/AFM
\emph{difference} is a difference of small numbers (cf.\ background-subtraction
pitfall in the replication skill).

\section{Comparison \& verdict}
\textbf{PARTIAL.} The proximity-induced, wall-localized equal-spin triplet
mechanism and its spin-resolved anisotropy reproduce; the emergent SOC does
generate triplets exactly where the paper says. But the \emph{defining} altermagnet
signature --- a fourfold hotspot pattern that is absent in the AFM limit --- is not
resolved by this semiclassical, non-self-consistent, coarse-grid BdG
implementation. We therefore cannot confirm the headline as stated, and we are
honest that the observed AM/AFM difference lives in the noise of a
difference-of-small-numbers.

\section{Honest gaps (numbered; see failure\_analysis.md)}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Fourfold-modulation null result.} AM$\approx$AFM angular structure;
$V_z$'s $d$-wave contribution is $\lesssim3\%$, not the dominant, AFM-vanishing
pattern the paper reports.
\item \textbf{Difference of small numbers.} Triplet is $0.4\%$ of singlet; the
AM$-$AFM contrast is not on statistically firm ground at this resolution.
\item \textbf{Coarse grid / small box.} $a=1.6$~nm, $R_0=20$~nm (vs paper
$\sim$50~nm); the $d$-wave anisotropy on the wall may be under-resolved.
\item \textbf{Semiclassical, non-self-consistent $\Delta$.} Uniform fixed $\Delta_0$;
the paper's Wigner-transform / self-consistent treatment (Eqs.~15--16) is
approximated by a discrete bond correlator.
\item \textbf{Scoped out entirely:} supercurrent-induced quadrupolar torque
(Sec.~VII), nodal$\leftrightarrow$gapped transitions, triplet momentum winding.
\item \textbf{Extraction tooling degraded (NOT physics):} \texttt{marker}/\texttt{nougat}
absent; interim \texttt{pdftotext}. \texttt{pdflatex} absent; this \texttt{.tex}
ships as source.
\end{enumerate}
