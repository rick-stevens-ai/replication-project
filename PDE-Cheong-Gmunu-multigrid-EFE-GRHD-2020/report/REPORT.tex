\begin{abstract}
We attempt an independent replication of the multigrid-convergence claim of Cheong, Lin \& Li (2020) [arXiv:2001.05723; Class.\ Quantum Grav.\ 37 145015], which introduces the code \emph{Gmunu} --- a general-relativistic hydrodynamics code that uses a Full Approximation Scheme (FAS) nonlinear cell-centred multigrid (CCMG) solver for the conformally-flat-condition (CFC) Einstein constraint equations. The paper's headline algorithmic claim (their Fig.~11) is that FAS multigrid gives an $O(10^3\text{--}10^4)\times$ speedup over lexicographic Gauss--Seidel relaxation for the highly nonlinear elliptic $\Delta\psi = -2\pi\rho\psi^5$ CFC Hamiltonian constraint, and that V-cycle depth monotonically improves convergence up to a saturation depth. Because no public Gmunu source repository could be located (paper claims ``open-source'' but no URL is anywhere in the paper, on the author's website, or on GitHub/GitLab), we built an independent $\sim$250-line FAS nonlinear multigrid solver in NumPy on a 2D Cartesian manufactured-solution analog of the CFC problem. We reproduce the qualitative V1-vs-V-cycle speedup pattern (V1 $= 259$ iters, V2--V6 $= 10$--$11$ iters), the monotone-then-saturate behavior with V-cycle depth, and second-order spatial accuracy (observed order $1.928 \to 1.988$ for $N=16\to128$). We do not test the paper's application-level physics benchmarks (TOV eigenmodes, shocktube, migration) because they require the full Gmunu stack. Two independent LLM judges (\texttt{argo:claude-sonnet-4.5} and \texttt{argo:gpt-5.2}) converge on verdict \textbf{PARTIAL}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Method}

\subsection{Model problem}
We solve on $\Omega = [0,1]^2$
\[
-\Delta u + c\, u^5 = f(x,y), \qquad c = 2\pi\rho_0,\; \rho_0 = 5,
\]
with Dirichlet BCs from a manufactured $u_{\rm exact}(x,y) = 1 + 0.3\sin(\pi x)\sin(\pi y)$ and analytical $f = \Delta u_{\rm exact} - c\,u_{\rm exact}^5$. This shares the exact $\psi^5$ nonlinearity structure of the CFC Hamiltonian constraint (paper Eq.~15).

\subsection{Solver ingredients}
Mirror Gmunu \S 4:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Smoother:} vectorized red--black nonlinear Gauss--Seidel with pointwise Newton, 2 Newton per point, 2 sweeps per level pass.
  \item \textbf{Cycle:} FAS V-cycle ($\gamma = 1$); $\nu_1=\nu_2=2$; coarsest-grid heavy relaxation (30 sweeps).
  \item \textbf{Restriction:} full-weighting cell-centred (2$\times$2 average).
  \item \textbf{Prolongation:} bilinear cell-centred 9-point stencil ($0.5625/0.1875/0.1875/0.0625$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Runs}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item V-cycle depth study parallel to paper Fig.~11: $N=64$, V1..V6, tol $= 10^{-7}$.
  \item Spatial-order study: $N \in \{16,32,64,128\}$, error vs $u_{\rm exact}$.
\end{enumerate}

Compared to the paper (BU8 lapse, $640\times 64$ spherical, V1 $\sim 10^5$, V6 $\sim 40$, ratio $\sim 2500\times$): our absolute numbers differ because our problem is smaller (Cartesian $64^2$ MMS, not BU8), but the \emph{shape} of the convergence curves (V1 catastrophically slow, V-cycles fast, monotone improvement with depth then saturation) is faithfully reproduced. Textbook $2^{\rm nd}$-order asymptotic. Confirms C3.

\subsection{Multigrid iteration scaling (C4)}

Iteration counts to tol $10^{-10}$ vs $N$: 7, 11, 16, 20 for $N = 16, 32, 64, 128$. Growth is mildly super-logarithmic but far below $O(N^2)$ that a pure smoother would give. Supports the near-optimal scaling claim.
