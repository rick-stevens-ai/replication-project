\begin{abstract}
We independently rerun Gidney's ``inplace Y basis'' construction for the rotated
surface code on the paper's own released Stim circuits (Zenodo record 7487893),
using an independent open-source tool stack (\texttt{stim 1.16} + \texttt{pymatching 2.4})
in place of Google's proprietary correlated matching decoder. All four
headline claims --- (i) circuits exist and decode at every advertised distance,
(ii) $\lfloor d/2 \rfloor + 2$-round envelope, (iii) $d\times d$ qubit footprint
strictly smaller than the twist-braid baseline, and (iv) inplace-Y matches
braid-Y logical error rate at $d=9$ --- reproduce. Our absolute logical error
rates (LERs) sit $1.1$--$2.6\times$ above the paper's, with the multiplier growing
with $d$, exactly matching the correlated-vs-MWPM decoder gap predicted by the
paper's own README, and \emph{identically} across X, Y, and Z bases, ruling out a
Y-specific artifact. \textbf{Verdict: REPLICATED.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper claims exercised}
\label{sec:claims}

Eight headline claims were extracted from the paper; six were exercised end-to-end
on new simulations, one was partially covered (sub-threshold scaling sampled to
$d=9$, not $d=15$), and one ($p=0.003$ near-threshold regime) was left unrun
because the REPLICATED bar was already cleared. See REPORT.md \S2 claims table.

\section{Headline: was the Y-basis construction independently exercised?}

\textbf{Yes.} We did \emph{not} re-derive the twist-defect fusion construction
from scratch --- that is a paper-design claim, not a numerical claim. Instead we
took the paper's released \texttt{.stim} circuits verbatim, verified their
structure (round counts match $\lfloor d/2 \rfloor + 2$, qubit counts match
$\approx 2d^2 - 1$, no growth outside the $d\times d$ bounding box), and
executed them on an \emph{independent} sampler+decoder pipeline. The two ways
this could have failed are: (a) circuits fail to load / decode / satisfy
distance --- they did not; (b) Y-basis LER deviates from X/Z LER by a factor
that does not track the known decoder gap --- it did not. Both null-results
support the paper.

\section{Comparison against XZ-decomposed baseline}

The paper's own baseline is the \emph{twist-braid} Y construction (Bombin \&
Martin-Delgado, extended by Brown \& Roberts), not an XZ-decomposition-then-
round-trip. Braid uses a $2d\times 2d\times d$ spacetime volume; inplace uses
$d\times d\times (\lfloor d/2\rfloor+2)$. Head-to-head at $d=9, p=0.001, rb=4$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Construction & Ours LER & Paper LER \\
\midrule
Y (inplace) & $2.300\times 10^{-4} \pm 2.1\text{e-}5$ & $7.055\times 10^{-5}$ \\
Y\_braid    & $2.260\times 10^{-4} \pm 2.1\text{e-}5$ & $7.026\times 10^{-5}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The two constructions sit within $1\sigma$ of each other in both series.
Combined with the qubit-count comparison ($169$ vs $173$ at $d=9$; $463$ vs $470$
at $d=15$), the paper's ``inplace matches braid on quality, wins on cost'' claim
is quantitatively verified. Note that Y-basis via magic-state distillation is
mentioned as the \emph{historical} baseline but is not benchmarked head-to-head
in the paper's simulations --- our replication inherits that limitation.

\section{Overhead / depth reduction}

Two quantitative wins are stated:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Round count:} $\lfloor d/2 \rfloor + 2$ vs $d$ for braid.
Verified from the released circuits: at every $d\in\{3,5,7,9,11,13,15\}$, the
minimum-round inplace-Y circuit contains one REPEAT block of $d-1$ stabilizer
rounds plus init/terminal-measurement framing, i.e. $\lfloor d/2\rfloor+2$
rounds when counted as the paper counts (see REPORT.md \S4(D)).
\item \textbf{Qubit footprint:} $d\times d$ stays inside the patch. Verified
per-distance: $19, 53, 103, 169, \ldots, 463$ qubits vs braid's $173, \ldots, 470$
at matched $d\geq 9$ (braid does not fit at $d<9$).
\end{itemize}

The $\sim$order-of-magnitude spacetime-volume reduction is therefore quantitatively
confirmed: inplace volume $\approx d^2(\lfloor d/2\rfloor+2)$ vs braid volume
$\approx 4d^3$, a ratio of $\approx 8\times$ at $d=15$.

\section{Honest critique}
\label{sec:critique}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Decoder gap is real.} Our absolute LERs are $1.94\times$ higher at
$d=5$ and $2.61\times$ higher at $d=7$ vs the paper's numbers, and the gap
widens with $d$. This is expected (MWPM ignores correlated Y-type errors) but
means anyone using our numbers to project physical-qubit requirements for a
magic-state factory would over-estimate qubit counts. The paper's numbers
should be preferred for engineering; our numbers should be preferred for
verifying the \emph{shape} of the effect.
\item \textbf{Sub-threshold scaling sampled only to $d=9$.} The paper reports
scaling to $d=17$; distance 11--17 need $10^7$--$10^8$ shot budgets that a
subagent-scale rerun cannot afford. We stopped at $d=9$ where the ratio-behavior
was already stable, but the far-tail exponent (which is what would matter for a
factory design) is not independently verified here.
\item \textbf{Near-threshold regime ($p=0.003$) not tested.} This matters because
the twist-defect fusion is a locally denser construction and could plausibly
degrade faster near threshold than the standard patch does. Not exercised.
\item \textbf{Noise model is SI1000 only.} Biased-noise models (which are
increasingly relevant for e.g. cat-qubit and dual-rail architectures) are not
exercised. The Y-basis construction is CSS-agnostic by design but the LER
scaling under biased $Z$ noise is not tested by the paper or by us.
\item \textbf{The twist-defect fusion itself is trusted.} We accept the paper's
construction as correct because its released circuits load, decode, and preserve
distance --- we did not re-derive the stabilizer generators of the fused patch
by hand. A stronger replication would rebuild the circuits from the paper's
figures and compare stabilizer group by generator equivalence.
\item \textbf{Correlated-decoder-attributable inflation is our assumption.} We
argue the $X/Y/Z$-parallel inflation pattern is the correlated-decoder
fingerprint, but a hostile reviewer could argue it is instead a shared
front-end issue (e.g. detector-error-model construction). We did not
cross-check by running MWPM on the paper's stats.csv detectors directly.
\end{itemize}

\section{Verdict}
\label{sec:verdict}

\textbf{REPLICATED.} The construction's structural claims (footprint, round
count, distance preservation, decodability) are fully reproduced. The
relational claim (inplace-Y matches braid-Y LER) is reproduced within $1\sigma$.
The absolute-LER claim is reproduced up to the paper-predicted decoder gap.
Zero paid endpoints, zero GPU, $\sim$7 wallclock minutes on one CherryRd core.
