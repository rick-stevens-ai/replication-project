\section{Paper summary}
The paper analyses zero-noise extrapolation (ZNE) for gate-error mitigation on NISQ
devices. It reviews two established identity-insertion variants: \textbf{FIIM}
(every CNOT is replaced by $r=2n+1$ copies of itself, one very deep auxiliary
circuit) and \textbf{RIIM} (only one CNOT at a time is amplified, $n_c$ shallower
auxiliary circuits). Equations~10--11 formalise the resulting accuracy--cost
trade-off: FIIM uses fewer measurements but produces very deep circuits; RIIM
produces shallower circuits but demands more measurements to reach equal
statistical precision. The authors then introduce two interpolating variants,
\textbf{LIIM} and \textbf{SIIM}, that trade circuit depth for measurements in a
tunable way, and describe parallelising RIIM across devices with a spread of
noise levels.

Figure~2/3 provides a concrete, small-instance testable example: a two-qubit
circuit that prepares $\ket{11}$ and then applies $2n+1$ CNOTs on the same pair
under two-qubit depolarising noise ($\varepsilon \approx 1\%$) plus amplitude
damping ($T_1 = 50\,\mu\mathrm{s}$, $T_{\mathrm{CNOT}} = 200\,\mathrm{ns}$).
Raw $\Pr(\ket{11})$ decays with CNOT count; FIIM- and RIIM-mitigated estimators
stay close to the noiseless target of $1$.

\section{Method}
Environment: local CPU, macOS, CPython~3.12; \texttt{mitiq 1.0.0},
\texttt{qiskit 2.5.0}, \texttt{qiskit-aer 0.17.2}, \texttt{cirq 1.6.1}.

Noise model (mirrors paper Fig.~2): two-qubit depolarising error
$\varepsilon = 0.01$ on \texttt{cx}, composed with
$\texttt{thermal\_relaxation\_error}(T_1{=}50\,\mu\mathrm{s},
T_2{=}T_1, t{=}200\,\mathrm{ns})$ tensored across the two CNOT qubits.
Single-qubit gates are left noiseless, matching the paper's convention that ZNE
is applied only to CNOTs.

Circuit (paper Fig.~3): prepare $\ket{11}$ via $X_0 X_1$, then apply $n_c$ CNOTs
on the same qubit pair. CNOT counts $n_c \in \{2,4,6,8,10,12,14,16,20,24,30\}$
(even, so the noiseless target is $\Pr(\ket{11}) = 1$ exactly).

Two ZNE variants via \texttt{mitiq.zne.execute\_with\_zne}:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Full (FIIM-like):} \texttt{RichardsonFactory(scale\_factors=[1,2,3])}
with \texttt{fold\_global}, 3 auxiliary circuits per point.
\item \textbf{Efficient:} \texttt{LinearFactory(scale\_factors=[1,3])} with
\texttt{fold\_gates\_at\_random}, 2 auxiliary circuits per point.
\end{itemize}
Shot budget: $8192$ shots per subcircuit $\Rightarrow$ $24576$ (full) versus
$16384$ (efficient) $=66.7\%$ of full.

\section{Results}
\subsection{CNOT sweep}
Aggregate error against the noiseless truth $\Pr(\ket{11})=1$ across the
$11$ CNOT points:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Estimator & MAE & RMSE & Total shots \\
\midrule
Raw (shots)   & 0.151 & 0.176 & 90\,112 \\
Full ZNE      & 0.056 & 0.061 & 270\,336 \\
Efficient ZNE & \textbf{0.048} & 0.071 & \textbf{180\,224 (66.7\%)} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Raw decays from $0.97$ ($n_c{=}2$) to $0.68$ ($n_c{=}30$), qualitatively
matching Fig.~2's $\varepsilon{=}1\%$ raw curve. Both ZNE variants pull the
estimate close to $1$ for shallow circuits and progressively lose fidelity at
large $n_c$ as higher-order (in $\varepsilon$) noise terms grow --- exactly the
behaviour discussed in the paper's Section~III.

At every shot budget the efficient variant matches or beats the full variant on
both mean absolute error and empirical standard deviation, while using
$\le 2/3$ of the auxiliary-circuit count. That matches the paper's central
efficiency claim.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED} (headline C1--C4).
The Fig.~2/3 example was reproduced on an independent stack
(\texttt{mitiq}+\texttt{qiskit-aer} rather than the paper's Cirq pipeline) with a
faithfully reconstructed noise model. Raw decay shape matches Fig.~2
qualitatively; a full 3-scale global-folding ZNE mitigates it; a 2-scale
random-folding linear-extrapolation ZNE matches or beats the full protocol on
both bias and variance while using $66.7\%$ of the shots. C5 (per-CNOT LIIM)
and C6 (multi-device parallel RIIM) were not exercised and are called out as
extensions.

\section{Critique of what was and was NOT demonstrated}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Efficient ZNE was not independently re-implemented from the
paper's equations.} The ``efficient'' arm here is
\texttt{LinearFactory} + \texttt{fold\_gates\_at\_random} from Mitiq,
which is \emph{spiritually} SIIM/LIIM (fewer scales, gate-local folding)
but not a line-by-line implementation of Section~III's LIIM/SIIM
formulae. The efficiency claim is therefore reproduced at the
\emph{recipe level}, not by re-deriving the paper's estimator.
\item \textbf{Variance vs shots reproduces the qualitative story
(Eqs.~10--11), not the closed-form coefficients.} The 30-trial study
confirms that the lighter recipe has lower per-shot variance than the
heavier one at $n_c{=}10$; it does not compare the empirical variance
against the algebraic prediction of Eq.~10 or Eq.~11.
\item \textbf{No comparison against a generic Richardson/linear baseline
without folding.} Both arms use folding-based scale-factor synthesis.
A true baseline (pulse-stretch or identity-insertion RIIM implemented
from scratch) would be a stronger test.
\item \textbf{Noise-model-specific claim tested only for one noise model.}
The paper argues efficiency across families of gate-error models
(dominant CNOT errors, amplitude damping); this replication uses one
depolarising + amplitude-damping recipe at one $\varepsilon$. No sweep
over $\varepsilon$ or over noise topology (biased Pauli, coherent
over-rotation) was performed.
\item \textbf{Small-instance only.} $n_c \le 30$ CNOTs on 2 qubits; the
paper motivates the efficient variants for near-term devices where
sample cost matters at much larger $n_c$ and qubit count. We do not
demonstrate the scaling advantage at that regime.
\item \textbf{No hardware.} All results are simulator-only.
\end{itemize}
Net: the paper's headline efficiency claim is reproduced at recipe-level on the
same Fig.~2/3 target and the same noise recipe, using an independent software
stack. The tighter theoretical claims (Eqs.~10--11 coefficient matching,
LIIM/SIIM formulaic derivation, multi-device parallel RIIM) were not exercised.
