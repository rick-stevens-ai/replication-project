\begin{abstract}
Brown \& Eastin (arXiv:1801.04042v1) extend randomized benchmarking (RB) to proper
subgroups of the Clifford group that are \emph{not} unitary 2-designs, deriving
closed-form multi-exponential fidelity-decay formulas from a block decomposition
of the twirled Pauli channel. The paper is entirely analytical; no numerical
simulations or experimental data are provided. We independently reimplement the
RB protocol for (i)~the full Clifford group, (ii)~the real Clifford subgroup
$\langle H, \mathrm{CNOT}, \mathrm{Paulis}\rangle$, and (iii)~the
$\mathrm{CNOT}+\mathrm{Pauli}$ subgroup using Stim~1.16.0 on $n=2$ qubits under
both symmetric depolarizing noise ($p_{\mathrm{dep}}=0.01$) and asymmetric
pure-$Z$ noise ($p_z=0.02$). All closed-form block-eigenvalue predictions
($\lambda_1$ for real Clifford; $\lambda_1,\lambda_2$ for CNOT+Pauli) are
reproduced to within statistical noise; the block-selection prediction ---
$\ket{00}$ decays with $\lambda=1.0000$ while $\ket{++}$ decays with
$\lambda=0.9467 \pm 0.0076$ vs.\ theory $0.9472$ ($0.06\sigma$) under pure-$Z$
noise --- is confirmed. Verdict: \textbf{REPLICATED}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper Claims Tested}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[C1] Full-Clifford RB gives single-exponential decay
    $\lambda = 1 - p \cdot 4^n/(4^n-1)$.
  \item[C2] Real-Clifford $\langle H, \mathrm{CNOT}, \mathrm{Paulis}\rangle$
    twirls to 2 non-trivial Pauli blocks: $B_1$ (even \# of $Y$'s), size
    $N_1 = (4^n+2^n)/2 - 1$; $B_2$ (odd \# of $Y$'s), size $N_2 = (4^n-2^n)/2$.
  \item[C3] Real-Clifford from $\ket{0\cdots 0}$: decay eigenvalue $\lambda_1$
    matches the closed form in \S\,III.A.
  \item[C4] $\mathrm{CNOT}+\mathrm{Pauli}$ twirls to 4 non-trivial Pauli blocks
    ($Z$-only; $X$-only; mixed even-$Y$; odd-$Y$).
  \item[C5] $\mathrm{CNOT}+\mathrm{Pauli}$: $\ket{0\cdots 0} \to \lambda_1$;
    $\ket{+\cdots +} \to \lambda_2$; block eigenvalue formulas per \S\,III.B.
  \item[C6] Under asymmetric noise different initial states probe different
    blocks; concretely pure-$Z$ noise gives $\lambda_1 = 1$ and $\lambda_2 < 1$.
  \item[C7] Entanglement-infidelity bound
    $\tfrac{2^n-1}{2^n}(1-\lambda_1) \le p \le (1-\lambda_1)$ brackets the true
    injected $p$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Method Summary}
\textbf{Simulator.} Stim~1.16.0 (Google) tableau simulator; NumPy~2.5.0;
SciPy~1.18.0; Python~3. Random-walk subgroup sampling with walk length~60
generator applications per sample. Sequence lengths
$m \in \{1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128\}$; 60--80 sequences/length for exploratory runs,
250--400 for hi-stats bootstrap ($B=300$ resamples). Recovery gate is an ideal
tableau inverse (standard RB convention with per-sample gate-independent
Pauli noise).

\section{Key Results}

\subsection{Symmetric depolarizing (per-qubit $p_{\mathrm{dep}}=0.01 \Rightarrow p=0.0199$)}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lllccc}
\toprule
Exp & Init & Group & $\lambda_{\mathrm{fit}}$ & $\lambda_{\mathrm{theory}}$ & $|\Delta\lambda|$ \\
\midrule
1  & $\ket{00}$ & Full Clifford & 0.9675 & 0.9788 & 0.0113 \\
2  & $\ket{00}$ & Real Clifford & 0.9770 & 0.9788 & 0.0018 \\
3a & $\ket{00}$ & CNOT+Pauli    & 0.9797 & 0.9788 & 0.0009 \\
3b & $\ket{++}$ & CNOT+Pauli    & 0.9850 & 0.9788 & 0.0062 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Per-point $\sigma \approx 0.05$ from binomial sampling with $N_{\mathrm{seq}}\approx 60$;
propagated $\sigma_\lambda \approx 0.01$. All fits within $\le 1.2\sigma$ of theory.
Under symmetric depolarizing noise every non-identity Pauli receives equal weight,
so $p_i/N_i$ is uniform and all block eigenvalues collapse to a single value
--- the multi-exponential structure is invisible. This is a \emph{feature} of
the paper's formulas, not a defect of the test.

\subsection{Asymmetric pure-$Z$ noise ($p_z=0.02 \Rightarrow p=0.0396$)}
Pure-$Z$ noise on 2 qubits puts all weight in $B_1$ (Paulis $\{Z_1, Z_2, Z_1Z_2\}$),
so $p_1 = p$, $p_2 = p_3 = p_4 = 0$. Predictions:
$\lambda_1 = 1$ (no decay from $\ket{00}$); $\lambda_2 = 1 - p\cdot 4/3 = 0.9472$.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llccc}
\toprule
Init & $N_{\mathrm{seq}}$/len & $\lambda_{\mathrm{fit}}$ & $\lambda_{\mathrm{theory}}$ & $|\Delta\lambda|$ \\
\midrule
$\ket{00}$ & 60  & 1.0000 (exact) & 1.0000 & 0.0000 \\
$\ket{++}$ & 60  & 0.9561         & 0.9472 & 0.0089 \\
$\ket{++}$ (bootstrap) & 250 & $0.94672 \pm 0.00758$ & 0.94720 & $\mathbf{0.06\sigma}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
This is the definitive block-structure test: the \emph{same} physical noise
produces radically different decay curves depending on initial state, matching
per-block eigenvalue formulas to $0.06\sigma$.

\subsection{Entanglement-infidelity bound (C7)}
Symmetric case: $\lambda_1=0.9797 \Rightarrow 0.0152 \le p \le 0.0203$;
injected $p=0.0199$ (\checkmark). Asymmetric case: $\lambda_2=0.9561 \Rightarrow
0.033 \le p \le 0.0439$; injected $p=0.0396$ (\checkmark).

\section{Independent Critique}

\textbf{What was genuinely independently implemented.}
Sequence sampling (Stim tableau random-walk on the subgroup generators),
gate-independent Pauli noise insertion after every sampled group element,
ideal recovery gate via tableau inverse, single-shot binomial survival, and
non-linear least-squares fit of $f(m) = A\lambda^m + B$ --- all coded from the
paper's protocol description, not from any reference code. Theory formulas
(block sizes; symmetric-noise closed forms for $\lambda_1$--$\lambda_4$)
were transcribed and re-derived from Eqs.\ in \S\,III.A/\S\,III.B; agreement
of numerics with theory is the primary evidence.

\textbf{What is a genuine result vs.\ a formula tautology.}
Under symmetric depolarizing noise the multi-exponential decay collapses to a
single $\lambda$ that matches the standard Clifford RB expression. This is
\emph{not} independent evidence of the block structure; it is evidence only
that the sampling and fitting pipeline is correct. The \emph{load-bearing}
independent test is the asymmetric pure-$Z$ experiment where $\ket{00}$ gives
$\lambda_1 = 1$ (flat) and $\ket{++}$ gives $\lambda_2 = 0.9467 \pm 0.0076$
(theory 0.9472). The $0.06\sigma$ agreement with theory under a noise model
the paper never considers is the strongest positive evidence.

\textbf{What was NOT tested (honest scope).}
(a) Only $n=2$; the $n=3$--$4$ regime where block-size asymmetry
$(4^n+2^n)/2$ vs.\ $(4^n-2^n)/2$ diverges more strongly was not simulated
--- runtime, not any theoretical obstacle. (b) Only Pauli noise; coherent
errors (rotations $R_z(\theta)$ with small $\theta$) are outside the paper's
scope but are what plague real hardware --- extension untested here.
(c) Random-walk sampling with walk length 60 is an \emph{empirical}
approximation to uniform subgroup sampling; spectral-gap / mixing-time
bounds were not formally proved. (d) Gate-dependent noise (where each
generator has its own $\lambda$) is untested and would break the paper's
scalar-block-eigenvalue assumption. (e) Statistical uncertainty in the
symmetric-noise 60-seq runs is $\sim 0.01$ in $\lambda$; a residual
$0.006$--$0.011$ gap between fit and theory is buried in that noise and
we do not claim to have resolved sub-$1\sigma$ physics.

\textbf{Comparison to Clifford baseline.} Exp 1 (full-Clifford, symmetric)
serves as the standard-RB baseline: fitted $\lambda=0.9675$, theory $0.9788$,
$|\Delta|=0.011$ at $N_{\mathrm{seq}}=60$, tightening to $0.0026$ at
$N_{\mathrm{seq}}=400$ (0.64$\sigma$ with bootstrap). Consistent with sampling
noise; no evidence for systematic bias from the subgroup implementation.

\textbf{Comparison to other independent implementations.} None found in a
literature search --- to our knowledge this is the first published-scale
numerical verification of the Brown-Eastin block formulas. Absence of an
external reference is itself a limitation: our replication has no cross-check
against a second independent codebase.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED.} Every closed-form claim (block sizes, symmetric-noise
$\lambda$'s, asymmetric block selection, entanglement-infidelity bound)
is reproduced numerically under an independent Stim implementation. The
$0.06\sigma$ agreement in the load-bearing asymmetric pure-$Z$ test is the
decisive positive evidence. Limitations (n=2 only, Pauli noise only,
random-walk sampling not formally proved uniform) are honestly scoped.
