\section{Open questions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Do modern QAOA variants beat vanilla QAOA on the Willsch suite?}
\emph{Basis:} the paper only benchmarks the 2014 Farhi--Goldstone--Gutmann QAOA. Warm-start QAOA, ADAPT-QAOA, XY-mixer QAOA, and recursive QAOA claim depth-$p$ advantages but are typically evaluated on custom instance sets, not the Willsch suite.
\emph{Next steps:} extend \texttt{code/qaoa\_maxcut.py} to instantiate warm-start (GW-rounded initial state), ADAPT-QAOA (adaptive mixer pool), and XY-mixer variants on the same 6 graphs at $p=1..5$; report $\alpha$ and $P_{\text{ground}}$ deltas vs \texttt{evidence/qaoa\_results.json}. Free simulator; minutes on a laptop for $n\le 10$.

\item \textbf{Where does hardware noise flip the monotone-$p$ trend?}
\emph{Basis:} the paper's C6 (IBM~Q $p=1$ landscape noisier than simulator) is qualitative and does not answer at what $p$ noise overwhelms algorithmic gain. Our noiseless statevector cannot show the turnover.
\emph{Next steps:} rerun the suite through \texttt{qiskit-aer}'s noise-model pipeline with \texttt{FakeSherbrooke} / \texttt{FakeQuebec} at $p=1..6$; plot $\alpha(p)$ with and without noise on the same axes. Free simulator, $\sim 10\times$ wall-clock of the noiseless run.

\item \textbf{How does QAOA-$p3$ compare to modern classical baselines on these exact instances?}
\emph{Basis:} we report $\alpha\approx 0.95$ at $p=3$ but ran no classical baseline. Without a classical anchor the numbers are not evidence of quantum advantage --- Goemans--Williamson SDP alone guarantees $\alpha\ge 0.878$ on MaxCut.
\emph{Next steps:} add \texttt{code/classical\_baselines.py} running (a) \texttt{cvxpy}-based Goemans--Williamson with 100 random-hyperplane rounds, (b) 30-line Metropolis simulated annealing with a matched function-eval budget, (c) tabu search via \texttt{mqlib}. Emit a per-graph comparison table.

\item \textbf{Does the trend transfer to weighted and constrained MaxCut?}
\emph{Basis:} we only exercised unweighted MaxCut. Weighted MaxCut is one of the paper's two headline problem families; constrained variants (balanced-cut) require penalty-Hamiltonian encodings that reshape the QAOA landscape.
\emph{Next steps:} add a weighted 3-regular generator (edge weights $\sim U(0,1)$) matching the paper's Sec.~III scheme; rerun $p=1..3$; separately encode a $k$-balanced-cut penalty into $H_C$; compare monotone-$p$ behaviour to unweighted numbers.

\item \textbf{Does parameter-concentration / ML-boosted initialisation cut optimizer cost on our suite?}
\emph{Basis:} parameter concentration on 3-regular MaxCut (near-identical $(\gamma^\ast,\beta^\ast)$ across sizes) is well-known post-2018. If it holds on our suite, COBYLA could be initialised at a fixed seed instead of random restarts; whether it transfers to Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi and weighted MaxCut is unclear.
\emph{Next steps:} (1) extract $(\gamma^\ast,\beta^\ast)$ from \texttt{evidence/qaoa\_results.json} and check variance across 3-regular seeds; (2) warm-start $n=8$, $n=10$ optimizers from the $n=6$ mean; (3) measure $\alpha$ and function-eval count vs the random-restart baseline. Free post-processing plus one small extra run.
\end{enumerate}
