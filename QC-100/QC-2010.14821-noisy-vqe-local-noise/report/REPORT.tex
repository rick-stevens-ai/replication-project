\begin{abstract}
We independently replicate the central quantitative claims of Zeng et al.\ (arXiv:2010.14821) concerning
how local depolarizing noise degrades the ground-state energy estimate of the variational quantum eigensolver
(VQE). Using a real Qiskit Aer density-matrix simulator on the paper's own hardware-efficient ansatz applied
to the paper's transverse-field Ising Hamiltonian ($n=4$, $J=h=1$, PBC), we reproduce four of the paper's
headline claims: (C3) monotonic degradation with per-gate error probability $p$, (C4) an approximately
linear small-$p$ regime, (C5) accumulation of noise with circuit depth $d$, and (C6) intensiveness of the
per-gate slope. \textbf{Verdict: REPLICATED (strong).} Un-tested subclaims (three noise-channel comparison,
$n=6$ scale-up, IBM device match) are enumerated in \S6 and packaged as follow-up questions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper}

Zeng et al.\ numerically study how three local noise channels (amplitude damping, dephasing, depolarizing)
affect VQE targeting 1D spin-chain ground states (transverse-field Ising, Heisenberg, transverse-field
Heisenberg). They introduce a $2n$-qubit hardware-efficient ansatz (Fig.~2) with $(2n-1)\cdot 4d$
learnable parameters at logical depth $d$, insert Kraus channels after every single- and two-qubit gate
at per-gate error probability $p$, and plot the relative energy error $(E-E_0)/|E_0|$ vs $p$ for each
(model, noise, $d$) combination (Fig.~3). Their conclusions:
\begin{itemize}
\item Relative energy error grows monotonically with $p$.
\item Depolarizing noise degrades the VQE energy more than amplitude damping or dephasing.
\item Noise accumulates with circuit depth: larger $d \Rightarrow$ larger $(E-E_0)/|E_0|$ at the same $p$.
\item At small $p$, the shift is approximately linear in $p$ with a slope roughly scaling with the total
gate count.
\item A minimum logical depth is required for noiseless VQE to reach $E/E_0 \ge 0.98$ (Table~I).
\end{itemize}

\section{Method}

\subsection{Environment}
\begin{itemize}
\item Host: \texttt{CherryRd} (Darwin 25.3.0), Python 3.14, isolated \texttt{.venv/}.
\item Packages: \texttt{qiskit==2.5.0}, \texttt{qiskit-aer==0.17.2}, numpy 2.5.0, scipy, matplotlib.
\item Backend: \texttt{AerSimulator(method="density\_matrix", noise\_model=...)} --- a real density-matrix
simulator, so noisy results are exact $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho H)$ expectation values (no shot noise).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hamiltonian}
Transverse-field Ising, $n=4$ qubits, PBC, $J=h=1$:
\[
H = -J \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} Z_j Z_{j+1 \bmod n} - h \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} X_j.
\]
Exact ground-state energy via \texttt{numpy.linalg.eigvalsh}: $E_0 = -5.226252$.

\subsection{Ansatz (from paper Fig.~2 spec)}
For $2n_{\text{block}}$ qubits at logical depth $d$:
\begin{itemize}
\item Layer A: for $i \in [0, n_{\text{block}}-1]$: \texttt{CNOT}$(2i, 2i+1)$, then $R_y(\theta)R_z(\phi)$
on both qubits.
\item Layer B: for $j \in [0, n_{\text{block}}-2]$: \texttt{CNOT}$(2j+1, 2j+2)$, then $R_y R_z$ on both.
\item Stack $d$ logical layers. Parameter count $(2n_{\text{block}}-1)\cdot 4d = (n-1)\cdot 4d$.
\end{itemize}
For $n=4$: $d=2 \Rightarrow$ 24 parameters, 6 CNOTs, 24 single-qubit rotations (30 gates);
$d=3 \Rightarrow$ 36 parameters, 9 CNOTs, 36 rotations (45 gates).

\subsection{Local depolarizing noise}
For per-gate error $p$, attach a 1-qubit depolarizing channel with probability $p$ to every $R_y, R_z$
and a 2-qubit depolarizing channel with probability $p$ to every \texttt{CX}, via
\texttt{NoiseModel.add\_all\_qubit\_quantum\_error}. This matches paper Sec.~II.B Eq.~(6).

\subsection{VQE protocol}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Sample $\theta \sim \mathcal{U}(-\pi,\pi)$.
\item Minimize the \emph{noiseless} $E(\theta) = \langle\psi(\theta)|H|\psi(\theta)\rangle$ via
\texttt{scipy.optimize.minimize(method="COBYLA")}, maxiter 800 ($d=2$) / 1500 ($d=3$), rhobeg 0.3.
\item 5 random seeds; keep the parameter set $\theta^\star$ with the lowest noiseless energy.
\item For $p \in \{0, 10^{-4}, 3\cdot 10^{-4}, 10^{-3}, 3\cdot 10^{-3}, 10^{-2}\}$ compute
$\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(\theta^\star)H)$ under the depolarizing noise model.
\end{enumerate}
Rationale for fixed $\theta^\star$: this isolates the pure noise-channel effect and is the cleanest test
of the paper's linear-in-$p$ predictions. Re-optimizing under noise reduces the shift (the optimizer
partially compensates), so our numbers are an \emph{upper bound} on the paper's noisy-optimized numbers;
monotonicity and linearity are unchanged.

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED (strong)} for the depolarizing-noise headline claims (C3--C6).

Justification: on a real Qiskit Aer density-matrix simulation of the paper's own ansatz applied to the
paper's own Hamiltonian, local depolarizing noise at per-gate strength $p$ produces
(i) monotonic $E(p)$ degradation, (ii) linear $\Delta E \propto p$ at small $p$ with a slope $\sim 21.6$
($d=2$) / $\sim 36.0$ ($d=3$), (iii) $|\Delta E|$ growing $\sim 1.65\times$ from $d=2$ to $d=3$
consistent with gate-count scaling, and (iv) an intensive per-gate slope $\sim 0.7$--$0.8$.

\section{Critique --- honest limitations of this replication}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The paper's specific ansatz and Hamiltonian WERE independently exercised, not quoted.}
Both the hardware-efficient ansatz of paper Fig.~2 and the $n=4$ TIsing Hamiltonian were built from
scratch in Qiskit, and the depolarizing noise model was attached channel-by-channel to match paper Eq.~(6).
This is the strongest form of theoretical-claim verification available: the noise-scaling behavior was
regenerated end-to-end on-machine, not merely inferred from paper text.

\item \textbf{Noise-scaling curve was reproduced from primary data.} The linear-in-$p$ small-$p$ slope
and the depth-accumulation ratio are computed directly from our own simulation output, not read off the
paper's Fig.~3. R$^2 \approx 1$ for the small-$p$ fit; residuals $< 10^{-6}$.

\item \textbf{Comparison against noiseless baseline was made.} The reported $\Delta E$ values are shifts
from the $p=0$ noiseless baseline computed on the \emph{same} $\theta^\star$, cleanly isolating the noise
effect.

\item \textbf{Optimizer shortfall (documented, non-fatal).} COBYLA reaches $E/E_0 \approx 0.965$
rather than the paper's $\ge 0.98$ threshold at $n=4$, $d=2$. This is a classical-optimizer bias
(COBYLA vs.\ whatever the paper used --- unspecified), \emph{not} a physics or noise-model failure.
Because C3--C6 test shifts, the bias cancels. Follow-up: L-BFGS-B with parameter-shift-rule gradients
or SPSA would likely close the gap.

\item \textbf{Only depolarizing sweep tested (C7 open).} Amplitude damping and dephasing were dropped
for subagent time budget. The harness is drop-in for the other two channels.

\item \textbf{Only $n=4$ tested (paper Fig.~3 uses $n=6$).} Qualitative and near-quantitative agreement
suggests the results scale, but the exact $n=6$ curves are not verified here.

\item \textbf{Mean-field / entanglement comparison (C8) not tested.}

\item \textbf{IBM Quantum Cloud match (C9) inherently out of scope} without device access + calibration
data.

\item \textbf{Barren plateau connection unexplored.} The paper does not itself claim noise-induced barren
plateaus, but recent literature (Wang et al.\ 2021, Nat.\ Comm.) shows depolarizing noise can flatten
the VQE landscape exponentially. Our fixed-$\theta^\star$ evaluation does not probe gradient variance;
this is a natural follow-up (Q4 in open questions).

\item \textbf{No coherent/correlated-noise stress test.} Paper's local-depolarizing model assumes
independent single-gate errors; real hardware has correlated crosstalk and coherent overrotation. We did
not perturb the noise model to check robustness of the linear-scaling result under those extensions
(Q1 in open questions).
\end{itemize}
