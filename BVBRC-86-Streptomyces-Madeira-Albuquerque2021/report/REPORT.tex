\begin{abstract}
We independently reproduce the core biological claims of Albuquerque et al.\ (2021, \textit{Marine Drugs} 19:621, DOI 10.3390/md19110621, PMID 34822492) on the assembled genomes of two deep-sea \textit{Streptomyces} isolates (MA3\_2.13 = \textit{S. profundus}, S07\_1.15 = \textit{S. xinghaiensis}) from the Madeira Archipelago. Consuming the deposited public assemblies (BioProject PRJNA754006; GCF\_020740535.1, GCF\_020739505.1) rather than re-sequencing, we recompute assembly statistics, structural annotation counts, average nucleotide identity, and antiSMASH biosynthetic gene cluster (BGC) profiles including MIBiG known-cluster matching. Every claim that depends only on the assembled sequence matches to the last digit; annotator-dependent counts and antiSMASH version-drift produce the expected small deviations without altering any biological conclusion; all three named MIBiG hits (atratumycin, triacsins, arsono-polyketide) are recovered. \textbf{Verdict: REPLICATED} (LLM-judge: Argo Claude Sonnet 4.6, free endpoint).
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper summary}
Albuquerque et al.\ generated hybrid PacBio + Illumina assemblies (Unicycler) of two \textit{Streptomyces} isolates recovered from 500--1500\,m sediments and coral-associated sponges in the Madeira Archipelago (NE Atlantic). Genomes were annotated with RAST + PGAP; taxonomy was established via MiGA / MLSA / whole-genome ANI; biosynthetic potential was profiled with antiSMASH 5.0, BAGEL4, and RiPPMiner. Their headline findings are: (i) MA3\_2.13 is a novel species (later formally described as \textit{S. profundus}) with a 7.65\,Mb, BGC-dense chromosome (32 BGCs, 23.1\% of chromosomal DNA, PKS-rich); (ii) S07\_1.15 is \textit{S. xinghaiensis} with a 7.25\,Mb genome (24 BGCs, 8.8\%, RiPP-rich, no type-I PKS); (iii) three MA3\_2.13 BGCs show significant MIBiG similarity to atratumycin (BGC \#8), triacsins (BGC \#14), and an arsono-polyketide (BGC \#24), with divergent gene identities suggesting novel congeners.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Data acquisition}
Paper OA PDF from EuropePMC (\texttt{work/paper.pdf}, 1.99\,MB). Assemblies resolved through NCBI E-utilities: PRJNA754006 $\rightarrow$ UIDs 11377691 (GCF\_020740535.1, MA3\_2.13, SAMN20720482) and 11376371 (GCF\_020739505.1, S07\_1.15, SAMN21157270). Reference genomes for ANI: GCA\_000220705.1 (\textit{S. xinghaiensis} S187) and GCA\_002128305.1 (\textit{Streptomyces} sp.\ SCSIO 3032) --- the exact references used in the paper.

\subsection{Assembly statistics}
Direct FASTA parsing in Python; GC\% computed as $(G+C)/(A+C+G+T) \times 100$; per-record contig lengths reported.

\subsection{Annotation counts}
NCBI PGAP GFF parsed by feature type; rRNA operons proxied by 16S rRNA count (cross-checked via \texttt{Dbxref=RFAM:RF00177}).

\subsection{ANI}
\texttt{skani dist} (v0.3.x, learned-ANI mode) and \texttt{fastANI} (v1.x). Species-boundary threshold 95--96\% (Jain et al.\ 2018).

\subsection{antiSMASH re-run}
Docker image \texttt{antismash/standalone:6.1.1} on uicgpu, 32 CPU per container. Pass 1 (general BGC counting) with \texttt{--cb-general --pfam2go --smcog-trees}; pass 2 (MIBiG knownclusterblast) with \texttt{--cb-knownclusters}. Region counts equal \texttt{feature.type == "region"} in the JSON output.

\subsection{LLM-judge}
Prompt at \texttt{work/llm\_judge\_input.md}, POSTed to \texttt{http://127.0.0.1:44497/v1/chat/completions} with model \texttt{argo:claude-sonnet-4.6}. Vocabulary constrained to the canonical set (REPLICATED / PARTIAL / SPOT-CHECK / NO-GO / CONTRADICTED / BLOCKED / FAILED). Full response at \texttt{report/evidence/llm\_judge\_response.txt}.

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{What replicated exactly}
Every numeric claim that depends only on the assembled sequence --- total bp, GC\%, contig counts, 16S rRNA operon counts --- matches to the last digit. Independent tooling (Python FASTA parser, NCBI PGAP GFF) consuming the same deposited artifact (CP082362 / JAJBZK010000001--002) confirms deposit integrity.

\subsection{Expected tool drift}
CDS and tRNA counts are 3--5\% lower under PGAP than the paper's RAST + PGAP hybrid annotation; this is documented annotator behaviour for short ORFs and pseudogenes. skani and fastANI produce ANI values slightly higher than PYANI-ANIb for divergent genomes (k-mer vs alignment estimator), but all three methods agree on both species-boundary calls. MA3\_2.13 total BGC count drops from 32 (v5.0) to 27 (v6.1.1) because antiSMASH v6 introduced stricter co-location and detector rules that merge protoclusters v5 called separately; S07\_1.15 is unchanged at 24; category composition is preserved in both cases.

\subsection{Strongest confirmation --- named MIBiG hits}
The paper's most specific biological claim is that three MA3\_2.13 BGCs match specific MIBiG entries. Our independent v6.1.1 \texttt{knownclusterblast} recovers all three as the top MIBiG hit for the corresponding regions, with high blast scores (11k--25k) and 18--23 gene-level hits per cluster. This cannot survive a re-run of a slightly different tool version by accident --- it requires the assembly to actually encode BGCs genuinely similar to the three MIBiG references.

\section{Genuine critique}
\label{sec:critique}

This is where a reviewer should push back. The replication is real and the verdict is defensible, but several honest weaknesses remain:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{We did not reassemble from raw reads.} By starting from the deposited FASTA, our exact matches on total bp / GC\% / contig count are essentially guaranteed --- they demonstrate deposit integrity, not sequencing pipeline reproducibility. A true PacBio + Illumina Unicycler reassembly under SRA PRJNA754006 was out of scope but would be the only way to confirm the assembly is not itself an artifact. This is a real limit on what ``REPLICATED'' means here.

\item \textbf{Annotator substitution muddies the CDS / tRNA comparison.} The paper used a RAST + PGAP hybrid; we used PGAP-only (RAST public server unreliable/deprecated). The observed 3--5\% delta in CDS and tRNA counts is a plausible annotator effect, but we did not run RAST to prove it. A pedantic reader could argue we tested annotator agreement rather than the paper's counts.

\item \textbf{PYANI-ANIb was the paper's ANI method; we substituted skani and fastANI.} The species-boundary conclusion is preserved and cross-method agreement is a genuine positive, but reproducing the paper's exact ANI numbers would require PYANI-ANIb. For the MA3 vs SCSIO 3032 comparison our fastANI is 3 percentage points higher than the paper's ANIb (80.85\% vs 77.9\%); this is well below the species threshold either way, but it is a real, unresolved numeric gap.

\item \textbf{antiSMASH version drift on MA3\_2.13 (32 $\rightarrow$ 27) is explained but not proven.} We attribute the drop to v6's stricter protocluster merging, and category patterns are preserved. Strictly speaking, we did not re-run v5.0 side-by-side to confirm the drift is entirely mechanistic; some fraction of the missing five BGCs could reflect genuine detector-rule improvements (i.e.\ five v5.0 calls might be false positives that v6 correctly discards). This is a positive story from a biology standpoint but a caveat for the ``REPLICATED'' verdict on C4.

\item \textbf{BAGEL4 / RiPPMiner / NRPSpredictor2 were not rerun.} The paper's RiPP and NRPS conclusions rest on these auxiliary tools; our verification is antiSMASH-only. Gross RiPP counts qualitatively support the paper's ``S07 is RiPP-rich'' claim, but we do not independently confirm any individual RiPP prediction.

\item \textbf{The per-gene identity claim to atratumycin NRPSs (49--57\%) was not tested.} We confirmed cluster-level MIBiG match; we did not run per-protein BLAST. The paper's specific numeric identity claims to atratumycin, triacsins, and arsono-polyketide NRPS proteins are therefore not directly reproduced.

\item \textbf{The formal species name \textit{S. profundus} is post-hoc.} The paper only proposes a novel species; the name was assigned later. Our replication treats the taxonomic novelty as a whole-genome ANI-based conclusion, which is defensible, but formal species descriptions typically require additional polyphasic evidence (phenotype, chemotaxonomy) that we neither reproduce nor evaluate.

\item \textbf{Docker container reproducibility.} We pinned \texttt{antismash/standalone:6.1.1}; databases inside the image are frozen to whatever the image build fetched. A future user pulling a re-tagged 6.1.1 image with refreshed MIBiG might see different top hits. We did not archive the container digest.

\item \textbf{LLM-judge is not a substitute for expert review.} The judge (Argo Claude Sonnet 4.6) was given a structured claims/results table and constrained vocabulary; its ``REPLICATED'' rationale is coherent but not independent evidence. A domain expert (natural-product genomics) reviewing the raw antiSMASH JSONs would strengthen the verdict.
\end{enumerate}

None of these individually threatens the verdict; collectively they narrow it to ``the biological conclusions of the paper are reproducible from deposited public data with modern tooling.'' They do \textit{not} establish ``the paper's exact pipeline reproduces its exact numbers,'' which was never our claim.
