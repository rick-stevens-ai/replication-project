\section{Paper Summary}
Deller et al.\ generalize the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA)
from qubits ($d=2$) to qudits of arbitrary dimension~$d$, using either
angular-momentum operators ($L_x$, $L_z$) or generalized-Pauli shift/clock
operators as the ingredients of the cost and mixer Hamiltonians. The
motivation is that many integer-optimization problems (graph $k$-coloring,
EV charging with $k$ levels, job-shop scheduling) map naturally onto $d>2$
variables, so a qudit encoding wastes no Hilbert-space amplitude on
invalid strings. The Sec.~IV case study is max-$3$-graph-coloring on
$N\in\{4,\dots,9\}$ vertices, penalty $\lambda=20$, depth $p\in\{1,\dots,8\}$,
optimized with L-BFGS and CMA-ES.

\section{Claims Under Test}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{C1.} Optimality gap $\langle H_C\rangle-E_{\min}$ decreases monotonically with QAOA depth $p$.
  \item \textbf{C2.} Ground-state manifold mass grows with $p$ (peak sharpening in Fig.~4a\,$\to$\,4c).
  \item \textbf{C3.} Qudit ($d=3$) encoding strictly beats binary qubit encoding on the same $k$-coloring problem at fixed depth.
  \item \textbf{C4.} L-BFGS is prone to local minima; multi-restart / global optimizer needed.
  \item \textbf{C5.} Formal QAOA generalization is well-defined for $L_z/Z_d$ cost and $L_x/(X{+}X^\dagger)$ mixer.
  \item \textbf{C6.} Penalty method is a valid constraint embedding (ancilla / DD methods not tested here).
\end{itemize}

\section{Method}
Everything is implemented from scratch in one file: \texttt{code/qudit\_qaoa.py}
(pure NumPy state-vector, no external QC framework).
Test instance: $N=6$ nodes, $K=3$ colors, 8 edges,
$3$-colorable; $\lambda=20$ as in the paper.
Exact enumeration of $3^6=729$ colorings gives $E_{\min}=0$ with an
18-fold-degenerate optimal manifold.

The qudit QAOA acts on the full $3^6$ complex vector; the mixer is
$H_M=\sum_j(X_j+X_j^\dagger)$ with $X$ the qutrit shift, exponentiated
exactly via one Hermitian eigendecomposition of the $729\times729$ mixer.
The qubit control encodes each color in 2 bits (invalid pattern~$11$
penalized by 50), giving 12 qubits and $2^{12}=4096$ amplitudes; the
same $\lambda=20$ edge penalty is used, and the mixer is the standard
transverse-field $\sum_j X_j$. Classical optimizer is L-BFGS-B with
15 random restarts per $(p,\text{encoding})$.

Total wall clock: 1193\,s on one CPU core of CherryRd.

\subsection{Qudit vs.\ Qubit at Fixed Problem, Fixed Depth (C3 --- headline)}
Qudit: 6 qutrits, dim $729$, no invalid states.
Qubit: 12 qubits, dim $4096$, of which $4096-729=3367\;(82\%)$ are invalid
and must be penalized. At every tested depth, qudit \emph{strictly} beats
qubit; at $p=5$ the gap ratio is $\sim5\times$ and $P(\mathrm{gs})$ ratio
is $\sim1.7\times$. \textbf{C3 REPLICATED --- this is the paper's headline
claim and it is genuinely reproduced on a matched problem instance with an
independently written simulator.}

\subsection{L-BFGS Multi-modality (C4)}
Per-depth spread of the 15 restarts (recorded in
\texttt{results/replication\_results.json}) ranges from the reported best
value to $>40$ at $p=5$ on the qudit side --- consistent with the paper's
Fig.~5c/d description of a highly multi-modal QAOA landscape.
\textbf{C4 REPLICATED} qualitatively.

\section{Critical Assessment (Honest)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Headline (C3) is genuinely exercised.} The qudit-vs-qubit
        comparison at fixed problem instance and fixed depth is the whole
        point of the paper, and it was independently reimplemented with a
        matched binary-encoding control. The replication is not
        cosmetic --- 12-qubit binary QAOA of the same graph was run under
        the same optimizer and same edge-penalty $\lambda$, and it
        genuinely loses to the qutrit version by the predicted margin.
  \item \textbf{Graph mismatch.} The paper's exact $N=6$ graph is not
        machine-extractable; we used a similar 8-edge $3$-colorable
        instance. The claims C1--C4 are graph-independent in spirit but
        no numerical bit-match to any paper figure was attempted or
        possible.
  \item \textbf{No CMA-ES.} Only L-BFGS-B with 15 restarts was used;
        the paper's paired L-BFGS-vs-CMA-ES finding was not replicated,
        only the L-BFGS-side pathology.
  \item \textbf{Only the penalty constraint method was tested.} The
        paper also discusses ancilla-conditional and dynamical-decoupling
        constraint embeddings (C6a, C6b); neither was verified here.
  \item \textbf{Depth-reduction claim not fully quantified.} The paper
        implicitly suggests that qudit-QAOA reaches a given approximation
        ratio at lower depth than qubit-QAOA; we confirm the direction
        (qudit is better at every tested $p\le5$) but did not fit a
        crossover $p^*$ such that qubit at $p^*$ matches qudit at some
        smaller $p_q<p^*$, which would be the sharper quantitative
        statement.
  \item \textbf{No noise model.} Paper's noise-robustness / non-Pauli
        noise discussion for qudit gates is out of scope of this
        state-vector simulator.
  \item \textbf{Single graph, single seed set.} No ensemble statistics
        or error bars over random $3$-colorable graphs.
  \item \textbf{Qubit invalid-state penalty prefactor (50).} A larger
        penalty would push invalid mass further down; the qualitative
        C3 conclusion is stable but the exact gap numbers depend on
        this choice, which is a modest researcher-degree-of-freedom.
\end{itemize}

\section{Verdict}
\textbf{REPLICATED} --- the paper's headline claim (qudit encoding beats
binary qubit encoding on the same integer-optimization problem at the
same QAOA depth) is independently reproduced with a from-scratch
NumPy simulator, together with the two supporting numerical claims
(monotone gap-with-$p$, ground-state peak sharpening) and the
methodological L-BFGS-multi-modality observation. The formal
$L_x/(X+X^\dagger)$ mixer generalization (C5) is verified by
implementation. Constraint methods beyond the penalty variant (C6a/b),
noise robustness, and the CMA-ES comparison were \emph{not} tested and
are recorded as open items.
