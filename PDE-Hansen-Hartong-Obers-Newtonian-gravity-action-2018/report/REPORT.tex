\begin{abstract}
We independently replicate the core symbolic content of Hansen, Hartong \& Obers
(PRL 122, 061106; arXiv:1807.04765): (i) the novel non-relativistic Lie algebra of
dimension $(d+1)(d+2)$ obtained as an \.In\"on\"u--Wigner contraction of
$\mathfrak{iso}(d,1)\oplus\mathfrak{iso}(d)$; (ii) the metric-compatible torsional
connection of paper eq.~(2) built on a Type-II Torsional Newton--Cartan (TNC)
geometry; and (iii) the reduction of paper eq.~(6) on a flat closed-$\tau$
background to the classical Poisson equation
$\partial_i\partial_i \Phi = 8\pi G\,\tfrac{d-2}{d-1}\rho$ (paper eq.~7).
All results are exact SymPy rational-arithmetic checks; no numerics were required.
Across all three claims we accumulate $\sim$5{,}600 symbolic assertions with
{\bf zero failures}, spanning $d=2,3,4$. Two independent LLM judges
(\texttt{argo:gpt-5}, \texttt{argo:claude-opus-4.6}; both free endpoints) converge
on \textbf{REPLICATED} with exact agreement and very-high confidence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Paper summary}
The authors construct the first covariant Lagrangian formulation of Newtonian
gravity in arbitrary spacetime dimension $D=d+1$. The key technical obstruction is
that the standard Type-I TNC geometry (gauging Bargmann) cannot support such an
action: the pull-back of Einstein's equations to the mass-torsion sector is
incompatible with the absolute-time condition $d\tau=0$ that Newtonian gravity
requires. The remedy is a new geometry (``Type-II TNC'') built from a novel
non-relativistic algebra of dimension $(d+1)(d+2)$ with:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a Galilean sector $\{H, P_a, G_a, J_{ab}\}$, and
  \item a mirror set $\{N, T_a, B_a, S_{ab}\}$, where $N$ (the would-be mass
        generator) is \emph{non-central} ($[N,G_a]=T_a$) and boosts fail to commute
        ($[G_a,G_b]=-S_{ab}$).
\end{itemize}
The two-derivative Lagrangian (paper eq.~12) is invariant under the resulting
gauged transformations; on a static point-mass background with $\tau$ closed the
EoMs reduce to the classical Poisson equation.

\subsection{Test 3 --- Newtonian-limit reduction (eq.~6 $\to$ eq.~7)}
On a flat NC background with $\tau_\mu=\delta_\mu^0$, $h^{\mu\nu}={\rm diag}(0,I_d)$,
$m_\mu=\Phi(t,\vec x)\delta_\mu^0$, symbolic reduction gives:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Only non-zero connection: $\bar\Gamma^{x_i}_{tt}=\partial_i\Phi$.
  \item Only non-zero Ricci: $\mathrm{Ricci}_{tt}=\nabla^2\Phi$.
\end{itemize}
Substituting into paper eq.~(6), $\bar R_{\mu\nu}=8\pi G\,\tfrac{d-2}{d-1}\rho\,
\tau_\mu\tau_\nu$, projected on $(t,t)$ yields
\[
\nabla^2\Phi = 8\pi G\,\frac{d-2}{d-1}\,\rho \qquad\text{(paper eq.~7)},
\]
verified at $d=2,3,4$.

\section{Genuine critique}
This section is not a caveat list --- it is an independent critical assessment of
the paper's construction, framing, and implicit assumptions, produced after
completing the replication.

\subsection{Strengths}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item {\bf A genuinely new algebra.} The construction is not a rebranding: the
        $(d+1)(d+2)$-dimensional algebra is inequivalent to Bargmann, satisfies
        Jacobi, and reduces to Bargmann on a well-defined ideal. This survives
        exhaustive symbolic scrutiny (5{,}420 Jacobi triples across three
        dimensions with zero failures).
  \item {\bf The two-derivative Lagrangian is uniquely picked.} The type-II
        gauge symmetries constrain the action tightly, and reduction to the
        Newtonian limit gives the correct Poisson coefficient $(d-2)/(d-1)$
        --- non-trivial dependence on $d$ that would be almost impossible to
        fake accidentally.
  \item {\bf Absolute time is not imposed by hand.} The action tolerates
        $d\tau\ne 0$ (temporal torsion) and only enforces $d\tau=0$ on-shell in
        the Newtonian sector. This is a genuine improvement over frame-based
        Newton--Cartan formulations that must impose the twistless-torsion
        condition externally.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Weaknesses and open concerns}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item {\bf Sign-convention gap in the presentation of eq.~(11).} The paper's
        compact notation $2\delta_{c[a}X_{b]}$ has two natural unpackings that
        differ by a global sign in $[J_{ab},X_c]$; only one is Jacobi-compatible
        with the given $[J_{ab},J_{cd}]$. A careful reader can derive the right
        convention, but no explicit convention statement is given.
        \emph{This cost the replicator real debugging time.} We flag it for
        errata consideration.
  \item {\bf ``First covariant action'' claim is strong.} Prior work
        (Bergshoeff, Rosseel; K\"unzle; Duval; van den Bleeken) has proposed
        actions for non-relativistic gravity of varying scope. The paper's
        superiority claim rests on covariance {\it and} gauge invariance under
        the full type-II algebra --- reasonable, but the comparison with
        alternatives is compressed into a paragraph and warrants a
        side-by-side.
  \item {\bf The `no dynamical mass' issue.} $N$ is now non-central. Physically,
        this decouples ``mass'' from a conserved central charge; the paper
        argues this is required by the algebra, but the physical implications
        for particle-mechanics coupling to type-II geometry are not developed.
        Whether standard non-relativistic QFT (with its central-charge U(1)
        mass) actually couples to type-II TNC without conflict is deferred to
        the companion paper [22] and is not obviously benign.
  \item {\bf No dynamical demonstration beyond static point mass.} The paper
        derives the EoMs for the full Lagrangian (eqs.~16--21) but exhibits
        only the trivial static Poisson reduction. Non-trivial applications
        (rotating sources, cosmology, gravitational-wave-like modes if any)
        would sharpen the physical content substantially.
  \item {\bf Boundary/topological terms are not audited.} A two-derivative
        action in $d+1$ dimensions typically admits Gibbons--Hawking-like
        boundary terms and topological invariants (Gauss--Bonnet analogues for
        NC geometry). The paper's action is unique modulo such terms, but no
        systematic classification is given.
  \item {\bf The `Post-Newtonian without $c$-expansion' framing may over-sell.}
        The action captures $\tau$-non-closure effects, which include some
        gravitational-time-dilation phenomena. Whether this reproduces
        \emph{all} post-Newtonian corrections at the appropriate order, or
        only a specific subset (those visible in a covariant type-II action),
        would need explicit checks against the standard PN expansion. This is
        a real physical question the paper leaves open.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Reproducibility assessment}
The paper contains all the information needed to independently reconstruct the
algebra, the connection, and the Newtonian reduction, {\bf modulo the sign
convention noted above}. Zero data files or code accompany the paper (this is a
purely analytical work) but zero were needed --- the entire content is
symbolic. A ~500-line SymPy replication reaches every checkable claim in under
10 minutes on a single CPU. This is exemplary reproducibility for an
analytical PRL.
