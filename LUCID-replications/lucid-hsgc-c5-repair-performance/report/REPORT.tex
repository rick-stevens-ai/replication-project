\begin{abstract}
This report documents the replication attempt of Sakata et al.\ 2021 (Cancers 13(23):6046,
DOI 10.3390/cancers13236046), a two-part study combining Geant4-DNA Monte Carlo
track-structure simulation of proton-induced DNA damage with a Two-Lesion Kinetics
(TLK) ODE curve-fit of cell survival fraction (SF) and DNA fragment-activity-released
(FAR) kinetics for the HSGc-C5 human salivary-gland carcinoma line under 70~MeV
protons. \textbf{Verdict: PARTIAL.} The TLK ODE curve-fit half is REPLICATED
(SF $R^2=0.96$, FAR $R^2=0.96$ on our joint refit; SF $R^2=0.91$, FAR $R^2=0.72$ on
the paper's Table~1 verbatim); the Geant4-DNA Monte Carlo half is NOT re-executed;
the paper's Appendix~A NB1RGB Table~A1 is NOT reproducible verbatim against the
published supplement data. Details, honest critique, and 5 open questions follow.
\end{abstract}

\section{What was replicated}
\subsection{TLK ODE system}
\begin{align}
\frac{dL_1}{dt} &= D(t)\,Y\,\Sigma_1 - \lambda_1 L_1 - \eta L_1 (L_1+L_2) \\
\frac{dL_2}{dt} &= D(t)\,Y\,\Sigma_2 - \lambda_2 L_2 - \eta L_2 (L_1+L_2) \\
\frac{dL_f}{dt} &= \beta_1 \lambda_1 L_1 + \beta_2 \lambda_2 L_2 + \gamma \eta (L_1+L_2)^2 \\
\mathrm{SF}(t) &= \exp(-L_f(t))
\end{align}
Solver: SciPy \texttt{solve\_ivp} (LSODA), two-phase integration during and after
irradiation. Cross-checked against RK4 to $10^{-6}$ relative tolerance.
The paper's Eq 6 has a PDF rendering artifact (\texttt{SF = ln(-Lf)}); the intended
Stewart-2001 form $\mathrm{SF}=\exp(-L_f)$ is what we use.

\subsection{Forward run vs Table 1}
Using paper's $\lambda_1{=}3.36$, $\lambda_2{=}9.9{\times}10^{-3}$,
$\eta{=}4.58{\times}10^{-6}$, $\beta_1{=}0$, $\beta_2{=}2.75{\times}10^{-2}$,
$\gamma{=}0.39$, and DSB yields recovered from Section 3.2/Discussion:
$\Sigma_1{=}4.11,\Sigma_2{=}1.04$ (0~mm PMMA); $\Sigma_1{=}4.69,\Sigma_2{=}1.53$
(32~mm PMMA). Results: SF $R^2 = 0.91$; FAR $R^2 = 0.72$.

\subsection{Inverse fit}
Joint nonlinear least squares (TRF, log-SF residuals) over 21 SF+FAR points,
$\beta_1$ fixed to 0. Converged in 22 evaluations. Recovers qualitative parameter
ordering but individual constants differ from paper by factors $0.3\text{--}5.5\times$,
consistent with documented TLK parameter degeneracy (only $\gamma\eta$ and
$\beta_2\lambda_2$ products are tightly constrained by SF+FAR alone).

\subsection{Re-pass arithmetic checks (M1--M11)}
Half-life arithmetic within 2.5\% of the paper's rounded values; Bragg-peak location
exactly at 33~mm as claimed; 6 of 7 lethality/DSB-fraction arithmetic claims pass;
the ``43\% complex-DSB increase'' claim gives $40.5\%$ from the paper's own numbers
($(1.04-0.74)/0.74=0.4054$) --- honest near-miss, paper-internal inconsistency.

\section{What was NOT replicated: Genuine Critique}

\subsection*{Critique 1: Geant4-DNA Monte Carlo half of the paper is NOT re-executed.}
The paper is fundamentally a \textbf{Monte-Carlo-plus-TLK} performance evaluation.
The Monte Carlo half --- 56,400 (0~mm) + 11,400 (32~mm) protons transported through
a Geant4-DNA fractal chromatin geometry with per-event single-strand and
double-strand break scoring per the Nikjoo clustering definition --- was
\textbf{not re-run}. We consume the paper's reported per-Gy/Gbp DSB yields
directly. Re-running would require Geant4 11+, the \texttt{molecularDNA} example,
the full DNA geometry inputs, and CPU-days. The paper does not publish per-event
ROOT trees, so the Monte Carlo half cannot be validated without re-execution.
This is the primary reason the verdict is PARTIAL and not REPLICATED.

\subsection*{Critique 2: The reference ``ground truth'' repair-performance benchmark was not re-generated.}
The paper's headline is a \emph{repair-performance evaluation}: how well the
TLK model, seeded by Geant4-DNA-simulated DSB yields, predicts measured
HSGc-C5 SF and FAR. The ``performance'' benchmark implicitly compares
paper predictions against the experimental cell-culture / electrophoresis
measurements of Sakata's team (Section 2.2.1). We did not re-generate the
ground-truth dataset (out of scope --- wet-lab). We only re-fit against the
paper's published supplement CSVs, i.e., we replicate the paper's fit
quality but not the underlying measurement.

\subsection*{Critique 3: Paper's own Appendix A Table A1 does not reproduce Figure A1 against paper's own supplement data.}
Forward-running the paper's published Table A1 NB1RGB parameters against the
paper's published NB1 SF supplement rows yields SF $R^2 = -3.20$ --- worse than
predicting the mean. FAR $R^2 = 0.38$. This is a genuine reproducibility red
flag: either the paper used a different (unpublished) $\Sigma$ for NB1RGB, or
Table A1 was typeset incorrectly (the published $\lambda_1{=}33062.9\,\mathrm{h}^{-1}$
implies a $75\,\mathrm{ms}$ fast-repair half-life which the paper itself flags as
non-physical), or the optimization stopped at a flat local minimum. This is a
finding \emph{about the paper}, not about our replication --- but it means
Appendix A cannot be certified replicated.

\subsection*{Critique 4: TLK parameter degeneracy means our ``better'' refit is not necessarily correct.}
Our joint SF+FAR NLS refit achieves $R^2{=}0.96$ (vs paper's Table 1 forward run
at $R^2{=}0.91,0.72$). Individual parameter values differ from the paper by
factors $0.3\text{--}5.5\times$. Only the products $\gamma\eta$ and $\beta_2\lambda_2$
are identifiable. Our ``better'' fit does not mean the paper is wrong, only that
the parameter space is under-determined by SF+FAR data alone. The paper likely
used additional Ceres-Solver regularization or V79-prior seeding we do not have.

\subsection*{Critique 5: Arithmetic self-inconsistency in the paper.}
The paper claims complex DSBs increase 43\% from 0~mm to 32~mm PMMA
$((1.04-0.74)/0.74)$. The correct arithmetic is $40.5\%$. Small
(2.5 percentage points), but it is a paper-side error we surface.

\subsection*{Critique 6: No third-party validation.}
All comparisons are against the paper's own published supplement data.
We inherit any measurement systematics of the original team.
